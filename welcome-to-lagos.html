🌆 Welcome to Lagos

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 40 min read

🌆 Chapter One – First Day in Lagos

The first thing Maya noticed about Lagos was the noise.

Cars were everywhere.

Buses were shouting for passengers.

People were walking fast.

Some were selling food.

Some were selling clothes.

Some were just looking for a way to survive the day.

Maya stood beside her small bag and looked around.

She had never seen a place like this before.

She came from a quiet town where everybody knew everybody.

Lagos was different.

Nobody seemed to have time for anybody.

But Maya was excited.

She believed Lagos was where her life would finally change.

She was twenty-four.

She had only a little money in her bag.

Her plan was simple.

Find a job.

Get a small room.

Work hard.

Send money home.

Then build something for herself.

She did not know how hard Lagos could be.

At the bus stop, she met a young woman named Chloe.

Chloe noticed Maya looking confused.

"First time in Lagos?"

Maya smiled.

"Is it that obvious?"

Chloe laughed.

"Very obvious."

They both laughed.

Chloe helped her find the right bus.

Before Maya entered, Chloe gave her one warning.

"Keep your phone and money close."

Maya nodded.

"Thank you."

The bus was packed.

Maya squeezed herself between two passengers.

The driver started moving before she was even ready.

She held the seat tightly.

People shouted.

The driver shouted back.

Someone was playing music from his phone.

Another passenger was complaining about traffic.

Maya looked through the window.

Tall buildings stood beside small shops.

Fancy cars moved beside old buses.

People were everywhere.

She smiled.

This was Lagos.

It was messy.

It was loud.

But it was alive.

By evening, Maya reached the place where she planned to stay.

The room was much smaller than she expected.

The wall had old marks.

The fan made a strange sound.

The window barely opened.

Maya looked at the room for a long time.

Then she dropped her bag.

"I will manage."

She had no choice.

The next morning, she wore her best clothes and started looking for work.

She walked from one office to another.

She dropped her CV.

She waited.

She called numbers.

She heard the same words again and again.

"We will get back to you."

Nobody called.

After three days, her money was already getting low.

Maya sat at a small roadside food spot.

She ordered the cheapest meal on the menu.

A young man sitting nearby noticed her.

His name was Ryan.

"You look like someone who just entered Lagos."

Maya laughed.

"Everybody keeps saying that."

Ryan smiled.

"Because Lagos always exposes newcomers."

Maya laughed again.

Ryan introduced her to two of his friends, Daniel and Jason.

They were all trying to survive in the city.

Daniel worked at a phone shop.

Jason was learning photography.

Ryan did small jobs wherever he could find them.

They became friends quickly.

They joked about traffic.

They talked about jobs.

They complained about rent.

They laughed about the crazy things they saw every day.

For the first time since arriving, Maya did not feel completely alone.

But Lagos was not finished testing her.

That night, she returned to her room tired and hungry.

She sat on the edge of her bed.

Her phone had no new message.

No job.

No good news.

She looked at the little money left in her wallet.

It was not much.

Maya almost cried.

Then she remembered why she came.

She wiped her face.

"I did not come here to give up."

She switched off the light.

Outside, Lagos was still awake.

Cars were still moving.

People were still talking.

Music was still playing somewhere.

And Maya had no idea that one of the friends she had just met would one day change her life completely.

But for now, she was just another young woman trying to find her place in Lagos.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🚌 Chapter Two – Lagos Does Not Wait

Maya woke up before her alarm.

The room was already hot.

The fan was making the same strange sound.

She checked her phone.

Still no job message.

She sighed and got up.

There was only enough money left for a few days.

She could not afford to sit and wait.

She dressed quickly and left the house.

Lagos was already moving.

People were rushing to work.

Buses were filling up.

Danfo drivers were calling passengers.

Maya stood by the roadside and watched everything.

She still found the city interesting.

There was always something happening.

A woman was selling breakfast.

A man was fixing a car.

A young boy was selling bottled water between cars.

Everyone seemed to have a plan.

Maya wanted one too.

She visited another office.

Then another.

At the third place, a man collected her CV.

"We may call you."

Maya smiled.

"Thank you, sir."

She walked outside feeling hopeful.

Five minutes later, she received a message.

We are not moving forward with your application.

Maya stopped walking.

She stared at the screen.

Then she put the phone inside her bag.

"Lagos."

She shook her head and continued walking.

By afternoon, she met Chloe again.

Chloe was standing beside a small shop.

"Maya!"

Maya turned and smiled.

"Chloe!"

They hugged.

Chloe asked about the job search.

Maya laughed.

"Lagos is showing me pepper."

Chloe laughed loudly.

"Don't worry. You will survive."

They sat together for a while.

Chloe told her about a small fashion store that needed someone.

"Go there tomorrow."

"Ask for Mrs. Linda."

Maya wrote the address down.

"Thank you."

That evening, Maya met Ryan, Daniel, and Jason.

They were sitting outside a small food spot.

Ryan saw her face.

"Another bad day?"

Maya sat down.

"Very bad."

Daniel laughed.

"Welcome to Lagos."

Everyone laughed.

Jason bought her a bottle of water.

"Tomorrow can be better."

Maya looked at him.

"I hope so."

The four friends talked until the evening became dark.

They talked about their dreams.

Ryan wanted to open a transport company.

Daniel wanted his own phone shop.

Jason wanted to become a famous photographer.

Maya wanted to build a career and have a comfortable life.

For a moment, Lagos did not feel so hard.

She had people around her.

People who understood the struggle.

The next morning, Maya went to the fashion store.

She met Mrs. Linda.

The woman looked at her CV.

"Have you worked in fashion before?"

Maya shook her head.

"No, ma."

Mrs. Linda looked at her again.

"But you know how to use a computer?"

Maya nodded.

"Yes, ma."

Mrs. Linda smiled.

"Come tomorrow."

Maya could not believe it.

She smiled.

"Thank you so much, ma."

She walked out of the store with a different feeling.

It was not a big job.

The pay was not much.

But it was a beginning.

She called Chloe first.

"I got the job!"

Chloe screamed with excitement.

"I told you!"

Maya laughed.

Later that evening, she told the boys.

Ryan stood up and clapped.

"See? Lagos has finally smiled at you."

Maya laughed.

"Don't celebrate yet. I have not received my first salary."

Daniel shook his head.

"You are already thinking like a Lagos girl."

They all laughed.

But Maya knew something had changed.

She had taken her first step.

She still had little money.

She still had a tiny room.

She still had a long way to go.

But she was working now.

And every morning when she entered the busy Lagos streets, she reminded herself of one thing.

"I came here to make it."

She did not know that the hardest part of her journey was still ahead.

She also did not know that among the people she had met, one person would soon open a door she never expected.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌇 Chapter Three – The City Has Two Faces

Maya was getting used to Lagos.

She knew the bus routes better now.

She knew where to buy cheap food.

She knew which roads to avoid when traffic was bad.

She even knew some of the people who sold food near her workplace.

Every morning, she entered the fashion store before nine.

She arranged clothes.

She answered customers.

She used the computer to record sales.

The work was tiring, but Maya was happy.

At least she was earning something.

Her first salary came after a few weeks.

It was not much.

But when Maya saw the money in her account, she smiled for a long time.

"My first Lagos money."

She sent some home.

She paid part of her rent.

Then she bought herself a simple meal.

For the first time since she arrived, she felt proud.

But Lagos had another side.

One Friday evening, Maya left work late.

The traffic was terrible.

Cars were barely moving.

Buses were full.

People were tired.

Some passengers were arguing with drivers.

Maya stood by the roadside for almost an hour.

Her legs were hurting.

The sky became dark.

She finally entered a crowded bus.

The journey that normally took thirty minutes lasted almost two hours.

When she finally reached home, she was exhausted.

She dropped her bag and sat on the floor.

"Why is Lagos like this?"

She laughed at herself.

The next morning, she woke up smiling again.

That was Lagos.

One day could make you tired.

Another day could make you happy.

Sometimes both happened on the same day.

That weekend, Maya met her friends.

Ryan brought good news.

"I got a new job."

Daniel jumped up.

"Seriously?"

Ryan nodded.

Jason congratulated him.

Maya smiled.

"Things are finally moving."

They decided to celebrate with food.

They found a small place where music was playing.

People were laughing.

Friends were sitting together.

Some were taking pictures.

Maya looked around.

This was another Lagos.

The Lagos that made people forget their problems for a few hours.

She enjoyed herself.

They talked about everything.

Work.

Money.

Relationships.

Future plans.

Maya told them about her dream of starting something of her own one day.

Ryan looked at her.

"You should start learning more about online business."

Maya nodded.

"I have been thinking about it."

Jason smiled.

"Then start."

Maya laughed.

"With what money?"

Everyone laughed.

But Jason became serious.

"You don't always need money to start learning."

Maya looked at him.

Those words stayed in her mind.

Over the next few weeks, Maya began learning in her free time.

She watched videos.

She read.

She practiced on her phone.

She asked questions.

Sometimes she became confused.

Sometimes she wanted to stop.

But she continued.

One evening, something happened at the fashion store.

A customer came in looking for someone who could help manage the store's online page.

Maya overheard the conversation.

She quietly told the customer that she was learning about online business.

The woman looked interested.

"Can you help us with our page?"

Maya became nervous.

"I can try."

The woman smiled.

"That is enough for now."

Maya went home that evening thinking about what had happened.

Maybe Lagos was not only about surviving.

Maybe it was also about noticing opportunities.

She called Jason.

"Something happened today."

Jason listened.

When Maya finished, he laughed.

"I told you to start learning."

Maya smiled.

"Maybe you were right."

Jason became quiet for a moment.

"Maybe this is your beginning."

Maya looked outside her window.

The streets were still busy.

Lights were shining from shops.

Cars were moving.

People were still going somewhere.

Lagos had frustrated her.

Lagos had also introduced her to people.

And without knowing it, Maya was getting closer to the opportunity that would change her life.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🚪 Chapter Four – The Door Nobody Saw

Maya's small work at the fashion store was slowly getting better.

The woman who asked for help with the store's online page liked what Maya did.

Maya took pictures of the clothes.

She posted them online.

She replied to customers.

She also helped arrange the orders.

It was not a big business yet.

But Maya was learning something new every day.

One evening, Jason came to see her.

He looked excited.

"Maya, I want you to meet someone."

Maya looked at him.

"Who?"

"Just come with me."

They walked to a small office not far from where Maya worked.

A man was sitting inside.

His name was Michael.

He ran a small digital business with a few young people.

Jason had met him through his photography work.

Michael looked at Maya.

"So you are Maya."

Maya smiled.

"Yes, sir."

Jason explained what she had been doing at the fashion store.

Michael looked interested.

"How long have you been doing this?"

"Not very long."

"But I am learning."

Michael smiled.

"That is what I like to hear."

He showed Maya some of the work his team was doing.

Maya watched carefully.

She asked questions.

Michael noticed that she was not afraid to ask when she did not understand something.

"You can learn fast if you keep this attitude."

Maya smiled.

Before she left, Michael gave her a small task.

"Try this and send it to me tomorrow."

Maya nodded.

"I will."

On her way home, she could not stop smiling.

Jason looked at her.

"Why are you smiling like that?"

Maya laughed.

"I don't know."

"I just feel like something good is coming."

Jason smiled.

"Keep that feeling."

That night, Maya worked on the task.

She made mistakes.

She corrected them.

She tried again.

She almost gave up around midnight.

Then she remembered the first day she arrived in Lagos.

She remembered how little money she had.

She remembered sitting alone in that tiny room.

She remembered telling herself that she would not give up.

She opened her laptop again.

She finished the work.

The next morning, she sent it to Michael.

A few hours later, her phone rang.

"Hello, Michael."

"I saw your work."

Maya became nervous.

"Is it okay?"

Michael laughed.

"It is not perfect."

Maya became quiet.

Then he continued.

"But you have something."

"You are willing to learn."

Maya smiled.

"Thank you."

Michael offered her a chance to work with his team.

The pay was better than what she was earning at the fashion store.

There was also room to learn.

Maya could not believe it.

She thanked him several times.

After the call ended, she called Jason.

"I got it!"

Jason laughed.

"I knew you would."

Maya became quiet.

"Jason."

"Yes?"

"Thank you."

Jason smiled.

"You did the work. I only introduced you."

Maya shook her head.

"Sometimes an introduction is all someone needs."

That evening, Maya sat outside her room.

She looked at the busy Lagos streets.

The same city that had frustrated her was now giving her another chance.

She knew she still had a long way to go.

But for the first time, she could see a better future.

And she knew one thing for sure.

The people you meet can change your journey.

But you still have to do the walking.

🌙 End of Chapter Four

🌆 Chapter Five – A Better Life

Maya's life started changing.

She left the fashion store and joined Michael's team.

The work was not easy.

She still had plenty to learn.

Some days were stressful.

Some clients were difficult.

Sometimes she stayed awake late to finish her work.

But this time, the struggle felt different.

She was learning.

She was earning better.

And she could finally see where she was going.

Her first month with Michael's team was busy.

She worked with Sarah, a young designer on the team.

Sarah became one of Maya's closest friends.

"You are improving fast."

Maya smiled.

"I still make plenty mistakes."

"Everyone does."

Sarah helped Maya understand some of the things she found difficult.

Maya also helped Sarah whenever she could.

Their friendship grew naturally.

Meanwhile, Ryan, Daniel, Jason, and Chloe were still part of her life.

They often met after work.

Sometimes they ate together.

Sometimes they just sat around and talked.

Everyone was slowly moving forward.

Ryan had started saving for his transport business.

Daniel was preparing to open his own phone shop.

Jason was getting more photography jobs.

Chloe had started selling clothes online.

Maya was proud of all of them.

One Saturday, Maya visited her old room.

She stood at the doorway for a moment.

The small room brought back many memories.

She remembered the nights she cried.

She remembered counting the little money in her wallet.

She remembered wondering if she had made the right decision by coming to Lagos.

Now things were different.

She had moved into a better place.

She could afford good food.

She could send money home.

She could even save something for herself.

Maya smiled.

"I actually made it this far."

That evening, she called her mother.

"Mum, how are you?"

"I'm fine, my daughter."

Maya told her everything.

Her new job.

Her new friends.

The people who had helped her.

Her mother became quiet for a moment.

"I always knew you would find your way."

Maya smiled.

"I was not so sure."

Her mother laughed.

"Sometimes you just need to keep moving."

Those words stayed with Maya.

A few weeks later, Michael called her into his office.

"I have something for you."

Maya sat down.

Michael handed her a document.

It was a new position in the company.

Maya looked at it carefully.

"You want me to handle this?"

Michael nodded.

"You have earned it."

Maya smiled.

She thanked him.

After leaving the office, she called Jason.

"Jason!"

"What happened?"

"I got promoted."

Jason laughed.

"I told you Lagos would eventually smile at you."

Maya laughed too.

She looked around at the busy streets.

People were rushing.

Cars were honking.

Traders were calling customers.

Music was coming from different places.

Lagos was still noisy.

Lagos was still stressful.

The traffic was still terrible.

Rent was still expensive.

Nothing about the city had suddenly become perfect.

But Maya had changed.

She had learned how to survive.

She had learned how to spot opportunities.

She had learned that good friends could make a difficult journey easier.

And she had learned that one person believing in you can sometimes open a door you could never open alone.

For Maya, that person had been Jason.

He had not given her money.

He had not solved all her problems.

He simply connected her to someone who saw what she could become.

Maya smiled as she continued walking.

Her Lagos story was far from over.

But she was no longer the frightened young woman standing at a bus stop with one small bag.

She had found her place.

And she was ready for whatever came next.
