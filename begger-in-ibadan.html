👵🏾 The Beggar of Ibadan

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🧺 Chapter One – The Beggar of Molete

Sometimes, not everyone who stretches out a hand for help truly needs it. Some people may come to you looking helpless, while hiding something you cannot see.

That was what happened many years ago in the busy city of Ibadan.

Ibadan was a beautiful and busy city. Its roads were always filled with cars, buses, traders, workers and people going about their daily lives.

Near Molete bus stop, there was a woman whom almost everyone knew.

People called her Iyalajo.

Nobody knew her real name.

Nobody knew where she came from.

Nobody knew if she had a family somewhere.

She simply appeared to have been sitting at that same place for many years.

Iyalajo always sat on an old mat beside the road.

She looked very poor.

Her clothes were old and torn. Her feet were cracked from walking barefoot on the dusty roads, and her body looked thin and weak.

Whenever people passed her, she stretched out her hand.

“Please, help me.”

“Anything you have.”

“God will bless you.”

Those were the words people heard from her every day.

Because she looked helpless, many people felt sorry for her.

They gave her money.

Some gave her food.

Others stopped to ask if she needed clothes or medicine.

But there was something strange about Iyalajo that most people never noticed.

Her eyes.

Her eyes were always watching.

Whenever someone gave her money, she looked carefully at the person's face.

It was almost as if she was studying the person.

But nobody paid much attention to it.

People were too busy feeling sorry for the poor woman sitting beside the road.

One morning, a man called Baba Remy passed through Molete on his way to his shop at Bodija Market.

Baba Remy was a hardworking trader.

He had spent many years building his provisions business.

He had two freezers, shelves filled with goods and a small generator that he had worked hard to pay for.

He was not a rich man, but he was doing well.

As Baba Remy passed Iyalajo that morning, he stopped.

He looked at her clothes.

He looked at her cracked feet.

Then he reached into his pocket.

He brought out 200 naira and placed it in her hand.

“Take this,” he said. “It is not much, but manage it.”

Iyalajo slowly looked up at him.

Her eyes stayed on his face for a few seconds.

Then she looked down at the money.

She did not smile.

She did not even say thank you.

Baba Remy thought nothing of it.

He continued to his shop.

That was the last morning his business was completely normal.

At first, one of his freezers stopped working.

He spent money repairing it.

Before he could recover from that expense, a supplier collected a large amount of money from him and disappeared.

Then some of his goods began to spoil.

Customers who had been buying from him for years slowly stopped coming.

Within a few months, the shop that had taken Baba Remy ten years to build was almost empty.

He sat inside his shop one evening, looking at the empty shelves.

He could not understand what had happened.

“How did everything disappear so quickly?” he asked himself.

He had worked hard.

He had done everything he knew to do.

Yet somehow, everything was falling apart.

Then there was Mama Tunde.

Mama Tunde was seven months pregnant.

She had just returned from the hospital after her antenatal checkup.

The doctor had told her that everything was fine.

Her baby was healthy.

She was happy.

As she passed through Molete, she saw Iyalajo sitting by the roadside.

Mama Tunde felt pity for her.

She reached into her purse and brought out 500 naira.

“Please, take this,” she said.

Iyalajo accepted the money.

Again, she looked directly into the woman's eyes.

Mama Tunde smiled and continued home.

But not long after that day, something terrible happened.

Mama Tunde lost her baby.

She was devastated.

Her family tried to comfort her, but she could not understand why it had happened.

Her pregnancy had been going well.

The doctors had said the baby was healthy.

There had been no warning.

She cried for days.

Then something even stranger happened.

Mama Tunde's sister began talking to other women in the area.

She discovered that Mama Tunde was not the only woman who had suffered a strange loss after giving money to Iyalajo.

There was Kunle, a young man who had been searching for work for months.

One afternoon, Kunle passed Iyalajo and gave her 100 naira.

Three days later, he received a call from a company that had already agreed to employ him.

They suddenly cancelled the job.

Kunle was confused.

“Why would they cancel it after everything was already settled?” he asked.

He had no answer.

There was also Mrs. Adeyemi, a hardworking woman who owned a small business.

She gave Iyalajo some money one morning.

Not long after, her biggest customer left her business and started buying from someone else.

Her sales dropped badly.

Then there was Biodun.

Biodun was a student preparing for his WAEC examination.

He was doing well in school and everyone expected him to pass.

One day, he gave Iyalajo some money while passing the roadside.

When his examination results came out, they were terrible.

Subjects he had always performed well in suddenly became his worst results.

His parents could not understand it.

His teachers were surprised.

But one thing connected all these people.

They had all given money to Iyalajo.

At first, nobody wanted to believe it.

People told themselves it was just coincidence.

“Maybe Baba Remy's business was simply unlucky.”

“Maybe Mama Tunde's loss was just something that happened.”

“Maybe Kunle was never meant to get that job.”

“Maybe Mrs. Adeyemi's customer simply found another seller.”

“Maybe Biodun just did badly in his exams.”

Everyone had an explanation.

And because there was no proof, people continued helping Iyalajo.

She remained at Molete.

Every morning, she returned to the same roadside.

She wore the same torn clothes.

She stretched out the same hand.

And people continued giving.

But nobody knew what happened after the sun went down.

Because when darkness covered the streets of Ibadan, Iyalajo was no longer the weak old beggar everyone thought she was.

And what she did with the money she collected was a secret that would soon shock the entire community.

🌙 End of Chapter One

👁️ Chapter Two – The Blessings She Stole

Every morning, Iyalajo returned to the same place at Molete bus stop.

She sat on her old mat, stretched out her hand and waited.

People still felt sorry for her.

They saw an old woman who looked hungry and forgotten.

They did not know that every night, something very different was happening.

When the streets became quiet and most people had gone home, Iyalajo would pick up her old mat and leave the roadside.

She did not walk like a weak woman anymore.

Her back became straight.

Her steps became firm.

She walked to a compound hidden away from the busy road.

When she entered the compound, she closed the gate behind her.

Then she removed the torn clothes she wore during the day.

She would sit down and bring out the money people had given her.

She arranged the money carefully in front of her.

Then she began to speak words that nobody outside the compound could hear.

As she spoke, the atmosphere around her changed.

The air became heavy.

There was a strange smell, almost like something burning.

She placed her hands over the money and continued speaking.

The money was not important to her because of its value.

She wanted something else.

She wanted the goodwill behind it.

She wanted the blessings connected to the kindness of the people who had given it.

She believed that when people gave her money with a good heart, something good followed that act of kindness.

And she wanted to take that goodness for herself.

She wanted to turn their kindness into her own strength.

That was why she watched people's faces whenever they gave her money.

She was not simply receiving money.

She was watching for what she could take from them.

The next morning, she would return to Molete.

She would put on her torn clothes again.

She would sit on the old mat.

And she would become Iyalajo the helpless beggar once more.

People continued to give.

Some lost their businesses.

Some lost opportunities.

Some suffered painful disappointments.

Others experienced things they could not explain.

Still, nobody could prove that Iyalajo was responsible.

Then one day, a man called Deji passed through Molete.

Deji was thirty-two years old.

He worked as an electronics technician.

He was not a wealthy man.

He lived a simple life and worked hard for everything he had.

But there was one thing Deji never played with.

Prayer.

Every morning before leaving his house, Deji prayed.

His prayer was simple.

“Lord, go with me today. Cover everything I touch. Let nothing evil attach itself to me or to what I do. Order my steps.”

He prayed before starting his work.

He prayed before travelling.

He prayed before making important decisions.

It had become part of his daily life.

One morning, Deji was walking through Molete when he saw Iyalajo.

She stretched out her hand.

“Please, help me.”

Deji stopped.

He looked at the woman.

She looked tired and helpless, just as she always did.

Deji reached into his pocket and brought out 100 naira.

But before giving it to her, something came to his mind.

He quietly prayed.

“Lord, I am giving this from a good heart. Let this kindness be blessed and not cursed. Let no evil attach itself to what I do right now. I give in your name. Cover me with your grace.”

Then he placed the money in Iyalajo's hand.

She looked at him.

For the first time in many years, something strange happened.

Iyalajo's expression changed.

She tried to do what she had always done.

She tried to take what she believed was attached to the man's kindness.

But she could not.

It was as if she had reached a wall she could not pass through.

She tried again.

Nothing happened.

The force she had used against others seemed to return to her.

Iyalajo quickly pulled her hand back.

She stared at Deji.

Deji did not understand what had happened.

He simply nodded and continued walking.

Iyalajo watched him disappear into the crowd.

For the first time, she looked afraid.

She had met people who gave her money before.

She had taken from them without difficulty.

But this man was different.

There was something covering his giving.

Something she could not break through.

Days passed.

Then weeks.

Deji continued with his normal life.

His work was going well.

His customers were happy with him.

New jobs kept coming.

Nothing strange happened to him.

Iyalajo noticed.

She watched him whenever he passed through Molete.

She waited for something to go wrong.

But nothing happened.

Deji remained fine.

Then Iyalajo began to wonder about the man.

What had he done differently?

Why could she not take from him?

And more importantly, how many other people had been protecting their kindness without even knowing it?

Unknown to Iyalajo, the answer was about to come from someone who had been watching her for a very long time.

🌿 Chapter Five – The Lesson Ibadan Never Forgot

🌿 Moral of the Story

Never stop being kind because of the evil actions of others. Help people with a sincere heart, but always pray and ask God for wisdom and protection.

What looks weak on the outside may not always be harmless, and what looks small may carry great consequences.

Kindness should never die. Give from a good heart, but always cover your actions with prayer and wisdom.
