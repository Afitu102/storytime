👵🏾 The Beggar of Ibadan

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🧺 Chapter One – The Beggar of Molete

Sometimes, not everyone who stretches out a hand for help truly needs it. Some people may come to you looking helpless, while hiding something you cannot see.

That was what happened many years ago in the busy city of Ibadan.

Ibadan was a beautiful and busy city. Its roads were always filled with cars, buses, traders, workers and people going about their daily lives.

Near Molete bus stop, there was a woman whom almost everyone knew.

People called her Iyalajo.

Nobody knew her real name.

Nobody knew where she came from.

Nobody knew if she had a family somewhere.

She simply appeared to have been sitting at that same place for many years.

Iyalajo always sat on an old mat beside the road.

She looked very poor.

Her clothes were old and torn. Her feet were cracked from walking barefoot on the dusty roads, and her body looked thin and weak.

Whenever people passed her, she stretched out her hand.

“Please, help me.”

“Anything you have.”

“God will bless you.”

Those were the words people heard from her every day.

Because she looked helpless, many people felt sorry for her.

They gave her money.

Some gave her food.

Others stopped to ask if she needed clothes or medicine.

But there was something strange about Iyalajo that most people never noticed.

Her eyes.

Her eyes were always watching.

Whenever someone gave her money, she looked carefully at the person's face.

It was almost as if she was studying the person.

But nobody paid much attention to it.

People were too busy feeling sorry for the poor woman sitting beside the road.

One morning, a man called Baba Remy passed through Molete on his way to his shop at Bodija Market.

Baba Remy was a hardworking trader.

He had spent many years building his provisions business.

He had two freezers, shelves filled with goods and a small generator that he had worked hard to pay for.

He was not a rich man, but he was doing well.

As Baba Remy passed Iyalajo that morning, he stopped.

He looked at her clothes.

He looked at her cracked feet.

Then he reached into his pocket.

He brought out 200 naira and placed it in her hand.

“Take this,” he said. “It is not much, but manage it.”

Iyalajo slowly looked up at him.

Her eyes stayed on his face for a few seconds.

Then she looked down at the money.

She did not smile.

She did not even say thank you.

Baba Remy thought nothing of it.

He continued to his shop.

That was the last morning his business was completely normal.

At first, one of his freezers stopped working.

He spent money repairing it.

Before he could recover from that expense, a supplier collected a large amount of money from him and disappeared.

Then some of his goods began to spoil.

Customers who had been buying from him for years slowly stopped coming.

Within a few months, the shop that had taken Baba Remy ten years to build was almost empty.

He sat inside his shop one evening, looking at the empty shelves.

He could not understand what had happened.

“How did everything disappear so quickly?” he asked himself.

He had worked hard.

He had done everything he knew to do.

Yet somehow, everything was falling apart.

Then there was Mama Tunde.

Mama Tunde was seven months pregnant.

She had just returned from the hospital after her antenatal checkup.

The doctor had told her that everything was fine.

Her baby was healthy.

She was happy.

As she passed through Molete, she saw Iyalajo sitting by the roadside.

Mama Tunde felt pity for her.

She reached into her purse and brought out 500 naira.

“Please, take this,” she said.

Iyalajo accepted the money.

Again, she looked directly into the woman's eyes.

Mama Tunde smiled and continued home.

But not long after that day, something terrible happened.

Mama Tunde lost her baby.

She was devastated.

Her family tried to comfort her, but she could not understand why it had happened.

Her pregnancy had been going well.

The doctors had said the baby was healthy.

There had been no warning.

She cried for days.

Then something even stranger happened.

Mama Tunde's sister began talking to other women in the area.

She discovered that Mama Tunde was not the only woman who had suffered a strange loss after giving money to Iyalajo.

There was Kunle, a young man who had been searching for work for months.

One afternoon, Kunle passed Iyalajo and gave her 100 naira.

Three days later, he received a call from a company that had already agreed to employ him.

They suddenly cancelled the job.

Kunle was confused.

“Why would they cancel it after everything was already settled?” he asked.

He had no answer.

There was also Mrs. Adeyemi, a hardworking woman who owned a small business.

She gave Iyalajo some money one morning.

Not long after, her biggest customer left her business and started buying from someone else.

Her sales dropped badly.

Then there was Biodun.

Biodun was a student preparing for his WAEC examination.

He was doing well in school and everyone expected him to pass.

One day, he gave Iyalajo some money while passing the roadside.

When his examination results came out, they were terrible.

Subjects he had always performed well in suddenly became his worst results.

His parents could not understand it.

His teachers were surprised.

But one thing connected all these people.

They had all given money to Iyalajo.

At first, nobody wanted to believe it.

People told themselves it was just coincidence.

“Maybe Baba Remy's business was simply unlucky.”

“Maybe Mama Tunde's loss was just something that happened.”

“Maybe Kunle was never meant to get that job.”

“Maybe Mrs. Adeyemi's customer simply found another seller.”

“Maybe Biodun just did badly in his exams.”

Everyone had an explanation.

And because there was no proof, people continued helping Iyalajo.

She remained at Molete.

Every morning, she returned to the same roadside.

She wore the same torn clothes.

She stretched out the same hand.

And people continued giving.

But nobody knew what happened after the sun went down.

Because when darkness covered the streets of Ibadan, Iyalajo was no longer the weak old beggar everyone thought she was.

And what she did with the money she collected was a secret that would soon shock the entire community.

🌙 End of Chapter One

👁️ Chapter Two – The Blessings She Stole

Every morning, Iyalajo returned to the same place at Molete bus stop.

She sat on her old mat, stretched out her hand and waited.

People still felt sorry for her.

They saw an old woman who looked hungry and forgotten.

They did not know that every night, something very different was happening.

When the streets became quiet and most people had gone home, Iyalajo would pick up her old mat and leave the roadside.

She did not walk like a weak woman anymore.

Her back became straight.

Her steps became firm.

She walked to a compound hidden away from the busy road.

When she entered the compound, she closed the gate behind her.

Then she removed the torn clothes she wore during the day.

She would sit down and bring out the money people had given her.

She arranged the money carefully in front of her.

Then she began to speak words that nobody outside the compound could hear.

As she spoke, the atmosphere around her changed.

The air became heavy.

There was a strange smell, almost like something burning.

She placed her hands over the money and continued speaking.

The money was not important to her because of its value.

She wanted something else.

She wanted the goodwill behind it.

She wanted the blessings connected to the kindness of the people who had given it.

She believed that when people gave her money with a good heart, something good followed that act of kindness.

And she wanted to take that goodness for herself.

She wanted to turn their kindness into her own strength.

That was why she watched people's faces whenever they gave her money.

She was not simply receiving money.

She was watching for what she could take from them.

The next morning, she would return to Molete.

She would put on her torn clothes again.

She would sit on the old mat.

And she would become Iyalajo the helpless beggar once more.

People continued to give.

Some lost their businesses.

Some lost opportunities.

Some suffered painful disappointments.

Others experienced things they could not explain.

Still, nobody could prove that Iyalajo was responsible.

Then one day, a man called Deji passed through Molete.

Deji was thirty-two years old.

He worked as an electronics technician.

He was not a wealthy man.

He lived a simple life and worked hard for everything he had.

But there was one thing Deji never played with.

Prayer.

Every morning before leaving his house, Deji prayed.

His prayer was simple.

“Lord, go with me today. Cover everything I touch. Let nothing evil attach itself to me or to what I do. Order my steps.”

He prayed before starting his work.

He prayed before travelling.

He prayed before making important decisions.

It had become part of his daily life.

One morning, Deji was walking through Molete when he saw Iyalajo.

She stretched out her hand.

“Please, help me.”

Deji stopped.

He looked at the woman.

She looked tired and helpless, just as she always did.

Deji reached into his pocket and brought out 100 naira.

But before giving it to her, something came to his mind.

He quietly prayed.

“Lord, I am giving this from a good heart. Let this kindness be blessed and not cursed. Let no evil attach itself to what I do right now. I give in your name. Cover me with your grace.”

Then he placed the money in Iyalajo's hand.

She looked at him.

For the first time in many years, something strange happened.

Iyalajo's expression changed.

She tried to do what she had always done.

She tried to take what she believed was attached to the man's kindness.

But she could not.

It was as if she had reached a wall she could not pass through.

She tried again.

Nothing happened.

The force she had used against others seemed to return to her.

Iyalajo quickly pulled her hand back.

She stared at Deji.

Deji did not understand what had happened.

He simply nodded and continued walking.

Iyalajo watched him disappear into the crowd.

For the first time, she looked afraid.

She had met people who gave her money before.

She had taken from them without difficulty.

But this man was different.

There was something covering his giving.

Something she could not break through.

Days passed.

Then weeks.

Deji continued with his normal life.

His work was going well.

His customers were happy with him.

New jobs kept coming.

Nothing strange happened to him.

Iyalajo noticed.

She watched him whenever he passed through Molete.

She waited for something to go wrong.

But nothing happened.

Deji remained fine.

Then Iyalajo began to wonder about the man.

What had he done differently?

Why could she not take from him?

And more importantly, how many other people had been protecting their kindness without even knowing it?

Unknown to Iyalajo, the answer was about to come from someone who had been watching her for a very long time.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🔥 Chapter Three – The Day the Secret Began to Come Out

After Deji's strange encounter with Iyalajo, life continued as usual at Molete.

People still passed by the roadside every day.

Traders opened their shops.

Drivers stopped to pick up passengers.

And Iyalajo continued sitting on her old mat, stretching out her hand.

But one woman had started paying closer attention.

Her name was SisiBisi.

SisiBisi sold pepper and tomatoes not far from where Iyalajo usually sat.

She had been around Molete for many years, so she knew many of the stories people told about the old beggar.

One afternoon, she saw Deji passing by.

She remembered seeing him give Iyalajo money some weeks earlier.

She also remembered the prayer he had whispered before giving it to her.

SisiBisi watched him carefully.

Six weeks later, she finally called him.

“Deji!”

Deji stopped and turned around.

“How are you doing?” SisiBisi asked.

“I am fine, ma. Thank God.”

SisiBisi looked at him for a moment.

“Nothing has happened to you since that day?”

Deji looked confused.

“What do you mean?”

“You know what I mean. Since you gave that woman money.”

Deji laughed.

“No. Nothing has happened. Everything is fine.”

SisiBisi nodded slowly.

She did not say much more.

But that conversation stayed in her mind.

Later that day, she told the woman who sold goods beside her.

“There is something strange about that beggar,” she said.

The story moved from one person to another.

Before long, someone mentioned it to a neighbour.

The neighbour told a pastor.

The pastor decided not to make noise about it.

Instead, he wanted to see things for himself.

On a Saturday, he came to Molete and watched Iyalajo from a distance.

He saw the way she watched people.

He saw how her eyes followed anyone who reached into their pocket.

He noticed how different her expression became whenever someone gave her money.

The pastor became concerned.

He spoke with a few trusted people in the area.

They agreed that they should not accuse the woman without understanding what was really happening.

So they began praying quietly.

They did not confront Iyalajo.

They simply prayed for the people around Molete and asked God to expose anything hidden.

For some days, nothing happened.

Then came Friday.

That afternoon, a man called Mr. Olarenwoju was walking past Iyalajo.

He had some money in his hand.

Iyalajo stretched out her hand as usual.

“Please, help me.”

Mr. Olarenwoju was about to give her the money.

But something unexpected happened.

A little boy, about five years old, suddenly slipped away from his mother.

He ran a few steps toward Iyalajo.

Then he stopped.

His face changed.

He stared at the woman in fear.

Then he screamed.

“Mummy! Mummy! That woman!”

His mother quickly ran toward him.

“What is wrong?”

The boy pointed at Iyalajo.

“She is scary!”

The mother looked at Iyalajo again.

For the first time, she did not see the weak old woman everyone had become used to seeing.

She looked carefully.

Then she slowly stepped backward.

Mr. Olarenwoju also looked at Iyalajo.

The money was still in his hand.

He quietly put it back into his pocket.

People around them began looking at one another.

Something was wrong.

Then a woman spoke.

“Wait.”

Everyone turned toward her.

It was Fumi, a jewellery seller.

She had been silent for a long time.

Fumi looked at Iyalajo.

“Years ago, I gave this woman money,” she said.

“Not long after that, my sales collapsed.”

People became quiet.

Fumi continued.

“I thought it was just bad business. I never connected it to her.”

Another trader stepped forward.

“Something similar happened to me.”

A mother spoke.

“And to me.”

A young man also joined them.

“I gave her money too. After that, the job I was expecting disappeared.”

Suddenly, people began remembering things.

Stories that had once sounded like coincidences were beginning to look different.

Then an elderly man slowly stepped forward.

His name was Paadisa.

He was about eighty years old and was highly respected in the community.

Everyone became quiet when he began to speak.

“I have watched this woman since 1998.”

The crowd looked at him.

“I have seen what she does.”

Someone asked, “What do you mean?”

Paadisa took a deep breath.

“I said nothing because I was afraid.”

He looked directly at Iyalajo.

“But I am eighty years old now, and I do not have time left to be afraid.”

The crowd became completely silent.

Paadisa continued.

“This woman is not what she appears to be.”

People began murmuring.

“She is a destroyer.”

Those words changed everything.

People started asking questions.

“What has she been doing?”

“How long has this been happening?”

“Why did nobody tell us?”

Iyalajo remained silent.

But something had changed on her face.

The helpless expression was disappearing.

Her eyes became cold.

Her back straightened.

For the first time, the people of Molete saw a different woman standing before them.

The old beggar they thought they knew was no longer there.

The mask had begun to fall.

And the people were finally ready to hear the truth.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🔥 Chapter Four – The Blessings Came Back

After Paadisa finished speaking, nobody at Molete knew what to say.

For years, people had looked at Iyalajo and seen a helpless woman who needed their help.

Now, many of them were beginning to look at her differently.

The stories began coming out one after another.

People remembered the businesses they had lost.

They remembered opportunities that suddenly disappeared.

They remembered painful things that had happened after they had given money to Iyalajo.

For the first time, the people of Molete began to connect the stories.

But they did not stop there.

They decided that the people who had suffered should be encouraged to start again.

Baba Remy was one of the first.

After losing almost everything in his provisions shop, he had spent months wondering how he would begin again.

Then something unexpected happened.

A new supplier came to him.

The man had heard about Baba Remy's business and offered to supply him with goods on credit.

Baba Remy could hardly believe it.

“You want to give me all these goods on credit?” he asked.

The supplier nodded.

“Yes. I believe you can pay me back.”

Baba Remy was grateful.

He slowly rebuilt his shop.

After some time, his business became even better than it had been before.

Then there was Mama Tunde.

She had carried the pain of losing her baby for a long time.

But life gave her another chance.

She became pregnant again.

This time, she was very careful.

She prayed.

She went for her checkups.

She followed the doctors' instructions.

Nine months later, she gave birth to a healthy baby girl.

She named the baby Grace.

When Mama Tunde held her daughter for the first time, she cried.

They were tears of happiness.

She knew she had been given another reason to smile.

Kunle also received good news.

He was offered another job in Lagos.

This time, the position was better than the one he had lost.

The salary was higher.

There were better opportunities for him to grow.

Kunle accepted the job with gratitude.

Mrs. Adeyemi's business also began to recover.

Customers slowly returned.

Her sales improved.

She worked hard and refused to give up.

Biodun was given another opportunity to write his examination.

He studied harder than before.

When his new results came out, he passed very well.

His parents were proud of him.

All these people began to understand something.

Their lives had not ended because they had suffered.

They could start again.

And they did.

One day, someone asked Deji why nothing had happened to him after giving Iyalajo money.

Deji simply smiled.

“I did not do anything special,” he said.

“I just did what I always do. I gave with God's name on it. That is all.”

People remembered his words.

It was not the amount of money he gave.

It was the heart with which he gave it.

And the prayer he made before giving.

Meanwhile, the community had to decide what to do about Iyalajo.

The elders called a meeting.

Pastors, imams, community leaders, traders and respected people from the area gathered together.

They did not want revenge.

They wanted the matter settled peacefully.

Iyalajo was asked to come.

She came without fighting.

For years, she had hidden behind the appearance of a helpless beggar.

Now everyone knew that there was more to her story.

Baba Remy spoke first.

He told them how his business had collapsed after giving her money.

Mama Tunde spoke about the pain she had experienced.

Kunle told them about the job he lost.

Fumi told them about her business.

Other traders, students, mothers and fathers also shared their experiences.

Iyalajo listened quietly.

She did not apologize.

She did not explain herself.

She simply stood there.

Finally, Paadisa stood up.

Everyone became quiet.

He looked at Iyalajo and spoke slowly.

“You are not welcome here anymore.”

There was silence.

“You came among us and took from people who gave you kindness.”

He continued.

“You took their businesses, their children and their futures. You turned mercy into a weapon.”

Paadisa shook his head.

“There is no longer any place for you in this city. Go and do not come back.”

The decision was final.

Two young men were asked to escort Iyalajo away from the area.

As she walked away from Molete, nobody followed her.

Nobody called her back.

The woman who had spent years sitting by the roadside was finally leaving.

But nobody knew what would happen to her next.

For the people of Ibadan, the most important thing was that the fear surrounding Molete had finally come to an end.

Or so they thought.

🌙 End of Chapter Four

🌿 Chapter Five – The Lesson Ibadan Never Forgot

After Iyalajo was sent away from Molete, life slowly returned to normal.

The roadside where she used to sit was empty.

People could pass there without fear.

Traders opened their shops every morning.

Mothers walked through the area with their children.

People continued with their businesses and their daily lives.

But the story of Iyalajo did not disappear.

It became a story that people told whenever they gathered together.

Some people said they had seen Iyalajo somewhere near Oyo.

Others said she had moved farther away.

But wherever she went, she was no longer surrounded by people giving her money.

She was alone.

She no longer had the same strength she once appeared to have.

She looked tired and hungry.

She had no crowd around her.

No traders knew her.

No one trusted her.

And no one was willing to give her their hard-earned money without thinking carefully.

Years passed.

Then one day, someone brought news back to Ibadan.

They said Iyalajo was dead.

Nobody knew exactly where she died.

Nobody knew what had happened to her.

There was no big funeral.

There was no crowd of people mourning her.

The woman who had once been known by almost everyone at Molete disappeared quietly from the lives of the people she had troubled.

But the people of Molete never forgot what happened.

They remembered Baba Remy.

They remembered Mama Tunde and baby Grace.

They remembered Kunle.

They remembered Mrs. Adeyemi.

They remembered Biodun.

And they remembered Deji.

Many people asked the same question.

“What was different about Deji?”

SisiBisi had an answer.

“It was not because he gave more money than everyone else,” she said.

“It was not because he was stronger than everyone else.”

“It was because he never stopped praying.”

Deji had made prayer part of his everyday life.

Before he went out, he prayed.

Before he worked, he prayed.

Before he helped someone, he prayed.

Even when he was giving just 100 naira, he remembered to ask God to guide and protect him.

That simple habit became a lesson for many people.

They learned that kindness should not die because some people misuse it.

There are still poor people who genuinely need help.

There are still hungry people who need food.

There are still people who need someone to show them kindness.

So the people of Molete did not decide to stop helping others.

Instead, they became wiser.

They learned to look beyond appearances.

They learned to ask God for wisdom and discernment.

They learned that a good heart should be protected, not closed.

And they remembered three important truths.

First: Not everything that looks broken on the outside is broken on the inside.

Someone may look helpless while hiding something completely different.

That is why we should ask God for wisdom before making decisions.

Second: Evil cannot hide forever.

No matter how long someone hides behind a mask, the truth can eventually come out.

Third: Never stop being kind.

Give from a sincere heart, but do so with wisdom and prayer.

One evening, an old resident of Molete stood near the place where Iyalajo used to sit.

The old mat was gone.

The woman was gone.

Only the empty roadside remained.

The man looked at the place for a while and smiled.

“Thank God,” he whispered.

Then he walked away.

The people of Ibadan eventually moved on with their lives.

But the story of the beggar at Molete was passed from one generation to another.

It reminded everyone that kindness is powerful.

It reminded them that wisdom is important.

And most importantly, it reminded them that helping others should never be done with fear, but with a good heart, wisdom and prayer.

🌿 Moral of the Story

Never stop helping people because someone once misused your kindness.

Be kind, but also be wise.

Do not judge people only by what you see on the outside.

And whenever you give, give with a sincere heart and ask God to guide and protect you.

Because sometimes, a small act of kindness can change someone's life.

📖 The End
