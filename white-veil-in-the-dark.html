👻 White Veil in the Dark

📚 Classic Authors • 🕒 25 min read

🌫 Chapter One – The Figure at the Window

Rain had stopped before midnight.

Water still clung to the leaves outside the old house, dropping one bead at a time onto the wooden porch.

Elias sat beside the kitchen window with a cup of warm milk between his hands.

He was not waiting for sleep.

He was waiting for the clock.

The old wall clock above the cupboard had been running for three days without stopping.

Tonight, its hands were moving toward twelve.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

Elias looked at the window.

The glass reflected his face.

Behind that reflection was the garden.

A small stone path crossed the grass and ended at an iron gate.

Beyond the gate stood a line of tall trees.

Their branches were still after the rain.

Then one branch moved.

Elias leaned closer.

Another branch moved.

Something had passed between the trees.

He put down the cup.

His older sister, Nia, was asleep upstairs.

Their aunt had left earlier that evening to spend the night with a friend in town.

The house was quiet.

Elias reached for the small torch on the table.

He switched it on.

The garden became a collection of wet leaves, dark soil and silver puddles.

Nothing moved.

He lowered the torch.

The clock showed 11:58.

Elias smiled to himself.

He had spent enough time staring through windows.

He picked up his cup.

Then the clock stopped.

12:00.

Elias looked at it.

The second hand remained still.

A soft sound came from the garden.

Not a knock.

Not a voice.

A slow scrape against the iron gate.

Elias walked toward the window again.

The gate was open.

He was certain it had been closed earlier.

He placed his palm against the glass.

The garden looked empty.

Then he saw it.

A pale figure stood near the far end of the path.

It was tall.

A long white covering fell from its head to its feet.

The cloth moved gently even though the trees were still.

Elias forgot about the clock.

He forgot about the cold milk.

His hand remained against the glass.

The figure turned.

There was no face.

Only two dark holes beneath the white veil.

Elias stepped backward.

The kitchen chair struck the floor.

The sound woke Nia.

Her bedroom door opened upstairs.

"Elias?"

He did not answer.

Nia came down the stairs.

She wore an old blue sweater and carried a small lamp.

"What happened?"

Elias pointed toward the window.

Nia looked.

The figure was gone.

She walked closer.

She opened the curtain.

The garden was empty.

Nia looked at Elias.

"Did you see someone?"

Elias nodded.

"By the gate."

Nia studied the wet grass.

A line of footprints crossed the garden.

They began at the gate.

They ended directly beneath the kitchen window.

Nia stopped speaking.

Elias slowly looked down.

The footprints were fresh.

Water filled each one.

Nia locked the window.

"Stay here."

She picked up the torch.

Elias followed her into the hallway.

They moved through the house together.

The front door was locked.

The back door was locked.

Every window was shut.

Nia checked each one.

When she reached the staircase, she stopped.

A wet footprint rested on the first step.

Elias stared at it.

Another footprint appeared on the second step.

Then the third.

Water slowly gathered inside each print.

Nia raised the torch.

The footprints continued upward.

Elias whispered,

"How did they get inside?"

Nia did not answer.

She started climbing.

Elias stayed close behind her.

At the top of the stairs, the footprints turned toward the old guest room.

The room had been locked since their aunt bought the house.

A brass key usually hung beside the kitchen door.

It was missing.

Nia reached for the guest-room handle.

The door opened before she touched it.

A cold breeze moved through the hallway.

The room was dark.

Nia shone the torch inside.

Dust covered the furniture.

An old wooden wardrobe stood against the wall.

A broken mirror leaned beside it.

And on the floor was a white piece of cloth.

Elias walked closer.

He recognized it.

It was the same material worn by the figure in the garden.

Nia crouched beside it.

There was writing stitched into the fabric.

Three words.

"LOOK AT THE WINDOW."

Elias turned.

The guest room had one window.

Nia raised the torch toward it.

A pale shape stood outside.

The figure was much closer now.

Its face pressed against the glass.

Elias stepped back.

The figure lifted one long hand.

One finger touched the window.

Then it slowly wrote a single number in the misted glass.

Seven.

The figure disappeared.

Nia stared at the number.

"Seven what?"

Elias looked around the room.

His eyes stopped at the wardrobe.

Seven small scratches marked its wooden door.

Nia walked toward it.

She pulled the handle.

The wardrobe opened.

There were no clothes inside.

Only a narrow staircase leading down.

A faint white light came from below.

Nia looked at Elias.

He looked back at her.

Neither spoke.

From somewhere beneath the house came the sound of a bell.

One ring.

Then another.

Nia tightened her grip on the torch.

"We're finding out what this house is hiding."

Elias looked into the narrow staircase.

The white light flickered.

Something moved below.

Then the staircase began to glow.
