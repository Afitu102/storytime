👻 Whispers from the Hollow

📚 Classic Authors • 🕒 40 min read

🌫 Chapter One – A Voice in the Fog

The fog came before the rain.

It rolled down the hill just after sunset, slow and thick, covering the old road one stone at a time.

Mara stood beside the kitchen window and watched it swallow the trees.

She had seen fog many times.

This one was different.

It had a sound.

A soft whisper.

At first, she thought it was the wind moving through the grass.

Then she heard her name.

"Mara..."

She froze.

The cup in her hand shook.

She looked toward the hallway.

The house was quiet.

Her father was asleep upstairs.

The old clock near the door ticked slowly.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

Mara turned back to the window.

The fog had reached the garden.

Something stood inside it.

A tall shape.

Still.

Too still.

Mara moved closer to the glass.

The shape lifted one hand.

A finger pointed toward the hill.

Then the fog closed around it.

Mara stepped away from the window.

"No."

She whispered it to herself.

She had promised her father she would never go near that hill after dark.

Not because of a ghost story.

Not because of some old village tale.

There was another reason.

Her mother had disappeared there.

Mara was seven years old when it happened.

She still remembered the red scarf her mother wore that evening.

She still remembered the smell of rain.

And she still remembered the last thing her mother said.

"Stay inside until I come back."

She never came back.

For years, Mara tried to forget the hill.

She almost managed it.

Until tonight.

A second whisper came through the fog.

"Mara..."

This time, the voice sounded closer.

She opened the back door.

Cold air rushed into the kitchen.

The fog touched her feet.

Mara looked toward the hill.

A small yellow light was moving between the trees.

It stopped.

Then moved again.

Three times.

Slow.

Careful.

Like someone carrying a lantern.

Mara stepped outside.

The door clicked behind her.

She did not turn around.

The light on the hill moved higher.

Mara followed.

The grass was wet against her shoes.

Branches scratched her arms as she entered the trees.

The village lights disappeared behind her.

Only the yellow light remained.

She reached the old stone path.

Her mother had walked this path on that rainy evening.

Mara remembered that now.

She had forgotten the path.

But her feet remembered.

She walked deeper into the forest.

The lantern ahead suddenly went out.

Darkness filled the trees.

Mara stopped.

A sound came from behind her.

One dry branch breaking.

She turned.

Nothing.

Then came another sound.

Footsteps.

Slow.

Heavy.

Coming from the direction of the house.

Mara held her breath.

The footsteps stopped.

A warm yellow light appeared between the trees.

A woman stepped into view.

She wore a red scarf.

Mara's heart stopped for a moment.

The woman looked exactly like her mother.

Same dark hair.

Same small smile.

Same silver ring on her left hand.

Mara took one step forward.

"Mom?"

The woman said nothing.

She simply turned toward an old stone wall.

A small wooden door was hidden inside it.

Mara had never seen that door before.

The woman placed her hand against it.

The door opened.

A warm golden light spilled onto the forest floor.

The woman looked back at Mara.

"Come."

Mara stood there.

Her mind told her to leave.

Her heart wanted one answer.

Why did her mother never come home?

Mara stepped toward the door.

The woman smiled.

Then she whispered something that made Mara stop.

"Your father lied to you."

The door began to close.

Mara rushed forward.

Her hand caught the edge.

The golden light vanished.

The woman was gone.

Mara stood alone beside the stone wall.

Then she heard a faint sound beneath her feet.

A heartbeat.

Slow.

Deep.

Coming from underneath the earth.

Mara looked down.

A thin crack had appeared between the stones.

Something blue was glowing inside it.

She touched the stone.

The whole hill trembled.

And somewhere beneath the forest, a woman's voice whispered her name again.

"Mara..."

🌙 End of Chapter One

🕯 Chapter Two – Beneath the Stone

Mara stayed beside the wall.

The blue light was still there.

It moved beneath the cracks like a tiny flame trapped inside the stone.

She touched it again.

The ground gave a soft pulse.

Mara pulled her hand back.

A thin line of blue light spread across the wall.

It formed a shape.

A circle.

Inside the circle was a small drawing of a house.

Mara stared at it.

It was her house.

Same roof.

Same chimney.

Same little window beside the kitchen.

Then the drawing changed.

A second house appeared beneath it.

Mara did not know that house.

The image faded.

A low rumble came from the ground.

The stone door opened again.

This time there was no woman waiting on the other side.

Only a narrow staircase.

Mara looked into the darkness.

Cold air rose from below.

She could hear water dripping somewhere deep inside.

One drop.

Then another.

She took out her phone.

No signal.

The screen showed 11:47.

She had been gone from the house for almost an hour.

Her father would be awake soon.

She should turn back.

Instead, she stepped down.

The staircase was made of old stone.

Roots pushed through the walls.

Some were thin.

Some were thick as her arm.

A faint blue glow followed her.

It came from small marks carved into the steps.

Each mark looked like a letter.

Mara recognized the first one.

It was the first letter of her mother's name.

She stopped.

More letters appeared farther down.

Then another.

Then another.

Together they formed a sentence.

"She left a door open."

Mara frowned.

A door.

Her mother had always hated leaving doors open.

Even when Mara was little, her mother checked every door before going to bed.

Front door.

Back door.

Bedroom door.

Every one.

Mara continued down.

The staircase ended inside a large underground room.

A pool of clear water covered most of the floor.

At the center stood a wooden chair.

Something rested on it.

A red scarf.

Mara stopped breathing for a second.

She knew that scarf.

It was the one her mother wore the night she disappeared.

Mara walked through the shallow water.

The scarf was dry.

Perfectly dry.

She picked it up.

A small silver key fell from the folds.

Mara caught it before it touched the water.

There was writing on the key.

Two words.

"Room Seven."

Mara looked around.

Seven doors stood along the far wall.

Six were covered with moss.

The seventh was clean.

A small silver lock sat in the middle.

Mara walked toward it.

The key fit perfectly.

She turned it.

Click.

The door opened.

Inside was a small room.

There was a wooden desk.

A lamp.

Several old books.

And a photograph.

Mara picked it up.

Her mother stood in front of the same stone wall.

But she was not alone.

A young man stood beside her.

Mara knew him too.

Her father.

They were both smiling.

On the back of the photograph was a date.

Four years before Mara was born.

Under the date was a short note.

"We found it."

Mara turned the photograph over again.

Something else was written beneath the picture.

"But we should never have opened it."

A sound came from behind the desk.

Mara slowly looked over.

One of the books had fallen onto the floor.

It was open.

The pages were turning by themselves.

Mara moved closer.

A drawing filled the page.

It showed the hill.

The stone wall.

The hidden staircase.

And beneath the hill was a large black circle.

Something had been drawn inside it.

A woman.

Mara leaned closer.

The woman was wearing a red scarf.

Her mother.

Then a new line appeared on the page.

"She is still waiting."

Mara dropped the book.

The lamp went out.

The room became dark.

Then came three knocks from inside the wall.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Mara stepped backward.

A woman's voice came through the stone.

"Do not trust your father."

Mara held the red scarf against her chest.

Her eyes filled with tears.

She whispered the only question that mattered.

"Where are you?"

The wall answered.

"Under your home."

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🔦 Chapter Three – The Room Under the House

Mara stood in the dark room.

Her mother's voice was still inside the wall.

Under your home.

Those three words kept running through her mind.

She switched on her phone light.

The beam moved across the room.

Books.

Dust.

The old desk.

Nothing else.

Then she noticed the floor.

There was a wooden panel beneath the desk.

Mara pulled the desk aside.

A small metal handle appeared.

She pulled it.

The panel lifted.

Cold air rushed up.

Below was another staircase.

Mara stared into it.

The blue glow was there again.

It came from somewhere far below.

She climbed down.

The stairs ended beneath her own house.

Mara knew because she could see the wooden beams above her.

One beam had a small crack.

She remembered it from her childhood.

It was directly below her bedroom.

Her heart started beating faster.

Her mother had been here.

Maybe many times.

Mara moved through the narrow space.

A soft blue light came from the far end.

She followed it.

The passage opened into a wide chamber.

There was a bed.

A small table.

A mirror.

A wardrobe.

The room looked lived in.

Fresh flowers stood inside a glass jar.

A cup sat beside the bed.

Steam was rising from it.

Mara stopped.

Someone had been here.

Very recently.

She walked to the bed.

A notebook lay on the pillow.

Her mother's name was written on the cover.

Mara opened it.

The first page was blank.

The second page had one line.

"If Mara ever finds this place, tell her I never left by choice."

Mara covered her mouth.

She turned the page.

Another message waited there.

"Your father knows the truth."

Mara closed her eyes.

The next pages were filled with dates.

Every month.

Every year.

For eighteen years.

At the bottom of the last page was today's date.

Under it were four words.

"He brought her back."

Mara looked toward the door.

A sound came from the passage.

Footsteps.

She quickly hid behind the wardrobe.

The footsteps came closer.

A man entered the room.

Mara recognized his shoes.

Her father.

He walked to the table.

He picked up the cup.

Then he looked toward the bed.

"She found the room."

Mara stayed still.

Her father placed the cup down.

"I knew she would."

He opened the wardrobe.

Mara pressed herself against the wall.

Her father reached inside.

He pulled out a small wooden box.

Then he turned around.

Mara stepped out.

"Dad."

He froze.

The box slipped from his hand.

It hit the floor.

The lid opened.

Inside was a silver necklace.

Mara knew it at once.

Her mother had worn it every day.

Her father looked at her.

"You should not have come here."

Mara stepped closer.

"Where is Mom?"

He said nothing.

Mara picked up the necklace.

"Is she alive?"

Her father's eyes filled with tears.

He nodded.

Mara's breath caught.

"Where?"

He looked toward the wall.

"Behind that door."

Mara turned.

A narrow door had appeared beside the mirror.

It had not been there before.

A red scarf hung from the handle.

Mara reached for it.

Her father grabbed her wrist.

"Wait."

Mara looked at him.

"What are you afraid of?"

He lowered his voice.

"Your mother is not the only thing behind that door."

The door handle slowly moved.

Mara's father released her hand.

A woman's voice came from the other side.

"Let her in."

Mara looked at her father.

He stepped back.

The door opened.

And behind it stood her mother.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🕯 Chapter Four – Her Mother's Secret

Mara could not move.

Her mother stood in the doorway.

Older than the woman in Mara's memories.

But it was her.

The same eyes.

The same small smile.

The same silver ring.

Mara took one slow step forward.

"Mom?"

Her mother reached out.

Mara ran into her arms.

For a few seconds, the underground room disappeared.

There was only the warmth of her mother's arms.

The smell of her old perfume.

The soft touch of her hand against Mara's hair.

Mara pulled away.

"Why did you leave me?"

Her mother looked at the floor.

"I didn't."

Mara stared at her.

"Then why were you gone for eighteen years?"

Her mother looked toward Mara's father.

He turned away.

"Because of him."

Mara's face changed.

Her father closed his eyes.

Her mother walked toward the small table.

She opened the wooden box.

Inside was a folded piece of blue cloth.

She unfolded it.

A strange symbol was sewn into the middle.

Mara had seen that symbol before.

On the stone wall.

On the old staircase.

Inside the book.

"What is it?"

Her mother touched the symbol.

"It was the mark of the Hollow."

Mara looked toward the ceiling.

"The place on the hill?"

Her mother nodded.

"The Hollow is not a place."

She folded the cloth again.

"It is a door between two worlds."

Mara felt a chill move across her arms.

Her father finally spoke.

"Your mother found the door before you were born."

Mara turned toward him.

"And you followed her?"

He nodded.

"We thought we could control what was behind it."

Her mother shook her head.

"We were wrong."

The lamp flickered.

A deep sound moved through the walls.

Mara looked around.

The water beneath the floor began to ripple.

Her mother grabbed her hand.

"We have to leave."

Mara looked at her.

"Leave for where?"

Her mother looked toward the passage.

"Home."

They started walking.

Mara's father followed behind them.

The passage seemed longer than before.

The blue marks on the walls were changing.

Some disappeared.

Others appeared.

One mark formed a sentence.

"She belongs to us."

Mara stopped.

Her mother pulled her forward.

"Don't read it."

Mara looked back.

The letters had changed.

"Mara."

Her name was now carved across the wall.

A cold wind rushed through the passage.

The lights went out.

Mara heard her father shout.

Then silence.

She reached for her mother.

Her hand found nothing.

"Mom!"

A soft voice answered from somewhere ahead.

"Keep walking."

Mara followed the voice.

A faint light appeared.

She stepped toward it.

The passage opened into the forest.

Morning light was beginning to appear between the trees.

Mara looked behind her.

The stone wall was gone.

Her mother stood beside the old tree.

Her father was there too.

But something was wrong.

Her mother was staring at the sky.

A black line had appeared across the clouds.

It stretched from one side of the forest to the other.

Her mother whispered,

"It found us."

Mara looked at her.

"What found us?"

Her mother took the silver necklace from Mara's hand.

She opened the small pendant.

Inside was a tiny piece of blue glass.

It began to glow.

Then a second voice came from the forest.

A voice that sounded exactly like Mara.

"Mom..."

Mara turned.

A girl stood between the trees.

She looked just like Mara.

Same face.

Same eyes.

Same red mark on her wrist.

Her mother stepped in front of Mara.

"Do not go near her."

The other girl smiled.

"But she already knows me."
