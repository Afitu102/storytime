👻 Whispers from the Hollow

📚 Classic Authors • 🕒 40 min read

🌫 Chapter One – A Voice in the Fog

The fog came before the rain.

It rolled down the hill just after sunset, slow and thick, covering the old road one stone at a time.

Mara stood beside the kitchen window and watched it swallow the trees.

She had seen fog many times.

This one was different.

It had a sound.

A soft whisper.

At first, she thought it was the wind moving through the grass.

Then she heard her name.

"Mara..."

She froze.

The cup in her hand shook.

She looked toward the hallway.

The house was quiet.

Her father was asleep upstairs.

The old clock near the door ticked slowly.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

Mara turned back to the window.

The fog had reached the garden.

Something stood inside it.

A tall shape.

Still.

Too still.

Mara moved closer to the glass.

The shape lifted one hand.

A finger pointed toward the hill.

Then the fog closed around it.

Mara stepped away from the window.

"No."

She whispered it to herself.

She had promised her father she would never go near that hill after dark.

Not because of a ghost story.

Not because of some old village tale.

There was another reason.

Her mother had disappeared there.

Mara was seven years old when it happened.

She still remembered the red scarf her mother wore that evening.

She still remembered the smell of rain.

And she still remembered the last thing her mother said.

"Stay inside until I come back."

She never came back.

For years, Mara tried to forget the hill.

She almost managed it.

Until tonight.

A second whisper came through the fog.

"Mara..."

This time, the voice sounded closer.

She opened the back door.

Cold air rushed into the kitchen.

The fog touched her feet.

Mara looked toward the hill.

A small yellow light was moving between the trees.

It stopped.

Then moved again.

Three times.

Slow.

Careful.

Like someone carrying a lantern.

Mara stepped outside.

The door clicked behind her.

She did not turn around.

The light on the hill moved higher.

Mara followed.

The grass was wet against her shoes.

Branches scratched her arms as she entered the trees.

The village lights disappeared behind her.

Only the yellow light remained.

She reached the old stone path.

Her mother had walked this path on that rainy evening.

Mara remembered that now.

She had forgotten the path.

But her feet remembered.

She walked deeper into the forest.

The lantern ahead suddenly went out.

Darkness filled the trees.

Mara stopped.

A sound came from behind her.

One dry branch breaking.

She turned.

Nothing.

Then came another sound.

Footsteps.

Slow.

Heavy.

Coming from the direction of the house.

Mara held her breath.

The footsteps stopped.

A warm yellow light appeared between the trees.

A woman stepped into view.

She wore a red scarf.

Mara's heart stopped for a moment.

The woman looked exactly like her mother.

Same dark hair.

Same small smile.

Same silver ring on her left hand.

Mara took one step forward.

"Mom?"

The woman said nothing.

She simply turned toward an old stone wall.

A small wooden door was hidden inside it.

Mara had never seen that door before.

The woman placed her hand against it.

The door opened.

A warm golden light spilled onto the forest floor.

The woman looked back at Mara.

"Come."

Mara stood there.

Her mind told her to leave.

Her heart wanted one answer.

Why did her mother never come home?

Mara stepped toward the door.

The woman smiled.

Then she whispered something that made Mara stop.

"Your father lied to you."

The door began to close.

Mara rushed forward.

Her hand caught the edge.

The golden light vanished.

The woman was gone.

Mara stood alone beside the stone wall.

Then she heard a faint sound beneath her feet.

A heartbeat.

Slow.

Deep.

Coming from underneath the earth.

Mara looked down.

A thin crack had appeared between the stones.

Something blue was glowing inside it.

She touched the stone.

The whole hill trembled.

And somewhere beneath the forest, a woman's voice whispered her name again.

"Mara..."

🌙 End of Chapter One

🕯 Chapter Two – Beneath the Stone

Mara stayed beside the wall.

The blue light was still there.

It moved beneath the cracks like a tiny flame trapped inside the stone.

She touched it again.

The ground gave a soft pulse.

Mara pulled her hand back.

A thin line of blue light spread across the wall.

It formed a shape.

A circle.

Inside the circle was a small drawing of a house.

Mara stared at it.

It was her house.

Same roof.

Same chimney.

Same little window beside the kitchen.

Then the drawing changed.

A second house appeared beneath it.

Mara did not know that house.

The image faded.

A low rumble came from the ground.

The stone door opened again.

This time there was no woman waiting on the other side.

Only a narrow staircase.

Mara looked into the darkness.

Cold air rose from below.

She could hear water dripping somewhere deep inside.

One drop.

Then another.

She took out her phone.

No signal.

The screen showed 11:47.

She had been gone from the house for almost an hour.

Her father would be awake soon.

She should turn back.

Instead, she stepped down.

The staircase was made of old stone.

Roots pushed through the walls.

Some were thin.

Some were thick as her arm.

A faint blue glow followed her.

It came from small marks carved into the steps.

Each mark looked like a letter.

Mara recognized the first one.

It was the first letter of her mother's name.

She stopped.

More letters appeared farther down.

Then another.

Then another.

Together they formed a sentence.

"She left a door open."

Mara frowned.

A door.

Her mother had always hated leaving doors open.

Even when Mara was little, her mother checked every door before going to bed.

Front door.

Back door.

Bedroom door.

Every one.

Mara continued down.

The staircase ended inside a large underground room.

A pool of clear water covered most of the floor.

At the center stood a wooden chair.

Something rested on it.

A red scarf.

Mara stopped breathing for a second.

She knew that scarf.

It was the one her mother wore the night she disappeared.

Mara walked through the shallow water.

The scarf was dry.

Perfectly dry.

She picked it up.

A small silver key fell from the folds.

Mara caught it before it touched the water.

There was writing on the key.

Two words.

"Room Seven."

Mara looked around.

Seven doors stood along the far wall.

Six were covered with moss.

The seventh was clean.

A small silver lock sat in the middle.

Mara walked toward it.

The key fit perfectly.

She turned it.

Click.

The door opened.

Inside was a small room.

There was a wooden desk.

A lamp.

Several old books.

And a photograph.

Mara picked it up.

Her mother stood in front of the same stone wall.

But she was not alone.

A young man stood beside her.

Mara knew him too.

Her father.

They were both smiling.

On the back of the photograph was a date.

Four years before Mara was born.

Under the date was a short note.

"We found it."

Mara turned the photograph over again.

Something else was written beneath the picture.

"But we should never have opened it."

A sound came from behind the desk.

Mara slowly looked over.

One of the books had fallen onto the floor.

It was open.

The pages were turning by themselves.

Mara moved closer.

A drawing filled the page.

It showed the hill.

The stone wall.

The hidden staircase.

And beneath the hill was a large black circle.

Something had been drawn inside it.

A woman.

Mara leaned closer.

The woman was wearing a red scarf.

Her mother.

Then a new line appeared on the page.

"She is still waiting."

Mara dropped the book.

The lamp went out.

The room became dark.

Then came three knocks from inside the wall.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Mara stepped backward.

A woman's voice came through the stone.

"Do not trust your father."

Mara held the red scarf against her chest.

Her eyes filled with tears.

She whispered the only question that mattered.

"Where are you?"

The wall answered.

"Under your home."

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🔦 Chapter Three – The Room Under the House

Mara stood in the dark room.

Her mother's voice was still inside the wall.

Under your home.

Those three words kept running through her mind.

She switched on her phone light.

The beam moved across the room.

Books.

Dust.

The old desk.

Nothing else.

Then she noticed the floor.

There was a wooden panel beneath the desk.

Mara pulled the desk aside.

A small metal handle appeared.

She pulled it.

The panel lifted.

Cold air rushed up.

Below was another staircase.

Mara stared into it.

The blue glow was there again.

It came from somewhere far below.

She climbed down.

The stairs ended beneath her own house.

Mara knew because she could see the wooden beams above her.

One beam had a small crack.

She remembered it from her childhood.

It was directly below her bedroom.

Her heart started beating faster.

Her mother had been here.

Maybe many times.

Mara moved through the narrow space.

A soft blue light came from the far end.

She followed it.

The passage opened into a wide chamber.

There was a bed.

A small table.

A mirror.

A wardrobe.

The room looked lived in.

Fresh flowers stood inside a glass jar.

A cup sat beside the bed.

Steam was rising from it.

Mara stopped.

Someone had been here.

Very recently.

She walked to the bed.

A notebook lay on the pillow.

Her mother's name was written on the cover.

Mara opened it.

The first page was blank.

The second page had one line.

"If Mara ever finds this place, tell her I never left by choice."

Mara covered her mouth.

She turned the page.

Another message waited there.

"Your father knows the truth."

Mara closed her eyes.

The next pages were filled with dates.

Every month.

Every year.

For eighteen years.

At the bottom of the last page was today's date.

Under it were four words.

"He brought her back."

Mara looked toward the door.

A sound came from the passage.

Footsteps.

She quickly hid behind the wardrobe.

The footsteps came closer.

A man entered the room.

Mara recognized his shoes.

Her father.

He walked to the table.

He picked up the cup.

Then he looked toward the bed.

"She found the room."

Mara stayed still.

Her father placed the cup down.

"I knew she would."

He opened the wardrobe.

Mara pressed herself against the wall.

Her father reached inside.

He pulled out a small wooden box.

Then he turned around.

Mara stepped out.

"Dad."

He froze.

The box slipped from his hand.

It hit the floor.

The lid opened.

Inside was a silver necklace.

Mara knew it at once.

Her mother had worn it every day.

Her father looked at her.

"You should not have come here."

Mara stepped closer.

"Where is Mom?"

He said nothing.

Mara picked up the necklace.

"Is she alive?"

Her father's eyes filled with tears.

He nodded.

Mara's breath caught.

"Where?"

He looked toward the wall.

"Behind that door."

Mara turned.

A narrow door had appeared beside the mirror.

It had not been there before.

A red scarf hung from the handle.

Mara reached for it.

Her father grabbed her wrist.

"Wait."

Mara looked at him.

"What are you afraid of?"

He lowered his voice.

"Your mother is not the only thing behind that door."

The door handle slowly moved.

Mara's father released her hand.

A woman's voice came from the other side.

"Let her in."

Mara looked at her father.

He stepped back.

The door opened.

And behind it stood her mother.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🕯 Chapter Four – Her Mother's Secret

Mara could not move.

Her mother stood in the doorway.

Older than the woman in Mara's memories.

But it was her.

The same eyes.

The same small smile.

The same silver ring.

Mara took one slow step forward.

"Mom?"

Her mother reached out.

Mara ran into her arms.

For a few seconds, the underground room disappeared.

There was only the warmth of her mother's arms.

The smell of her old perfume.

The soft touch of her hand against Mara's hair.

Mara pulled away.

"Why did you leave me?"

Her mother looked at the floor.

"I didn't."

Mara stared at her.

"Then why were you gone for eighteen years?"

Her mother looked toward Mara's father.

He turned away.

"Because of him."

Mara's face changed.

Her father closed his eyes.

Her mother walked toward the small table.

She opened the wooden box.

Inside was a folded piece of blue cloth.

She unfolded it.

A strange symbol was sewn into the middle.

Mara had seen that symbol before.

On the stone wall.

On the old staircase.

Inside the book.

"What is it?"

Her mother touched the symbol.

"It was the mark of the Hollow."

Mara looked toward the ceiling.

"The place on the hill?"

Her mother nodded.

"The Hollow is not a place."

She folded the cloth again.

"It is a door between two worlds."

Mara felt a chill move across her arms.

Her father finally spoke.

"Your mother found the door before you were born."

Mara turned toward him.

"And you followed her?"

He nodded.

"We thought we could control what was behind it."

Her mother shook her head.

"We were wrong."

The lamp flickered.

A deep sound moved through the walls.

Mara looked around.

The water beneath the floor began to ripple.

Her mother grabbed her hand.

"We have to leave."

Mara looked at her.

"Leave for where?"

Her mother looked toward the passage.

"Home."

They started walking.

Mara's father followed behind them.

The passage seemed longer than before.

The blue marks on the walls were changing.

Some disappeared.

Others appeared.

One mark formed a sentence.

"She belongs to us."

Mara stopped.

Her mother pulled her forward.

"Don't read it."

Mara looked back.

The letters had changed.

"Mara."

Her name was now carved across the wall.

A cold wind rushed through the passage.

The lights went out.

Mara heard her father shout.

Then silence.

She reached for her mother.

Her hand found nothing.

"Mom!"

A soft voice answered from somewhere ahead.

"Keep walking."

Mara followed the voice.

A faint light appeared.

She stepped toward it.

The passage opened into the forest.

Morning light was beginning to appear between the trees.

Mara looked behind her.

The stone wall was gone.

Her mother stood beside the old tree.

Her father was there too.

But something was wrong.

Her mother was staring at the sky.

A black line had appeared across the clouds.

It stretched from one side of the forest to the other.

Her mother whispered,

"It found us."

Mara looked at her.

"What found us?"

Her mother took the silver necklace from Mara's hand.

She opened the small pendant.

Inside was a tiny piece of blue glass.

It began to glow.

Then a second voice came from the forest.

A voice that sounded exactly like Mara.

"Mom..."

Mara turned.

A girl stood between the trees.

She looked just like Mara.

Same face.

Same eyes.

Same red mark on her wrist.

Her mother stepped in front of Mara.

"Do not go near her."

The other girl smiled.

"But she already knows me."

🌙 End of Chapter Four

🌲 Chapter Five – Girl Between the Trees

Mara stared at the girl.

For a moment, the forest seemed to hold its breath.

The girl had Mara's face.

But her eyes looked much older.

She wore a pale dress covered with tiny leaves.

Her bare feet rested on the wet ground.

Mara's mother moved closer to her.

"Stay behind me."

The girl tilted her head.

"She should know the truth."

Mara looked at her mother.

"Who is she?"

Her mother said nothing.

The girl answered instead.

"I'm the part of you that stayed here."

Mara felt a strange chill.

"What does that mean?"

The girl touched the tree beside her.

The bark began to glow.

A picture appeared across it.

Mara saw herself as a little girl.

She was standing beside her mother.

Her mother was holding the same silver necklace.

Then the picture changed.

Mara saw the night her mother disappeared.

But this time, she saw something she had never remembered.

Her mother was not walking toward the hill alone.

Mara's father was with her.

He carried a small wooden box.

They stopped beside the stone wall.

Her mother opened the box.

A blue light rose from it.

The little Mara in the memory began crying.

Her mother turned back.

She wanted to return.

Her father stopped her.

"We have to finish this."

The picture disappeared.

Mara turned toward her father.

"You left me there."

He looked down.

"I thought I was saving you."

Mara shook her head.

"From what?"

The girl between the trees stepped closer.

"From me."

Mara looked at her.

The girl raised her wrist.

The same red mark appeared there.

Then Mara looked at her own wrist.

Her mark was glowing.

The two marks answered each other.

A low hum moved through the forest.

The trees began shining with blue light.

Her mother grabbed Mara's shoulder.

"We need to go."

The girl smiled.

"You can't take her away again."

Her mother stopped.

"I won't."

The girl looked surprised.

Mara stepped forward.

Her mother tried to stop her.

"Let me talk to her."

She walked closer.

The other girl did not move.

Mara stood a few steps away.

"What's your name?"

The girl smiled.

"I don't have one."

Mara thought for a moment.

"Then I'll give you one."

The girl's expression softened.

"What name?"

Mara looked at the trees.

A small white flower had opened beside her foot.

"Elia."

The girl repeated it.

"Elia."

She smiled.

For the first time, she looked less like a shadow and more like a child.

Then the forest changed.

The black line in the sky widened.

Thunder rolled above the trees.

Elia looked upward.

"It's coming."

Mara's father stepped forward.

"What is coming?"

Elia pointed toward the hill.

A huge dark shape was rising behind the trees.

It looked like a tower.

But it had no windows.

No doors.

Only a large silver circle at the top.

Mara's mother stared at it.

"The Hollow has opened."

Mara looked at Elia.

"What happens now?"

Elia reached for Mara's hand.

Her fingers were warm.

"Now we go inside."

Mara looked toward the strange tower.

A doorway appeared at its base.

Blue light spilled from it.

Her mother took a deep breath.

Her father picked up the wooden box.

Mara held Elia's hand.

Together, they walked toward the tower.

The door opened wider.

A whisper came from inside.

"Welcome home, Mara."

🌙 End of Chapter Five

🗝 Chapter Six – The House That Remembered

The door opened without a sound.

Mara stepped inside with Elia beside her.

Her mother came next.

Her father stayed near the entrance.

The room beyond the door looked like an ordinary hallway.

Wooden floor.

Old paintings.

A long carpet running toward a staircase.

Mara looked at the walls.

She knew this place.

Not from a dream.

From home.

The same flower pattern was on the wallpaper in her bedroom.

The same small mirror hung beside the stairs.

Even the old crack above the window was there.

"This is our house."

Her mother nodded.

"It was built here before it was built in Bellmere."

Mara turned toward her.

"That makes no sense."

Her mother touched the wall.

The wallpaper moved beneath her fingers like water.

"That is what the Hollow does."

Elia walked ahead.

She stopped beneath a painting.

Mara looked at it.

The picture showed a family standing in front of the house.

Her grandfather stood in the middle.

Her mother was beside him.

Her father stood on the other side.

And between them was a little girl.

Mara.

But the girl beside her was Elia.

Mara stepped closer.

"That picture was never in our house."

Her father looked at it.

"Because I removed it."

Mara turned toward him.

"Why?"

He looked at Elia.

"Because it showed something I didn't want you to remember."

Elia looked away.

A door at the end of the hallway opened.

Warm light spilled across the carpet.

A voice came from inside.

"Bring the girl."

Mara stepped in front of Elia.

"She stays with me."

The voice answered.

"Then you stay too."

Mara looked at her mother.

Her mother nodded.

They entered the room.

It was a library.

Books filled every wall.

Some were old.

Some looked brand new.

A large round table stood in the center.

On it rested a silver bowl.

Inside the bowl was water.

The surface was perfectly still.

Elia walked toward it.

The water began to move.

A picture formed on the surface.

Mara saw her mother carrying a baby.

She was walking through the forest.

Her father followed behind her.

Then Mara saw the baby.

It was Elia.

Her mother placed the baby beside a blue tree.

A bright light surrounded the child.

Mara looked at her mother.

"You brought her here."

Her mother nodded.

"She was born with the mark."

Mara looked at Elia.

"What mark?"

Elia showed her wrist.

The red mark glowed again.

Mara looked at her own wrist.

Her mark answered with the same light.

The two lights joined.

The silver bowl cracked.

Water spilled across the table.

The books began falling from the shelves.

A deep sound shook the room.

Her mother grabbed Mara.

"Get away from the bowl."

Mara moved back.

Elia remained beside the table.

The water had gathered around her feet.

A shape rose from it.

A woman.

Mara recognized her.

Her grandmother.

Amara looked at Mara.

"The Hollow does not steal children."

Mara listened.

"It keeps what was given to it."

Her mother began crying.

"I gave her to save Mara."

Amara looked at her.

"And now the two must return together."

The room went quiet.

Mara looked at Elia.

"Return where?"

Amara raised her hand.

A door appeared behind Mara.

Beyond it was the forest.

But the forest was burning with blue light.

"To the place where the promise began."

Mara took Elia's hand.

Her mother followed.

Her father stayed behind for a moment.

Mara looked back at him.

"Are you coming?"

He looked at the broken bowl.

"Yes."

He walked toward them.

The four stepped through the door.

The library disappeared.

The forest stood before them.

At the center was the old stone wall.

But the wall was no longer broken.

A tall silver gate stood where the hidden door had been.

Mara's mother stopped.

Her father reached for her hand.

Together, they looked at the gate.

Elia whispered,

"It's awake."

The silver gate slowly opened.

Beyond it was a place filled with stars.

Mara stepped forward.

Then she heard a familiar voice behind her.

"Mara."

She turned.

Her mother was staring at the trees.

Something was standing there.

A man holding a lantern.

The same man Mara had seen on the hill.

Her father looked at him and whispered,

"He found us."

🌙 End of Chapter Six

🌘 Chapter Seven – Man with the Lantern

The man stood between the trees.

His lantern gave off a pale yellow light.

Mara could not see his face clearly.

Her father stepped in front of her.

"Stay back."

The man slowly raised the lantern.

Its light touched the ground.

A line of blue marks appeared in the soil.

They formed a path toward the silver gate.

"You brought them back."

Her father said nothing.

The man looked at Mara.

"You look just like her."

Mara stepped around her father.

"Who?"

The man smiled.

"Amara."

Her grandmother's name made the air feel colder.

Elia moved closer to Mara.

The man noticed her.

His smile disappeared.

"And you are the other one."

Elia held Mara's hand.

"My name is Elia."

The man looked at her for a long moment.

"Who gave you that name?"

"Mara."

The lantern flickered.

The man lowered it.

"Then she has already changed the story."

Mara frowned.

"What story?"

The man walked toward the silver gate.

"The one your family has been trying to finish."

Her mother stepped forward.

"Leave them alone."

The man looked at her.

"You were the one who opened the first door."

Her mother lowered her head.

"I was young."

"You knew what was behind it."

"I thought I could close it."

The man shook his head.

"Doors like this do not close. They wait."

Mara looked toward the silver gate.

A soft blue glow was moving behind it.

Something was approaching.

The man lifted his lantern.

The gate opened wider.

Mara could see a long road beyond it.

The road was covered with silver leaves.

Stars hung low above it.

At the end stood a large house.

Its windows were glowing.

Mara looked at her mother.

"Is that where this started?"

Her mother nodded.

"Yes."

Her father looked at the man.

"Why are you here?"

The man looked at him.

"To give you one chance."

He placed the lantern on the ground.

The flame turned blue.

"Walk through the gate."

Mara looked at Elia.

Elia nodded.

They stepped forward.

Her mother followed.

Her father came last.

The moment Mara crossed the gate, the forest vanished behind her.

She stood on the silver road.

The air felt warm.

The strange house ahead looked familiar.

Mara had seen it before.

Inside the first memory.

The house from the old painting.

The place where her grandfather had made the promise.

They reached the front door.

It opened before anyone touched it.

Inside was a large room.

A round table stood beneath a hanging lamp.

Four chairs surrounded it.

One chair was already occupied.

An old woman sat there.

Mara recognized her face.

Amara.

Her grandmother smiled.

"You came."

Mara walked toward her.

"Are you really here?"

Amara touched the chair beside her.

"Sit."

Mara sat down.

Elia sat beside her.

Their parents stood behind them.

Amara placed an old key on the table.

It was black.

A small silver moon was carved into it.

"This opens the final room."

Mara looked at the key.

"What is inside?"

Amara looked at Elia.

"The answer to why you were separated."

Mara picked up the key.

The moment her fingers touched it, the room shook.

A loud crack came from upstairs.

Amara stood.

"Too late."

Her mother looked toward the ceiling.

"What happened?"

Amara's face became serious.

"The Hollow has sent someone through."

The front door slammed shut.

The lantern outside went dark.

Then footsteps began moving across the roof.

Slowly.

One step.

Then another.

Mara held the black key tightly.

Amara looked at her.

"Whatever you hear next, do not open the upstairs door."

Mara looked toward the staircase.

A soft knock came from above.

Then a voice called her name.

"Mara..."

🌙 End of Chapter Seven

🌙 Chapter Eight – Door Above the Stairs

Mara stood beside the table.

The voice came again from upstairs.

"Mara..."

It sounded like her mother.

Mara turned toward the staircase.

Her mother caught her hand.

"Don't."

Mara looked at her.

"But that sounds like you."

Her mother shook her head.

"It knows what you remember."

Another knock came from upstairs.

This time, the whole house trembled.

Elia moved closer to Mara.

Amara picked up the black key.

"The final room is not upstairs."

Mara frowned.

"Then why is the voice coming from there?"

Amara looked beneath the table.

A small square of light appeared on the floor.

She moved the table aside.

A metal ring was hidden beneath the carpet.

Mara pulled it.

A trapdoor opened.

A staircase went down.

Mara looked at the stairs.

"We're already under the ground."

Amara smiled.

"Not under this ground."

She stepped through the opening.

Mara followed.

Elia came behind her.

Their parents followed last.

The trapdoor closed.

The voice upstairs disappeared.

They walked down.

The walls were covered with old photographs.

Mara stopped at one.

Her mother was standing beside a young Amara.

Between them was a baby.

Mara looked closer.

It was her.

But Elia was there too.

Two babies.

Two small blankets.

Two silver marks on their wrists.

Mara turned around.

"We were twins."

Her mother nodded.

"Yes."

Elia stared at the photograph.

"Why didn't you tell us?"

Her mother lowered her eyes.

"Because the Hollow wanted one of you."

Mara looked at her.

"Why?"

Amara answered.

"One carried the light."

She looked at Elia.

"The other carried the door."

Mara looked at her own wrist.

Her mark was glowing.

Elia's mark glowed too.

The two lights joined across the narrow passage.

A hidden door appeared.

Mara took out the black key.

She placed it into the lock.

Click.

The door opened.

Inside was a small round room.

A single chair stood in the center.

On the chair was a red scarf.

Mara's mother's scarf.

But beneath it was something else.

A small wooden box.

Mara picked it up.

Her father stepped forward.

"Leave it."

Mara looked at him.

"You knew about this?"

He nodded.

Mara opened the box.

Inside was a letter.

Her mother's handwriting covered the page.

Mara began reading.

"My dear Mara, if you are reading this, then Elia is beside you."

Mara looked at Elia.

She continued.

"I separated you because the Hollow could not take both of you."

"It followed Elia."

"It followed me."

"And I knew one day it would find you."

Mara's eyes filled with tears.

The letter continued.

"Your father believed he could destroy the Hollow."

"I knew better."

"The Hollow cannot be destroyed."

"It can only be changed."

Mara lowered the letter.

"How?"

Amara pointed toward the wall.

A large silver mirror appeared.

Mara walked toward it.

Her reflection stood inside.

Elia stood beside her.

Then the reflection changed.

The two girls became one figure.

The figure wore a silver crown.

Mara understood.

"We have to go back."

Her mother nodded.

"Together."

A loud crash came from above.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

The house began shaking.

Amara looked toward the stairs.

"It is here."

Mara held Elia's hand.

A black shadow appeared across the mirror.

A face slowly formed inside it.

Mara knew that face.

Her grandfather.

But his eyes were completely black.

He smiled.

"You finally found the door."

The mirror cracked.

A hand pushed through.

Mara stepped back.

Elia tightened her grip.

Amara raised her hand.

The silver room filled with light.

The hand stopped.

Then her grandfather's voice came through the broken mirror.

"Bring me the girls."

Mara looked at Elia.

"We're not going anywhere with him."

Elia smiled.

"Then let's finish this."

🌙 End of Chapter Eight

🌌 Chapter Nine – The Last Door

The mirror cracked from top to bottom.

A cold wind rushed through the room.

Mara held Elia's hand.

Neither girl moved.

Her grandfather's face remained inside the broken glass.

"You have no idea what you are carrying."

Mara looked at the mark on her wrist.

It was shining brighter than before.

"Then tell me."

Her grandfather smiled.

"Your mother never told you why she chose Elia."

Mara looked at her mother.

Her mother stayed silent.

Mara's grandfather continued.

"Elia was meant to become the key."

Elia stepped forward.

"Key to what?"

The old man's eyes moved toward her.

"The Hollow."

The room shook.

Amara raised both hands.

A silver circle appeared beneath Mara and Elia.

"Do not listen to him."

Mara looked at her grandmother.

"Then what are we supposed to do?"

Amara looked toward the broken mirror.

"Close the door from inside."

Mara understood.

She looked at Elia.

"We go together."

Elia nodded.

Their mother stepped toward them.

"I am coming too."

Mara shook her head.

"No."

Her mother looked surprised.

"You waited eighteen years for us."

Mara smiled through her tears.

"Now wait for us at home."

Her mother hugged her.

Then she hugged Elia.

Mara's father stepped closer.

He placed the silver necklace around Mara's neck.

"Your mother gave me this the night you were born."

Mara touched the pendant.

"Why give it to me now?"

He smiled sadly.

"Because it belongs with you."

The mirror opened.

A silver road appeared beyond it.

Mara and Elia stepped onto the road.

The light followed them.

Behind them, the mirror began closing.

Her grandfather shouted from inside.

"Mara!"

She turned.

His face was almost gone.

He looked less angry now.

He looked tired.

Mara stopped.

"Grandfather."

He looked at her.

"Do you remember the river?"

Mara nodded.

"Then remember this too."

His voice became soft.

"I was wrong."

The mirror closed.

Mara turned toward the road.

Elia was waiting.

Ahead stood a huge silver door.

There was no handle.

Only two handprints.

Mara placed her hand on one.

Elia placed hers on the other.

The door opened.

Beyond it was the Hollow.

It was not dark.

It was beautiful.

A wide valley stretched beneath a violet sky.

Silver trees grew beside a quiet river.

Hundreds of blue flowers floated above the water.

Mara walked slowly.

Elia followed.

At the center of the valley stood a stone arch.

The same words were carved across it.

"Give what you promised."

Mara touched the writing.

The ground opened beneath the arch.

A deep blue light rose from below.

Elia's mark became brighter.

Mara's mark answered.

The two lights joined.

A large shadow appeared above them.

It spread across the violet sky.

The Hollow was changing.

Mara understood what her mother had meant.

The Hollow did not need to be destroyed.

It needed someone to open the right door.

She held Elia's hand.

"Ready?"

Elia smiled.

"Ready."

Together, they stepped beneath the arch.

The blue light surrounded them.

The shadow above began shrinking.

The valley became brighter.

Then a voice filled the sky.

"Promise."

Mara looked at Elia.

"We promise to share the light."

The sky flashed white.

The shadow disappeared.

The silver trees began to bloom.

The river rose into the air like a shining ribbon.

The old stone arch broke apart.

Behind it was a new path.

A path leading home.

Mara looked back one last time.

The Hollow was no longer frightening.

It looked peaceful.

Elia smiled.

"Let's go."

They walked toward the new path.

Far behind them, a single blue flower opened.

Inside it was a tiny silver key.

🌙 End of Chapter Nine
