🤰 Pregnant Without Men

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🤰 Chapter One – The Woman Who Became Pregnant

In the quiet village of Umuorie, there was a woman named Nneka who was known by everyone, but understood by almost no one.

Nneka was twenty-eight years old. She was gentle, hardworking, and always kept to herself. Every morning, she woke before the first rooster crowed, swept the front of her small mud house, fetched water from the village stream, and went to the market to sell baskets of vegetables.

She had never been married.

She had never been known to have a boyfriend.

In fact, the women of the village often joked that Nneka was too careful with men.

“If a man even looks at her twice, she will carry her basket and disappear,” Mama Ijeoma once laughed.

Everyone knew Nneka's life. Or at least, they thought they did.

Then one morning, something strange happened.

Nneka was standing beside the village stream when she suddenly dropped the clay pot in her hands.

The pot crashed against the stones and broke into pieces.

Several women turned around.

“Nneka, what happened?” one of them asked.

Nneka did not answer.

She stood completely still, one hand pressed against her stomach.

Her face had gone pale.

“I feel sick,” she whispered.

The women helped her home.

At first, nobody thought much about it. They assumed she had eaten something bad.

But the sickness did not stop.

For several weeks, Nneka began waking up every morning feeling weak. She could no longer tolerate the smell of roasted fish. The smell of palm oil made her nauseous. Even the aroma of fresh soup cooking in the next compound made her run outside.

Then people began noticing something else.

Her stomach was growing.

At first, it was small enough to ignore.

Then it became impossible to hide.

By the time the moon had changed twice, the entire village knew.

Nneka was pregnant.

The news spread through Umuorie faster than fire through dry grass.

Women stopped their work just to whisper about her. Men gathered beneath the old iroko tree to discuss it. Even children began repeating the strange words they had heard from their parents.

“She is pregnant.”

“But who is the father?”

Nobody knew.

When Nneka's mother was asked, she became angry.

“Leave my daughter alone,” she warned.

But that only made the villagers more curious.

One evening, three elderly women visited Nneka's house.

They did not come to congratulate her.

They came to question her.

Nneka sat quietly on a wooden stool while the women stared at her stomach.

Finally, Mama Ijeoma spoke.

“Nneka, tell us the truth. Who is the man?”

Nneka lowered her eyes.

“There is no man,” she said.

The room became silent.

One of the women laughed nervously.

“Stop joking. A woman cannot become pregnant by herself.”

“I know,” Nneka replied softly.

“Then tell us his name.”

Nneka slowly raised her eyes.

“I don't know.”

The women stared at her.

“What do you mean you don't know?”

“I don't know how this happened.”

That answer frightened them more than anything else.

By the next morning, people had already started calling Nneka strange names.

Some said she was carrying a spirit child.

Others said she had secretly visited a witch in the forest.

Some believed she had been lying about her life all along.

And some simply refused to go near her.

Women who once sat beside her at the market now moved away whenever she approached.

Mothers warned their children not to enter Nneka's compound.

Even some of her closest friends stopped visiting.

Nneka became alone.

But the strangest part was that she never fought anyone.

She never shouted.

She never insulted those who insulted her.

She simply carried her growing stomach and continued with her life.

Then, one afternoon, something happened that made the entire village afraid.

Nneka was returning from the stream when she suddenly stopped on the narrow path leading into the village.

She looked down at her stomach.

For the first time, she felt the baby move.

But it was not an ordinary movement.

Nneka felt three slow knocks from inside her womb.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

She froze.

Then she heard something.

A faint whisper.

So faint that she almost believed she had imagined it.

“Mama...”

Nneka dropped the water pot.

She looked around the empty path.

Nobody was there.

Then the voice came again.

“Mama... don't let them find me.”

Nneka's blood ran cold.

She placed both hands over her stomach.

Her eyes filled with tears.

And for the first time since she discovered she was pregnant, Nneka became afraid of the child growing inside her.

Because she suddenly realized something terrifying.

Whatever was inside her was not only growing.

It could speak.

🌙 End of Chapter One

😨 Chapter Two – The Voice Inside Her

Nneka did not sleep that night.

She sat on the edge of her bed with her back against the wall, staring at the small lamp burning beside her.

Her hands rested gently on her stomach.

She was still thinking about what she had heard on the lonely path.

“Mama... don't let them find me.”

The words kept playing in her mind.

Who was inside her?

And what did it mean by, “Don't let them find me”?

Nneka wanted to tell someone.

But who would believe her?

The whole village already believed she was hiding something.

If she told them that the child inside her had spoken, they would surely call her a witch.

She covered her face and began to cry quietly.

Then suddenly, she felt another movement.

This time, it was gentle.

Almost like the child was trying to comfort her.

Nneka slowly removed her hands from her face.

“My child,” she whispered, “what are you?”

There was no answer.

Only silence.

The following morning, Nneka went to the market as usual.

But the moment she entered, the conversations around her became quiet.

Women looked at her stomach.

Some quickly turned away.

Others whispered behind their hands.

“That is the woman.”

“The one carrying a child without a husband.”

“I heard she refused to tell anyone who got her pregnant.”

“Maybe she knows exactly what she is doing.”

“Don't stand too close to her.”

Nneka heard everything.

She pretended not to.

She sat behind her small table and arranged her vegetables.

But nobody came to buy from her.

Even people who had bought from her for years suddenly avoided her stall.

By midday, an old woman named Mama Ebele walked past and stopped.

She stared at Nneka for a long moment.

Then she shook her head.

“My daughter,” she said, “there are things a person should never hide from the village.”

Nneka looked up.

“I am not hiding anything.”

Mama Ebele lowered her voice.

“Then tell us who the father is.”

“There is no father that I know of.”

The old woman stepped backward.

Her eyes widened.

“Don't say such things.”

“It is the truth.”

Mama Ebele stared at her for another few seconds before walking away.

That evening, the village chief called a meeting.

Almost everyone came.

Men sat beneath the huge iroko tree while the women gathered behind them.

Nneka stood alone in the middle of the crowd.

The chief looked at her seriously.

“Nneka, nobody wants to shame you,” he said. “But your situation has become a mystery that concerns the whole village.”

Nneka lowered her head.

“I understand.”

“Then answer one question. Who is the man responsible for your pregnancy?”

Nneka swallowed hard.

“There is no man.”

A loud murmur spread through the crowd.

The chief raised his hand for silence.

“Nneka, think carefully before you answer again.”

“I have thought about it every day.”

“Then how did you become pregnant?”

Nneka opened her mouth.

But before she could speak, she felt a sharp movement inside her stomach.

She suddenly bent over.

Everyone gasped.

Then something happened that nobody expected.

A deep voice came from somewhere behind the crowd.

“Leave her alone.”

Everyone turned.

Nobody knew who had spoken.

The crowd became restless.

“Who said that?”

“Was it you?”

“No.”

“I didn't say anything.”

The chief stood up.

“Silence!”

Nneka slowly lifted her head.

Her heart was beating violently.

She knew that voice.

It was the same voice she had heard on the path.

But this time, everyone had heard it.

The chief looked around the crowd.

“Who spoke?”

Nobody answered.

Then the voice came again.

“Leave her alone.”

This time, the sound seemed to come from Nneka herself.

The entire village went silent.

People stared at her stomach.

One woman screamed and ran away.

Another began praying.

A man shouted, “This is not a normal pregnancy!”

Within seconds, the meeting broke apart.

People ran in every direction.

Nneka stood frozen.

She could barely breathe.

The chief was the only person who remained near her.

He looked frightened, but he did not run.

“Nneka,” he whispered, “what is inside you?”

Tears rolled down her cheeks.

“I don't know.”

That night, nobody in Umuorie slept peacefully.

Doors were locked.

Children were kept indoors.

People gathered in small groups, discussing what had happened.

Some believed Nneka had been possessed.

Others believed she had made a secret agreement with something in the forest.

But there was one person who did not believe any of those stories.

Her name was Mama Chika.

She was the oldest woman in the village and had delivered more babies than anyone could remember.

She had been watching Nneka quietly from the beginning.

And that night, she finally decided to visit her.

Just after midnight, Mama Chika walked toward Nneka's house carrying a small lantern.

She knocked once.

No answer.

She knocked again.

“Nneka?”

The door slowly opened.

Mama Chika stepped inside.

Nneka was sitting on her bed.

Her eyes were red from crying.

Mama Chika sat beside her.

“I am not here to judge you,” the old woman said.

Nneka looked at her.

“Then why are you here?”

Mama Chika placed one hand gently on Nneka's stomach.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the baby moved.

Mama Chika's expression suddenly changed.

She pulled her hand away.

“Nneka...”

“What is it?”

The old woman stared at her with fear in her eyes.

“I have felt thousands of unborn children in my lifetime.”

She paused.

“But I have never felt anything like this.”

Before Nneka could ask another question, the lamp beside them suddenly went out.

The room became completely dark.

Then, from inside Nneka's womb, came a tiny whisper.

“Mama Chika...”

The old woman froze.

She slowly looked at Nneka.

“You heard that too?”

Nneka nodded.

Then the voice spoke again.

“Tell them... the truth is under the old tree.”

Mama Chika's hands began to tremble.

“The old tree?” she whispered.

Nneka stared at her.

“What old tree?”

Mama Chika did not answer.

She already knew exactly which tree the child meant.

The oldest tree in Umuorie.

The tree nobody was allowed to approach after sunset.

And according to an old story passed down through generations, something had been buried beneath its roots many years ago.

Something the villagers had promised never to uncover.

Mama Chika stood up slowly.

“Nneka,” she whispered, “tomorrow before sunrise, you and I are going to that tree.”

But neither of them knew that someone was already standing outside the house, listening to every word they had said.

And that person had heard the secret too.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌳 Chapter Three – The Secret Beneath the Old Tree

Before the first rooster crowed, Mama Chika was already standing outside Nneka's house.

She carried a small lantern in one hand and a walking stick in the other.

Nneka stepped outside quietly.

Neither woman spoke as they walked through the sleeping village.

The streets were empty.

Most people were still inside their houses, unaware that two women were walking toward the one place everyone in Umuorie feared.

The old tree stood at the far end of the village.

It was enormous.

Its branches stretched over the land like the hands of an old giant, and its roots rose from the ground like thick snakes.

Nobody went there after dark.

Children were warned never to play around it.

Even hunters avoided passing beneath it.

As Nneka and Mama Chika approached, Nneka suddenly stopped.

“My stomach is moving again,” she whispered.

Mama Chika turned around.

“Is the baby moving?”

Nneka nodded.

Then three soft knocks came from inside her womb.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Mama Chika's face became serious.

“It knows we are here.”

They continued walking.

When they finally reached the tree, Nneka felt a strange warmth pass through her body.

She placed one hand on the tree.

Suddenly, a sharp pain ran through her stomach.

She cried out and fell to her knees.

Mama Chika quickly held her.

“Nneka!”

Nneka looked up at her.

“I heard something.”

“What did you hear?”

Nneka pointed toward the roots.

“There.”

Mama Chika listened.

At first, she heard nothing.

Then came a faint sound beneath the earth.

A soft humming.

Mama Chika's eyes widened.

She knew that sound.

Many years ago, when she was still a young woman, the elders had told her a story about that tree.

But it was a story she had never dared to repeat.

“There is something buried here,” she whispered.

Nneka stared at her.

“What?”

Mama Chika slowly pointed at a large root.

“Help me move this.”

Together, they pushed away the loose soil around the root.

After several minutes, Nneka's fingers touched something hard.

She pulled it out.

It was a small clay pot.

The pot was covered in old markings.

Mama Chika stared at it.

Then she stepped backward.

“No.”

“What is it?” Nneka asked.

Mama Chika refused to answer.

Instead, she looked toward the village.

For a moment, she had the strange feeling that someone was watching them.

“Nneka, put that pot down.”

“Why?”

“Just put it down.”

Nneka slowly placed it on the ground.

The moment the pot touched the earth, the wind suddenly became strong.

The branches of the old tree began shaking violently.

Nneka grabbed Mama Chika's arm.

Then the voice came.

“Mama...”

Nneka froze.

Mama Chika heard it too.

The old woman looked at Nneka's stomach.

“Who are you?” she whispered.

The voice did not answer.

Instead, the clay pot cracked.

A thin line appeared across its surface.

Then another.

Suddenly, the pot broke open.

Inside was a small piece of faded cloth wrapped around something.

Nneka bent down and picked it up.

She carefully opened the cloth.

Inside was an old wooden necklace.

Mama Chika stared at it.

Her hands began to tremble.

“I remember this.”

“You know it?”

“Yes.”

“Whose is it?”

Mama Chika looked at Nneka.

“It belonged to your mother.”

Nneka's eyes widened.

“My mother?”

“She wore it when she was young.”

“But why was it buried here?”

Mama Chika remained silent.

Nneka became impatient.

“Tell me the truth.”

The old woman sighed.

“Your mother did not tell you everything about the day you were born.”

Nneka stared at her.

“What are you talking about?”

“Your mother came to this tree many years ago.”

“Why?”

Mama Chika looked down at the necklace.

“She came here because she could not have children.”

Nneka's mouth opened slightly.

“My mother?”

“Yes.”

“She had tried for many years. Nothing happened. Then one night, she came here alone.”

Nneka's heart began beating faster.

“What happened that night?”

Mama Chika looked toward the old tree.

“That is the part nobody wanted to remember.”

Before Nneka could ask another question, they heard footsteps behind them.

Both women turned.

A man was standing several metres away.

His face was hidden in the darkness.

Nneka immediately recognized his voice.

It was the same man who had been listening outside her house the previous night.

“Give me that necklace,” he said.

Mama Chika stepped in front of Nneka.

“Who are you?”

The man moved closer.

“You were never supposed to find that.”

Nneka held the necklace tightly.

“Why?”

The man stopped.

For several seconds, nobody spoke.

Then he looked directly at Nneka's stomach.

“You have no idea what you are carrying.”

Nneka's body went cold.

“What do you mean?”

The man turned and began walking away.

“Wait!” Mama Chika shouted.

He stopped without turning around.

Then he said something that left both women speechless.

“The child inside her was born once before.”

Nneka stared at him.

“What?”

The man disappeared into the darkness.

Nneka looked at Mama Chika.

The old woman was trembling.

“Mama Chika,” Nneka whispered, “what does he mean?”

Mama Chika did not answer.

She was staring at the wooden necklace in Nneka's hand.

Because she had suddenly remembered something she had kept secret for twenty-eight years.

Something about Nneka's birth.

Something that could explain why Nneka had become pregnant without a man.

And if Mama Chika was right, the truth was far more shocking than anyone in Umuorie could imagine.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🌑 Chapter Four – The Truth Her Mother Hid

Nneka could not move.

She stood beneath the old tree, holding the wooden necklace in her trembling hands while Mama Chika stared at her.

“The child inside her was born once before.”

The stranger's words kept repeating in her mind.

“What does that mean?” Nneka asked again.

Mama Chika looked away.

For twenty-eight years, she had kept a secret.

A secret she had promised Nneka's mother she would never reveal.

But now that Nneka was carrying a child under such strange circumstances, the old promise no longer felt more important than the truth.

“Nneka,” Mama Chika finally said, “your mother did not become pregnant with you the way everyone believed.”

Nneka's heart stopped for a moment.

“What are you saying?”

“Your mother was married to your father. But before you were born, something happened that frightened the entire village.”

Nneka shook her head.

“Tell me.”

Mama Chika took a deep breath.

“Your mother had been married for seven years without a child. She visited healers. She prayed. She fasted. She tried everything people told her to try.”

Nneka listened quietly.

“Then one evening, she came to this tree.”

“Alone?”

“Yes.”

“What happened?”

“She told me that she heard a voice calling her from beneath the tree.”

Nneka looked at the ground.

“A voice?”

Mama Chika nodded.

“She said the voice promised that she would have a child.”

“Did she agree to something?”

Mama Chika remained silent for a moment.

“That is what she refused to tell me.”

Nneka's eyes filled with tears.

“Then how did I come into this world?”

Mama Chika looked directly into her eyes.

“Three months after that night, your mother became pregnant.”

Nneka stepped backward.

“But I thought my father was the one who got her pregnant.”

“So did everyone else.”

A cold breeze passed through the trees.

Nneka looked at the necklace again.

“Why was this buried here?”

“Because your mother brought it here the night she came.”

Nneka suddenly remembered something her mother had once told her when she was a little girl.

Whenever Nneka asked where she was born, her mother would smile and say, “You came to me through a door that nobody can see.”

At the time, Nneka had thought it was only a mother's strange way of speaking.

Now the words frightened her.

“What happened to my mother?”

“She died before she could explain everything.”

Nneka covered her mouth.

She had always believed her mother's death was caused by illness.

But Mama Chika's next words made her blood run cold.

“Your mother died three days after she gave birth to you.”

“Why didn't anyone tell me?”

“Because your father begged us to keep quiet.”

“Why?”

Mama Chika looked toward the village.

“Because something happened the night you were born.”

Before she could continue, they heard a loud cry from the village.

“Help!”

Nneka and Mama Chika turned.

People were running toward the old tree.

The village chief was among them.

When he reached them, he looked at the necklace in Nneka's hand.

His face immediately changed.

“Where did you get that?”

Nneka held it up.

“It was buried under the tree.”

The chief stared at Mama Chika.

“You told me this secret was gone forever.”

Mama Chika shook her head.

“I didn't tell her. She found it herself.”

The chief looked frightened.

Then Nneka asked the question that had been burning inside her.

“What happened the night I was born?”

The chief said nothing.

“Tell me!”

The old man finally lowered his head.

“When you were born, your mother was already very weak. Your father was outside the hut. Suddenly, everyone heard a baby's cry.”

Nneka swallowed.

“I was born?”

“Yes.”

“But that was normal.”

“No,” the chief whispered. “It was not.”

Everyone around them became silent.

“What happened?”

The chief looked at Nneka's stomach.

“The baby stopped crying after a few minutes.”

Nneka's eyes widened.

“What baby?”

“You.”

The chief continued.

“Your mother thought you were dead. But when the midwife picked you up, you suddenly opened your eyes.”

Nneka stared at him.

“And then?”

The chief's voice became softer.

“You looked toward the doorway.”

“Why?”

“Because someone was standing there.”

“Who?”

“Nobody knows.”

A murmur passed through the crowd.

The chief continued.

“The person disappeared before anyone could reach them.”

Nneka felt a shiver run through her body.

Then suddenly, the baby inside her moved.

Everyone saw her stomach shift.

The villagers stepped backward.

One woman began praying.

Another pulled her child behind her.

But this time, Nneka did not feel afraid.

She felt something else.

Recognition.

The child was trying to tell her something.

She placed both hands over her stomach.

“Tell me,” she whispered.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the baby moved again.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Nneka suddenly heard the voice.

But this time, it was clearer than ever.

“Mama... I am not here to hurt you.”

Nneka's eyes filled with tears.

“Then why are you here?”

The voice became quiet.

Then it whispered something that made Mama Chika drop her walking stick.

“I came back because they buried my father beneath this tree.”

The entire crowd froze.

Nneka slowly looked at the old tree.

“Who is your father?”

There was a long silence.

Then the voice answered.

“The man everyone in this village believes died twenty-eight years ago.”

Mama Chika's face turned pale.

Because she knew exactly who the child meant.

Nneka's father.

And according to the village records, he had not died twenty-eight years ago.

He had disappeared.

Nobody had ever found his body.

Until that moment.

Everyone slowly looked down at the roots of the old tree.

And beneath the largest root, the ground had begun to move.

Something was pushing its way out.

🌙 End of Chapter Four
