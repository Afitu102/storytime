🤰 Pregnant Without Men

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🤰 Chapter One – The Woman Who Became Pregnant

In the quiet village of Umuorie, there was a woman named Nneka who was known by everyone, but understood by almost no one.

Nneka was twenty-eight years old. She was gentle, hardworking, and always kept to herself. Every morning, she woke before the first rooster crowed, swept the front of her small mud house, fetched water from the village stream, and went to the market to sell baskets of vegetables.

She had never been married.

She had never been known to have a boyfriend.

In fact, the women of the village often joked that Nneka was too careful with men.

“If a man even looks at her twice, she will carry her basket and disappear,” Mama Ijeoma once laughed.

Everyone knew Nneka's life. Or at least, they thought they did.

Then one morning, something strange happened.

Nneka was standing beside the village stream when she suddenly dropped the clay pot in her hands.

The pot crashed against the stones and broke into pieces.

Several women turned around.

“Nneka, what happened?” one of them asked.

Nneka did not answer.

She stood completely still, one hand pressed against her stomach.

Her face had gone pale.

“I feel sick,” she whispered.

The women helped her home.

At first, nobody thought much about it. They assumed she had eaten something bad.

But the sickness did not stop.

For several weeks, Nneka began waking up every morning feeling weak. She could no longer tolerate the smell of roasted fish. The smell of palm oil made her nauseous. Even the aroma of fresh soup cooking in the next compound made her run outside.

Then people began noticing something else.

Her stomach was growing.

At first, it was small enough to ignore.

Then it became impossible to hide.

By the time the moon had changed twice, the entire village knew.

Nneka was pregnant.

The news spread through Umuorie faster than fire through dry grass.

Women stopped their work just to whisper about her. Men gathered beneath the old iroko tree to discuss it. Even children began repeating the strange words they had heard from their parents.

“She is pregnant.”

“But who is the father?”

Nobody knew.

When Nneka's mother was asked, she became angry.

“Leave my daughter alone,” she warned.

But that only made the villagers more curious.

One evening, three elderly women visited Nneka's house.

They did not come to congratulate her.

They came to question her.

Nneka sat quietly on a wooden stool while the women stared at her stomach.

Finally, Mama Ijeoma spoke.

“Nneka, tell us the truth. Who is the man?”

Nneka lowered her eyes.

“There is no man,” she said.

The room became silent.

One of the women laughed nervously.

“Stop joking. A woman cannot become pregnant by herself.”

“I know,” Nneka replied softly.

“Then tell us his name.”

Nneka slowly raised her eyes.

“I don't know.”

The women stared at her.

“What do you mean you don't know?”

“I don't know how this happened.”

That answer frightened them more than anything else.

By the next morning, people had already started calling Nneka strange names.

Some said she was carrying a spirit child.

Others said she had secretly visited a witch in the forest.

Some believed she had been lying about her life all along.

And some simply refused to go near her.

Women who once sat beside her at the market now moved away whenever she approached.

Mothers warned their children not to enter Nneka's compound.

Even some of her closest friends stopped visiting.

Nneka became alone.

But the strangest part was that she never fought anyone.

She never shouted.

She never insulted those who insulted her.

She simply carried her growing stomach and continued with her life.

Then, one afternoon, something happened that made the entire village afraid.

Nneka was returning from the stream when she suddenly stopped on the narrow path leading into the village.

She looked down at her stomach.

For the first time, she felt the baby move.

But it was not an ordinary movement.

Nneka felt three slow knocks from inside her womb.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

She froze.

Then she heard something.

A faint whisper.

So faint that she almost believed she had imagined it.

“Mama...”

Nneka dropped the water pot.

She looked around the empty path.

Nobody was there.

Then the voice came again.

“Mama... don't let them find me.”

Nneka's blood ran cold.

She placed both hands over her stomach.

Her eyes filled with tears.

And for the first time since she discovered she was pregnant, Nneka became afraid of the child growing inside her.

Because she suddenly realized something terrifying.

Whatever was inside her was not only growing.

It could speak.

🌙 End of Chapter One

😨 Chapter Two – The Voice Inside Her

Nneka did not sleep that night.

She sat on the edge of her bed with her back against the wall, staring at the small lamp burning beside her.

Her hands rested gently on her stomach.

She was still thinking about what she had heard on the lonely path.

“Mama... don't let them find me.”

The words kept playing in her mind.

Who was inside her?

And what did it mean by, “Don't let them find me”?

Nneka wanted to tell someone.

But who would believe her?

The whole village already believed she was hiding something.

If she told them that the child inside her had spoken, they would surely call her a witch.

She covered her face and began to cry quietly.

Then suddenly, she felt another movement.

This time, it was gentle.

Almost like the child was trying to comfort her.

Nneka slowly removed her hands from her face.

“My child,” she whispered, “what are you?”

There was no answer.

Only silence.

The following morning, Nneka went to the market as usual.

But the moment she entered, the conversations around her became quiet.

Women looked at her stomach.

Some quickly turned away.

Others whispered behind their hands.

“That is the woman.”

“The one carrying a child without a husband.”

“I heard she refused to tell anyone who got her pregnant.”

“Maybe she knows exactly what she is doing.”

“Don't stand too close to her.”

Nneka heard everything.

She pretended not to.

She sat behind her small table and arranged her vegetables.

But nobody came to buy from her.

Even people who had bought from her for years suddenly avoided her stall.

By midday, an old woman named Mama Ebele walked past and stopped.

She stared at Nneka for a long moment.

Then she shook her head.

“My daughter,” she said, “there are things a person should never hide from the village.”

Nneka looked up.

“I am not hiding anything.”

Mama Ebele lowered her voice.

“Then tell us who the father is.”

“There is no father that I know of.”

The old woman stepped backward.

Her eyes widened.

“Don't say such things.”

“It is the truth.”

Mama Ebele stared at her for another few seconds before walking away.

That evening, the village chief called a meeting.

Almost everyone came.

Men sat beneath the huge iroko tree while the women gathered behind them.

Nneka stood alone in the middle of the crowd.

The chief looked at her seriously.

“Nneka, nobody wants to shame you,” he said. “But your situation has become a mystery that concerns the whole village.”

Nneka lowered her head.

“I understand.”

“Then answer one question. Who is the man responsible for your pregnancy?”

Nneka swallowed hard.

“There is no man.”

A loud murmur spread through the crowd.

The chief raised his hand for silence.

“Nneka, think carefully before you answer again.”

“I have thought about it every day.”

“Then how did you become pregnant?”

Nneka opened her mouth.

But before she could speak, she felt a sharp movement inside her stomach.

She suddenly bent over.

Everyone gasped.

Then something happened that nobody expected.

A deep voice came from somewhere behind the crowd.

“Leave her alone.”

Everyone turned.

Nobody knew who had spoken.

The crowd became restless.

“Who said that?”

“Was it you?”

“No.”

“I didn't say anything.”

The chief stood up.

“Silence!”

Nneka slowly lifted her head.

Her heart was beating violently.

She knew that voice.

It was the same voice she had heard on the path.

But this time, everyone had heard it.

The chief looked around the crowd.

“Who spoke?”

Nobody answered.

Then the voice came again.

“Leave her alone.”

This time, the sound seemed to come from Nneka herself.

The entire village went silent.

People stared at her stomach.

One woman screamed and ran away.

Another began praying.

A man shouted, “This is not a normal pregnancy!”

Within seconds, the meeting broke apart.

People ran in every direction.

Nneka stood frozen.

She could barely breathe.

The chief was the only person who remained near her.

He looked frightened, but he did not run.

“Nneka,” he whispered, “what is inside you?”

Tears rolled down her cheeks.

“I don't know.”

That night, nobody in Umuorie slept peacefully.

Doors were locked.

Children were kept indoors.

People gathered in small groups, discussing what had happened.

Some believed Nneka had been possessed.

Others believed she had made a secret agreement with something in the forest.

But there was one person who did not believe any of those stories.

Her name was Mama Chika.

She was the oldest woman in the village and had delivered more babies than anyone could remember.

She had been watching Nneka quietly from the beginning.

And that night, she finally decided to visit her.

Just after midnight, Mama Chika walked toward Nneka's house carrying a small lantern.

She knocked once.

No answer.

She knocked again.

“Nneka?”

The door slowly opened.

Mama Chika stepped inside.

Nneka was sitting on her bed.

Her eyes were red from crying.

Mama Chika sat beside her.

“I am not here to judge you,” the old woman said.

Nneka looked at her.

“Then why are you here?”

Mama Chika placed one hand gently on Nneka's stomach.

For several seconds, nothing happened.

Then the baby moved.

Mama Chika's expression suddenly changed.

She pulled her hand away.

“Nneka...”

“What is it?”

The old woman stared at her with fear in her eyes.

“I have felt thousands of unborn children in my lifetime.”

She paused.

“But I have never felt anything like this.”

Before Nneka could ask another question, the lamp beside them suddenly went out.

The room became completely dark.

Then, from inside Nneka's womb, came a tiny whisper.

“Mama Chika...”

The old woman froze.

She slowly looked at Nneka.

“You heard that too?”

Nneka nodded.

Then the voice spoke again.

“Tell them... the truth is under the old tree.”

Mama Chika's hands began to tremble.

“The old tree?” she whispered.

Nneka stared at her.

“What old tree?”

Mama Chika did not answer.

She already knew exactly which tree the child meant.

The oldest tree in Umuorie.

The tree nobody was allowed to approach after sunset.

And according to an old story passed down through generations, something had been buried beneath its roots many years ago.

Something the villagers had promised never to uncover.

Mama Chika stood up slowly.

“Nneka,” she whispered, “tomorrow before sunrise, you and I are going to that tree.”

But neither of them knew that someone was already standing outside the house, listening to every word they had said.

And that person had heard the secret too.
