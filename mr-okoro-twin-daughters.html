👭 Mr Okoro Twin Daughters

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌅 Chapter One – The Beautiful Twin Daughters

In the village of Umuoma, there lived a man called Mr Okoro.

Mr Okoro was a respected man.

He was not the richest man in the village, but he was known for his good heart, his hard work, and the way he cared for his family.

His wife was called Amara.

Amara was a quiet and hardworking woman.

She was the kind of woman who could wake up before the first cock crow and still be the last person to sleep at night.

She cooked for the family.

She washed their clothes.

She worked in the farm.

She also made sure that her children never forgot the good ways of the village.

But there was one thing that made Mr Okoro especially proud.

His two daughters.

Their names were Oge and Udoka.

They were twins.

From the day they were born, people knew that the two girls were special.

They looked almost exactly alike.

They had the same bright eyes.

They had the same beautiful smile.

They had the same smooth skin.

They even walked in almost the same way.

When they were still little, people often struggled to know who was Oge and who was Udoka.

Sometimes, even their neighbours would call one of them by the wrong name.

The girls would laugh.

“Papa, they called me Udoka again.”

Mr Okoro would laugh and say,

“Let them keep guessing. One day they will know you.”

But as the girls grew older, their beauty became even more noticeable.

Oge was beautiful.

Udoka was beautiful.

And when they stood together, people could not stop looking at them.

The two sisters did not only have beauty.

They were also very hardworking.

Every morning, they helped their mother before going about their own work.

They could fetch water.

They could help prepare food.

They could work on the farm.

They could clean the house.

They could also sit down and study for hours.

Their father always told people,

“My daughters are not only beautiful. They have good sense.”

And he was right.

Oge was very sharp.

She could understand things quickly.

She loved reading.

She was also very good at remembering things.

Udoka was just as smart.

She loved asking questions.

Whenever something confused her, she would not stop until she understood it.

The two girls were different in small ways, but they were very close.

If Oge had something, Udoka had it too.

If Udoka was sick, Oge stayed close to her.

If Oge was worried, Udoka would notice before anybody else.

They were sisters.

They were twins.

And they were best friends.

One evening, Mr Okoro returned from the farm.

He was tired.

His clothes were covered with dust.

He sat under the mango tree in front of his house.

Amara brought him water.

“Drink first,” she said.

Mr Okoro drank the water and smiled.

“Where are my daughters?”

Amara pointed towards the back of the house.

“They are washing the clothes.”

Mr Okoro smiled again.

“My girls are always working.”

Amara sat beside him.

“You are always praising them.”

“Why should I not praise them?”

He looked towards the back of the house.

“Look at what you gave me, Amara.”

Amara smiled.

“You mean what we were given.”

Mr Okoro laughed.

“No. I know what I am saying. You gave me these beautiful girls.”

Amara shook her head.

“You always know how to make me laugh.”

Mr Okoro became quiet for a moment.

Then he said,

“I thank God for you every day.”

Amara looked at him.

“And I thank God for you too.”

The two sat quietly under the mango tree.

They could hear the voices of their daughters behind the house.

Oge was laughing.

Udoka was laughing too.

Their laughter filled the compound.

Mr Okoro smiled.

“That sound is enough for me.”

Amara nodded.

“It is.”

As the years passed, the beauty of Oge and Udoka became known outside their village.

People from nearby villages began talking about them.

Travellers who passed through Umuoma heard about the beautiful twins.

Some people came just to see them.

Others came because they wanted to ask questions about them.

Then something else began to happen.

Men started looking for them.

Young men came.

Older men came.

Rich men came.

Poor men came.

Men from nearby villages came.

Men from far places came.

Everybody seemed to have heard about Oge and Udoka.

One day, a wealthy man named Chief Nwosu arrived in Umuoma.

He came with two men carrying baskets of gifts.

There was rice.

There was palm oil.

There were clothes.

There were drinks.

There were also other things meant for Mr Okoro and his family.

The village people watched as the men entered the compound.

Mr Okoro came out to greet them.

“Welcome.”

Chief Nwosu smiled.

“I came because I heard about your daughters.”

Mr Okoro looked at him.

“My daughters?”

“Yes.”

Chief Nwosu smiled.

“I heard they are beautiful girls.”

Mr Okoro laughed.

“They are.”

Chief Nwosu looked towards the house.

“Can I see them?”

Mr Okoro called out.

“Oge! Udoka!”

Two voices answered from inside.

“Yes, Papa!”

The two girls came out together.

Chief Nwosu stared at them.

For a moment, he said nothing.

The two sisters greeted him.

“Good afternoon, sir.”

“Good afternoon.”

Chief Nwosu looked at one.

Then he looked at the other.

He looked at them again.

He became confused.

“Which one is Oge?”

The sisters laughed.

Oge pointed at herself.

“I am Oge.”

Udoka pointed at herself.

“And I am Udoka.”

Chief Nwosu laughed.

“Now I understand why people say you are difficult to tell apart.”

Mr Okoro smiled.

“It has been like that since they were children.”

Chief Nwosu looked at the girls again.

“They are beautiful.”

Mr Okoro nodded.

“They are also hardworking.”

“They are educated too,” Amara added.

Chief Nwosu nodded.

“I can see that.”

But Oge and Udoka were not interested in the gifts.

They thanked him politely and returned to what they were doing.

Chief Nwosu watched them leave.

He turned to Mr Okoro.

“I want to speak with you.”

Mr Okoro knew what was coming.

“About my daughters?”

Chief Nwosu nodded.

“Yes.”

Mr Okoro sat down.

“Then speak.”

Chief Nwosu told him that he wanted to marry one of the twins.

Mr Okoro listened carefully.

He did not rush to answer.

He loved his daughters too much to give them away because of money.

Finally, he said,

“My daughters will choose for themselves.”

Chief Nwosu was surprised.

“But I have money.”

Mr Okoro smiled.

“Money is good.”

He paused.

“But my daughters are not things to be bought.”

Chief Nwosu became quiet.

He understood.

Before he left, he told Mr Okoro that he would wait.

But Chief Nwosu was not the only man interested.

A few days later, another man came.

Then another.

Then another.

Soon, people began joking that Mr Okoro's compound had become a meeting place for men looking for wives.

Even the village children noticed.

Whenever a strange man entered the compound, they would whisper,

“Another one has come.”

The adults would laugh.

But Oge and Udoka did not allow the attention to change them.

They still woke up early.

They still helped their mother.

They still studied.

They still respected their father.

And they still laughed together.

One evening, the twins sat outside the house.

Oge looked at Udoka.

“Do you know how many men came today?”

Udoka laughed.

“I stopped counting.”

Oge laughed too.

“Papa must be tired.”

Udoka shook her head.

“Papa enjoys it.”

They both laughed.

Then Udoka became serious.

“But one day, we will have to choose.”

Oge looked at her sister.

“Yes.”

“And when that day comes, I want us to choose well.”

Oge nodded.

“We will.”

Neither sister knew that their beauty and resemblance would soon bring them into a situation that would test their love for each other.

They did not know that one man would mistake one sister for the other.

They did not know that a secret would soon enter the Okoro family.

And they did not know that the strongest bond between them would be tested.

But for that evening, they remained together.

The sun slowly disappeared behind the trees.

The village became quiet.

Amara called them inside.

“Oge! Udoka! Come and eat.”

The twins stood up together.

They smiled at each other.

Then they walked into the house side by side.

Behind them, Mr Okoro watched his daughters with pride.

He looked at Amara.

“My wife,” he said softly.

Amara looked at him.

“Yes?”

“Thank you.”

“For what?”

“For giving me these two.”

Amara smiled.

“They are ours.”

Mr Okoro nodded.

“Yes.”

And as the night covered Umuoma, nobody in that family knew that the story of the beautiful twins was only beginning.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌿 Chapter Two – Two Sisters, One Heart

Morning came softly to the village of Umuora.

The first sound in the village was the sound of birds singing from the trees. Then came the sound of women sweeping their compounds. Soon after, smoke began to rise from the small kitchens as mothers prepared food for their families.

Inside the compound of Mr Okoro, Oge and Udoka were already awake.

The two sisters were standing beside a large basin of water. They were washing clothes together.

Oge held one side of a cloth while Udoka washed the other side.

They worked quietly at first.

Then Udoka started laughing.

Oge looked at her.

"Why are you laughing?"

Udoka smiled.

"Because I just remembered what happened yesterday."

"What happened?"

Udoka laughed again.

"That young man who greeted me three times because he thought I was you."

Oge laughed too.

"He did not even know who he was talking to."

"He called me Oge."

"And you answered him."

"I did not want to embarrass him."

The two sisters laughed together.

This was something that happened often in the village.

People found it hard to tell Oge and Udoka apart when they stood far away from each other.

Their faces looked alike.

Their smiles looked alike.

Even the way they walked sometimes looked alike.

But their mother, Amara, could always tell them apart.

Their father, Mr Okoro, could also tell them apart even when they were standing among many other girls.

To him, they were not just twins.

They were two different gifts from God.

Oge was calm and patient.

She liked to think before she spoke.

Udoka was more lively.

She liked to ask questions and make people laugh.

But both girls had something in common.

They were hardworking.

They were smart.

And they respected their parents.

That was what made Mr Okoro proud of them more than their beauty.

After washing the clothes, the sisters carried them to the rope behind the house.

They spread the clothes under the warm morning sun.

Their mother, Amara, watched them from the doorway.

She smiled.

"Come inside and eat something."

Oge looked at her mother.

"We are coming, Mama."

Udoka quickly added,

"Let us finish this one first."

Amara shook her head.

"Always working."

She smiled and went back inside.

A few minutes later, the two girls joined their parents.

Mr Okoro was already sitting down.

He looked at his daughters and smiled.

"My two beautiful daughters."

Udoka sat beside him.

"Daddy, you say that every morning."

"And I will continue saying it."

Oge smiled.

Mr Okoro looked at his wife.

"Amara, you gave me two beautiful daughters."

Amara smiled.

"They are not only beautiful. They have good hearts."

Mr Okoro nodded.

"That is what makes me proud."

He looked at Oge and Udoka again.

"Beauty can attract people, but good character keeps good people around you."

The girls listened quietly.

Their father always reminded them of this.

Many people in the village admired Oge and Udoka.

Some admired their beauty.

Some admired their intelligence.

Some admired how they worked.

And some simply admired the way the two sisters respected everyone.

But their parents did not want their daughters to become proud.

They always reminded them that beauty would not last forever.

Character would.

One afternoon, something happened that made the whole village talk again.

Oge and Udoka had gone to the market with their mother.

They were carrying baskets of vegetables.

As they walked through the market, people began looking at them.

One woman whispered to another.

"Look at them."

The other woman smiled.

"Those are Mr Okoro's daughters."

"Which one is Oge?"

The woman looked carefully.

"I don't know."

They both laughed.

Even some of the young men in the market found it hard to tell them apart.

One young man walked toward them.

"Oge."

Udoka turned around.

The young man stopped.

He looked confused.

"Sorry. I mean Udoka."

Oge laughed from behind him.

The young man turned quickly.

Now he was even more confused.

Amara laughed.

"Don't worry. Many people make that mistake."

The young man smiled with embarrassment.

"I have known them since they were small, but sometimes I still get confused."

Udoka smiled.

"Maybe you need to look better."

Everyone around them laughed.

The two sisters continued with their mother.

But their beauty was not the only thing people noticed.

At the market, they helped their mother arrange the vegetables.

They greeted the older women.

They helped a little girl who had dropped some oranges.

They also helped an old man carry his basket.

Mr Okoro had taught them to respect people no matter who they were.

Rich or poor.

Young or old.

Strong or weak.

Everyone deserved respect.

That was why many people loved them.

Later that evening, the family sat outside their house.

The sky was turning orange.

The evening breeze moved gently through the trees.

Mr Okoro sat beside Amara.

Oge and Udoka sat near their mother.

Mr Okoro looked at his daughters for a long time.

"My children."

They looked at him.

"I want you to remember something."

Oge nodded.

Udoka listened carefully.

"Many people will come because of your beauty."

He paused.

"Some will come because they hear that you are hardworking."

He looked at them again.

"Some will come because they hear that you are smart."

Amara listened quietly.

"But do not let the attention of people change you."

Oge lowered her head.

Udoka smiled softly.

Mr Okoro continued.

"The person who truly loves you will respect your heart, not only your face."

The two girls understood what their father meant.

They had already started hearing marriage talks from different families.

Some men came to greet their father.

Some sent their parents.

Some only came to look at the two sisters from a distance.

But Mr Okoro was not in a hurry.

He wanted his daughters to choose wisely.

That night, after everyone had gone inside, Oge and Udoka sat together.

Udoka looked at her sister.

"Oge."

"Yes?"

"Do you think one day people will stop confusing us?"

Oge smiled.

"I don't think so."

Udoka laughed.

"Then we will grow old together and people will still be asking which one is Oge."

Oge laughed.

"And which one is Udoka."

They held hands.

They had always been close.

They were sisters.

They were twins.

They were best friends.

And neither of them knew that life was about to bring something that would test their bond.

Far away from their village, a family had already heard about the beautiful daughters of Mr Okoro.

A wealthy man had heard stories about their beauty and good character.

He wanted to know which of the two girls was Oge.

But before he could find out, another man had already arrived in the village.

He came with a different reason.

And his arrival would soon change the quiet life of the two sisters.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🏡 Chapter Three – The Visitor From Afar

The next morning came with a strange feeling in the Okoro compound.

The sun had barely risen when Mr Okoro heard someone calling his name from outside.

“Mr Okoro!”

He came out of the house and saw a man standing near the gate.

The man was dressed in clean clothes and carried a walking stick.

He looked like someone who had travelled a long distance.

Mr Okoro walked towards him.

“Good morning.”

“Good morning, sir.”

“Who are you?”

The man smiled.

“My name is Emeka.”

Mr Okoro looked at him carefully.

“Emeka from where?”

“I came from the village of Ogbala.”

Mr Okoro nodded.

“Ogbala is far from here.”

“Yes. I travelled since yesterday.”

“Then come inside and rest.”

Emeka thanked him and followed him into the compound.

Amara came out when she heard the voices.

She greeted the visitor and brought him water.

After drinking, Emeka looked around the compound.

He seemed to be searching for someone.

Mr Okoro noticed it.

“You are looking for something.”

Emeka smiled.

“I heard about your daughters.”

Mr Okoro laughed.

“Again?”

Amara smiled too.

“These girls have made their father famous.”

Emeka laughed.

“I did not come because of their beauty alone.”

Mr Okoro became serious.

“What did you hear?”

“I heard that they are hardworking. I heard they are educated. I heard they respect their parents.”

Mr Okoro nodded slowly.

“That is true.”

Emeka looked towards the house.

“Can I meet them?”

Mr Okoro called them.

“Oge! Udoka!”

The two girls came out together.

They greeted the visitor.

“Good morning, sir.”

Emeka stood quietly.

He looked at Oge.

Then he looked at Udoka.

He smiled.

“So the stories are true.”

Udoka smiled.

“What stories?”

Emeka laughed.

“That there are two beautiful twins living in this compound.”

Oge laughed softly.

“People talk too much.”

Emeka nodded.

“They do.”

But something about the visitor made the sisters curious.

He was not behaving like the other men who had visited their father.

He did not stare at them.

He did not talk about their beauty.

He asked them about their studies.

He asked what kind of work they liked.

He asked about their plans for the future.

Oge liked the way he spoke.

Udoka also noticed it.

After some time, Emeka turned to Mr Okoro.

“I have something to discuss with you.”

Mr Okoro nodded.

“Let us talk.”

The two men moved to a quiet place near the mango tree.

Emeka explained that his family had heard about Oge and Udoka.

He said his older brother had asked him to come and speak with Mr Okoro.

Mr Okoro listened.

“Which of my daughters?”

Emeka smiled.

“That is the problem.”

Mr Okoro laughed.

“Why?”

“My brother does not know which one he wants.”

Mr Okoro laughed loudly.

“He has not even met them?”

“He has seen them from far.”

Mr Okoro shook his head.

“Then he should come himself.”

“That is what I told him.”

Emeka stood up.

“But before he comes, I wanted to see the family myself.”

Mr Okoro appreciated his honesty.

“You are welcome.”

While the two men talked, Oge and Udoka were inside the house with their mother.

Udoka looked at Oge.

“That man is different.”

Oge smiled.

“I noticed.”

“He did not even ask which one is more beautiful.”

Oge laughed.

“Maybe he already knows.”

Udoka smiled.

“Maybe.”

Amara listened to them.

“Do not start thinking too much.”

The girls laughed.

“Mama, we are only talking.”

“I know.”

Amara smiled.

“But remember what your father told you.”

Oge nodded.

“Beauty is not enough.”

“Exactly,” Amara said.

“Look at the heart of the person.”

The visitor stayed in the village for the rest of the day.

He ate with the family.

He spoke with Mr Okoro.

He also spent some time talking with Oge and Udoka.

Before sunset, he prepared to leave.

Mr Okoro walked him to the gate.

“I will tell my brother everything.”

“Tell him to come himself.”

“He will.”

Emeka smiled.

“I believe he will like this family.”

Mr Okoro smiled.

“Let him come first.”

Emeka left.

The girls watched him disappear down the road.

Udoka turned to Oge.

“Do you think his brother will come?”

Oge smiled.

“I think so.”

But that was not the only thing happening in the village.

That same evening, another man was asking questions about the Okoro family.

His name was Chinedu.

Unlike Emeka, Chinedu had grown up in Umuora.

He had known the twins for years.

But he had always admired them from a distance.

He wanted to marry one of them.

The problem was that he had never been brave enough to tell them.

That evening, Chinedu went to see his mother.

“Mama.”

His mother looked at him.

“What is it?”

“I want to marry.”

His mother smiled.

“Who is the woman?”

Chinedu became quiet.

“You know her.”

“Then tell me.”

“Mr Okoro's daughter.”

His mother immediately understood.

“Oge?”

Chinedu shook his head.

“Udoka?”

He became quiet again.

His mother laughed.

“You don't even know which one you want.”

“I know.”

“Then say her name.”

Chinedu smiled.

“Oge.”

His mother nodded.

“Then you must go and speak with Mr Okoro.”

Chinedu agreed.

But he did not know that several other men had already started planning the same thing.

The beauty of the twins had become a big story.

People talked about them in the market.

People talked about them at the farm.

People talked about them during village meetings.

Some people praised their beauty.

Others praised their good character.

Some even joked that Mr Okoro would soon need a bigger house because of the number of visitors coming to see him.

Mr Okoro laughed whenever he heard such jokes.

But deep inside, he knew something.

His daughters would not remain in his house forever.

One day, they would marry.

One day, they would have their own homes.

One day, the compound would become quiet.

The thought made him happy and sad at the same time.

That night, he sat beside Amara.

“Our girls are growing fast.”

Amara smiled.

“I know.”

“Soon they will leave us.”

Amara held his hand.

“That is life.”

Mr Okoro looked towards the room where the girls were sleeping.

“I only want them to be happy.”

“They will be.”

“I pray they choose good men.”

Amara nodded.

“They will choose with their hearts.”

Mr Okoro smiled.

“I trust them.”

Inside the room, Oge and Udoka were still awake.

They were talking quietly.

Udoka whispered,

“Oge.”

“Yes?”

“Do you ever think about leaving this house?”

Oge became quiet.

“Sometimes.”

“I don't want us to separate.”

Oge reached for her sister's hand.

“We will never stop being sisters.”

Udoka smiled.

“Promise?”

“Promise.”

They held hands for a moment.

Outside, the night wind moved through the trees.

The village was quiet.

But somewhere on the road, another visitor was already making his way toward Umuora.

He had heard about the twins.

He had heard about their beauty.

He had heard about their intelligence.

And he had a reason for coming that would make the story of Oge and Udoka even more interesting.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

💍 Chapter Four – The Choice That Changed Everything

The next morning, Umuora was already busy before the sun came up fully.

Women were sweeping their compounds. Farmers were preparing for the day. Children were running around the village.

But inside Mr Okoro's compound, everyone was talking about one thing.

The visitor who had arrived the previous night had finally introduced himself.

His name was Obinna.

He came from a wealthy family in a village far from Umuora.

He was not an old man.

He was young, calm and well respected by the people who knew him.

He had come with two older men from his family.

They waited outside Mr Okoro's house until he came out.

Mr Okoro greeted them.

"Welcome to my home."

Obinna bowed his head.

"Thank you, sir."

The older men greeted Mr Okoro too.

They sat under the mango tree.

Amara brought them water.

After everyone had settled down, one of the older men spoke.

"We have come because we have heard good things about your family."

Mr Okoro smiled.

"I hope the things you heard are good."

The man laughed.

"They are very good."

Obinna remained quiet.

He was listening.

The older man continued.

"We heard about your daughters."

Mr Okoro looked at Obinna.

"So that is why you came."

"Yes."

Mr Okoro nodded.

He did not say anything more.

He wanted Obinna to speak for himself.

After a short silence, Obinna finally spoke.

"I have heard about Oge and Udoka for some time."

Mr Okoro listened.

"I heard they are beautiful."

He paused.

"But I also heard they are hardworking and respectful."

Mr Okoro smiled.

"That is what matters."

Obinna nodded.

"I agree."

Mr Okoro then called his daughters.

"Oge! Udoka!"

The two sisters came outside.

They greeted everyone.

"Good morning."

The visitors greeted them.

Obinna looked at them.

For a moment, he did not know which one was Oge.

The two sisters noticed.

Udoka smiled.

Oge also smiled.

Mr Okoro laughed.

"You see why I always tell people to look carefully?"

Everyone laughed.

Obinna smiled.

"I understand now."

He looked at Oge.

Then he looked at Udoka.

"But I will take my time."

Mr Okoro liked that answer.

He did not want a man to choose his daughter simply because she was beautiful.

He wanted a man who would understand her heart.

The visitors stayed for a while.

Obinna asked Oge about her work.

He asked Udoka about her plans.

He listened more than he spoke.

That made Amara notice something.

When the visitors finally left, she looked at her husband.

"What do you think?"

Mr Okoro smiled.

"I am watching."

Amara laughed.

"You always say that."

"I am their father."

"I know."

"They will not marry because someone has money."

Amara nodded.

"They will marry because they are respected and loved."

"Exactly."

That same afternoon, Oge and Udoka went to the farm.

They helped their father with some work.

Oge carried a basket of vegetables.

Udoka helped clear a small part of the farm.

Mr Okoro watched them.

"My daughters."

They looked at him.

"You don't have to do everything."

Udoka smiled.

"We are your daughters."

Oge added,

"We want to help."

Mr Okoro smiled proudly.

This was what he loved about them.

They did not allow attention to change them.

They still worked.

They still helped their mother.

They still respected their father.

They still greeted people in the village.

And they still laughed like children when they were together.

Later that evening, something unexpected happened.

A young boy came running into the compound.

"Mr Okoro!"

Mr Okoro turned.

"What happened?"

The boy was breathing heavily.

"There is trouble near the old road."

Everyone became quiet.

"What kind of trouble?"

"A woman fell down while carrying firewood."

Oge stood up immediately.

"Where is she?"

"Near the old bridge."

Udoka looked at her sister.

They both started moving.

Mr Okoro followed them.

When they reached the old bridge, they saw an old woman sitting on the ground.

Her basket was beside her.

Some firewood had fallen into the grass.

Oge rushed to her.

"Mama, are you alright?"

The woman nodded slowly.

"My leg hurts."

Udoka picked up the basket.

"Don't worry. We will help you."

The sisters helped the woman stand.

They carried her firewood.

Then they walked with her slowly back to the village.

Mr Okoro watched them.

He felt proud.

The next morning, the story had already spread.

People were talking about what the twins had done.

An old woman told another woman,

"Those girls have good hearts."

Another person said,

"That is why many people want them."

But Oge and Udoka did not care about the praise.

They simply continued with their lives.

Meanwhile, Obinna had not forgotten what he saw.

He had returned to his village.

He told his family about Oge and Udoka.

His father asked him,

"Which one do you want?"

Obinna smiled.

"I am not sure yet."

His father laughed.

"How can you not know?"

"They look alike."

His mother laughed too.

"But their hearts are not the same."

Obinna nodded.

"That is why I want to know them better."

His father became serious.

"Then do not rush."

"I won't."

Back in Umuora, another family was also preparing to visit Mr Okoro.

This time, it was Chinedu's family.

Chinedu had finally gathered enough courage to speak to his mother.

"I want Oge."

His mother smiled.

"Then we will go."

Chinedu was nervous.

"What if her father says no?"

"Then you accept his answer."

Chinedu nodded.

The next day, his family arrived.

Mr Okoro welcomed them.

They sat under the mango tree.

After greetings, Chinedu's father spoke.

"We came to speak with you about your daughter."

Mr Okoro nodded.

"Which daughter?"

Everyone laughed.

Chinedu looked embarrassed.

"Oge."

Mr Okoro looked at him.

"Why Oge?"

Chinedu took a deep breath.

"Because she is kind."

He paused.

"She is hardworking."

Another pause.

"And she respects people."

Mr Okoro smiled.

He liked the answer.

But he did not give an answer immediately.

"Marriage is not something we decide in one day."

Chinedu nodded.

"I understand."

Mr Okoro looked at his wife.

Amara nodded quietly.

The family continued talking.

Later, Chinedu was allowed to speak with Oge.

They sat under a tree near the compound.

Chinedu looked nervous.

Oge noticed.

"You don't have to be nervous."

He smiled.

"I have wanted to talk to you for a long time."

"Why did you wait?"

"I was afraid."

Oge laughed softly.

"Afraid of me?"

"Not of you."

"Then what?"

"Of your father."

Oge laughed.

"My father is not a lion."

Chinedu smiled.

"He looks like one when he is serious."

Oge laughed again.

For the first time, Chinedu felt relaxed.

They talked for a long time.

They spoke about work.

They spoke about family.

They spoke about the future.

But Oge did not make any promise.

She wanted time.

She wanted to know him better.

And she wanted to be sure that his heart was honest.

When the sun started going down, Chinedu returned home with his family.

Oge watched them leave.

Udoka came beside her.

"You like him."

Oge looked at her sister.

"I did not say that."

Udoka smiled.

"You don't have to say it."

Oge pushed her gently.

"Go away."

Both sisters laughed.

They did not know what the future would bring.

They only knew that their lives were changing.

Their beauty had brought attention.

Their hard work had brought respect.

And their good hearts had brought people from far and near.

But among all the people coming into their lives, one person would soon prove that love was not about money, beauty or family name.

It was about trust.

It was about respect.

And most of all, it was about knowing the person behind the face.

🌙 End of Chapter Four

❤️ Chapter Five – The Sisters' Happy Home

The days passed quietly in Umuora.

The story of Oge and Udoka had now travelled far beyond their village.

People knew them for their beauty.

People knew them for their intelligence.

But those who knew them well knew something even better.

They had good hearts.

They worked hard.

They respected their parents.

And they never allowed the attention they received to make them proud.

Mr Okoro was happy whenever he watched his daughters.

Sometimes he would sit under the mango tree and remember when they were little girls.

He remembered how they used to run around the compound.

He remembered how Amara would call them when it was time to eat.

He remembered how the two girls would always hold hands.

Now they had grown into young women.

Many men had come to ask for their hands in marriage.

But Mr Okoro had never allowed money or family name to decide for his daughters.

He wanted them to be happy.

He wanted them to be respected.

And he wanted them to marry people who would treat them well.

One evening, Mr Okoro sat with Oge.

"My daughter."

Oge looked at him.

"Yes, Daddy?"

"Have you thought about what you want?"

Oge became quiet.

She knew what her father meant.

She had been thinking about Chinedu.

She liked the way he spoke to her.

She liked that he respected her.

He did not care only about her beauty.

He cared about her dreams.

He cared about her family.

And he never tried to force her to make a decision.

"I like Chinedu," Oge finally said.

Mr Okoro smiled.

"Do you trust him?"

"I am beginning to."

"Then take your time."

Oge nodded.

Mr Okoro placed his hand on her shoulder.

"Marriage should bring peace to your heart."

Oge smiled.

"I understand, Daddy."

On the other side of the compound, Udoka was talking with her mother.

Amara looked at her daughter.

"What about you?"

Udoka smiled.

"I am not ready yet."

Amara laughed.

"That is also an answer."

Udoka smiled.

"I want to build my own life first."

Amara nodded proudly.

"Then do it."

Udoka wanted to continue her studies and build a business of her own.

She had always been interested in helping women in the village.

She wanted to create a small place where women could learn different skills and make money for themselves.

Her father supported the idea.

He told her,

"If your heart is telling you to help people, do not be afraid to follow it."

Udoka smiled.

"Thank you, Daddy."

A few weeks later, Chinedu's family returned to the Okoro compound.

This time, they came with gifts.

They greeted Mr Okoro and Amara.

The family sat together under the mango tree.

Chinedu looked nervous.

His father spoke first.

"We have come again."

Mr Okoro smiled.

"You are welcome."

"We have spoken with our son."

Mr Okoro nodded.

"And?"

"He is still asking for Oge."

Everyone laughed.

Mr Okoro looked at Chinedu.

"Are you sure?"

Chinedu smiled.

"Yes, sir."

"Why?"

Chinedu looked at Oge.

"Because she has a good heart."

He paused.

"She respects people."

He smiled.

"And she makes me want to become a better man."

Mr Okoro became quiet.

He liked what he heard.

Amara looked at her husband.

They both understood each other without saying much.

Mr Okoro finally turned to Oge.

"My daughter, what do you say?"

Oge smiled.

"I want him too."

Chinedu smiled with relief.

The family celebrated.

Udoka ran to her sister and hugged her.

"I am happy for you."

Oge hugged her back.

"And I am happy for you too."

Udoka laughed.

"For what?"

"For your new business."

Udoka smiled.

"That one is coming soon."

The wedding preparations began.

People from different villages came to help.

Women prepared food.

Men arranged seats.

Children helped carry small things.

The compound became full of happiness.

Even people who had once come hoping to marry Oge came to celebrate with her.

There was no fighting.

There was no jealousy.

Everyone wished her well.

When the wedding day finally came, the village was full of music.

Oge looked beautiful.

Udoka stood beside her.

For a moment, some people became confused again.

One old man looked at them and asked,

"Which one is the bride?"

Everyone laughed.

Udoka pointed at Oge.

"This one."

Oge laughed.

"Finally."

The celebration continued.

Mr Okoro stood beside Amara.

He looked at his daughter with tears in his eyes.

Amara held his hand.

"You are crying."

Mr Okoro smiled.

"I am happy."

"I know."

He looked at Amara.

"Thank you."

"For what?"

"For giving me these two beautiful girls."

Amara smiled.

"They are our blessing."

Mr Okoro nodded.

"Our greatest blessing."

After the wedding, Oge and Chinedu started their new life together.

Chinedu treated her with respect.

Oge continued working.

She also remained close to her family.

Udoka continued with her plans.

She started the small business she had dreamed about.

She helped many women learn new skills.

Some women learned how to make clothes.

Some learned how to cook and sell food.

Others learned how to make small businesses of their own.

Udoka became known for helping people.

Her father was proud of her.

Whenever people praised Oge and Udoka, Mr Okoro would smile.

But he always said the same thing.

"Beauty brought attention to my daughters, but their character gave them respect."

The people of Umuora never forgot the two sisters.

They were still sometimes confused when they saw them together.

They still laughed about it.

Oge and Udoka also laughed about it.

They knew that no matter where life took them, they would always remain sisters.

Years later, when they had their own families, they still returned to their father's compound.

The mango tree was still there.

The old house was still there.

And Mr Okoro still sat under the tree whenever the evening breeze came.

He would look at his daughters and smile.

His home was no longer as quiet as he once thought it would be.

Their children filled the compound with laughter.

Oge and Udoka would sit together and remember the days when people could not tell them apart.

Then they would laugh.

Their mother Amara would watch them from the doorway.

She would smile and say,

"Some things never change."

And Mr Okoro would answer,

"Thank God for that."

🌿 Moral of the Story

Beauty is good, but a good heart is better.

A person should not choose a husband or wife because of money or beauty alone.

Respect, kindness, honesty and good character are what make a home peaceful.

Oge and Udoka showed everyone that beauty becomes more valuable when it comes with a good heart.

📖 The End
