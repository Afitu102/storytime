🌧️ The Dance of the Rain

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌧️ Chapter One – Katu and the Rain

In a small village surrounded by tall green trees, there lived a boy named Katu.

Katu was not the strongest boy in the village. He was not the fastest runner either. But there was one thing Katu could do better than almost everybody.

He could dance.

And Katu loved dancing in the rain.

Whenever the dark clouds gathered in the sky and the first drops of rain began to fall, Katu would look up and smile.

Sometimes he would even shout,

"Rain! My friend has come!"

Then before anyone could stop him, Katu would run outside.

He would jump into the wet ground, raise his hands, turn around and dance as if the whole village was watching him.

And funny enough, the whole village was watching him.

The other children would stand under the trees, laughing and pointing at him.

"Look at Katu!"

"See how he is dancing!"

"Is he dancing or is the rain carrying him?"

Katu did not care.

He would keep dancing.

He would stamp his feet in the water, shake his body and turn around until his little wrapper became heavy with rain.

Sometimes he would slip.

And when he fell, the children would laugh so hard that some of them would fall too.

Katu would simply get up, wipe the water from his face and say,

"The rain pushed me because I was dancing too well!"

That would make everyone laugh even more.

Even the elders of the village enjoyed watching him.

They would sit beneath the big tree, looking at Katu with wide smiles.

One elder would shake his head and say,

"That boy and this rain, they are the same thing. Once they start, nobody can stop them."

Another elder would laugh and say,

"Leave him. Maybe the rain came because it wanted to watch Katu dance."

The children loved that idea.

Soon, one child would run into the rain.

Then another.

Then another.

Before long, Katu was no longer dancing alone.

The whole playground would be full of children jumping, spinning and splashing water everywhere.

Katu would stand in the middle of them like a little dancing king.

He would lift his hands and shout,

"Come on! Don't let the rain dance alone!"

And the children would shout back,

"Katu! Katu! Katu!"

For Katu, rain was not just water falling from the sky.

Rain was happiness.

Rain was music.

Rain was the best friend he could ask for.

Sometimes, when the rain became heavy, Katu would stop dancing for a moment and look up.

He would close his eyes and let the drops fall on his face.

Then he would smile and say,

"Thank you, rain. You always know when I need you."

The elders would look at him quietly.

They could not understand how a small boy could love the rain so much.

But Katu understood.

Whenever the rain came, his heart felt light.

And whenever the rain went away, Katu already missed it.

That was how Katu became known throughout the village.

Not because he was the strongest.

Not because he was the richest.

But because whenever the sky opened and rain touched the earth, Katu would be there dancing like nobody was watching.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🥁 Chapter Two – When Katu Called the Rain

As the days passed, everybody in the village already knew what happened whenever rain came.

Katu would disappear from his mother's sight.

One moment, he would be helping to carry firewood.

The next moment, nobody would see him again.

"Where is Katu?" his mother would ask.

And somebody would simply point towards the big tree.

There was Katu.

Waiting for the rain.

Sometimes the clouds would gather but the rain would refuse to fall.

That was when Katu became very serious.

He would look at the sky and say,

"Rain, what are you waiting for?"

The children would laugh.

"Maybe the rain is sleeping!"

Another child would say,

"Maybe the rain went to another village."

Katu would shake his head.

"No. It knows I am waiting for it."

Then he would begin his own little rain dance.

He would clap his hands.

Tap his feet.

Turn around.

Jump twice.

Then he would look at the sky again.

"Come on, Rain! I am ready!"

The children would copy him.

Soon the whole playground would be full of clapping and dancing.

The elders sitting under the tree would watch them and laugh.

"These children will soon dance the rain out of the sky," one elder would say.

Sometimes Katu's dance would become so funny that even the oldest men in the village would laugh until their stomachs hurt.

But there were days when the rain did not come at all.

Those were the days Katu did not like.

He would sit under the big tree with his chin resting on his knees.

The other children would come and sit beside him.

"Katu, why are you quiet today?"

He would point at the dry ground.

"Look at it. Even the ground is waiting."

The children would look at the ground and nod.

Katu would sigh.

"I don't like when the sky forgets us."

Then one of his friends would try to cheer him up.

"Don't worry. When the rain comes, we will all dance."

Katu would smile a little.

"Promise?"

"Promise!"

And that was enough to make him feel better.

But the elders noticed something about Katu.

His love for the rain was not just about playing.

Whenever rain came, Katu became happier.

He would talk to it.

He would dance for it.

He would even thank it.

One evening, an old elder called Katu closer.

"Why do you love the rain so much, my boy?"

Katu looked at him for a moment.

Then he smiled.

"Because when the rain comes, everybody becomes happy."

The elder smiled.

Katu continued,

"The children come out. The trees look fresh. The ground smells nice. Even you elders smile more."

The old man laughed.

"So you have been watching us?"

Katu nodded.

"Yes. I know all of you smile when I dance."

The elders laughed again.

From that day, whenever the clouds appeared in the sky, everyone began looking for Katu.

They knew that once the first drop touched the ground, the village playground would soon become a dancing ground.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌿 Chapter Three – The Long Dry Season

Then one year, the dry season came and stayed longer than anyone expected.

The first few weeks did not worry anybody.

The sun was hot, the roads were dusty, and the children still played under the big tree.

But Katu kept looking at the sky.

Every morning, he hoped to see dark clouds.

Every afternoon, he waited for the first drop.

But nothing happened.

The sky stayed bright.

The ground became dry.

The leaves on some trees began to lose their fresh green colour.

Even the village stream became smaller.

Katu did not like it at all.

He would sit under the big tree and stare at the sky.

One morning, a friend came to him.

"Katu, come and play."

Katu shook his head.

"Play what?"

"The running game."

"No."

"The rope game?"

"No."

"Then what do you want?"

Katu pointed at the sky.

"I want rain."

His friend laughed.

"Can you not play without rain?"

Katu looked offended.

"I can play. But it is not the same."

The children laughed.

They knew Katu was serious.

Every day, he became more impatient.

He would walk around the village looking at the clouds.

Sometimes he would see one small cloud and start shouting.

"Rain is coming!"

The children would run outside.

Everyone would look up.

Then the cloud would slowly disappear.

The children would turn and look at Katu.

Katu would scratch his head.

"Maybe it changed its mind."

Everyone would burst into laughter.

Even Katu would laugh.

But deep inside, he was sad.

He missed the sound of rain hitting the leaves.

He missed jumping in the wet ground.

He missed the smell of fresh earth.

Most of all, he missed dancing.

One afternoon, Katu went to the elders.

They were sitting under a tree and talking.

Katu stood in front of them.

"Can I ask you something?"

One of the elders smiled.

"Ask."

"Why is the rain angry with us?"

The elders looked at one another.

Then they laughed.

"The rain is not angry with you."

"Then why is it hiding?"

One elder pointed toward the sky.

"Rain comes when the time is right."

Katu folded his arms.

"But I have been waiting."

"We know."

"I have even prepared my dance."

The elders laughed again.

"Keep your dance ready."

Katu looked at them.

"For how long?"

"Until the rain comes."

Katu sighed.

"That may take forever."

The elders laughed so loudly that Katu started laughing too.

That evening, something strange happened.

Katu was walking home when he heard a soft sound.

Tap.

He stopped.

Tap.

Another drop landed on his arm.

Katu looked up.

A small dark cloud had appeared above the village.

His eyes became wide.

Another drop touched his face.

Then another.

Katu shouted at the top of his voice.

"RAIN!"

He dropped everything he was carrying and ran toward the playground.

"Rain is here!"

The children heard him.

They came running from their homes.

The elders came outside too.

The rain became heavier.

Katu lifted both hands.

He started dancing.

The children joined him.

Some jumped.

Some clapped.

Some simply stood in the rain and laughed.

Katu spun around and shouted,

"I knew you would come!"

The children laughed.

One elder watched him from under the tree.

"Look at that boy."

Another elder smiled.

"His friend has finally returned."

Katu danced harder.

He jumped into a small pool of water and splashed everyone nearby.

"Hey!"

"You did that on purpose!"

Katu laughed.

"The rain made me do it!"

Everyone chased him.

The playground became full of laughter once again.

After the long dry season, the rain had finally returned.

And Katu danced as if he had been waiting for that moment his whole life.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🌧️ Chapter Four – When Everyone Joined Katu

After the long dry season, the rain began to visit the village more often.

And whenever the rain came, everyone already knew where Katu would be.

Under the big tree.

Dancing.

The funny thing was that Katu was no longer dancing alone.

The children had started waiting for him.

The moment they heard the rain touching the leaves, they would run outside.

"Katu is dancing!"

"Let us go!"

Soon, children from different parts of the village would gather around him.

Some came carrying small drums.

Some came clapping their hands.

Some did not even know how to dance, but that never stopped them.

They would simply jump around and laugh.

Katu would look at them and smile.

"Come closer! The rain is for everybody!"

The children would move closer.

Then Katu would begin his special dance.

He would raise one hand.

Stamp his right foot.

Turn around.

Jump.

Then splash the water with both feet.

The children tried to copy him.

But most of them could not follow his steps.

One child would turn the wrong way.

Another would jump too early.

Another would fall into the mud.

Then everybody would laugh.

Even Katu would stop dancing just to laugh at them.

"That is not my dance!"

The children would shout,

"Then teach us!"

So Katu would teach them again.

The elders also started enjoying the show.

They would sit under the tree and watch the children.

Some would shake their heads.

Some would clap slowly.

Some would laugh when the children fell.

One old man named Elder Oko would always say,

"I have seen many dancers in my life, but I have never seen anyone who dances with rain like Katu."

Another elder would answer,

"Maybe the rain taught him."

Katu would hear them and shout,

"Yes! The rain is my teacher!"

Everyone would laugh.

One afternoon, the rain came harder than usual.

The sky became dark.

The wind moved through the trees.

The rain fell heavily on the village.

The children ran toward the playground.

But this time, Katu did not start dancing immediately.

He stood quietly and looked at the rain.

His friends noticed.

"Katu, what happened?"

He smiled.

"Listen."

Everyone became quiet.

They listened to the sound of rain falling on the leaves.

They heard water hitting the ground.

They heard the wind moving through the branches.

Katu smiled.

"That is music."

The children listened again.

One of them nodded.

"It sounds like a drum."

Another said,

"It sounds like many drums."

Katu laughed.

"Then let us dance to it."

The children started dancing.

This time, nobody tried to copy Katu perfectly.

Everyone danced in their own way.

Some moved slowly.

Some jumped.

Some spun around.

The older children danced with the younger ones.

Even some women came outside to watch.

The village became alive with laughter.

Katu looked around and became very happy.

"This is why I love the rain."

One of the children asked,

"Why?"

Katu pointed around him.

"Because it brings everybody together."

The child smiled.

Katu continued dancing.

He did not care about getting wet.

He did not care about getting muddy.

He only cared about enjoying the moment.

The elders watched him carefully.

They began to understand that Katu's love for the rain had become something bigger than dancing.

He had taught the village to enjoy something they often took for granted.

The rain brought water.

It helped the crops.

It washed the dusty roads.

It filled the streams.

And it brought smiles to the faces of the children.

One elder stood up and clapped.

"Katu!"

Katu stopped.

"Yes, Elder?"

"Continue dancing."

Katu laughed.

"Until when?"

"Until the rain stops."

The children cheered.

Katu raised his hands.

"Then nobody is going home today!"

The elders burst into laughter.

"Do not listen to him!" one elder shouted.

"Your mothers are waiting for you!"

The children laughed even harder.

They knew the elders were right.

But before they went home, they danced one more time.

And that became one of the happiest rainy days the village had ever seen.

From that day, whenever rain came, the children no longer waited for someone to tell them what to do.

They simply ran to the big tree.

They danced.

They laughed.

They played.

And somewhere in the middle of them, Katu would always be smiling.

🌙 End of Chapter Four
