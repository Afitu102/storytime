🕷 Web of the Hollow

📚 Classic Authors • 🕒 35 min read

🕷 Chapter One – The Thing Above the Trees

The forest had no birds that night.

No crickets.

No sound from the leaves.

Only the slow sound of footsteps.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Mara Vey walked along the old forest road.

She held a small iron lamp in one hand.

The flame inside it was weak.

Each time the wind passed, the flame bent low.

Mara stopped and looked behind her.

Nothing.

The road was empty.

Tall trees stood on both sides.

Their branches joined above her head.

They looked like long black fingers.

Mara pulled her dark cloak closer.

She had been walking for almost an hour.

The old map said the village was close.

Very close.

But the village had not appeared.

Not even a single light.

Not one house.

Mara reached into her coat and touched the small silver watch hanging from a chain.

11:47.

She frowned.

The watch had stopped moving three hours ago.

Yet the hands had suddenly started again when she entered the forest.

That was the first thing that frightened her.

The second was the sound.

A soft scratching sound came from somewhere above.

Mara slowly raised the lamp.

The light touched the branches.

Nothing.

She lowered it.

Scratch.

She looked up again.

Still nothing.

Then something fell from the tree.

It landed beside her.

Mara jumped back.

A dead crow lay on the road.

Its wings were spread.

There was no blood.

No wound.

Its eyes were open.

Mara stepped closer.

A thin white thread was wrapped around one of its legs.

She knew that thread.

Her grandfather had told her about it when she was a child.

Spider silk.

But not normal spider silk.

He had called it black silk.

He had also told her one rule.

"Never follow the silk."

Mara remembered laughing at him.

She was twelve then.

Her grandfather had looked at her with a serious face.

"If you ever see a strand tied to a tree, leave the forest."

She had asked why.

He had not answered.

He had only said one more thing.

"Some spiders build webs."

"Some spiders build doors."

Mara stared at the white strand beside the dead crow.

Her grandfather had been dead for eleven years.

Yet his warning suddenly felt very close.

She turned around.

The way back was gone.

Mara froze.

The road behind her had changed.

There was no road.

Only trees.

Thick trees.

Black trees.

She raised the lamp higher.

The forest looked endless.

Mara took one slow step backward.

A branch cracked behind her.

She spun around.

Something moved between the trees.

A tall shape.

It stood very still.

Mara lifted the lamp.

The light reached the shape.

A person stood there.

A long black cloak covered the body.

The hood hid the face.

Mara could not see the hands.

She held the lamp tighter.

"Hello?"

The figure did not move.

Mara took a step forward.

The figure took one step backward.

Mara stopped.

The figure stopped too.

She felt a cold drop slide down her neck.

She looked at the ground.

White silk covered the roots of the trees.

It stretched from one trunk to another.

Then she noticed something worse.

The silk was moving.

Not from the wind.

It was pulling itself toward the figure.

Mara raised her eyes.

The cloaked figure slowly lifted one arm.

It pointed above her.

Mara looked up.

Her breath stopped.

Something huge was hanging between the trees.

At first she thought it was a mass of branches.

Then it moved.

Eight long legs slowly unfolded from the darkness.

The creature was bigger than a horse.

Its body was covered in thick black hair.

Its legs stretched from tree to tree.

The huge spider lowered its body.

Its face appeared above Mara.

Two black eyes stared down.

Then two more opened.

Then two more.

Mara stumbled backward.

The lamp fell from her hand.

The flame went out.

Darkness swallowed the road.

The spider moved.

One leg struck the ground beside Mara.

She screamed and ran.

Branches hit her face.

Roots caught her boots.

She did not stop.

Behind her came the sound of many legs moving over leaves.

Fast.

Too fast.

Mara looked back.

The spider was coming.

It moved between the trees like it owned the forest.

Its legs struck the ground.

The earth shook with every step.

Mara ran harder.

She saw a fallen tree ahead.

She jumped over it.

Her boot slipped.

She fell hard.

The silver watch flew from her hand.

It landed in the leaves.

Mara reached for it.

The spider stopped.

Silence returned.

Mara slowly looked up.

The creature was standing only a few steps away.

Its front legs were raised.

It did not attack.

It only watched her.

Mara grabbed the watch.

The hands had started moving.

11:48.

One minute had passed.

The spider lowered its head.

A long strand of silk dropped from its mouth.

It touched the ground.

Mara stared at it.

The silk slowly formed letters in the dirt.

"YOU CAME BACK."

Her heart began beating faster.

She had never been here before.

At least she thought she had not.

The spider moved closer.

Mara closed her eyes.

She waited for the pain.

Nothing happened.

Instead, she heard a voice.

Not from the spider.

From behind her.

"Mara."

Her eyes opened.

The cloaked figure stood there.

Closer than before.

Mara turned toward it.

"Who are you?"

The figure slowly raised one hand.

A pale finger appeared beneath the sleeve.

It pointed toward the spider.

The giant creature lowered itself.

Its huge head came close to the ground.

Mara could see her own face reflected in its dark eyes.

Then the spider moved aside.

Behind it was a narrow opening between the trees.

Mara had not seen it before.

A path.

The cloaked figure walked toward the path.

It stopped.

Then it looked back at Mara.

Mara knew what it wanted.

Follow.

Every part of her told her not to.

But something else pulled her forward.

A memory.

Her grandfather standing beside a window.

His hand resting on the same silver watch.

"If the spider ever lets you pass..."

"Do not look behind you."

Mara swallowed.

She stepped onto the path.

The spider moved behind her.

She heard its legs touch the leaves.

One.

Two.

Three.

The cloaked figure walked ahead.

Mara kept her eyes forward.

The forest became darker.

The path became smaller.

Then she heard a familiar voice behind her.

"Mara, don't leave me."

She stopped.

It was her grandfather's voice.

The same voice she remembered from childhood.

Her body wanted to turn.

She almost did.

Then the cloaked figure stopped walking.

It raised one hand.

The forest became silent.

Mara understood.

The voice behind her was not her grandfather.

She kept walking.

The voice changed.

"Mara."

Now it sounded like her mother.

Then her brother.

Then her own voice.

Each one begged her to turn.

She refused.

At last, the path opened into a wide clearing.

The cloaked figure stood in the center.

A huge web covered the trees behind it.

Something was trapped inside the web.

Mara stepped closer.

The shape was small.

Human.

She raised her lamp again.

There was no flame.

Yet the lamp began to glow.

The light touched the web.

Mara saw a face.

Her own face.

The person trapped inside the web looked exactly like her.

The figure reached through the silk.

Its mouth opened.

"You finally found me."

Mara could not move.

The cloaked figure turned toward her.

For the first time, it removed its hood.

Mara stared at the face beneath it.

It was her grandfather.

But he had been dead for eleven years.

He smiled.

"Now you understand why I told you never to follow the silk."

Behind him, the giant spider slowly opened its mouth.

A new strand of silk fell toward Mara.

And written across it were three words.

"YOU ARE NEXT."

🌙 End of Chapter One

🕸 Chapter Two – The Room Behind the Web

Mara did not move.

The words were still hanging in the silk.

"YOU ARE NEXT."

The giant spider watched her from the trees.

Its many eyes did not blink.

Mara looked at the man standing in front of her.

Her grandfather.

The same grey coat.

The same crooked walking stick.

Even the small cut above his left eyebrow was there.

Mara took one step backward.

"You're dead."

The old man smiled.

"I know."

That answer frightened her more than anything else.

She turned to run.

The path behind her was covered in silk.

A wall of white strands had appeared between the trees.

Mara touched it.

The silk tightened around her fingers.

She pulled away.

The old man shook his head.

"Don't fight the web."

Mara stared at him.

"Then tell it to let me go."

He looked toward the giant spider.

The creature lowered its head.

A deep clicking sound came from its mouth.

The web behind Mara loosened.

She stepped away from it.

The old man walked toward the huge web covering the trees.

Mara followed without meaning to.

Something inside the web moved.

The shape that looked like Mara was still there.

It pressed both hands against the silk.

Its face was pale.

Its eyes were closed.

Mara stood beside her grandfather.

"Who is that?"

He did not answer at once.

Instead, he raised his walking stick and touched the web.

The strands moved apart.

A narrow opening appeared.

Cold air came through it.

Mara heard water.

Dripping water.

Deep underground.

The old man pointed inside.

"Go."

Mara shook her head.

"Not until you tell me what this is."

He looked at her.

"That is the part of you that never left."

Mara frowned.

"Never left where?"

The old man stepped aside.

"The hollow."

A cold wind passed through the clearing.

The spider moved above them.

Its legs scraped against the branches.

Mara looked at the dark opening.

She had come to the forest searching for the truth about her grandfather.

Now the forest seemed to know more about her than she did.

She entered.

The web closed behind her.

The light from outside vanished.

Mara held the iron lamp close.

The flame returned.

It burned blue.

She followed the sound of water.

The tunnel became narrower.

Roots pushed through the ceiling.

Some were as thick as her arms.

Others moved when she passed.

Mara kept walking.

Then she noticed small marks on the wall.

Names.

Hundreds of them.

Some were old.

Some were fresh.

She ran her fingers across one.

A name appeared beneath it.

Mara Vey.

She stepped back.

The name was carved deep into the stone.

Beside it was a date.

The date was tomorrow.

Mara's hand began to shake.

She moved farther down the tunnel.

Another name appeared.

Then another.

Every name had a date.

Some dates were years old.

Some were from decades ago.

At the end of the wall was one name she knew.

Her grandfather.

His date was eleven years earlier.

Mara touched the carving.

The stone was warm.

A whisper came from behind her.

"You should not have touched that."

Mara turned.

A young woman stood in the tunnel.

She wore a long green dress.

Her hair was wet.

Water dripped from the ends.

Mara raised the lamp.

"Who are you?"

The woman looked at the wall.

Then she looked at Mara.

"My name is Selene."

Mara waited.

"And you?"

"Mara."

Selene gave a small smile.

"I know."

Mara tightened her grip on the lamp.

"How?"

Selene pointed at the wall.

"Your name has been here for a long time."

Mara looked at the carving again.

"Who put it there?"

Selene walked closer.

"You did."

Mara shook her head.

"I have never been here."

Selene's smile disappeared.

"That is what they made you believe."

A sound came from the tunnel behind Selene.

Three slow knocks.

Selene looked back.

Her face changed.

"We have to move."

She grabbed Mara's arm.

They ran.

Mara looked back once.

The tunnel was empty.

Then something crawled across the ceiling.

A small spider.

Then another.

Then dozens.

Selene pulled Mara through a stone doorway.

The door slammed shut.

The spiders hit the other side.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Selene pressed both hands against the door.

The noise stopped.

Mara looked around.

They were standing inside a round room.

There was a table in the center.

A silver bowl sat on it.

Inside the bowl was dark water.

Selene walked toward it.

"Look."

Mara leaned over the bowl.

The water began moving.

A picture appeared.

The forest.

The old road.

The giant spider.

Then the image changed.

Mara saw herself as a child.

She was standing beside her grandfather.

They were in the same forest.

Mara watched the memory.

Her younger self was holding the silver watch.

Her grandfather was kneeling in front of her.

"Listen carefully."

The little Mara nodded.

"If you ever forget this place, the watch will bring you back."

The memory faded.

Mara stared at the bowl.

"I was here."

Selene nodded.

"Yes."

Mara touched the watch.

Its hands began spinning.

Fast.

Faster.

Then they stopped.

12:00.

The room shook.

The stone door behind them cracked.

Selene stepped away from it.

"It's awake."

Mara looked at her.

"What is?"

A long black leg pushed through the crack.

Then another.

The door began breaking apart.

Selene grabbed the silver bowl.

"Run."

Mara ran toward the opposite doorway.

Behind them came a deep clicking sound.

The giant spider was entering the room.

Mara reached the doorway.

She stopped.

Someone was standing on the other side.

Her grandfather.

But this time he was not smiling.

He looked terrified.

"Mara, don't trust Selene."

Selene froze behind her.

The old man's eyes moved toward the silver bowl.

"She is the one who opened the web."

Mara looked at Selene.

Selene said nothing.

The spider clicked behind them.

Mara looked back.

Selene was gone.

Only the silver bowl remained on the floor.

And inside the dark water was a new message.

"DO NOT TRUST HIM EITHER."

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌲 Chapter Three – The House Under the Roots

Mara stared at the message in the silver bowl.

"DO NOT TRUST HIM EITHER."

She looked toward her grandfather.

He was still standing in the doorway.

His face had lost its calm look.

For the first time, Mara saw fear in his eyes.

"Where is Selene?"

He looked behind her.

"She was never supposed to come this far."

Mara stepped away from him.

"That is not an answer."

The old man lowered his walking stick.

"You want answers?"

Mara nodded.

"Then stop looking at me like I am your grandfather."

Mara went still.

The room seemed to shrink around her.

"What does that mean?"

The old man looked at the silver bowl.

"It means the real man died eleven years ago."

Mara felt the floor move beneath her.

She held the table.

"Then who are you?"

The old man did not answer.

Something struck the stone door behind Mara.

Once.

Twice.

The third strike broke the door.

A huge black leg pushed through.

Mara ran.

The old man grabbed her hand.

"This way."

They rushed through the doorway.

The tunnel opened into a wide chamber.

Thick roots covered the ceiling.

Some roots moved slowly.

Mara stopped.

"Roots don't move."

The old man looked up.

"These do."

A root slipped across the wall.

Then another.

They moved like fingers searching in the dark.

Mara followed the old man deeper into the chamber.

They reached a wooden staircase.

It went down.

Mara looked at him.

"How deep does this place go?"

"Far enough."

She followed.

The steps were wet.

Water ran between them.

Mara kept one hand on the wall.

The stone was warm.

Too warm.

Then she heard something.

A child's voice.

"Mara."

She stopped.

The old man kept walking.

Mara turned toward the darkness.

"Who is there?"

No answer.

Then the voice came again.

"Mara, I found your room."

Mara's chest tightened.

She knew that voice.

It was her own voice from childhood.

The old man returned.

"Don't answer it."

Mara looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because it will learn your voice."

They continued down.

The voice followed them.

"Mara."

"Mara."

"Mara."

Each call became closer.

Mara reached the bottom.

A small wooden door stood there.

It had no handle.

The old man placed his palm against it.

The door opened.

Mara stepped inside.

Her breath caught.

It was a bedroom.

A small bed stood near the window.

A wooden horse sat on the floor.

A blue blanket rested on the bed.

Mara knew the room.

It was her childhood bedroom.

The one from the old house.

The one she had not seen since she was twelve.

She walked toward the bed.

A small drawing was pinned above it.

A little girl had drawn a giant spider.

Beside it was a tiny house.

Under the drawing were three words.

"MY SECRET HOME."

Mara touched the paper.

The room changed.

She was twelve again.

Her grandfather was sitting beside her.

He held the silver watch.

"You must promise me something."

Young Mara looked at him.

"What?"

"Never tell your mother about this room."

Young Mara frowned.

"Why?"

Her grandfather looked toward the window.

"Because she already knows."

The memory stopped.

Mara stepped backward.

The room returned to normal.

She looked at the old man.

"My mother knew?"

He nodded.

"She knew everything."

Mara looked around the room.

A small wooden chest sat beneath the bed.

She pulled it out.

The lid opened by itself.

Inside were photographs.

Letters.

A red ribbon.

And another silver watch.

Mara picked it up.

Both watches began ticking.

The sound filled the room.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

Then the wall behind the bed opened.

A dark passage appeared.

The old man stepped back.

"I cannot go in there."

Mara looked at him.

"Why not?"

He pointed at the second watch.

"Because that room belongs to you."

Mara held the watch tightly.

She entered the passage.

The door closed behind her.

She was alone.

The passage was narrow.

Spider silk covered the walls.

But there was no spider.

At the end was a round room.

A single chair stood in the middle.

Someone was sitting in it.

Mara raised the watch like a weapon.

The person slowly turned.

It was Selene.

Her wet hair covered half her face.

She looked tired.

"You found me."

Mara stared at her.

"You disappeared."

Selene stood.

"I was hiding."

"From the spider?"

Selene shook her head.

"From you."

Mara stepped back.

"Why?"

Selene pointed toward the wall.

A large web covered it.

Inside the web was a photograph.

Mara's photograph.

But it was not from childhood.

It was recent.

It showed Mara standing outside the forest.

The picture had been taken only two days earlier.

Mara touched it.

"Who took this?"

Selene looked toward the ceiling.

"She did."

A slow clicking sound came from above.

Mara looked up.

The ceiling was covered with hundreds of small eyes.

Not one spider.

Many.

They were watching her.

Selene grabbed Mara's wrist.

"Don't look at them."

Mara lowered her eyes.

The room went quiet.

Then something whispered from the wall.

"Welcome home, Mara."

The second watch in Mara's hand stopped ticking.

The first watch answered from somewhere far behind the door.

One loud tick.

Then another.

The wall began to split.

A long black finger pushed through.

Selene pulled Mara backward.

The finger curled around the edge of the wall.

Then a voice came through.

It sounded exactly like Mara.

"Let me out."

Mara stared at the opening.

Something on the other side laughed softly.

"I have been waiting for you for twelve years."
