🕷 Web of the Hollow

📚 Classic Authors • 🕒 35 min read

🕷 Chapter One – The Thing Above the Trees

The forest had no birds that night.

No crickets.

No sound from the leaves.

Only the slow sound of footsteps.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Mara Vey walked along the old forest road.

She held a small iron lamp in one hand.

The flame inside it was weak.

Each time the wind passed, the flame bent low.

Mara stopped and looked behind her.

Nothing.

The road was empty.

Tall trees stood on both sides.

Their branches joined above her head.

They looked like long black fingers.

Mara pulled her dark cloak closer.

She had been walking for almost an hour.

The old map said the village was close.

Very close.

But the village had not appeared.

Not even a single light.

Not one house.

Mara reached into her coat and touched the small silver watch hanging from a chain.

11:47.

She frowned.

The watch had stopped moving three hours ago.

Yet the hands had suddenly started again when she entered the forest.

That was the first thing that frightened her.

The second was the sound.

A soft scratching sound came from somewhere above.

Mara slowly raised the lamp.

The light touched the branches.

Nothing.

She lowered it.

Scratch.

She looked up again.

Still nothing.

Then something fell from the tree.

It landed beside her.

Mara jumped back.

A dead crow lay on the road.

Its wings were spread.

There was no blood.

No wound.

Its eyes were open.

Mara stepped closer.

A thin white thread was wrapped around one of its legs.

She knew that thread.

Her grandfather had told her about it when she was a child.

Spider silk.

But not normal spider silk.

He had called it black silk.

He had also told her one rule.

"Never follow the silk."

Mara remembered laughing at him.

She was twelve then.

Her grandfather had looked at her with a serious face.

"If you ever see a strand tied to a tree, leave the forest."

She had asked why.

He had not answered.

He had only said one more thing.

"Some spiders build webs."

"Some spiders build doors."

Mara stared at the white strand beside the dead crow.

Her grandfather had been dead for eleven years.

Yet his warning suddenly felt very close.

She turned around.

The way back was gone.

Mara froze.

The road behind her had changed.

There was no road.

Only trees.

Thick trees.

Black trees.

She raised the lamp higher.

The forest looked endless.

Mara took one slow step backward.

A branch cracked behind her.

She spun around.

Something moved between the trees.

A tall shape.

It stood very still.

Mara lifted the lamp.

The light reached the shape.

A person stood there.

A long black cloak covered the body.

The hood hid the face.

Mara could not see the hands.

She held the lamp tighter.

"Hello?"

The figure did not move.

Mara took a step forward.

The figure took one step backward.

Mara stopped.

The figure stopped too.

She felt a cold drop slide down her neck.

She looked at the ground.

White silk covered the roots of the trees.

It stretched from one trunk to another.

Then she noticed something worse.

The silk was moving.

Not from the wind.

It was pulling itself toward the figure.

Mara raised her eyes.

The cloaked figure slowly lifted one arm.

It pointed above her.

Mara looked up.

Her breath stopped.

Something huge was hanging between the trees.

At first she thought it was a mass of branches.

Then it moved.

Eight long legs slowly unfolded from the darkness.

The creature was bigger than a horse.

Its body was covered in thick black hair.

Its legs stretched from tree to tree.

The huge spider lowered its body.

Its face appeared above Mara.

Two black eyes stared down.

Then two more opened.

Then two more.

Mara stumbled backward.

The lamp fell from her hand.

The flame went out.

Darkness swallowed the road.

The spider moved.

One leg struck the ground beside Mara.

She screamed and ran.

Branches hit her face.

Roots caught her boots.

She did not stop.

Behind her came the sound of many legs moving over leaves.

Fast.

Too fast.

Mara looked back.

The spider was coming.

It moved between the trees like it owned the forest.

Its legs struck the ground.

The earth shook with every step.

Mara ran harder.

She saw a fallen tree ahead.

She jumped over it.

Her boot slipped.

She fell hard.

The silver watch flew from her hand.

It landed in the leaves.

Mara reached for it.

The spider stopped.

Silence returned.

Mara slowly looked up.

The creature was standing only a few steps away.

Its front legs were raised.

It did not attack.

It only watched her.

Mara grabbed the watch.

The hands had started moving.

11:48.

One minute had passed.

The spider lowered its head.

A long strand of silk dropped from its mouth.

It touched the ground.

Mara stared at it.

The silk slowly formed letters in the dirt.

"YOU CAME BACK."

Her heart began beating faster.

She had never been here before.

At least she thought she had not.

The spider moved closer.

Mara closed her eyes.

She waited for the pain.

Nothing happened.

Instead, she heard a voice.

Not from the spider.

From behind her.

"Mara."

Her eyes opened.

The cloaked figure stood there.

Closer than before.

Mara turned toward it.

"Who are you?"

The figure slowly raised one hand.

A pale finger appeared beneath the sleeve.

It pointed toward the spider.

The giant creature lowered itself.

Its huge head came close to the ground.

Mara could see her own face reflected in its dark eyes.

Then the spider moved aside.

Behind it was a narrow opening between the trees.

Mara had not seen it before.

A path.

The cloaked figure walked toward the path.

It stopped.

Then it looked back at Mara.

Mara knew what it wanted.

Follow.

Every part of her told her not to.

But something else pulled her forward.

A memory.

Her grandfather standing beside a window.

His hand resting on the same silver watch.

"If the spider ever lets you pass..."

"Do not look behind you."

Mara swallowed.

She stepped onto the path.

The spider moved behind her.

She heard its legs touch the leaves.

One.

Two.

Three.

The cloaked figure walked ahead.

Mara kept her eyes forward.

The forest became darker.

The path became smaller.

Then she heard a familiar voice behind her.

"Mara, don't leave me."

She stopped.

It was her grandfather's voice.

The same voice she remembered from childhood.

Her body wanted to turn.

She almost did.

Then the cloaked figure stopped walking.

It raised one hand.

The forest became silent.

Mara understood.

The voice behind her was not her grandfather.

She kept walking.

The voice changed.

"Mara."

Now it sounded like her mother.

Then her brother.

Then her own voice.

Each one begged her to turn.

She refused.

At last, the path opened into a wide clearing.

The cloaked figure stood in the center.

A huge web covered the trees behind it.

Something was trapped inside the web.

Mara stepped closer.

The shape was small.

Human.

She raised her lamp again.

There was no flame.

Yet the lamp began to glow.

The light touched the web.

Mara saw a face.

Her own face.

The person trapped inside the web looked exactly like her.

The figure reached through the silk.

Its mouth opened.

"You finally found me."

Mara could not move.

The cloaked figure turned toward her.

For the first time, it removed its hood.

Mara stared at the face beneath it.

It was her grandfather.

But he had been dead for eleven years.

He smiled.

"Now you understand why I told you never to follow the silk."

Behind him, the giant spider slowly opened its mouth.

A new strand of silk fell toward Mara.

And written across it were three words.

"YOU ARE NEXT."

🌙 End of Chapter One

🕸 Chapter Two – The Room Behind the Web

Mara did not move.

The words were still hanging in the silk.

"YOU ARE NEXT."

The giant spider watched her from the trees.

Its many eyes did not blink.

Mara looked at the man standing in front of her.

Her grandfather.

The same grey coat.

The same crooked walking stick.

Even the small cut above his left eyebrow was there.

Mara took one step backward.

"You're dead."

The old man smiled.

"I know."

That answer frightened her more than anything else.

She turned to run.

The path behind her was covered in silk.

A wall of white strands had appeared between the trees.

Mara touched it.

The silk tightened around her fingers.

She pulled away.

The old man shook his head.

"Don't fight the web."

Mara stared at him.

"Then tell it to let me go."

He looked toward the giant spider.

The creature lowered its head.

A deep clicking sound came from its mouth.

The web behind Mara loosened.

She stepped away from it.

The old man walked toward the huge web covering the trees.

Mara followed without meaning to.

Something inside the web moved.

The shape that looked like Mara was still there.

It pressed both hands against the silk.

Its face was pale.

Its eyes were closed.

Mara stood beside her grandfather.

"Who is that?"

He did not answer at once.

Instead, he raised his walking stick and touched the web.

The strands moved apart.

A narrow opening appeared.

Cold air came through it.

Mara heard water.

Dripping water.

Deep underground.

The old man pointed inside.

"Go."

Mara shook her head.

"Not until you tell me what this is."

He looked at her.

"That is the part of you that never left."

Mara frowned.

"Never left where?"

The old man stepped aside.

"The hollow."

A cold wind passed through the clearing.

The spider moved above them.

Its legs scraped against the branches.

Mara looked at the dark opening.

She had come to the forest searching for the truth about her grandfather.

Now the forest seemed to know more about her than she did.

She entered.

The web closed behind her.

The light from outside vanished.

Mara held the iron lamp close.

The flame returned.

It burned blue.

She followed the sound of water.

The tunnel became narrower.

Roots pushed through the ceiling.

Some were as thick as her arms.

Others moved when she passed.

Mara kept walking.

Then she noticed small marks on the wall.

Names.

Hundreds of them.

Some were old.

Some were fresh.

She ran her fingers across one.

A name appeared beneath it.

Mara Vey.

She stepped back.

The name was carved deep into the stone.

Beside it was a date.

The date was tomorrow.

Mara's hand began to shake.

She moved farther down the tunnel.

Another name appeared.

Then another.

Every name had a date.

Some dates were years old.

Some were from decades ago.

At the end of the wall was one name she knew.

Her grandfather.

His date was eleven years earlier.

Mara touched the carving.

The stone was warm.

A whisper came from behind her.

"You should not have touched that."

Mara turned.

A young woman stood in the tunnel.

She wore a long green dress.

Her hair was wet.

Water dripped from the ends.

Mara raised the lamp.

"Who are you?"

The woman looked at the wall.

Then she looked at Mara.

"My name is Selene."

Mara waited.

"And you?"

"Mara."

Selene gave a small smile.

"I know."

Mara tightened her grip on the lamp.

"How?"

Selene pointed at the wall.

"Your name has been here for a long time."

Mara looked at the carving again.

"Who put it there?"

Selene walked closer.

"You did."

Mara shook her head.

"I have never been here."

Selene's smile disappeared.

"That is what they made you believe."

A sound came from the tunnel behind Selene.

Three slow knocks.

Selene looked back.

Her face changed.

"We have to move."

She grabbed Mara's arm.

They ran.

Mara looked back once.

The tunnel was empty.

Then something crawled across the ceiling.

A small spider.

Then another.

Then dozens.

Selene pulled Mara through a stone doorway.

The door slammed shut.

The spiders hit the other side.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Selene pressed both hands against the door.

The noise stopped.

Mara looked around.

They were standing inside a round room.

There was a table in the center.

A silver bowl sat on it.

Inside the bowl was dark water.

Selene walked toward it.

"Look."

Mara leaned over the bowl.

The water began moving.

A picture appeared.

The forest.

The old road.

The giant spider.

Then the image changed.

Mara saw herself as a child.

She was standing beside her grandfather.

They were in the same forest.

Mara watched the memory.

Her younger self was holding the silver watch.

Her grandfather was kneeling in front of her.

"Listen carefully."

The little Mara nodded.

"If you ever forget this place, the watch will bring you back."

The memory faded.

Mara stared at the bowl.

"I was here."

Selene nodded.

"Yes."

Mara touched the watch.

Its hands began spinning.

Fast.

Faster.

Then they stopped.

12:00.

The room shook.

The stone door behind them cracked.

Selene stepped away from it.

"It's awake."

Mara looked at her.

"What is?"

A long black leg pushed through the crack.

Then another.

The door began breaking apart.

Selene grabbed the silver bowl.

"Run."

Mara ran toward the opposite doorway.

Behind them came a deep clicking sound.

The giant spider was entering the room.

Mara reached the doorway.

She stopped.

Someone was standing on the other side.

Her grandfather.

But this time he was not smiling.

He looked terrified.

"Mara, don't trust Selene."

Selene froze behind her.

The old man's eyes moved toward the silver bowl.

"She is the one who opened the web."

Mara looked at Selene.

Selene said nothing.

The spider clicked behind them.

Mara looked back.

Selene was gone.

Only the silver bowl remained on the floor.

And inside the dark water was a new message.

"DO NOT TRUST HIM EITHER."

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌲 Chapter Three – The House Under the Roots

Mara stared at the message in the silver bowl.

"DO NOT TRUST HIM EITHER."

She looked toward her grandfather.

He was still standing in the doorway.

His face had lost its calm look.

For the first time, Mara saw fear in his eyes.

"Where is Selene?"

He looked behind her.

"She was never supposed to come this far."

Mara stepped away from him.

"That is not an answer."

The old man lowered his walking stick.

"You want answers?"

Mara nodded.

"Then stop looking at me like I am your grandfather."

Mara went still.

The room seemed to shrink around her.

"What does that mean?"

The old man looked at the silver bowl.

"It means the real man died eleven years ago."

Mara felt the floor move beneath her.

She held the table.

"Then who are you?"

The old man did not answer.

Something struck the stone door behind Mara.

Once.

Twice.

The third strike broke the door.

A huge black leg pushed through.

Mara ran.

The old man grabbed her hand.

"This way."

They rushed through the doorway.

The tunnel opened into a wide chamber.

Thick roots covered the ceiling.

Some roots moved slowly.

Mara stopped.

"Roots don't move."

The old man looked up.

"These do."

A root slipped across the wall.

Then another.

They moved like fingers searching in the dark.

Mara followed the old man deeper into the chamber.

They reached a wooden staircase.

It went down.

Mara looked at him.

"How deep does this place go?"

"Far enough."

She followed.

The steps were wet.

Water ran between them.

Mara kept one hand on the wall.

The stone was warm.

Too warm.

Then she heard something.

A child's voice.

"Mara."

She stopped.

The old man kept walking.

Mara turned toward the darkness.

"Who is there?"

No answer.

Then the voice came again.

"Mara, I found your room."

Mara's chest tightened.

She knew that voice.

It was her own voice from childhood.

The old man returned.

"Don't answer it."

Mara looked at him.

"Why?"

"Because it will learn your voice."

They continued down.

The voice followed them.

"Mara."

"Mara."

"Mara."

Each call became closer.

Mara reached the bottom.

A small wooden door stood there.

It had no handle.

The old man placed his palm against it.

The door opened.

Mara stepped inside.

Her breath caught.

It was a bedroom.

A small bed stood near the window.

A wooden horse sat on the floor.

A blue blanket rested on the bed.

Mara knew the room.

It was her childhood bedroom.

The one from the old house.

The one she had not seen since she was twelve.

She walked toward the bed.

A small drawing was pinned above it.

A little girl had drawn a giant spider.

Beside it was a tiny house.

Under the drawing were three words.

"MY SECRET HOME."

Mara touched the paper.

The room changed.

She was twelve again.

Her grandfather was sitting beside her.

He held the silver watch.

"You must promise me something."

Young Mara looked at him.

"What?"

"Never tell your mother about this room."

Young Mara frowned.

"Why?"

Her grandfather looked toward the window.

"Because she already knows."

The memory stopped.

Mara stepped backward.

The room returned to normal.

She looked at the old man.

"My mother knew?"

He nodded.

"She knew everything."

Mara looked around the room.

A small wooden chest sat beneath the bed.

She pulled it out.

The lid opened by itself.

Inside were photographs.

Letters.

A red ribbon.

And another silver watch.

Mara picked it up.

Both watches began ticking.

The sound filled the room.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

Then the wall behind the bed opened.

A dark passage appeared.

The old man stepped back.

"I cannot go in there."

Mara looked at him.

"Why not?"

He pointed at the second watch.

"Because that room belongs to you."

Mara held the watch tightly.

She entered the passage.

The door closed behind her.

She was alone.

The passage was narrow.

Spider silk covered the walls.

But there was no spider.

At the end was a round room.

A single chair stood in the middle.

Someone was sitting in it.

Mara raised the watch like a weapon.

The person slowly turned.

It was Selene.

Her wet hair covered half her face.

She looked tired.

"You found me."

Mara stared at her.

"You disappeared."

Selene stood.

"I was hiding."

"From the spider?"

Selene shook her head.

"From you."

Mara stepped back.

"Why?"

Selene pointed toward the wall.

A large web covered it.

Inside the web was a photograph.

Mara's photograph.

But it was not from childhood.

It was recent.

It showed Mara standing outside the forest.

The picture had been taken only two days earlier.

Mara touched it.

"Who took this?"

Selene looked toward the ceiling.

"She did."

A slow clicking sound came from above.

Mara looked up.

The ceiling was covered with hundreds of small eyes.

Not one spider.

Many.

They were watching her.

Selene grabbed Mara's wrist.

"Don't look at them."

Mara lowered her eyes.

The room went quiet.

Then something whispered from the wall.

"Welcome home, Mara."

The second watch in Mara's hand stopped ticking.

The first watch answered from somewhere far behind the door.

One loud tick.

Then another.

The wall began to split.

A long black finger pushed through.

Selene pulled Mara backward.

The finger curled around the edge of the wall.

Then a voice came through.

It sounded exactly like Mara.

"Let me out."

Mara stared at the opening.

Something on the other side laughed softly.

"I have been waiting for you for twelve years."

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🕯 Chapter Four – The Girl Behind the Wall

Mara could not take her eyes away from the opening.

The black finger was still curled around the stone.

It did not move.

It simply waited.

Selene pulled Mara farther into the room.

"Do not answer it."

Mara kept her voice low.

"It sounds like me."

Selene nodded.

"That is why it is dangerous."

The thing behind the wall spoke again.

"Mara."

The voice sounded tired.

Almost human.

Mara looked at the photograph inside the web.

Her face stared back at her.

Two days earlier.

Outside the forest.

She had been wearing the same coat.

The same boots.

The same silver watch.

But there was something strange in the picture.

Someone stood behind her.

A little girl.

Mara moved closer.

The girl was holding a wooden horse.

Mara knew that horse.

It had been in her childhood room.

She turned toward Selene.

"Who is the girl?"

Selene looked at the photograph.

"You."

Mara shook her head.

"No."

"Look at the shoes."

Mara looked again.

The little girl wore red shoes.

Mara remembered them.

She had worn those shoes when she was twelve.

She looked at the date.

The photograph was recent.

Impossible.

Selene took the photograph from the web.

The web closed behind it.

"The forest does not show time the way we do."

Mara touched her silver watch.

"Then what is it showing me?"

Selene looked toward the floor.

"Something you forgot."

A soft sound came from the wall.

Scratch.

Scratch.

Scratch.

The black finger disappeared.

Mara waited.

Nothing came through.

Selene walked to the center of the room.

She knelt beside the chair.

Her fingers found a small crack in the floor.

She pushed a loose stone aside.

A key was hidden underneath.

It was made of bone.

Mara stared at it.

"Where did that come from?"

"It was left for you."

Selene handed it to her.

The moment Mara touched the key, the room changed.

The walls became brighter.

The web disappeared.

The chair vanished.

The stone floor became wooden boards.

Mara heard laughter.

Children running.

A kettle boiling.

She knew the sound.

She knew the smell.

Home.

Her childhood home.

A little girl ran across the room.

Mara watched her.

The girl stopped beside a window.

She was holding the wooden horse.

Another child came into the room.

A boy.

He was older.

Mara recognized him.

Her brother, Corin.

Corin took the wooden horse from the girl's hands.

"Come outside."

The girl shook her head.

"Grandfather said not to."

Corin smiled.

"He says that about everything."

The memory moved.

The children ran through the garden.

They stopped beside the old tree.

A small wooden door was built into the trunk.

Mara watched her younger self open it.

Inside was a narrow tunnel.

Corin looked inside.

"There's something down there."

The girl stepped back.

"Let's go."

Corin shook his head.

"I'm going."

He entered the tunnel.

The girl followed.

Mara felt her chest tighten.

She had forgotten this.

Or someone had made her forget.

The memory moved again.

Corin stood inside a dark room.

The girl was crying.

Something moved behind them.

A giant spider.

Not the huge creature from the forest.

A younger one.

Smaller.

But still much bigger than any normal spider.

It moved toward the children.

Corin grabbed the girl.

"Run!"

They raced toward the tunnel.

The spider followed.

The girl fell.

Corin turned back.

He pulled her up.

The spider reached them.

Then a man appeared.

Their grandfather.

He stood between the children and the creature.

He raised the silver watch.

The spider stopped.

The memory ended.

Mara opened her eyes.

She was back inside the round room.

Selene stood beside her.

"Corin came here with you."

Mara stared at her.

"My brother?"

"Yes."

Mara shook her head.

"Corin disappeared when he was sixteen."

Selene nodded.

"That is what everyone remembers."

"But that is not what happened."

Mara stepped closer.

"Then what happened?"

Selene pointed toward the wall.

The stone was moving.

A doorway slowly appeared.

Beyond it was a staircase.

Selene looked at Mara.

"Your brother never left."

Mara felt cold.

She stepped onto the first stair.

Then the second.

Then the third.

The staircase went down for a long time.

The air became colder.

Mara heard music.

A soft tune played on an old piano.

Selene followed behind her.

At the bottom was a large room.

A piano stood beside a window.

Someone was playing.

Mara knew the tune.

Her mother used to play it.

The player stopped.

A young man slowly turned around.

Mara stopped breathing.

He looked older.

His hair was longer.

But she knew him.

Corin.

Her brother.

"Mara."

She ran toward him.

Corin caught her.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Mara held him tightly.

"You're alive."

Corin looked over her shoulder.

"Not for much longer."

Mara released him.

"What happened to you?"

Corin looked toward Selene.

His expression changed.

"She brought you here."

Selene shook her head.

"No."

Corin stood.

"Then why is she here?"

Selene said nothing.

A loud sound came from above.

The entire house shook.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

Corin grabbed Mara's hand.

"We don't have much time."

Mara looked at him.

"Time for what?"

Corin pointed toward the far wall.

A giant clock was hanging there.

Its hands were moving backward.

12:00.

11:59.

11:58.

Mara looked at the silver watch in her hand.

Its hands were moving forward.

The two clocks moved in opposite directions.

Corin's face became pale.

"When they meet, the forest chooses someone."

Mara looked at him.

"Who?"

Corin slowly pointed at her.

"You."

A huge scratching sound came from behind the wall.

The lights went out.

Then a voice came from the darkness.

It was Mara's voice.

"Corin, move away from her."

Corin froze.

Mara turned toward the sound.

Someone was standing behind him.

A little girl.

Red shoes.

Wooden horse in her arms.

The girl looked at Mara.

"You left me here."

Mara could not speak.

The little girl smiled.

"Now you have to take my place."

🌙 End of Chapter Four

🕯 Chapter Five – The Promise in the Dark

Mara stared at the little girl.

The wooden horse rested against the child's chest.

Her red shoes were covered in dust.

She looked small.

Almost harmless.

But Mara could feel something wrong in the room.

The air had become heavy.

Even the piano had stopped making a sound.

Corin moved in front of Mara.

"Stay behind me."

The little girl smiled.

"You always did that."

Corin went pale.

"Do what?"

"Stand between her and me."

Mara looked at Corin.

He did not answer.

The girl took one small step forward.

The wooden horse made a soft clicking sound.

Mara remembered that sound.

She had heard it beside her bed when she was twelve.

Every night.

Three clicks.

Then silence.

She had always believed it was the old wooden floor.

Now she knew better.

"Who are you?"

Mara asked the question quietly.

The girl tilted her head.

"You know."

Mara shook her head.

"I don't."

The girl looked at Corin.

"Tell her."

Corin closed his eyes.

Mara waited.

"Tell me what?"

Corin finally spoke.

"She is the part of you that stayed behind."

Mara looked at the girl again.

The child smiled.

"Your memories were taken away."

Mara felt a sharp pain in her head.

A picture flashed through her mind.

Her grandfather.

The forest.

The little door in the tree.

Then another picture.

Her mother crying.

Corin holding her hand.

Then a third.

A huge white web.

Mara opened her eyes.

She was on the floor.

Corin was kneeling beside her.

"Are you alright?"

Mara pushed herself up.

"I remembered something."

Corin looked worried.

"What?"

Mara looked at him.

"Mom brought us here."

Corin said nothing.

Mara continued.

"She knew about the forest."

Corin lowered his eyes.

"Yes."

Mara stood.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because you were safer without knowing."

The little girl laughed softly.

"Safer?"

Her voice echoed through the room.

The walls answered.

"Safer."

Then another voice answered.

"Safer."

Then another.

Soon the whole room was filled with whispers.

Mara covered her ears.

Corin grabbed her hand.

"Don't listen."

The little girl lifted the wooden horse.

The whispers stopped.

She walked toward the piano.

She placed the horse on top of it.

A hidden drawer opened beneath the keys.

Inside was a small bundle wrapped in cloth.

The girl took it out.

She carried it toward Mara.

"Your mother left this."

Mara reached for it.

The moment she touched the cloth, the room became warm.

She slowly opened it.

Inside was a letter.

The paper was old.

The writing was faded.

Mara recognized the handwriting.

Her mother's.

She unfolded the letter.

"My dear Mara."

Her eyes moved across the page.

"If you are reading this, then the forest has called you back."

Mara looked at Corin.

He was watching the door.

"Do not be angry with your brother."

Mara continued reading.

"He stayed because someone had to keep the door closed."

She stopped.

"What door?"

Corin looked at the floor.

The little girl answered.

"The first one."

Mara looked at her.

The girl pointed toward the far end of the room.

A wooden door stood there.

It had not been there before.

Mara walked toward it.

The letter continued.

"The thing beneath the forest does not want your life."

"It wants your place."

Mara stopped.

"Your memories keep it asleep."

"Every memory you forget makes it stronger."

Mara looked around the room.

The walls were covered in photographs.

Some showed her as a child.

Some showed Corin.

Some showed their mother.

Then Mara noticed something strange.

Every photograph had a small piece missing.

A face.

A hand.

A doorway.

A name.

Something had been erased from every memory.

Mara touched one photograph.

The missing piece returned.

She saw her mother standing beside the forest door.

A man stood beside her.

Mara's grandfather.

And between them was a tall woman wearing a white dress.

Mara looked at Selene.

Selene was standing at the back of the room.

Mara's heart stopped.

"That's you."

Selene looked at the photograph.

She slowly nodded.

"Yes."

Corin stepped forward.

"You knew our mother."

Selene looked at him.

"I knew her before you were born."

Mara felt the room shift.

"How old are you?"

Selene smiled.

"Older than this house."

The little girl walked toward Selene.

She stopped beside her.

For a moment they looked exactly alike.

Mara noticed it.

The same eyes.

The same small smile.

The same calm face.

Mara stepped backward.

"You're related."

Selene looked at her.

"No."

She looked at the little girl.

"We are the same thing."

The little girl smiled.

Then the wooden door opened.

A cold wind rushed through the room.

The candle flames went out.

The giant spider appeared beyond the doorway.

It did not enter.

It simply watched.

Mara held the letter against her chest.

"What is behind that door?"

Corin answered.

"The place where our family made the promise."

Mara looked at him.

"What promise?"

Corin took a deep breath.

"One child stays."

Mara looked at the little girl.

The girl smiled.

"And one child leaves."

Mara's eyes widened.

She looked at Corin.

He slowly shook his head.

"That was the promise."

The little girl stepped toward Mara.

"You left."

"I stayed."

Mara understood.

Her memories had not simply disappeared.

They had been divided.

One part had gone with her.

The other had remained inside the forest.

The little girl was that forgotten part.

Mara looked at the door.

The spider moved away from it.

The path beyond was clear.

Corin picked up a lantern.

"We have to finish what they started."

Mara folded her mother's letter.

She placed it inside her coat.

Then she walked through the door.

Corin followed.

Selene came behind them.

The little girl stayed in the room.

Mara turned.

"Are you coming?"

The girl shook her head.

"Not yet."

The door began closing.

Mara reached for it.

The girl smiled.

"When you see what is under the house..."

The door almost shut.

"Remember who you promised to protect."

The door slammed.

Mara stood in darkness.

Then a single light appeared ahead.

A small golden light.

It moved closer.

Mara realized it was a lantern.

Someone was carrying it.

A man stepped into view.

He looked exactly like her grandfather.

But this time, he was much younger.

He looked at Mara.

"You came back sooner than I expected."

Mara stared at him.

The young man smiled.

"Now I can finally show you what your family buried."

🌙 End of Chapter Five

🔑 Chapter Six – Beneath the Old House

Mara stood still.

The young man held the lantern between them.

Its golden light touched his face.

Mara knew that face.

It was her grandfather.

But not the grandfather she remembered.

This man looked young.

His hair was dark.

His back was straight.

There were no grey hairs on his head.

No walking stick.

No tired eyes.

Mara took a slow step toward him.

"You were young."

The man smiled.

"I was."

Corin moved beside Mara.

"Don't listen to everything he says."

The young man looked at Corin.

"You still don't trust me."

Corin shook his head.

"I remember what happened."

Mara looked between them.

"Then tell me."

Neither man spoke.

The tunnel around them seemed to wait.

Mara stepped closer.

"No more secrets."

The young man raised the lantern.

"Then follow me."

He turned.

Mara and Corin followed.

The tunnel slowly widened.

The walls changed from stone to old brick.

Mara noticed small lamps hanging from the ceiling.

They were not electric.

Each one burned with a blue flame.

They lit up one after another as the young man passed.

Mara looked behind her.

The lights went dark again.

The darkness followed them.

Step by step.

Mara touched the silver watch in her pocket.

It was warm.

The second watch was cold.

She had kept both.

One from the room.

One from the forest.

The young man stopped at a stone wall.

There was no door.

He placed his hand against the wall.

A deep sound came from inside.

Something unlocked.

The bricks moved.

A hidden room appeared.

Mara entered first.

Her eyes moved around the room.

Shelves covered every wall.

Books filled them.

Old boxes.

Glass bottles.

Small clocks.

Photographs.

Thousands of them.

Mara picked up one photograph.

It showed her mother.

She was standing beside the forest door.

The young grandfather stood beside her.

But someone else was there.

A woman.

Mara looked closer.

She recognized her.

Selene.

The woman looked exactly the same as she did now.

Mara lowered the photograph.

"She hasn't aged."

The young man nodded.

"Neither has the forest."

Mara looked at him.

"Why?"

He walked toward a large wooden table.

A thick book rested on it.

The cover had no title.

He opened it.

The pages were filled with names.

Mara saw her mother's name.

Her grandfather's name.

Corin's name.

Then hers.

The young man placed his finger beneath it.

"Every family that enters this forest leaves something behind."

Mara looked at the page.

"What did my family leave?"

He turned the page.

There was a drawing.

A giant spider.

A house.

Two children.

And a woman standing between them.

Mara touched the drawing.

"Who is the woman?"

The young man looked at Corin.

Corin looked away.

Mara noticed.

"You know."

Corin sighed.

"Yes."

Mara waited.

"That woman was our mother."

Mara looked at the drawing.

Her mother stood beside the giant spider.

But she was not afraid.

She was holding its leg.

Mara's fingers went cold.

"She knew the spider."

Corin nodded.

"She was the one who taught it our names."

Mara stared at him.

"Why would she do that?"

The young grandfather closed the book.

"Because the spider was not the danger."

Mara looked toward him.

"Then what was?"

He pointed toward the floor.

A faint vibration passed through the room.

The bottles on the shelves shook.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

Something was moving underneath them.

Mara stepped back.

"What is down there?"

The young man looked serious.

"The reason your mother made the promise."

A crack appeared across the floor.

Mara moved away.

The crack spread toward the table.

The book fell.

Its pages opened by themselves.

A single sentence appeared.

"THE DOOR MUST HAVE A KEEPER."

Mara looked at Corin.

His face was pale.

"You have been the keeper."

Corin nodded.

"All these years."

Mara moved toward him.

"Then why bring me here?"

Corin looked at the floor.

"Because I can't keep it closed anymore."

The crack became wider.

A warm wind rose from below.

It carried the smell of flowers.

Mara knew that smell.

Her mother's garden.

The young grandfather stepped away from the crack.

"It is waking."

Mara looked at him.

"What does it want?"

He answered softly.

"Someone who remembers."

Mara touched her watch.

The hands started moving.

12:01.

12:02.

12:03.

The second watch started too.

Both clocks were now moving forward.

The room became bright.

The crack opened completely.

A staircase appeared below them.

It led into a huge space.

Mara could see trees down there.

A whole forest beneath the house.

Tiny lights moved between the branches.

Corin grabbed her arm.

"Don't go down."

Mara looked at him.

"I have to."

The young grandfather shook his head.

"Once you enter, the forest will remember you completely."

Mara looked down the stairs.

"Maybe that is what I came for."

She stepped onto the first stair.

The blue lamps went out.

She stepped onto the second.

The house became silent.

She stepped onto the third.

A familiar tune began playing somewhere below.

Her mother's song.

Mara continued.

At the bottom, she found a garden.

Flowers grew under a black sky.

Small white lights floated above the grass.

At the center stood a stone well.

Mara walked toward it.

Something was carved into the stone.

Her name.

Mara Vey.

She placed her hand on it.

The garden changed.

Her mother appeared beside the well.

Mara could see her clearly.

She looked young.

She smiled.

"You finally remembered."

Mara's eyes filled with tears.

"Mom?"

Her mother reached toward her.

But her hand stopped before touching Mara.

"I cannot stay."

Mara stepped closer.

"Then tell me what to do."

Her mother looked toward the well.

"Open it."

Mara turned.

The stone cover moved by itself.

A deep light rose from inside.

Her mother spoke one last sentence.

"Whatever you see below, remember this."

Mara waited.

"The forest never wanted a sacrifice."

Her mother disappeared.

Mara leaned over the well.

There was no water.

Only darkness.

Then two familiar eyes opened below.

A voice rose from the darkness.

"Mara."

She knew the voice.

It was her own.

But older.

Much older.

The voice whispered again.

"I have been waiting beneath you."

Mara stepped back.

The ground began shaking.

The giant spider appeared behind her.

But this time it did not attack.

It bowed its head.

As if it was afraid of what was inside the well.

Mara looked into the darkness one more time.

Something reached upward.

A pale hand appeared over the edge.

Then another.

And slowly, a woman began climbing out.

She had Mara's face.

But her hair was completely white.

She looked at Mara.

"Now you know where your missing years went."

🌙 End of Chapter Six

🌘 Chapter Seven – The Woman in the Well

Mara could not breathe.

The woman stood on the edge of the well.

She had Mara's face.

But the face looked older.

Much older.

Her white hair moved even though there was no wind.

She looked at Mara with tired eyes.

"You finally came."

Mara stepped backward.

The giant spider stayed behind her.

It did not move.

It seemed to be waiting.

"Who are you?"

The woman looked toward the well.

"I am what you forgot."

Mara shook her head.

"That doesn't make sense."

The woman smiled.

"It will."

She touched her own face.

"Every memory you lost came here."

Mara looked down into the well.

The darkness had disappeared.

She could see a long staircase below.

At the bottom were hundreds of small doors.

Each door had a name.

Mara saw hers.

She saw Corin's.

She saw her mother's.

She saw her grandfather's.

Then she saw another name.

Selene.

Mara turned around.

Selene stood among the trees.

Her green dress was dry now.

Her face looked calm.

"You found the memory room."

Mara walked toward her.

"Did you know about this?"

Selene nodded.

"I helped build it."

Mara stopped.

"Why?"

Selene looked at the white-haired woman.

"Because your mother asked me to."

The woman from the well lowered her eyes.

Mara felt a strange calm come over her.

The pieces were beginning to fit.

"My mother knew I would return."

"She hoped you would."

Selene walked toward the well.

She picked up a small stone.

There was a symbol carved into it.

A circle.

With a line through the middle.

Mara recognized it from her mother's old jewelry.

"What does it mean?"

Selene placed the stone beside the well.

"Two sides of the same memory."

Mara looked at the white-haired woman.

The woman walked closer.

She looked into Mara's eyes.

"You left half of yourself here."

Mara whispered.

"The little girl."

The woman nodded.

"She was your fear."

Mara remembered the girl with the red shoes.

She remembered the wooden horse.

She remembered the little door.

She remembered running.

Then another memory came.

Her mother kneeling in front of her.

Her hands resting on Mara's shoulders.

"Fear is not something you throw away."

"It is something you learn to carry."

Mara closed her eyes.

The memory became clearer.

She saw her mother holding the little wooden horse.

She saw Corin standing near the forest door.

She saw her grandfather holding both watches.

Then she saw herself.

A frightened child.

Crying beside the well.

The giant spider stood nearby.

It had not attacked.

It had protected her.

Mara opened her eyes.

"The spider saved me."

Selene nodded.

"Many times."

Mara looked at the creature.

It lowered one enormous leg toward her.

Mara slowly reached out.

Her fingers touched its rough skin.

The spider became still.

A warm feeling passed through Mara's hand.

She saw another memory.

Her mother standing in the garden.

The spider beside her.

Her mother was holding a small bundle.

Inside was the silver watch.

She placed it beneath the old tree.

Then she looked directly toward Mara.

"One day, she will come back."

The memory ended.

Mara pulled her hand away.

The spider stepped back.

Corin came running through the trees.

He looked exhausted.

"Mara!"

She turned.

"Where were you?"

Corin looked toward the well.

"I heard the bells."

Mara frowned.

"What bells?"

A faint sound came from below.

One bell.

Then another.

Then another.

Seven bells rang beneath the earth.

The white-haired woman looked worried.

"It has started."

Mara looked at her.

"What has started?"

The woman pointed toward the sky.

The black clouds were moving.

Not across the sky.

Around something.

A huge shape was rising behind the trees.

Mara could not see its body.

Only two enormous pale eyes.

The spider moved in front of Mara.

Corin stepped beside it.

Selene raised the lantern.

The white-haired woman whispered.

"The keeper is awake."

Mara stared into the darkness.

"I thought Corin was the keeper."

The woman shook her head.

"He kept the door."

She pointed toward Mara.

"You kept the memory."

Mara felt the two watches begin ticking together.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

The sound grew louder.

The giant eyes moved closer.

A voice came from the trees.

"Mara Vey."

She knew the voice.

It was the voice from the well.

But now it sounded enormous.

The trees shook.

The ground opened in front of them.

A long stone bridge rose from below.

It stretched toward the dark shape.

At the end of the bridge stood a small house.

Mara stared at it.

The house looked exactly like her childhood home.

Even the blue curtains were there.

A warm light glowed through the kitchen window.

Mara heard someone humming inside.

Her mother's song.

Corin grabbed her hand.

"Don't go."

Mara looked at him.

"She is inside."

Corin shook his head.

"That is not our mother."

The front door of the house opened.

A woman stepped outside.

She wore the same blue dress Mara remembered.

She looked toward the bridge.

Then she smiled.

"Mara."

Mara's heart began racing.

The woman lifted one hand.

"Come home."

The giant spider suddenly moved.

It blocked the bridge.

Its legs spread wide.

The woman at the house stopped smiling.

Her face changed.

Her eyes became completely black.

Mara stepped backward.

The woman opened her mouth.

Her voice was no longer gentle.

"You should have stayed forgotten."

The house lights went out.

The stone bridge began breaking apart.

Mara grabbed Corin.

Selene pulled the white-haired woman away from the edge.

The spider leaped forward.

Its enormous body vanished into the darkness beyond the bridge.

A terrible sound followed.

Then silence.

Mara looked at the two watches.

Both had stopped.

12:12.

A small door appeared in the stone beside her.

Mara touched it.

The door opened.

Inside was the little girl with the red shoes.

She held the wooden horse.

But this time, she was crying.

"Mara..."

Mara knelt.

"What happened?"

The girl pointed behind her.

A dark figure stood at the end of the small room.

It was holding something.

A silver key.

The girl whispered.

"He found the last door."

Mara stood.

The figure raised the key.

Then the door slammed shut between them.

From behind it came a voice.

"Now you must choose which memory survives."
