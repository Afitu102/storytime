💰 Badamosi and His Ten Wives

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

💰 Chapter One – The Greedy Man With Nine Wives

Badamosi was so greedy that if you gave him a handshake, he would probably look at your hand to check whether you had left some money inside.

If he had ten pieces of meat on his plate, he would stretch his hand towards someone else's plate and quietly take one piece.

And if the owner complained, Badamosi would look even more surprised than the person whose meat he had collected.

“My brother, look at my body now. Do I look like somebody that has eaten enough protein?” he would ask. “I need this protein more than you do.”

That was the kind of man Badamosi was.

He was not only greedy with money. He was greedy with food, other people's possessions, and even women.

If someone came to Badamosi to beg him for financial assistance, the man would listen carefully and nod as though he truly cared.

But after listening to the person's problem, Badamosi would sometimes ask for a small loan from the same person who had come to beg him for money.

That was how greedy Badamosi was.

But there was one thing he wanted even more.

More wives.

Badamosi had married his first wife, Aduni, and for a while, everybody thought the man would finally be satisfied.

But no.

A few months later, he married Busi, his second wife.

Then came Yetundi, Ronke, Fumi, Shadi, Tola, Kemi, and finally Aisha.

Nine wives.

All within five years.

But even with nine wives, Badamosi still did not feel satisfied.

That was his biggest problem.

He always wanted more than he actually needed.

Badamosi owned several farmlands and a poultry business. He clearly had more money than most people in the community.

Still, he was extremely greedy and stingy.

That combination made life particularly difficult for his nine wives.

He expected them to cook, take care of his farms, help with his poultry, and manage the house all at once.

But whenever it was time to give them money, his hands suddenly became tighter than a budget.

His wives worked hard, but Badamosi hated spending money on them.

He believed that because they were his wives, they should simply do whatever he wanted without asking for anything in return.

And while Badamosi had everything he needed, he was still looking for something else.

He wanted another wife.

This time, he wanted a woman from the city.

Her name was Lola.

Lola was a young woman from the city who had come to the community for her NYSC service.

Everyone simply called her Copper, just the way people in the area casually referred to youth service corps members.

Lola had been posted to the community secondary school for her national service.

She carried herself with the confidence of someone who had no intention of allowing village stress disturb her.

But Badamosi did not see Lola as an NYSC member.

He saw her as wife number ten.

Within a few days, he had already convinced himself that marrying a city girl was the greatest investment he had ever considered.

He imagined that Lola would know important people in the city.

He imagined that through her, he would establish himself in the city.

Then his nine wives could remain in the village to supervise his farms and poultry business.

To Badamosi, everything sounded like an excellent business plan.

There was only one problem.

Lola had already heard a lot about him.

She had heard about his nine wives.

She had heard about his greed.

She had even heard stories about how Badamosi could negotiate over the price of one orange for thirty minutes.

So when Badamosi finally approached Lola with his marriage proposal, she was not flattered.

She was amazed by his boldness.

“Did he actually think I would say yes?” Lola thought to herself.

Still, she decided not to turn him down immediately.

Instead, she smiled.

Badamosi saw the smile and became excited.

He had no idea that the smile was the beginning of his biggest mistake.

The next day, Badamosi went to visit Lola.

But Lola refused to give him an audience.

She told him that if he wanted to visit her, he had to pay a visitation fee first.

Badamosi was confused.

“Visitation fee?”

Since when did people start paying visitation fees in the village?

But instead of asking too many questions, Badamosi smiled and told himself that maybe that was how they did things in the city.

He brought out some money from a properly tied nylon he had hidden inside his pocket and paid the said amount.

Lola collected the money without saying much.

Badamosi smiled.

In his mind, everything was going according to plan.

He did not know that Lola had already started playing a game he would soon find very expensive.

And the next time he came to see her, there would be another fee waiting for him.

🌙 End of Chapter One

👩🏽 Chapter Two – Badamosi Meets Lola

The next time Badamosi wanted to see Lola, he went to her residence with a smile on his face.

He had already started imagining his new life in the city.

In his mind, Lola was not just a beautiful young woman. She was an investment.

But Lola was ready for him.

When Badamosi arrived, she reminded him about the visitation fee.

He paid it.

Badamosi smiled and sat down, thinking everything was going well.

Then he invited Lola to come and chill with him at the bar.

That was when she introduced another fee.

“Hanging-out fee,” Lola said calmly.

Badamosi hesitated.

He was no longer finding it funny.

“Hanging-out fee?” he asked.

But then he remembered his plans and his dream of establishing himself in the city.

“This is just an investment,” he reminded himself.

So he paid.

Another time, Badamosi mentioned marriage to Lola.

Instead of becoming excited, Lola told him that there was another fee.

“Consideration fee,” she said.

Badamosi looked at her.

Lola explained that since he wanted her to seriously consider him as a suitor, he needed to provide something that would encourage her to consider him seriously.

Badamosi did not like the idea, but after much hesitation, he paid that one too.

And Lola did not stop there.

Whenever Badamosi wanted something, another fee seemed to appear.

If he wanted to speak to her parents over the phone, there was an airtime fee.

If Lola needed to gather strength before discussing serious marriage matters with him, there was another fee.

Badamosi kept paying.

The funny thing was that Lola had never promised him marriage.

She had never told him that she would become his wife.

But Badamosi kept giving her more money because he wanted to prove himself worthy.

He had to convince himself that he was not wasting his money.

He was simply investing in city life and his soon-to-be tenth wife.

Meanwhile, Lola quietly saved the money.

She did not spend it all.

She had a plan.

While Badamosi was busy calculating what he would gain from marrying a city girl, Lola was paying attention to something else.

She had noticed that the community was struggling with access to clean water.

The village leaders and the men of the locality had agreed to contribute towards completing a borehole project.

Everyone contributed the little they had.

Everyone except Badamosi.

When the village head approached him about the project, Badamosi gave the same excuse he always gave.

His business was suffering.

His farms were not doing well.

His chickens were almost dying.

It was the same excuse every time.

Badamosi even suggested that perhaps the villagers should contribute money to help him.

He did not feel even a little ashamed after saying it.

He simply went back home and continued counting his money.

Meanwhile, Lola's service year was getting closer to an end.

She knew she would soon leave the community.

But she made sure not to tell Badamosi the exact day she would be leaving.

She continued collecting his money quietly.

Badamosi, on the other hand, was already planning the future.

He imagined Lola becoming his tenth wife.

He imagined himself visiting the city through her.

He imagined meeting important people and expanding his business.

He was so busy planning what Lola could do for him that he never stopped to ask what she was doing with all the money he kept giving her.

Then the day of Lola's departure finally came.

She packed her things quietly.

But before leaving the community, she had somewhere important to go.

She picked up the money Badamosi had been giving her all those months and placed it carefully into two separate envelopes.

Then she carried the envelopes straight to the village head's office.

The old man was surprised when he saw what she had brought.

But when Lola explained where the money came from, he was even more stunned.

He could hardly believe that Badamosi had voluntarily let go of such a large amount of money.

Then Lola told him exactly what she wanted the money to be used for.

Part of it would go towards completing the unfinished community borehole project.

The rest would go to the women's group so they could learn different skills and start small businesses for themselves.

She also promised to speak with some NGOs in the city that helped small communities learn different informal skills.

The village head listened carefully.

He was deeply impressed by what Lola had decided to do.

But there was still one more thing she had prepared.

A letter.

And that letter was meant to be read to the women.

By the time Badamosi returned home that evening, he had no idea that the money he thought he was using to secure his tenth wife was already changing the lives of the people around him.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

💸 Chapter Three – The Fees That Never Ended

Badamosi continued to visit Lola, and every visit seemed to come with a new fee.

At first, he thought it was funny.

After all, he believed he was investing in his future.

But the more he wanted from Lola, the more money he had to bring out of his pocket.

If he wanted to visit her, there was a visitation fee.

If he wanted her to come and spend time with him at the bar, there was a hanging-out fee.

If he wanted her to seriously consider his marriage proposal, there was a consideration fee.

If he wanted to speak with her parents over the phone, there was an airtime fee.

And whenever Lola said she needed to gather strength before discussing serious marriage matters with him, another fee appeared.

Badamosi would complain, but he would still pay.

He had already convinced himself that he was not wasting money.

“This is an investment,” he would tell himself.

He believed that once Lola became his tenth wife, everything he had spent would come back to him in one way or another.

He imagined himself travelling to the city and meeting important people through her.

He imagined expanding his businesses.

He imagined his nine wives remaining in the village to supervise his farms and poultry business while his new city wife helped him build a bigger life.

To Badamosi, the plan was perfect.

There was only one person who knew exactly what was happening.

Lola.

Lola had never promised Badamosi marriage.

She had never told him that she would become his wife.

She simply listened to him, smiled when necessary, and collected the money whenever he brought it.

But she did not spend the money carelessly.

She was saving it.

While Badamosi was busy calculating what he would gain from marrying a city girl, Lola had been paying attention to the problems in the community.

One problem stood out to her.

The community had serious difficulty getting clean water.

The village leaders and the men of the locality had already agreed to contribute towards completing a borehole project.

Everyone was contributing whatever little they could afford.

Everyone except Badamosi.

The village head had spoken to him about it more than once.

But Badamosi always had an excuse.

Sometimes his business was suffering.

Other times, he said his farms were not doing well.

Another time, he complained that his chickens were almost dying.

It was always one problem or another.

And on one occasion, Badamosi even suggested that the villagers should contribute money to help him.

He said it without shame.

Afterwards, he returned home and continued counting his money.

Meanwhile, Lola's service year was almost over.

She knew her time in the community was coming to an end.

But she did not tell Badamosi the exact day she would leave.

Then one morning, she quietly packed her belongings.

She picked up the money Badamosi had been giving her all this while.

She divided it into two separate envelopes and went straight to the village head's office.

The old man looked surprised when Lola placed the envelopes in front of him.

When he saw the amount inside, his eyes widened.

Then Lola explained where the money had come from.

The village head was even more surprised.

He could not believe that Badamosi had voluntarily let go of such a large amount of money.

Lola smiled and explained that she wanted the money to be put to good use.

“I've seen the struggles of this community since my stay here,” she said calmly.

She suggested that part of the money should go towards completing the unfinished community borehole project.

The rest, she explained, should go to the women's group.

She wanted the women to learn different skills and start small businesses for themselves.

Lola also promised to speak with some NGOs in the city that helped small communities learn different informal skills for little amounts of money.

The village head listened carefully.

He was deeply impressed by her courage and generosity.

Before leaving, Lola handed him a letter.

She asked him to make sure it was read to the women.

Then she picked up her suitcase and quietly left the village.

At least, the stingy man's money had been put to good use.

When the women heard what had happened, they were even more impressed.

Some of the women who had initially been jealous of Lola were now singing her praises.

They had assumed that the beautiful Copper would come and steal their husband's attention, especially Badamosi's wives.

Instead, she had helped them find a way to stand on their own feet.

Meanwhile, Badamosi had no idea what was happening.

Later that evening, he walked towards Lola's residence, swinging both arms like a child going to collect candy.

He was already thinking about his future tenth wife.

But when he got there, Lola was nowhere to be found.

A neighbour informed him that Lola had left earlier that day because her service in the community was over.

Badamosi chuckled nervously.

“You must be joking, Abi,” he said.

But his smile quickly disappeared.

All he could think about were the visitation fees, consideration fees, and all the other money he had spent.

His beautiful business plan suddenly looked different.

Badamosi quickly ran to the village head's office with both hands on his head.

When the village head confirmed that Lola had left, Badamosi almost lost consciousness.

But he was not prepared for what would happen next.

The money he had spent chasing his tenth wife was about to change the lives of the nine women already living in his house.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🚰 Chapter Four – The Money That Changed the Village

After Lola left the village, life slowly began to change.

The money she had given to the village head was put to good use.

Part of it went towards completing the community borehole project.

The women also began taking their skill acquisition seriously.

For the first time, many of them had hope of earning their own money.

They started learning different skills and preparing themselves to become less dependent on their husbands.

Badamosi noticed that something was different.

His wives were always going somewhere.

At first, he thought they were simply doing rehearsals for a competition or something.

He had no idea that part of the money he had been hoarding was the same money his wives and other women were using to better themselves.

Then things became even worse for Badamosi.

His wives were no longer as willing to work endlessly on his farms without compensation.

Whenever he asked them to help with certain tasks, they began asking an important question.

“How much are you going to pay us?”

Badamosi was shocked.

He had never expected to hear such a question from his wives.

For years, he had depended on them for free labour.

They cooked for him, managed the house, helped on his farms, and assisted with his poultry business.

Now they were beginning to understand their own value.

Badamosi could no longer expect them to work endlessly without compensation.

He had to start hiring workers to work on his farms and take care of his poultry.

To make matters worse, some of his wives had started considering leaving him.

They now had the hope of earning their own money.

They no longer felt completely trapped by financial dependence.

And Lola had promised in her letter to remain in contact with the women.

She also promised to help connect them with opportunities in the city once they were ready.

When Badamosi heard about everything Lola had done with his money, his head ached all over again.

He had spent so much money chasing a woman he believed would become his tenth wife.

Instead, that same money had helped the women around him become more independent.

Badamosi had gathered money for years, but now he was beginning to realize that money alone could not make people stay with you.

He had nine wives, but none of them wanted to remain completely dependent on him.

And the city woman he had spent so much money pursuing was gone.

One night, Badamosi walked into his room and saw his first wife, Aduni, packing her belongings.

He could not believe his eyes.

He rushed towards her.

“Please, my first love, you can't also leave me,” he begged.

“The whole house would be empty.”

For the first time in his life, Badamosi went down on his knees.

He begged and promised to change.

He swore that he would treat his wives better and promised them heaven and earth.

But Aduni did not even look at him.

She continued packing.

Just as she was about to walk through the door, Badamosi stretched out his hands desperately.

“Please don't go!”

Then suddenly, Badamosi woke up.

He sat upright on his bed.

His heart was beating very fast, and his singlet was soaked in sweat.

He looked around.

All his wives were still there.

Nobody was packing a bag.

Nobody was threatening to leave him.

Everything had been a dream.

A very bad dream.

Badamosi was too stunned to speak.

The dream had frightened him more than any real-life problem ever had.

He sat quietly on the bed, thinking about everything he had seen.

His wives leaving him.

His money disappearing.

His businesses suffering.

For the first time, Badamosi began to understand what his greed could cost him.

And that same day, he decided to do something his wives had never seen him do before.

He called a meeting with all of them.

What he was about to say would leave every woman in that house completely shocked.

🌙 End of Chapter Four

😱 Chapter Five – The Dream That Changed Badamosi

🌿 Moral of the Story

Greed can make a person lose sight of the people around them. Wealth means little when it is used only for yourself. Treat people with kindness, share what you have, and never take advantage of those who depend on you.

📖 The End
