🌿 Alala Market Road

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌿 Chapter One – The Woman Who Never Reached the Market

Every market day, something strange happened on Alala Market Road.

A woman named Adesewa would leave her village before the sun became hot.

She always carried a large basin on her head.

Inside the basin were fresh vegetables from her farm.

Green peppers.

Okra.

Garden eggs.

Fresh leaves.

And sometimes baskets of ripe tomatoes.

Adesewa was not the richest woman in the village.

Her house was small.

Her farm was not very large.

She did not have many workers.

But there was one thing everyone knew about her.

Adesewa was always smiling.

Whenever she met an elder on the road, she stopped.

She greeted them properly.

If an old woman was carrying something heavy, Adesewa helped her.

If a child fell on the road, she stopped to help.

If someone had no money to buy a little food, Adesewa sometimes gave them something from her basin.

She never complained about it.

She would simply smile and continue walking.

One morning, Adesewa left home with her daughter, Morenike.

Morenike was nine years old.

She liked walking beside her mother because market days were always interesting.

As they walked down Alala Market Road, they met an old man sitting under a tree.

His name was Pa Durojaiye.

Adesewa stopped immediately.

She lowered her basin.

"Good morning, Baba."

Pa Durojaiye smiled.

"Good morning, my daughter."

Adesewa noticed the old man's empty calabash.

She brought out two garden eggs and some fresh leaves.

"Please take these home."

The old man shook his head.

"You are going to the market. Keep your goods."

Adesewa smiled.

"I will sell the rest."

She placed the food in his hands.

"Please eat well."

Morenike watched her mother.

When they continued walking, she asked:

"Why do you always give things away?"

Adesewa laughed.

"Because we have enough to share."

Morenike looked at the heavy basin.

"But Mama, what if you reach the market and have nothing left to sell?"

Adesewa smiled.

"Then I will come home with an empty basin."

Morenike laughed.

"That will be a very bad market day."

Adesewa shook her head.

"Not if the basin is empty because someone needed what was inside."

They continued down the road.

A little farther away, they met Mama Kudirat.

She was carrying firewood on her back.

Adesewa stopped again.

She helped her put the firewood down.

Then she carried part of it for her.

Mama Kudirat smiled.

"Adesewa, you will be late for the market."

Adesewa laughed.

"The market will still be there."

Morenike smiled.

They walked together for a while.

Then Mama Kudirat noticed the vegetables.

Her eyes became wide.

"How much for the okra?"

Adesewa told her the price.

Mama Kudirat bought everything.

Then she bought the garden eggs.

Then the peppers.

Then the fresh leaves.

Adesewa looked inside her basin.

Almost nothing was left.

Morenike stared.

"Mama..."

Adesewa smiled.

"Yes?"

"We have not reached the market."

Adesewa laughed.

"I know."

Mama Kudirat laughed too.

She paid Adesewa and continued home.

Adesewa lifted the basin again.

Only a few tomatoes were left.

Morenike looked at them.

"Will you sell these too?"

Adesewa nodded.

"If someone wants them."

Before they walked another hundred steps, a woman from the next village appeared.

Her name was Bisola.

She was carrying an empty basket.

"Adesewa!"

Adesewa turned.

Bisola looked inside the basin.

"Is that all you have?"

Adesewa nodded.

Bisola bought the last tomatoes.

Adesewa's basin was now completely empty.

Morenike stared at it.

"Mama, we are going home?"

Adesewa smiled.

"Yes."

Morenike laughed.

"But we never reached Alala Market!"

Adesewa placed the empty basin on her head.

"Maybe the market came to us."

They both laughed.

But something else was happening at Alala Market.

The other women were waiting for Adesewa.

Salewa had already arranged her tomatoes.

Enitan had arranged her peppers.

Morayo had filled three baskets with vegetables.

They had all come early.

They were ready to sell.

Then someone came running down the road.

"Adesewa has sold everything again!"

The women became quiet.

Salewa frowned.

"Again?"

The messenger nodded.

"She did not even reach the market."

Enitan looked at the road.

"How does she do it?"

Morayo shook her head.

"Maybe she knows something we don't know."

Salewa folded her arms.

"Or maybe she has very good luck."

Nobody noticed Adesewa walking home with her empty basin.

She was smiling.

She had sold everything.

But she did not feel proud because she had made more money.

She felt happy because everyone who bought from her had gone home smiling too.

That evening, another surprise waited for her.

Before the next market day, people from two nearby villages began asking about her vegetables.

They wanted to buy from Adesewa before she left home.

And nobody knew that the next market day would be even stranger.

Because this time, Adesewa would leave home with more goods than ever before.

And before she reached Alala Market...

Someone would make her an offer that could change her life.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌧️ Chapter Two – The Morning the Buyers Came

The next market day came before Adesewa expected it.

She woke up while the village was still quiet.

The sky was grey.

Rain clouds covered the hills.

Adesewa stepped outside.

She looked at her farm.

The vegetables were fresh.

There were more than she had ever harvested before.

Morenike came outside.

"Mama, look at all these vegetables."

Adesewa smiled.

"Today we will have plenty to sell."

They filled two large basins.

They filled three smaller baskets.

Adesewa tied her wrapper properly.

Morenike carried the smallest basket.

They started toward Alala Market.

But they had barely reached the first bend when they saw a group of women coming toward them.

They were from a nearby village.

At the front was a woman named Yejide.

She greeted Adesewa.

"Good morning, my sister."

Adesewa smiled.

"Good morning. Where are you all going this early?"

Yejide laughed.

"We came for your vegetables."

Adesewa looked surprised.

"All of you?"

Another woman stepped forward.

"We heard your vegetables are fresh."

Morenike looked at her mother.

She smiled.

Adesewa opened the first basket.

The women gathered around.

They bought the peppers.

Then the okra.

Then the garden eggs.

Then the fresh leaves.

One woman bought tomatoes for her restaurant.

Another bought vegetables for her family.

Someone else bought enough to share with her neighbours.

Adesewa kept counting the money.

Morenike kept looking inside the baskets.

One basket became empty.

Then another.

Then another.

Yejide bought the last basket.

She looked at the empty basin.

"Adesewa, you are not going to the market again?"

Adesewa laughed.

"Not today."

The women laughed.

Morenike shook her head.

"Mama, you have done it again."

Adesewa smiled.

"What did I do?"

"You sold everything before reaching the market."

Adesewa laughed.

"Then let's go home."

They turned back.

But before they reached home, they met an old woman sitting beside the road.

Her name was Iya Abeni.

She looked tired.

Adesewa stopped.

"Good morning, Mama."

The old woman smiled.

"Good morning, my daughter."

Adesewa noticed the old woman's basket.

It was empty.

"Have you eaten today?"

The old woman shook her head.

Adesewa opened her small food bag.

She had brought some roasted corn for herself and Morenike.

She gave it all to Iya Abeni.

Morenike looked at her.

"Mama, what will we eat?"

Adesewa smiled.

"We will find something."

Iya Abeni raised her hands.

"May your hands never be empty."

Adesewa smiled.

"Amen."

They continued home.

That same afternoon, Adesewa was sitting outside her house when a young boy came running.

His name was Femi.

"Aunty Adesewa!"

She looked up.

"What happened?"

Femi was breathing hard.

"My mother wants to buy vegetables."

Adesewa laughed.

"Tell her I have sold everything."

Femi shook his head.

"She says she will wait until tomorrow."

Adesewa nodded.

"Tell her I will keep some for her."

Femi smiled and ran away.

Morenike watched him leave.

"Mama, why does everyone want your vegetables?"

Adesewa shrugged.

"Maybe they like them."

Morenike smiled.

"That is not all."

Adesewa looked at her.

"What do you mean?"

Morenike pointed at the money.

"Other women sell vegetables too."

Adesewa nodded.

Morenike continued.

"But people keep coming to you."

Adesewa became quiet.

She looked toward the road.

"Maybe people come because they know I will treat them well."

Morenike smiled.

"So your secret is being nice?"

Adesewa laughed.

"I don't have a secret."

She touched her daughter's head.

"Just be good to people."

But at Alala Market, not everyone was smiling.

Salewa stood beside her empty stall.

She had brought three baskets of tomatoes.

She had sold only one.

Enitan had sold even less.

Morayo looked down the road.

"How does Adesewa do this?"

Salewa frowned.

"She does not even come to the market anymore."

Enitan folded her arms.

"And yet she sells everything."

A woman passing by heard them.

"Maybe she is lucky."

Salewa looked at her.

"Lucky every market day?"

The woman shrugged.

Salewa became silent.

She had an idea.

A bad one.

She wanted to follow Adesewa the next market day.

She wanted to discover why people kept buying from her.

She wanted to know what Adesewa was doing that the other women were not doing.

She did not know that following Adesewa would teach her something she never expected.

Because the next market morning,

Salewa would see Adesewa do something that would change the way she looked at her forever.
