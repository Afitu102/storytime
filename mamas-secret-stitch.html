🧵 Mama's Secret Stitch

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 25 min read

🧵 Chapter One – The Thread That Would Not Break

Before sunrise, while the village was still quiet, a young woman named Zuri sat alone beside a small wooden window.

She was sewing.

Not for a wedding.

Not for a festival.

Not even for a new dress.

She was sewing a small piece of cloth that nobody had asked her to make.

The cloth was deep blue.

Tiny red patterns covered one side.

But the strange thing was the thread.

It was gold.

And there was no gold thread in the village.

Zuri pulled it through the cloth.

The needle went in.

It came out.

She pulled the thread tight.

Then something happened.

The thread moved by itself.

Zuri froze.

She stared at the cloth.

The golden thread slowly formed a small line.

Then another.

Then another.

It was making a pattern.

Zuri did not touch the needle.

She watched.

The thread formed the shape of a river.

Then a tree.

Then a small house.

Zuri leaned closer.

At the end of the pattern, the thread formed one tiny door.

She whispered:

"What are you trying to show me?"

The thread stopped moving.

Zuri breathed out.

Then she heard three knocks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

She turned toward the door.

Nobody came that early.

Zuri placed the cloth on her lap.

Another knock came.

This time, it was softer.

She stood.

The wooden floor made a small sound under her feet.

She walked to the door.

Before she opened it, she heard a child's voice.

"Zuri."

She knew the voice.

It belonged to Kato, her younger brother.

She opened the door quickly.

Kato stood outside.

His eyes were wide.

He was holding something in his hands.

A broken needle.

Zuri looked at it.

"Where did you get that?"

Kato looked behind him.

"From the old sewing room."

Zuri's face changed.

Nobody entered that room.

Not since their mother died.

Zuri took the broken needle.

It was cold.

Very cold.

"Why did you go there?"

Kato pointed toward the back of the house.

"I heard someone sewing."

Zuri became still.

"Sewing?"

Kato nodded.

"Someone was humming too."

Zuri looked at the cloth on her lap.

The golden thread had started moving again.

Kato saw it.

He stepped backward.

"Zuri..."

The thread pulled itself through the cloth.

One stitch.

Two stitches.

Three.

Then it stopped.

A word appeared.

Zuri stared at it.

It was not her name.

It was her mother's name.

AYANA.

Kato's mouth opened.

"Mama."

Zuri touched the letters.

The cloth suddenly became warm.

A small drop of water appeared on the golden thread.

Then another.

Then another.

The drops rolled down the cloth.

They joined together and formed a tiny wet line.

A river.

Kato looked frightened.

"Why is the cloth showing a river?"

Zuri did not answer.

She knew the river.

It was the same river where their mother had disappeared.

For years, people had said Ayana fell into the water during a storm.

But Zuri had never believed them.

Her mother knew the river better than anyone.

She used to wash cloth there.

She used to sit under the trees and sew.

And she always carried a small red sewing basket.

Zuri still remembered it.

She had never found it after her mother's death.

Kato pulled Zuri's hand.

"Come."

Zuri looked at him.

"Where?"

Kato pointed toward the sewing room.

"There is something inside."

They walked down the narrow hallway.

The house was quiet.

Too quiet.

Zuri stopped before the old sewing room.

The door was covered with dust.

Nobody had touched it for years.

But there was something strange on the floor.

A golden thread.

It came from beneath the door.

Zuri crouched down.

She touched it.

The thread moved.

It slipped back beneath the door.

Kato whispered:

"It wants us to follow."

Zuri slowly opened the door.

The room smelled of old cloth.

Wooden shelves covered the walls.

Bundles of fabric sat everywhere.

Old needles.

Buttons.

Spools.

Scissors.

A broken sewing machine stood near the window.

Zuri stepped inside.

Her eyes moved across the room.

Then she stopped.

The red sewing basket was sitting on the table.

Zuri's heart jumped.

She walked toward it.

Kato stayed near the door.

"That is Mama's."

Zuri nodded.

She lifted the lid.

Inside were several pieces of cloth.

But one thing was missing.

The needle.

Zuri looked at the broken needle in her hand.

It fit the empty space perfectly.

She placed it inside.

The moment it touched the basket, the sewing machine started.

Zuri jumped back.

The machine moved without anyone touching it.

The wheel turned.

The needle went up.

Then down.

Up.

Down.

The machine began sewing a piece of white cloth.

Kato ran toward Zuri.

Zuri held him close.

The machine stopped.

Silence returned.

A small piece of cloth fell to the floor.

Zuri picked it up.

There was a message sewn into it.

"Do not follow the river."

Zuri stared at the words.

Kato swallowed.

"But Mama is there."

Zuri looked at him.

Then she looked at the golden thread in her hand.

The thread suddenly pulled toward the window.

It slipped through a small crack.

Zuri followed it with her eyes.

Outside, the first light of morning had appeared.

Beyond the house stood the river.

The golden thread stretched all the way toward it.

Zuri whispered:

"Then why are you showing me the river?"

The thread pulled tight.

Then it snapped.

Zuri looked down.

The broken end of the thread was still moving.

It slowly curled across the floor.

It formed another word.

BEHIND.

Zuri looked behind her.

There was nothing.

Then Kato screamed.

Zuri turned.

The old sewing machine was moving again.

But this time, it was sewing a red cloth.

A face was slowly appearing on it.

Zuri knew that face.

It was her mother's.

And beneath the face, another sentence appeared.

"Your mother did not drown."

Zuri stopped breathing.

The needle went down one last time.

The machine stopped.

Then someone knocked from inside the wall.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Zuri slowly turned toward the wall.

A thin golden thread appeared from a crack in the wood.

It moved across the wall.

And began sewing a hidden door.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🪡 Chapter Two – Door Behind the Cloth

Zuri stood in front of the wall.

The golden thread was still moving.

It slipped through the old wood like a tiny snake.

Kato held Zuri's arm.

"Don't open it."

Zuri looked at him.

"What if Mama is behind it?"

Kato said nothing.

The thread made another stitch.

Then another.

Slowly, the shape of a door appeared on the wall.

There was no handle.

No lock.

Only a small round hole.

Zuri remembered the red sewing basket.

She went back to the table.

She searched beneath the pieces of cloth.

Under the basket was a small wooden button.

She picked it up.

It had a hole in the middle.

Zuri looked at the wall.

Then at the button.

"It fits."

She walked back.

Kato stepped behind her.

Zuri placed the button inside the small hole.

Nothing happened.

She waited.

Then the button turned.

Click.

The wooden wall moved.

A narrow door opened.

Cold air rushed into the room.

Kato covered his nose.

"It smells like rain."

Zuri looked inside.

There were steps.

They went down.

Very far down.

A golden thread ran along the steps.

Zuri picked up a small lantern from the floor.

The lantern lit by itself.

Kato stared at it.

"That was not there before."

Zuri smiled a little.

"Neither was the door."

They began to walk down.

Step.

Step.

Step.

The sound of their feet echoed below.

The air became colder.

Then Zuri heard something.

A soft humming.

She stopped.

Kato almost walked into her.

"What?"

Zuri raised one finger.

They listened.

The humming came again.

It was a song.

A song Zuri knew.

Her mother used to sing it while sewing.

Zuri's eyes became wet.

"Mama."

She moved faster.

Kato followed.

The steps ended at a small underground room.

A wooden table stood in the middle.

A sewing lamp burned above it.

Beside the lamp sat a woman.

Her back was turned toward them.

She was sewing.

Slowly.

Carefully.

Zuri could see the golden thread passing through the cloth.

Her heart beat hard.

"Mama?"

The woman stopped sewing.

Her hand remained still.

She did not turn.

Kato whispered:

"Maybe we should go."

The woman placed the needle on the table.

Then she spoke.

"Zuri."

Zuri could not move.

It was her mother's voice.

The same soft voice.

The same calm way of saying her name.

Zuri stepped forward.

"Is it really you?"

The woman slowly turned.

Zuri stopped.

It was not her mother.

The woman looked much older.

Her hair was grey.

Her hands were covered with tiny cuts from years of sewing.

But she had her mother's eyes.

Kato moved closer to Zuri.

"Who are you?"

The woman smiled.

"My name is Nala."

Zuri looked at the cloth in her hands.

It was covered with names.

Hundreds of names.

Some were written in red.

Some in blue.

Some in gold.

Zuri noticed her mother's name.

AYANA.

She pointed at it.

"Why is my mother's name here?"

Nala looked at her.

"Because your mother came here."

Zuri took a step forward.

"When?"

Nala looked at the golden thread.

"Many years ago."

Zuri's hands shook.

"Why did she never come back?"

Nala looked down.

"She tried."

Kato frowned.

"What stopped her?"

Nala stood.

She walked to the far wall.

There hung a large piece of cloth.

It showed the village.

The river.

The fields.

The houses.

And one road leading away.

Nala touched the road.

"Your mother found this place when she was looking for something."

Zuri moved closer.

"What was she looking for?"

Nala looked at her.

"A missing piece of cloth."

Zuri looked confused.

"Cloth?"

Nala nodded.

"Your family has carried the same cloth for many years."

She pointed toward Zuri's blue cloth.

Zuri looked down.

The golden pattern had changed.

The little house was gone.

The river was gone.

Only a single tree remained.

Under the tree was a small figure.

A woman.

Holding a baby.

Zuri touched the picture.

"Who is that?"

Nala's smile disappeared.

"Your mother's mother."

Zuri stared at her.

She had never heard anyone speak about her grandmother.

Not even once.

"Why did nobody tell us?"

Nala returned to the table.

"Because your family made another mistake."

Zuri waited.

"They believed the cloth brought bad luck."

Kato shook his head.

"Does it?"

Nala laughed softly.

"No."

"Cloth does not bring bad luck."

"People bring fear when they do not understand what they see."

Zuri looked at the old sewing table.

There were many needles.

Each needle had a different colored thread.

Nala picked up a red one.

"Red remembers blood."

She picked up a blue one.

"Blue remembers water."

She picked up a green one.

"Green remembers the land."

Then she picked up the golden thread.

"And gold remembers promises."

Zuri looked at the golden thread.

It moved gently between Nala's fingers.

Zuri swallowed.

"What promise did my mother make?"

Nala looked toward the ceiling.

For a few moments, she said nothing.

Then she opened a drawer.

Inside was a folded piece of cloth.

She placed it on the table.

Zuri unfolded it.

A single sentence had been sewn across it.

"I will finish what my mother began."

Zuri touched the words.

"She wrote this?"

Nala nodded.

"Your mother came here to finish the cloth."

Zuri looked at her.

"Did she finish it?"

Nala shook her head.

Kato stepped forward.

"Why?"

Nala pointed toward the back of the room.

A narrow passage opened behind the sewing table.

It was covered with loose threads.

At the end stood another door.

But this door was not made of wood.

It was made of cloth.

Thick cloth.

Blue.

Red.

Green.

And gold.

Zuri stared at it.

Nala whispered:

"Your mother went through that door."

Zuri took a step forward.

"Where does it lead?"

Nala looked at her.

"That is what you must find out."

The golden thread suddenly pulled tight.

It stretched from Zuri's hand toward the cloth door.

Then the door began to move.

Not open.

It began to breathe.

In.

Out.

In.

Out.

Kato grabbed Zuri.

"Zuri, look!"

A handprint slowly appeared on the cloth.

From the other side.

Then another.

Someone was pressing against the door.

Zuri stepped closer.

The handprint moved.

It came toward her.

Then a voice whispered through the cloth.

"Zuri..."

She knew that voice.

Her mother's voice.

Then the cloth door opened a little.

A golden thread slipped through the gap.

It curled around Zuri's wrist.

And pulled.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🧶 Chapter Three – Where the Needle Led

Zuri pulled her hand back.

The golden thread tightened around her wrist.

It did not hurt.

It felt warm.

Almost like a hand holding hers.

Kato moved closer.

"Zuri, don't go."

Zuri looked at Nala.

"That is my mother's voice."

Nala nodded.

"I know."

The cloth door opened a little wider.

Beyond it was darkness.

But there was no cold air this time.

Instead, the smell of fresh grass came through.

Then came the sound of water.

A river.

Zuri stepped closer.

The golden thread pulled again.

She looked down.

It had left her wrist.

Now it was moving across the floor.

It made a line toward the door.

Zuri understood.

"It wants me to follow."

Nala picked up the lantern.

"Then follow it."

Kato looked at her.

"You are letting her go?"

Nala smiled.

"She is not going alone."

Nala handed the lantern to Kato.

Then she took three small spools of thread from the table.

One red.

One blue.

One green.

She gave them to Zuri.

"Keep them safe."

Zuri looked at the spools.

"What are they for?"

Nala answered softly.

"You will know when you need them."

Zuri stepped through the cloth door.

Kato followed.

Nala came last.

The moment all three entered, the door closed behind them.

Zuri turned.

The door was gone.

There was only a tall wall of trees.

Kato looked around.

"Where are we?"

Zuri did not answer.

She knew this place.

She had seen it before.

Not in real life.

In the cloth.

The river flowed between tall green trees.

Birds flew above the water.

Small yellow flowers covered the ground.

And in the middle of the river stood a large flat stone.

Zuri walked toward it.

The golden thread moved ahead of her.

It crossed the grass.

It crossed the river.

Then it stopped on the stone.

Zuri stepped into the water.

Kato followed.

The water reached their ankles.

Then their knees.

Zuri stopped.

The water was warm.

She looked down.

Something was shining beneath the water.

She bent down and reached for it.

Her fingers touched metal.

She pulled it out.

It was a small silver needle.

The needle had a red thread tied to it.

Zuri stared at it.

"Mama's needle."

Nala looked at it carefully.

"Yes."

Kato looked toward the trees.

His face changed.

"Someone is there."

Zuri turned.

A young woman stood beneath a tree.

She wore a simple blue dress.

Her hair was tied behind her.

She held a red sewing basket.

Zuri stopped breathing.

The woman looked exactly like the woman in the old photograph.

Her mother.

Young.

Alive.

Sitting beneath the tree.

Sewing.

Zuri took one step toward her.

"Mama?"

The woman looked up.

She smiled.

But she did not answer.

Instead, she continued sewing.

Zuri moved closer.

She could see the cloth in her mother's hands.

It was the same blue cloth Zuri had been holding.

But it was not finished.

One side had no stitching.

Zuri looked at the silver needle.

Then at her mother.

The golden thread moved toward the woman.

Zuri followed it.

The woman suddenly stopped sewing.

She looked directly at Zuri.

Her eyes filled with tears.

"You came."

Zuri covered her mouth.

Her mother stood.

For a moment, neither of them moved.

Then Zuri ran toward her.

Her mother opened her arms.

They held each other.

Zuri cried.

"I thought you were dead."

Her mother held her tightly.

"I know."

Kato stood nearby.

He was crying too.

Ayana opened one arm.

"Come here, little one."

Kato ran into her arms.

For a while, nobody spoke.

Only the river made a soft sound.

Then Zuri pulled away.

"Why did you leave us?"

Ayana looked down.

"I did not leave you."

"I was trying to finish something."

She picked up the unfinished cloth.

"This."

Zuri touched it.

"Why is it important?"

Ayana looked toward Nala.

"Because your grandmother started it."

Nala lowered her eyes.

Zuri looked at her.

"Grandma?"

Nala nodded.

"Your grandmother knew the old sewing path."

Ayana continued.

"Every family has a cloth."

"Not always one you can touch."

"Some are made from stories."

Zuri listened.

"Long ago, our people used cloth to keep memories."

"Every stitch carried something."

"A birth."

"A wedding."

"A promise."

"Sometimes even a warning."

Ayana touched the unfinished part.

"But someone tried to cut the story."

Zuri looked at the cloth.

"Who?"

Ayana did not answer.

The river suddenly became quiet.

Even the birds stopped singing.

Zuri looked around.

Something had changed.

The golden thread had stopped moving.

It was hanging in the air.

Straight.

Tight.

Pointing toward the forest.

Ayana looked at it.

"We have to go."

Zuri frowned.

"Why?"

Ayana quickly packed the cloth into her basket.

"Because they know you are here."

Kato looked frightened.

"Who?"

Ayana took Zuri's hand.

"The ones who wanted the story forgotten."

A sound came from the forest.

Crack.

A branch had broken.

Then another.

Crack.

Someone was walking through the trees.

Slowly.

Coming closer.

Ayana picked up her sewing basket.

"Do not run."

Zuri stared into the forest.

A dark shape moved between the trees.

Then another.

Then another.

Three people stepped into view.

They wore plain brown clothes.

Each one carried a pair of scissors.

Nala stepped in front of the children.

Ayana held the unfinished cloth tightly.

The tallest man looked at Zuri.

"Give us the needle."

Zuri closed her hand around it.

"No."

The man smiled.

"Then we will cut the thread another way."

He raised his scissors.

The golden thread suddenly flashed.

It wrapped around Zuri's wrist.

Then it pulled her toward the river.

Ayana shouted.

"Zuri, hold on!"

Zuri grabbed Kato.

The water began to rise.

The three strangers stepped forward.

And from somewhere beneath the river came the sound of a sewing machine.

Zuri looked down.

Something was moving beneath the water.

Something large.

Something wrapped in blue cloth.

Then two hands rose from the river and held out a needle.

Zuri stared.

Her mother's face went pale.

"That is not possible."

The hands slowly lifted higher.

And the voice beneath the water whispered:

"Finish the stitch."

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🧵 Chapter Four – The Stitch Beneath the River

Nobody moved.

The hands remained above the water.

They were not reaching for Zuri.

They were holding the needle.

The silver needle shook gently between the fingers.

Zuri looked at Ayana.

"Mama, who is that?"

Ayana did not answer.

She stared at the river as if she had seen something she never wanted to see again.

The three men stood near the trees.

Their scissors were still in their hands.

But none of them moved.

Even they were afraid.

Nala stepped closer to the river.

"It has found the needle."

Zuri looked at her.

"What does that mean?"

Nala pointed at the water.

"It means the last stitch can be finished."

The hands slowly lowered.

The silver needle disappeared beneath the water.

Then the river became still.

No ripples.

No sound.

Nothing.

Zuri looked down at the needle in her hand.

It was no longer there.

She opened her fingers.

Only the red thread remained.

Ayana took Zuri's hand.

"Listen to me."

Zuri looked at her.

"Whatever happens next, do not cut the red thread."

Zuri nodded.

One of the men stepped forward.

"Enough."

He raised his scissors.

The golden thread shot from the ground.

It wrapped around his wrist.

He tried to pull away.

The thread tightened.

His scissors dropped into the grass.

The other two men stepped back.

Nala picked up the scissors.

She threw them into the river.

The water swallowed them.

The man stared at her.

"You should not have done that."

Nala smiled.

"Neither should you."

A loud wind rushed through the trees.

Leaves flew everywhere.

The golden thread rose from the ground.

It moved around Zuri.

Then it formed a circle.

Inside the circle, the ground began to change.

Grass disappeared.

The soil opened.

Underneath was an old wooden floor.

Zuri stared.

"What's under us?"

Ayana looked at the floor.

"Another sewing room."

Kato stepped closer.

"Under the river?"

Ayana nodded.

"Not under the river."

"Under the memory of the river."

Zuri frowned.

She did not understand.

Ayana touched the wooden floor.

A small door appeared.

She opened it.

Warm light came from below.

There were shelves.

There were baskets.

There were hundreds of pieces of cloth.

Each one had a name.

Zuri climbed down.

Kato followed.

Nala came behind them.

Ayana stayed at the top for a moment.

She looked at the three men.

They had not followed.

They stood far away from the opening.

Ayana smiled.

"They cannot enter."

Zuri looked up.

"Why?"

Ayana pointed toward the golden thread.

"Because they cut stories."

"And this place is made from stories."

She climbed down.

The wooden door closed above them.

The room became quiet.

Zuri walked between the shelves.

She touched one piece of cloth.

A picture appeared on it.

A young boy carrying water.

She touched another.

A woman planting maize.

Another showed a wedding.

Another showed a baby taking its first steps.

Kato looked around.

"Are these people's memories?"

Nala nodded.

"Every stitch keeps something."

Zuri stopped at a small basket.

Her mother's name was written on it.

AYANA.

She opened it.

Inside was a half-finished piece of blue cloth.

Ayana saw it.

She became silent.

Zuri looked at her.

"This is yours."

Ayana nodded.

"I started it when I was your age."

Zuri touched the cloth.

There was a picture of a young girl.

She was standing beside a woman.

The woman looked like Grandma Sewa.

Zuri stared at the picture.

"That is Grandma."

Ayana smiled.

"Yes."

Zuri looked at the other girl.

"Who is this?"

Ayana took a breath.

"Your aunt."

Zuri's eyes widened.

"I have an aunt?"

Ayana nodded.

"You did."

The room suddenly became colder.

Zuri looked at her.

"What happened to her?"

Ayana looked away.

"She disappeared."

Kato came closer.

"Like you?"

Ayana looked at him.

"Yes."

Zuri looked back at the cloth.

Her aunt's face had been stitched in blue.

But one part was missing.

Her mouth.

Zuri touched the empty space.

The golden thread suddenly moved.

It passed through the needle.

Then it pulled itself across the cloth.

A mouth appeared.

One sentence was stitched.

"She knows where the first cloth is."

Zuri looked at Ayana.

"Where?"

Ayana shook her head.

"I don't know."

The golden thread moved again.

It formed a small arrow.

The arrow pointed toward the back of the room.

There stood a tall wooden cupboard.

Zuri walked toward it.

She opened the door.

Inside were old clothes.

Behind them was another hidden space.

A tiny red box sat inside.

Zuri pulled it out.

She opened it.

Inside was a single piece of black thread.

Nala stepped back.

"Put it down."

Zuri looked at her.

"Why?"

Nala's face had changed.

"That thread does not remember."

"It makes people forget."

Zuri quickly closed the box.

But it was too late.

Kato looked at Zuri.

"Who are you?"

Zuri froze.

"What?"

Kato looked confused.

"I can't remember your name."

Ayana rushed toward him.

She took the red thread from Zuri's hand.

She tied it around Kato's wrist.

His eyes became clear.

"Zuri."

He hugged her.

Zuri held him tightly.

Nala looked at the black thread.

"Now you understand why they wanted it."

Zuri nodded.

She picked up the box.

"We have to destroy it."

Ayana shook her head.

"No."

"We have to find out who made it."

The room went silent.

Then someone began knocking above them.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Zuri looked up.

Ayana's face became pale.

"That is not one of them."

Zuri whispered:

"Who is it?"

Ayana looked toward the ceiling.

"Your grandmother."

Zuri's heart jumped.

A voice came from above.

"Zuri."

It was Grandma Sewa.

But the voice sounded afraid.

"Come out."

Zuri stepped toward the door.

Then another voice came from behind her.

"Don't listen to her."

Zuri slowly turned.

A young woman stood between the shelves.

She looked exactly like the girl in the old cloth.

Ayana stared at her.

"Adira..."

Zuri's eyes widened.

Her missing aunt had finally returned.

Adira looked at Zuri.

"Your mother has not told you the whole story."

Then she pointed at the red box in Zuri's hand.

"And that box should never have been opened."

🌙 End of Chapter Four

🪶 Chapter Five – Adira's Hidden Story

Nobody spoke.

Adira stood between the shelves, watching Zuri hold the red box.

She looked young.

Too young.

She looked almost the same age as she had looked in the old cloth.

Zuri slowly lowered the box.

"Are you really my aunt?"

Adira smiled sadly.

"I am."

Kato stared at her.

"But Mama said you disappeared."

Adira looked at Ayana.

"Your mother did not lie."

She looked back at Zuri.

"But she did not tell you everything."

Zuri looked at Ayana.

"Why?"

Ayana looked down.

"Because I wanted you to have a normal life."

Adira gave a small laugh.

"There was never anything normal about our family."

The lantern flickered.

Zuri stepped closer to Adira.

"Where have you been all these years?"

Adira touched one of the old cloths on the shelf.

"Here."

Zuri frowned.

"Here?"

Adira nodded.

"I never left this place."

Kato looked around the room.

"How?"

Adira pointed at the cloths.

"Some people hide behind walls."

"Some hide in forests."

"And some are hidden inside memories."

Zuri felt a chill.

She looked at the red box.

"Was this what kept you here?"

Adira nodded.

"Part of it."

She walked toward the table.

Ayana followed her.

Nala stayed quiet.

Adira pointed at the black thread.

"That thread was made by a man called Bako."

Zuri waited.

"Who was Bako?"

Adira's face became serious.

"He was the keeper of the first cloth."

Zuri looked confused.

"What is the first cloth?"

Adira picked up a small blue piece of fabric.

It was old.

Very old.

The edges were torn.

But the stitching was still bright.

"Before your grandmother made our family cloth, there was another one."

She placed it on the table.

"The first cloth carried the story of our people."

"Every family added one stitch."

"Every stitch carried a memory."

Zuri touched the cloth.

A picture appeared.

People gathered around a fire.

Children danced.

Women sang.

Men played drums.

Then the picture changed.

The fire went out.

People ran.

Someone was chasing them.

Zuri pulled her hand away.

"What happened?"

Adira looked at the cloth.

"Someone wanted the memories gone."

Zuri remembered the three men outside.

"The men with the scissors."

Adira nodded.

"They are not the first."

"And they will not be the last."

Kato moved closer to Zuri.

"Why do they want the memories?"

Adira looked at him.

"Because a person who remembers cannot easily be controlled."

The words stayed in the room.

Zuri looked at Ayana.

"So what happened to Grandma?"

Ayana closed her eyes.

"She found the first cloth."

Zuri's heart beat faster.

"Where is it?"

Adira answered.

"Nobody knows."

A soft sound came from the red box.

Scratch.

Scratch.

Scratch.

Everyone looked at it.

Zuri slowly placed the box on the table.

The lid moved.

Adira stepped back.

"Don't touch it."

The lid lifted by itself.

The black thread rose from inside.

It floated in the air.

Then it moved toward Zuri.

Zuri stepped back.

The thread stopped.

It formed a small circle in the air.

Inside the circle appeared a picture.

A young Ayana.

A young Adira.

And Grandma Sewa.

They were standing beside a large tree.

Behind them stood Bako.

Zuri watched.

Bako was holding a pair of scissors.

Grandma Sewa was holding the first cloth.

She gave it to Ayana.

Then Bako moved toward them.

Grandma Sewa shouted.

The picture shook.

The black thread cut across the image.

The memory ended.

Zuri looked at Ayana.

"Why did Grandma give the cloth to you?"

Ayana answered quietly.

"Because she knew Bako would come for it."

"She told me to hide it."

Adira shook her head.

"But you did not hide it."

Ayana looked at her.

"I tried."

"Then they found me."

Zuri stared at her mother.

"Is that why you disappeared?"

Ayana nodded.

"I came here to keep them away from you."

Zuri's eyes filled with tears.

"You could have told us."

Ayana stepped closer.

"I was afraid."

Zuri looked surprised.

Her mother had always seemed brave.

But now she looked tired.

"Afraid of what?"

Ayana pointed toward the ceiling.

A faint tapping sound came from above.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Nala looked up.

"They found us."

Adira moved quickly.

She grabbed the blue cloth.

"We need to leave."

Zuri picked up the red box.

Adira stopped her.

"Leave it."

Zuri shook her head.

"No."

"If this can make people forget, we cannot leave it behind."

Adira stared at her.

Then she smiled.

"You sound like Grandma."

They moved toward the back of the room.

Nala opened a small wooden door.

A narrow path waited behind it.

Zuri looked back.

The black thread was still floating.

It suddenly stretched across the room.

It pointed toward one shelf.

Zuri stopped.

"Wait."

She walked to the shelf.

There was a small white cloth hidden behind the books.

She pulled it out.

A name was sewn into it.

SEWA.

Zuri held it tightly.

Then the cloth became warm.

A new message appeared.

"Follow the woman who remembers the song."

Zuri looked at Nala.

Nala looked confused.

"I don't know what that means."

A woman's voice suddenly came from the passage.

She was humming.

Softly.

Slowly.

The same song Ayana used to sing while sewing.

Ayana froze.

Adira looked at her.

"Do you know that song?"

Ayana whispered:

"Yes."

Zuri looked toward the passage.

Someone was coming.

The humming grew louder.

A shadow appeared on the wall.

It was the shadow of a woman carrying a sewing basket.

Zuri took a step forward.

The woman stopped.

Then she spoke.

"Zuri, you should never have opened that box."

Zuri tightened her grip.

She knew the voice.

It belonged to Grandma Sewa.

But Grandma was supposed to be outside.

And there was something wrong with her shadow.

The shadow had no head.

Zuri slowly backed away.

Then the woman stepped into the light.

It was Grandma Sewa.

But she was holding a pair of black scissors.

And behind her stood the three men.

🌙 End of Chapter Five

🪡 Chapter Six – Grandma's Black Scissors

Zuri could not move.

Grandma Sewa stood at the end of the passage.

She held the black scissors in one hand.

The three men stood behind her.

Nobody spoke.

Then Grandma Sewa looked at Zuri.

"Give me the box."

Zuri held it tighter.

"No."

Grandma Sewa's face became hard.

"You don't know what you are holding."

Zuri stepped back.

"Then tell me."

Grandma Sewa raised the scissors.

"That box should have stayed closed."

Ayana moved in front of Zuri.

"Mother, stop."

Grandma Sewa looked at her daughter.

"You brought her here."

Ayana shook her head.

"She found the way herself."

Grandma Sewa looked at Zuri again.

For a short moment, her eyes became soft.

Then they changed again.

"Give me the box."

Zuri did not move.

Kato stepped beside her.

"We are not giving it to you."

One of the men laughed.

"Children should not speak in grown people's matters."

Kato frowned.

"You followed us."

The man stopped laughing.

Nala stepped forward.

"Enough."

She pulled the red thread from her pocket.

She tied one end around her finger.

Then she pulled.

The red thread stretched across the passage.

A warm light filled the room.

The three men covered their faces.

Grandma Sewa raised her scissors.

The red thread shook.

Then the scissors came down.

Snip.

The red thread broke.

The light disappeared.

Nala fell to her knees.

Zuri ran toward her.

"Nala!"

Nala grabbed her hand.

"Run."

Zuri looked at Ayana.

Ayana nodded.

"Go!"

They turned and ran.

Kato held the lantern.

Adira carried the blue cloth.

Zuri held the red box.

Ayana ran behind them.

The passage became darker.

They could hear footsteps behind them.

Fast footsteps.

Very close.

Kato looked back.

"They are coming!"

Adira grabbed his hand.

"Don't look back."

They kept running.

The passage turned left.

Then right.

Then left again.

Zuri almost fell.

Ayana caught her.

"Keep going."

Zuri looked at her.

"Where are we going?"

Ayana pointed ahead.

A small light could be seen.

"To the old tree."

They ran toward it.

The light became brighter.

Then they came out of the passage.

They were standing in a large open field.

In the middle stood an enormous tree.

Its branches reached high into the sky.

Its roots covered the ground like thick ropes.

Zuri stopped.

She knew the tree.

It was the same tree from the cloth.

"This is where Grandma found the first cloth."

Ayana nodded.

"Yes."

Adira placed the blue cloth on one of the roots.

The tree began to shake.

Leaves fell around them.

Kato looked up.

"Is the tree alive?"

Ayana smiled.

"Everything here remembers."

Zuri placed the red box beside the cloth.

The box opened.

The black thread rose into the air.

It floated toward the tree.

Then it wrapped around one of the branches.

The branch bent down.

A small opening appeared in the trunk.

Inside was a wooden shelf.

On the shelf sat an old sewing basket.

Zuri stared at it.

There was a name carved into the lid.

SEWA.

Grandma's name.

Ayana stepped forward.

"Mother kept it here."

Adira shook her head.

"No."

"Mother was afraid of this place."

Zuri looked at her.

"Then why did she come here?"

Adira looked toward the trees.

"Because she had no choice."

A sound came from behind them.

Footsteps.

Grandma Sewa and the three men had reached the field.

Grandma stopped when she saw the tree.

Her face became pale.

"No..."

Zuri looked at her.

"What?"

Grandma Sewa slowly lowered the scissors.

"It remembers me."

The tree shook harder.

The ground moved.

The old sewing basket fell from the trunk.

Zuri caught it.

She opened the lid.

Inside was a folded piece of cloth.

She opened it.

There were three names sewn into it.

SEWA.

AYANA.

ADIRA.

Below the names was another space.

It was empty.

Zuri looked at it.

"Why is this part empty?"

Nobody answered.

The golden thread slowly moved toward Zuri.

It rested on the empty space.

Then it formed a letter.

Z.

Zuri's eyes widened.

"My name."

Ayana looked at her.

She was crying.

"Yes."

Zuri touched the cloth.

The needle appeared beside her hand.

The silver needle.

She picked it up.

The golden thread entered the eye of the needle by itself.

Zuri looked at Grandma.

"Why did you hide all this from us?"

Grandma Sewa looked down.

Her voice became weak.

"Because I made the first cut."

Ayana stared at her.

"Mother..."

Grandma closed her eyes.

"I thought I was protecting the family."

"I thought if I cut the story, the danger would end."

Zuri held the needle.

"But it did not."

Grandma shook her head.

"No."

"It only made the danger grow."

One of the men stepped forward.

"Enough talking."

He reached for the cloth.

The tree suddenly moved.

A thick root rose from the ground.

It struck the man's feet.

He fell.

The other two men ran.

Grandma Sewa did not move.

She looked at the tree.

Then at Zuri.

"Finish the stitch."

Zuri held the cloth against the tree.

She pushed the needle through.

One stitch.

The tree became quiet.

Second stitch.

The sky darkened.

Third stitch.

The river began to roar somewhere far away.

Zuri pulled the thread tight.

The empty space slowly filled.

Her name appeared.

ZURI.

Then something else appeared beneath it.

Not a name.

A message.

"ONE STORY REMAINS."

Zuri looked up.

The tree opened wider.

Inside the trunk was a narrow staircase.

It led down into darkness.

Kato looked at Zuri.

"Are we going down there?"

Zuri looked at Ayana.

Ayana nodded slowly.

"That is where the first cloth was hidden."

Zuri took the lantern.

Adira picked up the blue cloth.

They stepped toward the stairs.

Grandma Sewa remained outside.

Zuri looked back.

"Grandma, aren't you coming?"

Grandma shook her head.

"I have already gone there once."

"I never came back the same."

Zuri stared at her.

Then she heard a whisper from inside the tree.

"Bring the woman who cut the thread."

Grandma Sewa froze.

The scissors fell from her hand.

Zuri looked at her.

The tree was calling for Grandma.

And Grandma looked terrified.

👵🏿 Chapter Seven – What Grandma Left Behind

Grandma Sewa stared at the opening inside the tree.

Her lips moved, but no words came out.

Zuri waited.

The voice came again from inside the tree.

"Bring the woman who cut the thread."

Grandma closed her eyes.

Then she bent down and picked up the black scissors.

She looked at them for a long moment.

Then she threw them into the grass.

"I will go."

Zuri moved aside.

Grandma walked toward the tree.

With every step, the ground made a soft sound.

Step.

Step.

Step.

When she reached the opening, the tree became still.

Grandma placed one hand on the trunk.

A small tear rolled down her cheek.

"I am sorry."

Zuri looked at her.

"Who are you apologizing to?"

Grandma did not answer.

She stepped inside.

The tree closed behind her.

Zuri rushed forward.

"Grandma!"

The opening opened again.

Grandma's voice came from inside.

"Come."

Zuri looked at Ayana.

Ayana nodded.

They entered.

Adira followed.

Kato stayed close to Zuri.

The tree closed behind them.

Darkness covered them.

Zuri lifted the lantern.

A narrow staircase appeared.

It went down between thick wooden walls.

Grandma was already walking ahead.

Her steps were slow.

Zuri followed.

The deeper they went, the colder the air became.

Then they heard something.

A soft tapping.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Kato whispered:

"Someone is sewing."

Grandma stopped.

She turned around.

"Do not follow that sound."

Zuri frowned.

"But you heard it too."

Grandma nodded.

"I know."

They kept walking.

The tapping grew louder.

Tap.

Tap.

Tap.

Then the staircase ended.

A large room opened before them.

Old cloth hung from the ceiling.

Red.

Blue.

Green.

Gold.

Some pieces were torn.

Some were covered with names.

Others had no writing at all.

At the center of the room stood a wooden chair.

On the chair was a red sewing basket.

Grandma stared at it.

"It is still here."

Zuri walked closer.

"What is it?"

Grandma pointed at the basket.

"That was your great-grandmother's."

Zuri looked at Ayana.

Ayana nodded.

"Her name was Amara."

Grandma moved toward the chair.

She touched the basket.

The room suddenly became warm.

A light appeared above them.

It showed an old woman sitting beside the same tree.

She was sewing.

A little girl sat beside her.

It was Grandma Sewa.

She was only a child.

Zuri watched quietly.

The old woman handed the little Sewa a piece of cloth.

Then she pointed at the needle.

The little girl smiled.

She began sewing.

The picture changed.

Sewa was older.

Then older again.

Then she was a young woman.

She was holding the first cloth.

A man stood in front of her.

Bako.

He held out his hand.

"Give it to me."

Young Sewa shook her head.

Bako became angry.

He grabbed the cloth.

Sewa pulled it back.

The cloth tore.

Zuri gasped.

Grandma looked down.

"That was the first cut."

The memory continued.

Young Sewa ran into the forest.

She hid the torn cloth inside the tree.

Then she took a pair of scissors.

She cut away another piece.

And another.

And another.

Zuri looked at Grandma.

"You cut it yourself."

Grandma nodded.

"I thought I could destroy the part they wanted."

"I thought the rest would be safe."

Adira stepped closer.

"But every cut created another lost memory."

Grandma nodded.

"Yes."

The light above them faded.

Zuri walked toward the red basket.

She opened it.

Inside was a small folded cloth.

She opened it.

There were only four words.

"Find what was cut."

Zuri looked at Grandma.

"Where?"

Grandma pointed toward the floor.

There were many loose threads.

Some were red.

Some were blue.

Some were green.

But one was gold.

Zuri followed it.

It went under the chair.

She moved the chair.

A small hole appeared in the floor.

Inside was a bundle of cloth.

Zuri pulled it out.

The bundle was tied with black thread.

Adira stepped back.

"That is what Bako was looking for."

Zuri held the bundle.

"Should I open it?"

Grandma looked at her.

"You must."

Zuri untied the black thread.

The moment it fell away, the room shook.

The hanging cloths began moving.

The lantern went out.

Kato grabbed Zuri's hand.

A voice whispered from the darkness.

"Finally."

Zuri froze.

Someone laughed.

Not Grandma.

Not Ayana.

Not Adira.

A man's voice.

Bako.

Grandma stepped in front of the children.

"He followed us."

A shadow appeared on the wall.

It grew larger.

Then a man stepped out from behind the hanging cloth.

He was holding the black scissors.

The same scissors Grandma had thrown outside.

Zuri stepped back.

"Bako?"

The man smiled.

"Your grandmother remembers my name."

Grandma raised her head.

"I wish I didn't."

Bako looked at Zuri.

"You have something that belongs to me."

Zuri held the bundle tighter.

"No."

Bako laughed.

"Your family has said that for years."

He raised the scissors.

The golden thread suddenly flew from the floor.

It wrapped around Zuri's hand.

Then it pulled the bundle open.

A large piece of cloth unfolded in the air.

Everyone stared.

It was the first cloth.

The missing part.

The room filled with pictures.

Families.

Births.

Deaths.

Weddings.

Promises.

Secrets.

Then one picture appeared above them.

Zuri saw Grandma Sewa standing beside Bako.

But this time, Grandma was not a frightened girl.

She was older.

She was holding a baby.

Zuri stared at the baby.

She looked at Grandma.

"Who is that baby?"

Grandma's eyes filled with tears.

She whispered:

"Your mother."

Zuri looked at Ayana.

But Ayana was staring at another part of the cloth.

Her face had gone pale.

"Mother..."

Grandma looked at her.

Ayana pointed at the cloth.

A second baby was standing beside her.

Two babies.

One was Ayana.

The other was wrapped in a yellow cloth.

Zuri looked at Grandma.

"Who is the other baby?"

Grandma said nothing.

Bako smiled.

"Now you finally know."

The room went silent.

Then the golden thread stitched one name beneath the second baby.

AMINA.

Adira stepped back.

"That cannot be."

Bako lowered the scissors.

"She is alive."

Zuri looked around the room.

Her heart was beating hard.

"Where is she?"

Bako smiled.

"That is what the next stitch will show you."
