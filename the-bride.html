💍 The Bride

🌼 Chapter One – The Beautiful Lady From Lagos

Clara was the kind of lady people noticed the moment she walked into a place.

Not because she was always trying to get attention.

No.

It was just something about her.

Her beauty.

The way she carried herself.

The way she dressed.

Even the way she smiled could make someone forget what they were saying.

Clara lived in Lagos, in a beautiful house that her parents had left for her after years of hard work. She was young, comfortable, and had enough money to take care of herself without asking anybody for help.

She had a nice car.

Nice clothes.

Nice shoes.

And a life many young ladies could only dream about.

But there was one thing money could never buy Clara.

True love.

That was where her problem started.

Men loved Clara.

Or at least, that was what she thought at first.

They came from everywhere.

Some met her through friends.

Some saw her at restaurants.

Some followed her on social media.

And some simply saw her car and decided that they wanted to know the beautiful lady driving it.

Every man came with a sweet mouth.

“Clara, you are different.”

“You are the woman I have been looking for.”

“I have never met someone like you before.”

“I don't care about your money.”

That last one was always her favorite.

Because deep down, Clara wanted to believe it.

She wanted someone who would look at her and see Clara.

Not her house.

Not her car.

Not her bank account.

Just Clara.

But unfortunately, most of the men who came into her life had another plan.

One man called Daniel stayed with her for almost six months.

He was always calling.

Always checking on her.

Always sending sweet messages.

Every morning, he would say,

“Good morning, my queen.”

And every night,

“Sleep well, my love.”

Clara thought she had finally found the one.

Until Daniel suddenly had a business problem.

He needed money.

Not small money.

A lot of money.

Clara helped him.

She trusted him.

She even defended him when her friends warned her.

Then one morning, Daniel disappeared.

His phone was off.

His house was empty.

And the money Clara gave him?

Gone.

Just like that.

She cried for days.

But she told herself it would never happen again.

Then came another man.

His name was Victor.

Victor was different.

At least, he looked different.

He came with flowers.

He bought gifts.

He took Clara out.

He told her stories about how his family was looking forward to meeting her.

After some months, Victor proposed.

Clara said yes.

Her friends celebrated.

Her mother was happy.

For the first time in a long time, Clara thought maybe she had finally escaped the bad luck.

But she was wrong.

Very wrong.

A few months after the engagement, Victor started asking questions.

“How much do you have in your account?”

“How much is the house worth?”

“What business do you have?”

At first, Clara didn't think much of it.

Until one evening, she came home and found that some of her expensive jewelry was missing.

Victor was the last person who had visited her.

She called him.

He didn't answer.

She called again.

Nothing.

The next day, she discovered that he had disappeared.

The engagement ring was still with her.

But the man who gave it to her was nowhere to be found.

That was when Clara stopped believing in love.

She told herself,

“Maybe love is not for me.”

From that day, whenever a man approached her, she already knew what she believed was inside his mind.

Money.

Money.

And more money.

So Clara built a wall around her heart.

Nobody was getting through.

She became careful.

Very careful.

If a man complimented her, she became suspicious.

If a man bought her something, she wondered what he wanted in return.

If a man said he loved her, she almost laughed.

She had heard it too many times.

Then one afternoon, something happened that Clara never expected.

She was driving through Lagos when her car suddenly started making a strange sound.

“What's this now?” she muttered.

She slowed down.

The sound became worse.

Then the car stopped.

Just like that.

Clara tried starting it again.

Nothing.

She tried again.

Still nothing.

She looked around.

Cars were passing.

People were moving.

Lagos was doing what Lagos always did.

Nobody seemed to care.

Clara came down from the car and opened the bonnet.

She stared at the engine.

She knew absolutely nothing about cars.

“Jesus,” she whispered.

She picked up her phone and called her mechanic.

No answer.

She called again.

Still nothing.

She was already getting frustrated when she heard a voice behind her.

“Madam, you need help?”

Clara turned.

A young man was standing beside a keke.

He was wearing a simple shirt and trousers.

Nothing expensive.

No flashy watch.

No designer shoes.

Just a young man looking at her car.

Clara immediately became cautious.

“No, I'm fine,” she replied.

The young man smiled.

“Madam, if you were fine, you wouldn't be staring inside the engine like that.”

Clara looked at him.

For the first time that day, she almost laughed.

“Do you know anything about cars?” she asked.

“A little.”

He walked closer.

He checked something around the engine.

Then he asked Clara to try the ignition.

She did.

The car made the same strange sound.

The young man nodded.

“I think I know what the problem is.”

Clara folded her arms.

“You do?”

“Yes.”

“Then how much?”

The young man looked confused.

“How much for what?”

“To fix it.”

He laughed.

“Madam, I didn't say I was fixing it for money.”

Clara stared at him.

That answer surprised her.

Every man she had met usually had a price.

Sometimes they hid it.

Sometimes they showed it immediately.

But this one was different.

The young man introduced himself.

“My name is Ken.”

“Ken?”

“Yes.”

Clara looked at his keke.

Then she looked back at him.

“You drive keke?”

Ken smiled.

“Yes, madam.”

Something about the way he said it made Clara feel comfortable.

He wasn't ashamed.

He wasn't trying to impress her.

He wasn't asking what she did for a living.

He wasn't even asking for her number.

He simply helped her.

After some time, the car finally started.

Clara breathed out in relief.

“Thank you,” she said.

“You're welcome.”

Ken turned around and started walking back to his keke.

Clara watched him for a few seconds.

Then she called him.

“Ken.”

He turned.

“Yes?”

“Can I have your number?”

Ken smiled.

“Sure.”

They exchanged numbers.

Then Ken left.

Clara entered her car and drove away.

She didn't think much about him.

In fact, she almost forgot about him before she reached home.

To Clara, Ken was just another young man she had met.

A keke driver.

Nothing more.

But Clara didn't know something.

Ken was not who she thought he was.

And the simple man she had just met beside the road was about to change the way she looked at love forever.

Because Ken had a secret.

A secret Clara would never have guessed.

And when she finally discovered it...

She would have to ask herself one painful question.

“Did I almost lose the man I had been waiting for all my life?”

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌹 Chapter Two – The Man Behind the Keke

For the next few days, Clara kept thinking about the man who had helped her beside the road.

She did not even understand why.

It was supposed to be just another small meeting. Her car had broken down. A keke driver had stopped to help her. They talked for a few minutes, exchanged numbers and went their different ways.

That was all.

At least, that was what Clara kept telling herself.

But whenever her phone rang, she somehow hoped it was Ken.

One evening, Clara was sitting in her room, looking through her phone when she saw his name.

Ken: "Good evening Clara. I hope you are doing well."

She looked at the message for a while.

She wanted to reply immediately, but something stopped her.

She had seen this kind of message many times before.

Men always started gently.

They would say good morning.

They would ask if she had eaten.

They would tell her she was beautiful.

Then, after some time, the real reason would come out.

Money.

One man had once told her that he loved her more than anything in the world.

Three weeks later, he needed money for a business opportunity.

Another one had promised to marry her.

He disappeared after taking money from her to "settle an urgent family problem."

Another one even went as far as meeting her parents.

Clara thought she had finally found the right man.

But after some months, she discovered that the man was seeing another woman.

That one broke something inside her.

Since then, Clara had stopped believing sweet words.

So when Ken sent his message, she simply dropped the phone beside her.

"Not again," she whispered.

But a few minutes later, she picked up the phone and replied.

Clara: "Good evening. I'm fine."

Ken replied almost immediately.

Ken: "Glad to hear that. And your car?"

Clara smiled slightly.

Clara: "It's fine now."

Ken: "Good. I was hoping you wouldn't have another problem on the road."

She laughed.

Clara: "Why? You don't want to rescue me again?"

Ken replied,

Ken: "I don't mind rescuing you. But next time, please don't wait until your car is already smoking."

Clara laughed properly this time.

It had been a long time since a man made her laugh without asking her for anything.

They continued talking.

Nothing serious.

No talk about money.

No request for help.

No strange compliments.

Just normal conversation.

That was what surprised Clara the most.

Ken did not behave like the men she knew.

Still, she refused to let her guard down.

She reminded herself that people could pretend.

Maybe Ken was only taking his time.

Maybe he was waiting for the right moment to show his true colours.

So Clara decided to keep him at a distance.

But Ken did not force himself into her life.

If she replied, they talked.

If she did not reply, he did not complain.

Days turned into weeks.

And slowly, Clara began to notice something strange.

She was beginning to look forward to their conversations.

One Saturday afternoon, Clara was preparing to go out when her phone rang.

It was Ken.

She looked at the screen before answering.

"Hello?"

"Hello Clara. I hope I'm not disturbing you."

"No, you're not."

Ken paused.

"I wanted to ask you something."

"What is it?"

"Have you eaten?"

Clara laughed.

"Is that what you called me for?"

"Yes."

"Why?"

"Because the way you people from Lagos rush around, somebody has to remind you to eat."

Clara smiled.

"You sound like my mother."

"Then your mother must be a very wise woman."

Clara laughed again.

That conversation stayed in her mind for the rest of the day.

But there was something Ken did not know.

Clara had already started asking questions about him.

Not directly.

She didn't want him to know she was interested.

She simply wanted to know who he really was.

One afternoon, she mentioned his name to her close friend, Amaka.

"Ken?" Amaka asked.

"Yes."

"The keke driver?"

Clara nodded.

Amaka laughed.

"Please don't tell me you are falling for a keke driver."

"I'm not falling for anybody."

"Then why are you asking me about him?"

Clara became quiet.

"I just want to know what kind of person he is."

Amaka looked at her carefully.

"Clara, be careful. You know what happened the last time."

"I know."

"And the one before that."

"I know."

"And the man you almost married?"

Clara sighed.

"I said I know."

Amaka softened.

"I'm only trying to protect you."

"I know."

"But why him?"

Clara looked away.

"I don't know."

And that was the truth.

She really didn't know.

Ken was not the type of man she normally noticed.

He was not driving a big car.

He did not wear expensive clothes.

He did not come from a family Clara had heard about.

At least, that was what she believed.

To Clara, Ken was simply a young keke driver who had happened to help her.

She had no idea that there was a story behind the man she thought she knew.

A story Ken had never told her.

The following week, Clara was passing through the area where she had first met Ken when she saw him.

He was sitting inside his keke, waiting for passengers.

Their eyes met.

Ken smiled.

Clara smiled back.

She wanted to drive away.

Instead, she stopped.

"Ken!"

He quickly stepped down.

"Clara?"

"What are you doing here?"

Ken looked at the keke.

"I'm working."

She laughed.

"I can see that."

They stood there talking for a few minutes.

Then Clara noticed a book on the seat beside him.

She picked it up.

"What is this?"

Ken quickly reached for it.

"Nothing."

Clara looked at him suspiciously.

"Nothing?"

"Just an old book."

She looked at the cover again.

It was a university textbook.

Clara looked at Ken.

"You read?"

Ken smiled.

"Sometimes."

"Sometimes?"

"Yes."

She laughed.

"You're a strange keke driver."

Ken laughed.

"You haven't seen anything yet."

Clara looked at him for a moment.

There was something about the way he said those words.

Something she couldn't explain.

She was about to ask him what he meant when a passenger called Ken.

He turned around.

"I have to go."

Clara nodded.

"Okay."

Ken entered the keke and drove away.

Clara watched him disappear into the busy Lagos road.

Then she looked at the road ahead.

For some reason, her heart was beating faster.

That night, Clara couldn't sleep properly.

The book kept coming back to her mind.

Why was a keke driver carrying a university textbook?

And what did he mean when he said she hadn't seen anything yet?

The next morning, Clara received a message from Ken.

Ken: "I forgot to tell you something yesterday."

Clara stared at the message.

Clara: "What?"

Ken typed for a while.

Then his message arrived.

Ken: "I didn't start driving keke because I wanted to."

Clara's eyes widened.

Before she could reply, another message came in.

Ken: "I actually went to the University of Lagos."

Clara froze.

She read the message again.

Then again.

"University of Lagos?"

She sat quietly on her bed.

For the first time, Clara realized that the man she had already judged might not be the man she thought he was.

But there was still one question.

Why was a university graduate driving a keke around Lagos?

And what had happened to him after graduation?

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌹 Chapter Three – The Man Behind the Keke

Clara did not know when it started.

Maybe it was the way Ken always greeted her with respect.

Maybe it was the way he never asked her for money.

Or maybe it was the way he listened whenever she talked.

Whatever it was, something about Ken was slowly breaking the wall Clara had built around her heart.

But Clara was fighting it.

She had fought feelings before.

She had trusted men before.

And every time she opened her heart, somebody found a way to make her regret it.

So she told herself that Ken was different only because she had not known him long enough.

"Give it time," she whispered to herself one evening while sitting in her room.

"Every man looks good at the beginning."

She remembered all the men who had come before him.

One had loved her because of her money.

Another had promised marriage but disappeared after borrowing a large amount of money from her.

There was one who had convinced her to invest in a business with him, only for Clara to discover later that the business never existed.

And there was another one who had stayed with her for almost a year.

That one hurt the most.

He knew everything about her.

He knew her fears.

He knew her dreams.

He knew how much she wanted a peaceful home.

Yet, when he finally got what he wanted, he left her with nothing but excuses.

Since then, Clara had made one promise to herself.

Never trust a man too quickly.

That promise was the reason she kept Ken at a distance.

But Ken did not seem bothered.

He never forced himself into her life.

If Clara called, he answered.

If she did not call, he waited.

If she needed help, he came.

And if she tried to give him money for every little thing he did, he would simply smile.

"Clara, keep your money."

"Why?"

"Because I didn't help you because of your money."

Those words always left Clara quiet.

One Saturday afternoon, Clara's car refused to start again.

She stood beside it, staring at the engine like it had personally betrayed her.

She called her mechanic.

The mechanic promised to come.

Thirty minutes passed.

Then one hour.

Still nothing.

Clara sighed and picked up her phone.

She stared at Ken's name for a few seconds.

She almost dropped the phone.

"Why am I even thinking of calling him?"

She laughed at herself.

Then she called.

Ken answered on the second ring.

"Hello, Clara."

"My car has refused to start."

There was a short silence.

"Where are you?"

She told him.

"I'll come."

"You don't have to."

"I know."

"Then why are you coming?"

Ken laughed.

"Because you called me."

Less than twenty minutes later, Ken arrived.

He was still wearing the simple clothes he usually wore while driving his keke.

He walked over to the car and opened the bonnet.

Clara folded her arms.

"Are you a mechanic now?"

Ken looked at her.

"No."

"Then what are you doing?"

"Trying to save you from standing here until tomorrow."

Clara laughed.

For the first time in a long time, she felt completely comfortable around a man.

There was no pressure.

No sweet speech.

No talk about her house.

No talk about her car.

No request for money.

Just Ken.

After a few minutes, he discovered the problem.

"It is the battery connection."

"You are sure?"

"Yes."

He fixed it temporarily and asked her to start the car.

The engine came alive.

Clara smiled.

"Wow."

Ken wiped his hands.

"Take it to your mechanic tomorrow."

Clara looked at him.

"You could have just told me to call a mechanic."

"I could have."

"But you came."

"Yes."

"Why?"

Ken looked at her for a moment.

"Because sometimes people don't need money. Sometimes they just need somebody to show up."

Clara became quiet.

Those words entered a part of her heart she had tried very hard to keep locked.

That evening, Clara could not stop thinking about him.

She picked up her phone.

She wanted to call him.

But she stopped herself.

"No."

She dropped the phone.

A few seconds later, she picked it up again.

She checked if Ken was online.

He was not.

She smiled.

"What is wrong with me?"

The next morning, Ken sent her a simple message.

"Good morning. I hope your car is fine."

Clara stared at the message.

She smiled.

"Good morning. It is fine. Thank you."

Ken replied.

"Good."

That was all.

Clara expected more.

She waited.

Nothing came.

She frowned.

"Is that all?"

She put the phone down.

Then she picked it up again.

She typed.

"Are you busy?"

She stared at the message before sending it.

Then she finally pressed send.

Ken replied a few minutes later.

"I'm working."

Clara smiled.

"Driving?"

"Yes."

She wanted to ask more.

But she stopped.

Something was changing.

And Clara was beginning to notice it.

She was beginning to look forward to his messages.

She was beginning to look forward to seeing him.

She was beginning to miss him when he was not around.

And that frightened her.

One evening, Clara invited Ken to a small restaurant.

It was not a date, she told herself.

She just wanted to thank him.

At least that was what she told herself.

Ken arrived on time.

They sat opposite each other.

Clara looked at him.

"You know, I still don't understand you."

Ken smiled.

"What don't you understand?"

"You."

"I'm not that complicated."

"You are."

Ken laughed.

Clara leaned closer.

"You don't behave like most men I know."

"Is that a compliment?"

"I don't know yet."

They both laughed.

Then Clara became serious.

"Ken, can I ask you something?"

"Ask."

"Why do you drive a keke?"

Ken paused.

For the first time since Clara had known him, his smile disappeared.

He looked down at the table.

Then he looked back at her.

"Because I need to."

"That's not an answer."

"It is."

"No, it isn't."

Ken smiled slightly.

"One day I'll tell you."

"One day?"

"Yes."

"When?"

"When I know you trust me enough to hear it."

Clara leaned back.

She did not know what to say.

There was something behind his eyes.

Something she had not seen before.

That night, Clara went home with one question in her mind.

Who exactly was Ken?

The next morning, something happened that would change everything.

Clara was passing through the University of Lagos when she noticed a group of young people gathered outside a building.

She slowed down.

Something caught her attention.

A familiar face.

She looked again.

Her heart stopped.

It was Ken.

But he was not wearing his usual clothes.

He was dressed in a clean shirt and trousers, standing among several young graduates.

One of them suddenly shook his hand.

"Congratulations, Ken!"

Clara's eyes widened.

"Congratulations?"

She watched from inside her car.

Then she saw something that left her completely speechless.

Ken was holding a university certificate.

Clara stared at him.

The man she thought was just a keke driver was standing there as a university graduate.

And suddenly, everything she thought she knew about him began to fall apart.

Why had he never told her?

Why was a graduate driving a keke?

And what was Ken hiding from her?
