🌳 The Village Playground

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌳 Chapter One – The Village Playground

In the village of Umuoma, there was one place every child loved more than anywhere else. It was the wide shade beneath an enormous old tree that stood at the edge of the village.

The tree was so big that many children could sit beneath it without touching one another. Its branches stretched far across the sky, and during the hot afternoons, its thick leaves protected the children from the burning sun.

But the tree was not famous because of its size alone. It was famous because it was the village playground.

Every morning, as soon as the children finished their breakfast, they began thinking about the same thing.

“Are you going to the playground today?”

“Of course!”

“Then I will beat you there!”

Sometimes, a child would hear the sound of other children running past his house and immediately jump up.

“Mama, I am going outside!”

“Outside to do what?”

“To play!”

“Have you finished your chores?”

The child would suddenly become quiet.

“Not yet.”

“Then finish them first.”

But that did not stop some of them from trying to escape.

There were children who would sweep half the compound, hide the broom behind the house and quietly disappear. Others would carry a bucket halfway to the stream, drop it somewhere along the path and run toward the playground.

They all wanted one thing.

They wanted to be the first child under the tree.

As soon as the first child arrived, he would shout,

“I am here! Who is coming next?”

Before long, another child would appear from the narrow path leading from the farms. Then another would come running from the houses near the stream. Soon, children would begin appearing from every direction.

Some came barefoot.

Some came laughing.

Some came carrying sticks, ropes or small pieces of wood they had turned into toys.

The moment they reached the tree, nobody cared where they had come from. They were all part of the same game.

The children had many games, but one rule remained the same.

Everyone had to take turns.

When one child finished, another stepped forward. When one team won, the losing team waited for its turn to challenge them again.

There was always shouting.

There was always laughter.

And there was always someone saying, “That is not fair! Let me try again!”

The children could play for hours without noticing how quickly the sun was moving across the sky.

Sometimes their mothers would call from far away.

“Come home and eat!”

“I am coming!”

But the voice would become weaker as the child continued playing.

Another hour would pass.

Then another.

Some children became so carried away that they completely forgot about food.

Their stomachs would growl, but they would simply say, “After this round.”

Then another round would begin.

The most exciting moment came whenever they decided to choose a winner.

Everyone gathered around the tree and watched closely.

The children cheered for their friends. Some jumped up and down. Others clapped their hands as loudly as they could.

When the final winner was announced, the playground would explode with excitement.

“Champion!”

“Champion!”

“Champion!”

The winner would stand proudly in the middle of the children, smiling from ear to ear.

Sometimes the winner would even be carried on the shoulders of friends while everyone followed behind, laughing and celebrating.

But nobody stayed a winner forever.

The next day, another child might take the crown.

And that was what made the playground special.

Nobody was too important to play with anyone else. The rich played with the poor. The older children played with the younger ones. Boys and girls ran together, laughed together and competed together.

Under that great old tree, the children forgot about everything else.

They forgot about the chores waiting at home.

They forgot about the food waiting on the table.

They forgot about the time.

All they wanted was one more game.

And as the evening sun began to disappear behind the hills, the children would slowly hear their mothers calling their names from different parts of the village.

“Chinedu!”

“Amara!”

“Obinna!”

“Come home!”

The children would look at one another.

“One last game?”

“One last game!”

And under the old tree, the laughter would begin all over again.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🏃 Chapter Two – The Children Who Could Not Wait

The next morning, the village had barely woken up when the children were already thinking about the playground.

Some had not even finished washing their faces.

Some were still eating.

And some were already standing outside their houses, looking toward the big tree.

They knew that another day of games was waiting for them.

Among all the children, there was one boy named Nnamdi.

Nnamdi loved the playground more than anyone.

He was always trying to reach the tree before everyone else.

Every morning, he would wake up and listen carefully.

The moment he heard children running along the village path, he would jump out of bed.

His mother, Mama Ngozi, knew exactly what he was thinking.

“Nnamdi, where are you going?”

“To the playground.”

“Have you swept the compound?”

Nnamdi looked at the broom.

Then he looked toward the road.

Then he looked at his mother.

“I will do it when I come back.”

His mother shook her head.

“You said that yesterday.”

Nnamdi smiled.

“Today will be different.”

Before his mother could answer, he ran away.

“Nnamdi!”

But he was already gone.

He ran through the narrow path with all his strength.

He passed the old well.

He passed the cassava farm.

He passed two boys who were also running toward the playground.

“I will beat you!” Nnamdi shouted.

“You wish!” one of the boys answered.

They all ran faster.

When Nnamdi reached the tree, he stopped and laughed.

“I am first!”

But a small girl was already sitting under the tree.

Nnamdi stared at her.

“How did you get here?”

The girl smiled.

“I woke up early.”

Her name was Adaeze.

She was one of the best runners in the village.

Nnamdi pointed at her.

“You cheated.”

Adaeze laughed.

“I did not cheat. I only ran faster than you.”

Soon, the other children arrived.

The playground became noisy.

Some children wanted to race.

Others wanted to play with the rope.

Some wanted to play a game where two teams would compete against each other.

They argued for a while about which game should come first.

Then an older girl named Ifeoma stood up.

“We will take turns.”

Everyone became quiet.

“First, the runners will race.”

“After that, the rope game.”

“Then the teams will play.”

Nobody complained.

The first race began.

The children stood beside the tree.

Ifeoma counted.

“One!”

“Two!”

“Three!”

They ran.

Their feet hit the dusty ground.

The children under the tree screamed and cheered.

“Run!”

“Faster!”

“Do not stop!”

Adaeze ran like the wind.

Nnamdi was close behind her.

For a moment, it looked like he might pass her.

But Adaeze pushed harder.

She reached the finish line first.

Everyone shouted.

“Adaeze!”

“Adaeze!”

“Adaeze!”

She smiled proudly.

Nnamdi laughed and shook his head.

“Tomorrow I will win.”

Adaeze replied,

“You have been saying that for one month.”

Everyone laughed.

The day continued.

They played one game after another.

Nobody noticed that the sun had moved far across the sky.

Around midday, one of the younger boys complained.

“I am hungry.”

“Go home and eat,” Ifeoma said.

The boy looked at the game.

“I will eat later.”

That became the common answer.

Later.

Later.

Later.

Some children had mothers waiting for them at home.

Some had chores waiting.

But once they reached the playground, it was difficult for them to leave.

There was always another game.

Another race.

Another challenge.

Another chance to become champion.

One afternoon, something happened that nobody expected.

The children were playing near the far side of the tree when they heard a strange sound.

A loud crack came from one of the branches.

Everyone stopped.

The tree moved slightly.

The children looked upward.

A large dry branch was hanging above the place where they normally played.

Nobody moved.

Then the branch fell.

It crashed onto the ground.

Dust filled the air.

The children screamed and ran backward.

For a few seconds, nobody spoke.

Then Nnamdi said,

“What if we were sitting there?”

Adaeze looked at the fallen branch.

“We could have been hurt.”

The children became quiet.

The playground that had always felt safe suddenly looked different.

Ifeoma looked around.

“We should tell the elders.”

Everyone agreed.

That evening, the children went home earlier than usual.

They told their parents what had happened.

The news quickly spread through the village.

The elders came to inspect the tree.

They looked at the fallen branch.

Then they looked at the other branches above the playground.

One of the elders shook his head.

“This tree needs attention.”

The villagers gathered the next morning.

The children stood nearby and watched.

The men cut away the weak branches.

The women cleared the dry leaves.

The children helped by carrying small pieces of wood away from the playground.

When the work was finished, the playground looked safer than before.

The children cheered.

But the elders gave them a warning.

“Never play beneath a weak branch.”

The children promised.

From that day, they learned something new.

The playground was their happy place.

But they also had to protect it.

They began checking the tree before they played.

They removed dangerous sticks.

They kept the ground clean.

And whenever they saw something unsafe, they told an adult.

Still, their love for the playground did not change.

Every morning, they returned.

They still raced.

They still laughed.

They still celebrated their winners.

And they still argued about who would win the next game.

But now they understood that the place they loved needed their care too.
