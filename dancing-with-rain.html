🌧 Dancing with Rain

📚 African Folktales • 🕒 25 min read

🌧 Chapter One – When the Rain Came Singing

Rain had not fallen in Kumbira for three moons.

The river had become a thin brown line.

The farms were hard.

The grass had turned yellow.

Even the old mango trees had stopped giving shade.

Then, one evening, the drums began.

No one was playing them.

They were coming from the forest.

Slow beats.

One after another.

Dum.

Dum.

Dum.

People came out of their huts.

Children stood beside their mothers.

Old men looked toward the dark trees.

Nobody spoke.

Then the first drop of rain touched the dry ground.

Another fell.

Then another.

A strong wind moved through the village.

And from inside the forest came a sound that made everyone stop breathing.

Someone was singing.

It was a woman's voice.

Soft.

Slow.

But the song had no words anyone knew.

Only a tune.

A strange old tune.

Kato heard it from his mother's doorway.

He was twenty-one.

Tall.

Quiet.

He had lived in Kumbira all his life.

He had heard every village story.

He had laughed at most of them.

But he had never heard that song.

His younger sister, Amara, came running toward him.

"Kato."

He turned.

"What?"

She pointed toward the forest.

"Someone is dancing."

Kato looked.

At first, he saw nothing.

Then lightning crossed the sky.

For one short moment, a figure appeared between the trees.

A man.

Barefoot.

Wearing a dark cloth around his waist.

His arms were raised.

He was dancing in the rain.

But the strange part was not the dance.

It was the rain around him.

The drops seemed to move with his feet.

When he stepped left, the rain followed.

When he turned, the rain turned.

When he stopped, every drop around him seemed to hang in the air.

Amara grabbed Kato's arm.

"Did you see that?"

Kato nodded.

"Stay here."

"Where are you going?"

"To find out who that is."

Amara pulled him back.

"Grandmother said never to follow strange music into the forest."

Kato smiled.

"Grandmother says many things."

Amara did not smile.

"She also said people who hear that song do not always come back."

Kato stopped.

He looked at the forest again.

The singing had stopped.

The drums had stopped too.

Only rain remained.

Kato walked back inside.

His mother, Sade, was sitting near the fire.

She had heard everything.

"You heard it."

Kato sat opposite her.

"What song was that?"

His mother looked at the fire.

"Forget it."

Kato frowned.

"Who was dancing?"

Sade lifted her eyes.

"Forget that too."

Amara entered the hut.

"Mother, you know something."

Sade stood.

She walked to the old wooden box beneath her bed.

She pulled it out.

For a moment, she did not open it.

Then she removed a small black cloth.

Inside was a broken wooden mask.

Kato stared at it.

The mask had long narrow eyes.

A wide mouth.

And a small mark carved above the forehead.

The same mark was drawn on the man's chest.

Kato stood.

"Where did you get this?"

Sade's hands began to shake.

"Your father."

Kato went quiet.

His father had disappeared when Kato was nine.

No body was found.

No grave was made.

The village had always said the forest took him.

Kato never believed it.

Not fully.

He touched the mask.

It was cold.

Very cold.

Then something scratched the roof.

Everyone looked up.

Scratch.

Scratch.

Scratch.

Sade quickly covered the mask.

"Do not touch it again."

Kato looked at her.

"Why?"

She whispered.

"Because it remembers."

The scratching stopped.

A moment later, something tapped the window.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Kato moved toward it.

His mother shouted.

"Kato, don't!"

He pulled the curtain aside.

Nobody was there.

Only rain.

He opened the window.

A wet footprint sat on the wooden floor outside.

Then another appeared.

Then another.

They moved toward the forest.

Kato stared.

The footprints were fresh.

And they were barefoot.

Amara whispered behind him.

"Kato."

He turned.

The wooden box was open.

The black cloth lay on the floor.

The mask was gone.

Sade stood frozen beside the bed.

Then the drums started again.

This time they were much closer.

Dum.

Dum.

Dum.

Kato looked outside.

At the edge of the forest stood the dancer.

He was facing the village.

Rain ran down his face.

He slowly raised one hand.

In it was the broken mask.

Kato's heart stopped.

The dancer placed the mask over his face.

Then he pointed directly at Kato.

The drums stopped.

The dancer disappeared.

But his footprints remained.

They were no longer leading toward the forest.

They were leading straight toward Kato's hut.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🥁 Chapter Two – Footprints at the Door

Kato did not move.

He kept staring at the wet footprints.

One after another, they crossed the floor.

They stopped beside his bed.

Sade quickly closed the window.

"Nobody leaves this hut tonight."

Kato looked at her.

"Mother, what is happening?"

She did not answer.

Amara picked up a small stick from beside the fire.

"Should I wake Grandmother?"

Sade shook her head.

"She already knows."

A loud clap of thunder shook the hut.

The fire jumped.

Then it went out.

Darkness filled the room.

Kato heard breathing.

Not his.

Not Amara's.

Not his mother's.

Someone was breathing near the door.

Kato reached for the lamp.

His fingers found the handle.

He struck the flint.

Nothing.

He struck it again.

A tiny flame appeared.

The door was open.

Nobody had touched it.

Rain blew across the floor.

The footprints were gone.

Kato stepped closer.

A low sound came from outside.

A drum.

Just one beat.

Dum.

Then silence.

Kato looked at his mother.

"I need to know who he is."

Sade grabbed his wrist.

"You will not go outside."

"Why?"

She looked toward the open door.

"Because your father made that mistake."

Kato froze.

"What did Father do?"

Sade's eyes filled with fear.

"He followed the rain."

A soft knock came from the wall behind them.

Everyone turned.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

The sound came from inside the wall.

Amara stepped closer to Kato.

"Someone is inside."

Sade whispered.

"No."

Another knock came.

This time, a voice followed.

"Kato."

Kato's blood went cold.

He knew that voice.

It was older.

Weak.

But he knew it.

His father's voice.

"Father?"

Sade covered her mouth.

The voice came again.

"Kato."

He walked toward the wall.

Sade tried to stop him.

He pulled away.

His palm touched the mud wall.

It was wet.

Water began running down it.

Kato moved his hand away.

A small crack had appeared.

The crack grew.

It spread across the wall like a thin black snake.

Amara screamed.

The wall opened.

Behind it was a narrow passage.

Kato stared into the darkness.

There were steps.

They went down.

Deep beneath the hut.

Sade began crying.

"I sealed that place years ago."

Kato looked at her.

"What place?"

She shook her head.

"Your grandfather called it the rain path."

The voice came from below.

"Kato."

He stepped onto the first stone.

A cold wind rose from the passage.

He heard water.

Then singing.

The same song from the forest.

Kato turned back.

Amara was holding the lamp.

Sade stood behind her.

His mother looked at him.

"If you go down there, you may not come back the same."

Kato looked into the dark.

"Maybe I was never the same after Father disappeared."

He took another step.

The stone moved beneath his foot.

A hidden bell rang below.

Once.

The rain outside suddenly became heavier.

Kato kept going.

The passage became narrower.

Roots hung from the ceiling.

Water ran along the walls.

He saw marks carved into the stones.

Names.

Dozens of them.

Some were old.

Some were fresh.

Then he saw his father's name.

Kato stopped.

The name was followed by a date.

Then another line.

Kato moved the lamp closer.

The second line had been scratched recently.

It said:

"Returned with the rain."

Kato heard footsteps behind him.

He turned quickly.

Amara stood there.

"I told you to stay home."

She lifted the lamp.

"You thought I would let you go alone?"

Kato almost smiled.

Then the lamp went out.

Amara grabbed his hand.

Something moved above them.

A wet body crawled across the ceiling.

Kato pulled Amara against the wall.

They stayed silent.

The thing passed over them.

Its long fingers touched the stones.

Then it stopped.

Kato heard it sniff.

Amara held her breath.

The thing moved again.

Its shadow disappeared into the dark.

Kato whispered.

"Run."

They ran.

Their feet struck the wet stones.

The passage twisted.

Kato slipped.

Amara pulled him up.

Behind them came a heavy scrape.

Something was following.

They reached a wooden door.

Kato pushed it.

It opened.

Beyond it was a huge underground room.

Water covered the floor.

Old drums stood around the walls.

Hundreds of them.

All different sizes.

None had anyone playing them.

Yet every drum began beating.

Dum.

Dum.

Dum.

Amara looked at Kato.

"Why are they beating?"

Kato looked across the room.

A man stood beside the largest drum.

His back was turned.

Rainwater ran from his shoulders.

Kato stepped forward.

The man slowly turned.

He was older.

His face was thin.

His hair was white.

But Kato knew him.

He dropped the lamp.

"Father."

The man stared at him.

His lips moved.

But no sound came.

Then he pointed behind Kato.

Kato turned.

The doorway was gone.

And standing where the door had been was the masked dancer.

He slowly lifted both hands.

Every drum stopped.

Then the dancer whispered one sentence.

"Your father is not the one you came to save."

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌿 Chapter Three – Drums Beneath Kumbira

Kato could not answer.

He stood in the middle of the underground room.

His father was only a few steps away.

The masked dancer stood behind him.

Amara held Kato's arm so tightly that her fingers hurt.

The drums were silent.

Not one of them moved.

Then Kato's father lifted his hand.

He pointed toward the water.

Kato looked down.

The water was moving.

Small circles were spreading across its surface.

One.

Two.

Three.

Then something broke through.

A wooden staff rose from the water.

It stood upright by itself.

Carved into its top was a bird with open wings.

Kato had seen that bird before.

It was carved above the entrance of his grandfather's old house.

His father walked toward the staff.

The masked dancer did not stop him.

Kato followed.

"Father."

The man turned.

He looked at Kato for a long moment.

Then he smiled.

Kato had not seen that smile since he was a child.

His father's mouth moved.

This time, a voice came out.

"You should not have come."

Kato swallowed.

"I searched for you."

His father looked at Amara.

"You brought her too."

"She followed me."

Amara stepped forward.

"I am not leaving."

For the first time, Kato's father laughed.

It was a tired laugh.

"You have your mother's courage."

Kato looked at him.

"What happened to you?"

His father touched the carved staff.

"I came here looking for rain."

Kato frowned.

"Rain?"

The man nodded.

"Kumbira was dying."

He looked at the ceiling.

"The river was shrinking. The farms were failing. People were leaving."

His fingers tightened around the staff.

"Your grandfather told me about this place."

Kato stepped closer.

"What did he tell you?"

His father looked at the masked dancer.

The dancer lowered his head.

"He told me that Kumbira had a bargain with the rain."

Amara stared at him.

"A bargain?"

The man nodded.

"Long ago, our people promised something to the forest."

Kato looked at the drums.

"What did they promise?"

Nobody answered.

The largest drum began to move.

Its skin tightened.

A deep sound rolled through the room.

Dum.

The water trembled.

Dum.

The walls shook.

Dum.

A crack appeared across the floor.

Kato pulled Amara back.

The crack opened.

Warm air rose from below.

Then came the smell of wet earth.

His father whispered.

"It has woken."

Kato looked at him.

"What has woken?"

The answer came from the darkness beneath the floor.

"Rain."

Water burst through the crack.

It hit the ceiling.

It fell across the room.

Within seconds, the water reached Kato's ankles.

Amara screamed.

"Kato!"

He grabbed her hand.

The masked dancer stood completely still.

Rain fell around him.

But none of it touched his body.

Kato's father walked toward him.

"Give me the mask."

The dancer shook his head.

"You know what happens if you wear it again."

Kato stared at them.

"Again?"

His father looked away.

Kato stepped closer.

"You wore that mask?"

Silence.

Then his father nodded.

"Once."

"When?"

"The night I disappeared."

Kato felt his chest tighten.

"Why?"

His father looked directly at him.

"Because someone had to dance."

A sudden wind rushed through the room.

The drums began beating again.

Faster this time.

Dum.

Dum.

Dum.

The masked dancer started moving.

One step.

Another.

Then a turn.

The rain followed him.

Kato remembered what he had seen from the village.

The strange movement.

The rain moving with the dancer.

Now he understood.

The dancer was not controlling the rain.

He was calling it.

The dancer stamped his foot.

The whole room shook.

Water rose to Kato's knees.

Amara pulled him toward the steps.

"We have to leave."

Kato looked at his father.

"Come with us."

His father shook his head.

"I cannot."

"Why?"

The man pointed toward the masked dancer.

"Because he is waiting for me."

The dancer stopped.

He removed the mask.

Kato expected to see the strange face again.

Instead, he saw a young man.

He looked no older than Kato.

His eyes were bright.

His skin was covered with tiny marks.

Kato stared.

"Who are you?"

The young man smiled.

"Your father."

Kato looked at the older man.

Then at the younger one.

His mind refused to understand.

The older man stepped back.

The young man stepped forward.

They looked exactly alike.

Amara whispered.

"Kato..."

The young man spoke.

"Your father never left the forest."

Kato shook his head.

"Then who is standing here?"

The young man pointed at the older man.

"His memory."

The older man slowly disappeared.

His body became rain.

The water dropped into the flooded room.

Kato shouted.

"Father!"

Nothing answered.

Only the drums.

Dum.

Dum.

Dum.

The young man placed the mask on the floor.

Then he pointed toward the far wall.

A door appeared.

It was made from black wood.

There was no handle.

Only a carved symbol.

The same bird with open wings.

Kato walked toward it.

Amara followed.

The young man stayed behind.

Kato touched the symbol.

The door opened.

Beyond it was not another room.

It was a village.

But not Kumbira as it was now.

It was Kumbira from many years ago.

People were dancing.

Children were running.

The river was full.

The trees were green.

And in the middle of the village stood a woman holding a baby.

Kato stared at her.

He knew that face.

It was his mother.

But she was much younger.

The baby in her arms opened its eyes.

Kato took a step closer.

The baby looked directly at him.

Then it smiled.

Amara grabbed Kato's shoulder.

"That baby..."

Kato whispered.

"It's me."

His younger mother suddenly turned toward them.

Her face changed.

Fear entered her eyes.

She pointed straight at Kato.

Then she shouted something.

Kato could not hear her.

The drums drowned out every sound.

Then she dropped the baby.

Kato screamed.

But the baby did not fall.

A pair of dark hands caught it.

The hands belonged to someone standing behind his mother.

Kato could not see the person's face.

Only a long shadow.

The shadow slowly turned toward him.

And when it lifted its head, Kato saw his own face staring back.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🌩 Chapter Four – Face Beneath the Rain

Kato forgot how to breathe.

The face inside the dark shadow was his.

Not someone who looked like him.

His own face.

Same eyes.

Same mouth.

Same small mark above the left eyebrow.

The shadow held the baby close.

The baby was still Kato.

The younger Sade stood frozen beside the old village well.

People around her kept dancing.

Nobody seemed to see the strange figure.

Nobody saw Kato and Amara either.

It was like they were watching a memory that could not see them.

Amara pulled Kato backward.

"Come away."

Kato did not move.

The shadow turned its head.

Its eyes met his.

Then it smiled.

Kato stepped forward.

"Who are you?"

The shadow did not answer.

It placed one finger against the baby's forehead.

The little boy laughed.

Then the whole village stopped.

Every dancer.

Every child.

Every bird.

Even the smoke above the cooking fires froze in the air.

A drum sounded from somewhere far away.

Dum.

The shadow raised one hand.

Rain began falling inside the old village.

Only around Kato.

Amara reached for him.

Her hand passed through the rain.

She could not touch him.

Kato looked down.

The ground beneath his feet had become soft mud.

His feet began sinking.

"Amara!"

She pulled harder.

Nothing happened.

Kato sank to his ankles.

Then his knees.

The shadow watched quietly.

Kato grabbed the edge of the black doorway.

His fingers slipped.

The old village started fading.

The younger Sade disappeared first.

Then the dancers.

Then the trees.

Only the shadow remained.

It leaned closer.

"You were not born in Kumbira."

Kato stared at it.

"What?"

The shadow smiled again.

"You were brought here."

The black doorway slammed shut.

Kato fell backward.

He landed in the underground room.

Amara fell beside him.

The water was gone.

Every drum was dry.

The young man who had called himself Kato's father was gone too.

Only the mask remained.

It lay in the middle of the floor.

Kato crawled toward it.

Amara grabbed his shoulder.

"Don't touch it."

Kato stopped.

The mask moved by itself.

It turned slowly.

Its empty eyes faced Kato.

Then it rolled across the floor.

It stopped beside an old stone.

Something was carved into the stone.

A name.

Kato moved closer.

His heart began beating faster.

The name was not his father's.

It was his mother's.

SADE.

Under her name was another line.

Kato wiped the dirt away.

He read it slowly.

"Keeper of the first child."

Amara stared at the words.

"What does that mean?"

Kato shook his head.

"I don't know."

A small sound came from behind them.

Footsteps.

Slow footsteps.

Coming down the passage.

Kato stood.

Amara picked up a broken piece of wood.

A figure appeared at the end of the passage.

It was an old woman.

She carried a basket.

Her clothes were soaked.

Her hair was white.

Kato knew her.

Grandmother.

She walked toward them without fear.

"You opened it."

Kato frowned.

"Opened what?"

She pointed at the black doorway.

"Your first memory."

Kato felt anger rise inside him.

"Why did everyone lie to me?"

Grandmother placed her basket down.

"Because some truths are like fire."

She looked at Amara.

"They burn the person who carries them."

Kato stepped closer.

"Who was that shadow?"

Grandmother's face changed.

For the first time, she looked afraid.

"Do not call him by his name."

"Why?"

She looked at the drums.

"Because names give old things a road back."

A drum beat once.

Dum.

Grandmother closed her eyes.

Another drum answered.

Dum.

Then another.

Dum.

The sound moved around the room.

Left.

Right.

Behind them.

Above them.

Grandmother opened her basket.

Inside were small pieces of cloth.

Each one had a name stitched into it.

Kato saw his mother's name.

His father's.

His grandfather's.

Then he saw his own.

He reached for it.

Grandmother caught his hand.

"Not yet."

Kato looked at her.

"What happens if I take it?"

She slowly released his wrist.

"You remember everything."

Kato reached into the basket.

His fingers touched the cloth.

The moment he lifted it, the room vanished.

He was standing in the rain.

But he was a child again.

Nine years old.

His father stood before him.

His mother stood behind him.

Both were crying.

Kato looked around.

They were at the forest edge.

His father held the same mask.

His mother held a baby.

But the baby was not Kato.

It was Amara.

Kato looked at his younger self.

The boy was holding another baby.

A baby nobody had told him about.

His father placed the mask over the boy's face.

His mother whispered.

"Forgive us."

The young Kato looked toward the forest.

A figure stood between the trees.

The same shadow.

The same face.

But this time Kato could hear what it said.

"Give me one child."

His father stepped forward.

His mother held Amara tighter.

Then the younger Kato turned around.

He looked directly at the older Kato watching the memory.

His eyes widened.

He whispered something.

Kato heard it clearly.

"Don't let them give me away."

The memory broke.

Kato opened his eyes.

He was back underground.

Amara was crying beside him.

Grandmother was kneeling near the wall.

The drums had stopped.

Kato looked at the cloth in his hand.

A drop of fresh rain fell onto it.

Then another.

Grandmother whispered.

"Now you know why the rain came for you."

Kato slowly looked toward the passage.

Someone was standing there.

A small boy.

Barefoot.

Soaked from head to toe.

He looked exactly like Kato at nine years old.

The boy raised one hand.

He was holding a small drum.

He smiled.

"Come home."

🌙 End of Chapter Four

🌧 Chapter Five – Child Beneath the Tree

Kato stared at the boy.

The boy stared back.

Neither of them moved.

Rainwater dripped from the boy's hair.

His small hands held the drum against his chest.

Kato knew that face.

He had seen it in the memory.

Nine years old.

The same eyes.

The same scar near the chin.

Even the same small gap between the front teeth.

Amara moved behind Kato.

"Who is that?"

Grandmother did not answer.

She was staring at the boy.

Her face had gone pale.

"Grandmother?"

She finally spoke.

"That is the child we left behind."

Kato felt cold.

"Left behind where?"

The boy raised the drum.

He struck it once.

Dum.

The walls shook.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

The old woman grabbed Kato's arm.

"Do not answer him."

The boy smiled.

"Kato."

Kato's mouth opened.

Grandmother tightened her grip.

"Don't."

The boy lowered the drum.

His smile disappeared.

"You promised."

Kato looked at Grandmother.

"What did I promise?"

She looked away.

The boy took one step forward.

Water spread beneath his feet.

The dry stones became wet.

A small stream began moving toward Kato.

Amara stepped back.

"That water is coming from him."

Kato watched it.

The stream stopped at his feet.

Then the water formed a circle around him.

Inside the circle, words appeared.

"ONE CHILD MUST RETURN."

Kato looked at Grandmother.

"What does that mean?"

She slowly released his arm.

"It means the old bargain is not finished."

Kato stepped out of the circle.

"Then finish it."

Grandmother shook her head.

"It does not work that way."

Amara stepped beside Kato.

"Then tell us the truth."

Grandmother looked at both of them.

She took a slow breath.

"Many years ago, Kumbira had a great drought."

The drums stayed quiet.

"Your ancestors went deep into the forest. They found a place where rainwater came from beneath the ground."

Kato listened.

"They begged for water."

Grandmother looked at the boy.

"The forest gave it."

She paused.

"But it asked for a promise."

"What promise?"

Her eyes moved to Kato.

"Every generation would give one child to guard the rain path."

Amara shook her head.

"That is not a promise. That is giving children away."

Grandmother looked down.

"Yes."

Kato felt anger rise in him.

"Did you give my father?"

Grandmother closed her eyes.

"No."

Kato waited.

The silence became heavy.

"Then who?"

Grandmother whispered.

"You."

Kato stepped backward.

Amara grabbed his hand.

"But he was nine."

Grandmother nodded.

"Your father refused to give you."

Kato stared at the old woman.

"So he took your place?"

She nodded again.

"He wore the mask."

The boy lifted his drum.

Dum.

The room changed.

The stone walls faded.

Kato was standing in a forest.

Rain fell hard around him.

His father was young again.

He stood beside a large tree.

The mask covered his face.

Kato's younger self was hiding behind a rock.

His father turned toward him.

"Run home."

Young Kato shook his head.

"I won't leave you."

His father walked toward him.

He knelt.

He placed both hands on Kato's shoulders.

"One day, you will understand."

Young Kato cried.

"I don't want to understand."

His father smiled.

"Good."

A strange sound came from the trees.

A long wooden creak.

His father stood.

A dark shape moved between the branches.

Young Kato ran.

His father stayed.

The memory faded.

Kato opened his eyes.

He was back underground.

Amara was beside him.

The boy was gone.

Grandmother stood near the black doorway.

Something was different.

The doorway was now open.

Beyond it stood a huge tree.

Its roots covered the ground.

Rain fell from its branches.

Kato walked toward it.

Amara followed.

They entered.

The moment Kato stepped through, the doorway disappeared.

He turned.

Nothing was there.

Only trees.

Tall trees.

Wet trees.

Their branches moved even though there was no wind.

A path appeared ahead.

Small wet footprints marked it.

The boy's footprints.

Kato followed them.

The path went deeper into the forest.

They passed an old stone hut.

Then a broken bridge.

Then a tree covered with hundreds of small white cloths.

Each cloth carried a name.

Kato stopped.

He saw his father's name.

Then his grandfather's.

Then another name.

His mother's.

Kato touched it.

The cloth became warm.

A voice came from behind the tree.

"She never wanted you to know."

Kato turned.

The masked dancer stood there.

But the mask was in his hand.

His face was now clear.

He looked young.

Too young.

Kato stared at him.

"Are you the boy from the room?"

The dancer nodded.

"Then why do you look like me?"

The dancer walked closer.

"Because you were made from the same memory."

Kato frowned.

"What does that mean?"

The dancer raised the mask.

"This is not a mask."

He turned it around.

There was a small mirror inside.

Kato looked into it.

He did not see his own face.

He saw the old forest.

He saw his father.

He saw his mother.

And he saw himself as a baby.

Then the mirror showed something else.

A woman walking away from the village.

She carried a baby.

Kato leaned closer.

The woman turned.

It was Sade.

But she was carrying a child Kato had never seen.

The baby had a small white mark across its forehead.

The dancer whispered.

"Your brother."

Kato's eyes widened.

Amara stared at him.

"Kato..."

The mirror cracked.

A deep bell rang somewhere in the forest.

Once.

Then the boy's voice came from behind them.

"He is still alive."

Kato turned.

The boy stood at the end of the path.

He raised his little drum.

And behind him, something much larger moved between the trees.

Kato could only see two bright eyes.

Then the boy smiled.

"Your brother has been waiting for you."

🌙 End of Chapter Five

🌲 Chapter Six – Child Beyond the Trees

Kato stood still.

The bright eyes were still between the trees.

Amara held his arm.

"Something is there."

Kato nodded.

He could hear it breathing.

Slow.

Heavy.

The young dancer stepped between Kato and the trees.

"Do not be afraid."

Kato looked at him.

"You said my brother is alive."

The dancer nodded.

"He has been here all this time."

Kato moved forward.

The bright eyes moved too.

Something stepped out from behind the tree.

It was not an animal.

It was a boy.

He looked about twelve years old.

His clothes were made from old pieces of cloth.

His feet were bare.

Rainwater ran down his face.

But he did not seem cold.

He looked at Kato.

Then he smiled.

"You took long."

Kato could not speak.

The boy walked closer.

He had the same eyes as Kato.

The same small mark above the eyebrow.

Kato slowly reached for him.

The boy did not move away.

Their hands touched.

Warm.

Real.

Kato pulled him into a hug.

The boy held him tightly.

Amara watched them.

"What's your name?"

The boy looked at her.

"Jalen."

Kato stepped back.

"Mother gave you that name?"

Jalen shook his head.

"Father did."

Kato's face changed.

"Father is here?"

Jalen pointed toward the trees.

"Sometimes."

Kato looked around.

"Where?"

Jalen smiled.

"He does not like being seen."

A cold wind passed through the forest.

The young dancer lifted his head.

"We have to move."

Kato looked at him.

"Why?"

The dancer pointed toward the sky.

The clouds had turned black.

Lightning flashed without thunder.

Jalen grabbed Kato's hand.

"Come."

They ran.

Amara followed.

The trees became thicker.

Branches scratched their arms.

Mud covered their feet.

Behind them came a sound.

A drum.

Dum.

Kato looked back.

Nobody was there.

Dum.

The sound came closer.

Jalen pulled harder.

"Do not look back."

Kato kept running.

They reached a narrow stream.

Jalen jumped across it.

Amara followed.

Kato jumped.

His foot slipped.

He fell into the water.

The stream was deeper than it looked.

He sank.

Amara grabbed his hand.

Jalen pulled from the other side.

Together, they dragged Kato out.

He coughed.

Then he heard a voice.

"Kato."

He froze.

It was his father.

The voice came from across the stream.

Kato stood.

"Father!"

A figure stood beneath a large tree.

Older now.

Weak.

But real.

Kato stepped toward him.

Jalen held his arm.

"Wait."

Kato looked at him.

"That's my father."

Jalen shook his head.

"Not yet."

The figure under the tree raised his face.

His eyes were completely black.

Kato stopped.

The figure smiled.

"Come closer."

Jalen stepped in front of Kato.

"That's not him."

The figure's smile disappeared.

The forest became silent.

Then the figure's body changed.

Its arms became longer.

Its shoulders bent.

Its face became dark.

Amara screamed.

Jalen pulled Kato away.

They ran again.

Behind them came a terrible cry.

It was not a human voice.

The trees shook.

Birds flew from the branches.

Kato looked back for one second.

The creature was running after them.

It moved on all fours.

Too fast.

Too close.

Jalen pushed open a wooden gate.

They rushed inside.

The gate slammed shut.

The creature hit it.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

The gate shook.

Jalen pressed both hands against it.

"Do not let it hear your name."

Kato whispered.

"Why?"

Jalen looked at him.

"Because it collects names."

The creature stopped.

Silence returned.

Jalen slowly removed his hands.

Kato looked around.

They were inside a small clearing.

At its center stood an old hut.

A lantern burned beside the door.

Smoke rose from the roof.

Someone was inside.

Jalen walked toward it.

Kato followed.

The door opened before they reached it.

An old man stood there.

He had a long white beard.

His eyes were covered with a strip of cloth.

"Come in."

Jalen entered.

Amara followed.

Kato stayed at the door.

"Who are you?"

The old man smiled.

"I am the last person who remembers your father."

Kato stepped inside.

The door closed.

The old man reached for a small clay bowl.

Inside was clear water.

He pushed it toward Kato.

"Look."

Kato leaned over the bowl.

The water moved.

A picture appeared.

His father was standing beside a river.

He was holding the same white cloth from the tree.

Then another person appeared beside him.

A woman.

Kato knew her.

His mother.

But she was holding Jalen as a baby.

Kato looked at Jalen.

Then back at the bowl.

The old man spoke.

"Your mother brought your brother here herself."

Kato's eyes widened.

"Why would she do that?"

The old man touched the water.

The picture changed.

Sade was crying.

She placed baby Jalen beside the river.

Then she turned away.

But before she left, she looked back.

Someone stood behind the baby.

A tall figure wearing the white mask.

Kato stared at the bowl.

The old man whispered.

"Your mother did not give your brother to the forest."

Kato looked up.

"Then what did she do?"

The old man smiled sadly.

"She gave him to someone she trusted."

The lantern suddenly went out.

A loud drum sounded outside.

Dum.

Jalen stood quickly.

His face had changed.

"He's here."

Kato looked toward the door.

A shadow appeared beneath it.

Two bare feet stopped outside.

Then a man's voice came through the wood.

"Jalen."

Jalen began shaking.

The voice came again.

"Kato."

Kato looked at Jalen.

The boy whispered.

"Do not answer."

The door slowly opened.

A hand appeared around the edge.

The hand held a small brass bell.

The bell rang once.

And every drop of rain outside stopped in the air.

Then a familiar voice spoke from the darkness.

"Son, it is time to come home."

🌙 End of Chapter Six

🌊 Chapter Seven – Voice at the Door

Nobody moved.

The door stood half open.

Rain hung in the air outside.

Every drop had stopped falling.

The brass bell in the stranger's hand was still.

Kato stared at the dark space beyond the door.

He knew that voice.

He had heard it in his dreams.

He had heard it in the underground room.

He had heard it when he was a child.

His father's voice.

Jalen stepped backward.

His face had lost its color.

"Don't answer him."

Kato looked at him.

"But he called me."

"That is how he gets inside."

The old man with the covered eyes stood up.

He moved toward the door.

He did not look afraid.

He simply raised one hand.

The door closed.

The brass bell outside rang.

Once.

Then silence.

Kato waited.

Nothing happened.

Jalen sat on the floor.

He looked tired.

"Who is outside?"

Jalen rubbed his hands together.

"Someone who has worn many faces."

Amara sat beside him.

"And Kato's father?"

Jalen looked at her.

"Your father is still somewhere in the rain path."

Kato stepped closer.

"Then the man outside is not my father."

"No."

"Then who is he?"

Jalen looked toward the closed door.

"Something that learned his voice."

A soft knock came.

Everyone froze.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

The old man walked to a small shelf.

He took down a clay jar.

Inside were dried leaves.

He crushed them between his fingers.

Then he threw them into the fire.

Blue smoke filled the hut.

Kato coughed.

Amara covered her mouth.

Jalen closed his eyes.

The knocking stopped.

The old man spoke.

"It cannot cross the door while the old rain is burning."

Kato looked at the fire.

"How long will it last?"

The old man shook his head.

"Not long."

Kato looked at Jalen.

"Tell me everything."

Jalen stayed quiet.

Kato waited.

Finally, the boy spoke.

"When Mother brought me here, I was a baby."

Kato sat down.

Jalen continued.

"She told me I had to stay hidden."

"From who?"

Jalen looked at the old man.

The old man nodded.

"From the rain keeper."

Kato frowned.

"The dancer?"

Jalen shook his head.

"Not him."

He pointed toward the door.

"Him."

A heavy sound struck the roof.

The hut shook.

Dust fell from the ceiling.

The blue fire grew smaller.

Kato stood.

The old man grabbed his shoulder.

"Stay inside."

Kato pulled away.

"My father is outside somewhere."

"Not outside."

The old man's voice became low.

"Below."

Kato looked at the floor.

The old man pointed toward a wooden board.

Kato lifted it.

A narrow hole appeared.

Cold air rose from below.

He heard water.

Then a voice.

"Kato."

He knew it was real this time.

His father's voice sounded weak.

"Please."

Kato looked at Jalen.

"Is that him?"

Jalen nodded.

"That is him."

Kato climbed into the hole.

Amara followed.

Jalen came after them.

The old man stayed above.

The wooden board closed behind them.

Darkness covered the passage.

Kato moved slowly.

His hands touched wet stone.

The passage went down.

Far below, water rushed.

Then he saw a small light.

A lantern.

Someone was holding it.

Kato moved closer.

A man sat beside an underground river.

His back was against a tree root.

His clothes were torn.

His hair was gray.

But his face was clear.

It was his father.

Kato stopped.

"Father."

The man lifted his head.

He smiled.

"My son."

Kato rushed forward.

He dropped to his knees.

His father held his face.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Kato finally hugged him.

His father held him tightly.

"I knew you would come."

Kato pulled away.

"Why did you leave us?"

His father looked at the river.

"I did not leave."

"Then why did you never come home?"

His father looked at Jalen.

"Because I was protecting him."

Jalen stepped closer.

His father reached for him.

Jalen took his hand.

Kato watched them.

Something inside him became quiet.

His father looked at both sons.

"I made a mistake."

Kato frowned.

"What mistake?"

His father looked toward the river.

"I thought I could break the bargain."

The river became louder.

"I thought hiding Jalen would end it."

He shook his head.

"It only made the rain path angry."

Amara stepped closer.

"How do we end it?"

His father looked at her.

He smiled.

"You have already started."

Amara looked confused.

"How?"

He pointed at Kato.

"He came back for his family."

Kato looked at the river.

Something was moving inside it.

A small wooden boat came floating toward them.

There was no person inside.

Only a brass bell.

Kato picked it up.

A name was carved into it.

SADE.

His mother.

Kato stared at the name.

"Why is Mother's name here?"

His father became silent.

Jalen looked away.

Amara noticed.

"You know something."

Kato's father slowly nodded.

"Your mother was not the first person to make the bargain."

Kato waited.

"And she was not the last."

The river suddenly stopped.

Not slowed.

Stopped.

Every drop became still.

Then a voice came from the water.

"Sade."

Kato looked around.

The voice came again.

"Sade."

His father stood quickly.

His face filled with fear.

"She has come."

Kato looked at the river.

A woman slowly rose from the water.

Her dress was white.

Her hair floated around her face.

Kato took one step backward.

Amara grabbed his hand.

Jalen whispered.

"Mother."

The woman opened her eyes.

She looked at Kato.

Then at Jalen.

Tears ran down her face.

"My children."

Kato could not speak.

His father moved toward her.

She raised one hand.

He stopped.

The woman looked at Kato.

"You must not give either of them to the rain."

Kato stared at her.

"Then what does it want?"

His mother looked behind him.

Kato turned.

The passage was gone.

The old underground river had opened into a huge dark chamber.

Hundreds of white cloths hung from the ceiling.

Each cloth carried a name.

At the center stood the masked dancer.

He was holding the final brass bell.

His eyes were fixed on Kato.

He raised the bell.

Then he spoke.

"It does not want a child anymore."

Kato's heart began to race.

The dancer smiled.

"It wants you to remember who started the bargain."

The bell rang.

The whole chamber filled with rain.

And every white cloth turned at once.

Kato looked up.

One name was written across all of them.

His own.
