🍲 Rosemary the Best Cook

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 35 min read

🍲 Chapter One – The Little Girl Who Could Cook

In a small African town called Umudike, there lived a little girl named Rosemary.

Rosemary was only six years old.

She was a quiet child, but she loved staying close to the cooking fire.

While other children were outside playing, Rosemary would sit beside her mother, watching every little thing she did.

She watched how her mother washed the vegetables.

She watched how she measured the spices.

She watched how she knew when the fire was too hot.

One afternoon, Rosemary was sitting beside her mother, Mama Grace, while she prepared food for the family.

"Mother, can I cook today?"

Mama Grace laughed.

"You?"

Rosemary nodded.

"Yes, me."

Her mother looked at her small hands.

"Rosemary, you are only six years old."

Rosemary smiled.

"I know."

"But I can do it."

Mama Grace did not want to discourage her.

So she gave her a small pot and a few vegetables.

"Alright then. Show me what you can do."

Rosemary sat close to the fire.

She arranged the wood carefully.

She placed the pot on top.

Then she started cooking.

Her mother watched quietly.

At first, Mama Grace thought Rosemary was only playing.

But after a few minutes, something strange happened.

The smell coming from the pot filled the whole compound.

Mama Grace stopped what she was doing.

She turned around.

"Rosemary, what did you put inside that pot?"

Rosemary looked at her mother.

"The same things you always use."

Her mother walked closer.

She tasted the food.

She became quiet.

Rosemary looked at her.

"Is it bad?"

Mama Grace shook her head.

"No."

She tasted it again.

"It is very good."

Rosemary smiled.

"So I can cook?"

Her mother laughed.

"You can cook."

From that day, Rosemary kept returning to the fire.

Every time she cooked, the food tasted better than the last one.

Her mother began asking questions.

"How did you know when to add this?"

Rosemary would simply shrug.

"I just knew."

Mama Grace could not understand it.

She had spent many years learning how to cook.

Yet her six-year-old daughter was doing things she had never taught her.

Soon, people around the compound started noticing.

One afternoon, Rosemary cooked a simple meal for the family.

Her uncle, Samuel, took one bite and looked at Mama Grace.

"Who cooked this food?"

Mama Grace pointed at Rosemary.

Samuel looked at the little girl.

"Rosemary?"

Rosemary nodded.

Samuel laughed.

"At six years old?"

Mama Grace smiled.

"Yes."

Samuel tasted the food again.

"This girl has something special."

The news slowly spread around the town.

People started coming to the house just to taste Rosemary's food.

Some were amazed.

Some were confused.

Some even whispered among themselves.

"How can a little girl cook like this?"

"Is she using something we don't know about?"

"Could this child be a witch?"

Mama Grace did not like hearing such things.

One evening, she sat beside Rosemary after cooking.

"My child, do you know what people are saying?"

Rosemary looked at her mother.

"What are they saying?"

Mama Grace smiled gently.

"They are wondering how you learned to cook so well."

Rosemary looked at the fire.

"I don't know, Mama."

Her mother placed her hand on Rosemary's head.

"Maybe you don't know yet."

Mama Grace looked toward the sky.

"But whatever this is, we will thank God for it."

Rosemary smiled.

She did not know what her gift would become.

She did not know that one day people from far away would come looking for her.

She did not know that hotels and restaurants would one day ask for her recipes.

She only knew one thing.

Whenever she stood beside the fire, something inside her felt happy.

And whenever she cooked, people smiled.

That little gift was just beginning.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🥘 Chapter Two – The Taste That Shocked the Town

Rosemary was still very young, but her cooking had already become a topic in Umudike.

People who tasted her food always came back with questions.

One morning, her mother's friend, Auntie Beatrice, came to the house.

"Grace, I heard your little girl is the one cooking all this food."

Mama Grace smiled.

"She is."

Beatrice looked at Rosemary.

"Can you really cook?"

Rosemary nodded.

"I can try."

Beatrice laughed.

"Then let me taste what you can do."

Rosemary went to the kitchen.

She brought out some vegetables, tomatoes, onions and spices.

Her mother stood nearby.

Beatrice watched everything.

Rosemary did not hurry.

She prepared each ingredient carefully.

Then she started cooking.

The smell soon filled the compound.

Beatrice looked at Mama Grace.

"Are you sure you are not helping her?"

Mama Grace laughed.

"I have not touched anything."

When the food was ready, Beatrice took the first spoon.

She stopped.

She took another spoon.

Then another.

Rosemary watched her carefully.

"Is it good?"

Beatrice looked at her.

"Good?"

"This is better than good."

Mama Grace laughed.

Beatrice shook her head in amazement.

"Grace, what did you teach this girl?"

Mama Grace pointed at herself.

"Me?"

"She is the one teaching me now."

They all laughed.

But the matter became serious when more people started asking Rosemary to cook.

Neighbours began bringing ingredients to the house.

They would say,

"Rosemary, please help us cook this."

Rosemary never complained.

She enjoyed it.

One Saturday, the town held a small celebration.

Many women were preparing food.

Mama Grace was asked to help with the cooking.

She brought Rosemary along.

At first, nobody paid much attention to the little girl.

But after Rosemary helped prepare one of the pots, people started noticing the smell.

A woman named Mrs. Evelyn Okoro came closer.

"Who prepared this?"

Mama Grace pointed at Rosemary.

Evelyn stared at the little girl.

"Her?"

Rosemary nodded.

Evelyn tasted it.

Her eyes became wide.

"This child is six years old!"

Another woman came to taste it.

Then another.

Soon, a small crowd gathered around Rosemary.

"How did you learn this?"

"Who taught you?"

"What did you put inside it?"

Rosemary smiled.

"I don't know."

The women looked at one another.

One woman whispered,

"There must be something behind it."

Another woman replied,

"Maybe she has a secret."

Mama Grace heard them.

She looked at her daughter and smiled.

She knew Rosemary had no secret.

The girl simply had a gift.

That evening, Mama Grace sat with Rosemary beside the cooking fire.

"Do you know why people like your food?"

Rosemary shook her head.

Her mother smiled.

"Because God gave you something special."

Rosemary looked at the fire.

"Can I keep cooking?"

Mama Grace hugged her.

"Yes, my child."

"Keep learning."

Rosemary smiled.

She picked up the spoon again.

The fire burned gently.

The smell of food filled the evening air.

And somewhere in Umudike, people were already talking about the little girl who could cook like someone who had spent a lifetime in the kitchen.

Nobody knew where the gift would take her.

But Rosemary was happy doing what she loved.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🔥 Chapter Three – The Gift Nobody Could Explain

As the years passed, Rosemary's cooking became even better.

She was still young, but people were already coming from nearby villages to taste her food.

Mama Grace had started learning from her daughter.

"Rosemary, how did you know the pepper was enough?"

Rosemary smiled.

"I just tasted it, Mama."

Her mother laughed.

"That is what you always say."

One afternoon, a woman named Mrs. Clara Benson came to their house.

She owned a small restaurant in a nearby town.

"Is this the girl everyone is talking about?"

Mama Grace nodded.

"Yes. This is Rosemary."

Clara looked at Rosemary.

"Can I taste your cooking?"

Rosemary smiled.

"Yes."

Rosemary went to the kitchen.

She prepared a simple local meal.

Nothing fancy.

Just the food her family normally ate.

When it was ready, Clara tasted it.

She became quiet.

She took another spoon.

Then she looked at Mama Grace.

"Who taught her this?"

Mama Grace smiled.

"Nobody."

Clara looked surprised.

"Nobody?"

"She watched me when she was little. After that, she started doing things I never showed her."

Clara looked at Rosemary.

"Would you like to cook in my restaurant one day?"

Rosemary looked at her mother.

Mama Grace smiled.

"If you want to."

Rosemary nodded.

"I would like that."

The news spread quickly.

Soon, other restaurant owners started hearing about Rosemary.

Some wanted to meet her.

Some wanted to taste her food.

Some wanted to know her recipes.

But Rosemary never understood why everyone was so interested.

To her, cooking was just something she loved.

One evening, she asked her mother a question.

"Mama, why does everybody want to know how I cook?"

Mama Grace smiled.

"Because you have something special."

"Is it magic?"

Her mother laughed.

"No, my child."

"It is a gift."

Rosemary looked at the fire.

"Who gave me the gift?"

Mama Grace touched her forehead gently.

"God."

Rosemary smiled.

She looked at her small hands.

"Then I should use it well."

Her mother nodded.

"That is exactly what you should do."

Not everyone in the town understood Rosemary.

Some people still whispered.

"How can she be this good?"

"Maybe there is something she is hiding."

But Rosemary had nothing to hide.

She was simply grateful.

Whenever she cooked, she prayed first.

She thanked God for the food.

She thanked God for her family.

And she asked for wisdom to keep learning.

Her family soon noticed another change.

People were buying more food from their home.

Neighbours were ordering meals.

Visitors were coming from nearby towns.

Mama Grace was able to save money.

Her father, Michael Williams, used some of the money to repair their old house.

Rosemary's younger brothers were able to return to school with better supplies.

The family became happier.

One night, Michael sat beside Rosemary.

"My daughter, do you know what your cooking has done for this family?"

Rosemary shook her head.

Her father smiled.

"It has given us hope."

Rosemary smiled back.

She never thought her little gift could do so much.

And this was only the beginning.
