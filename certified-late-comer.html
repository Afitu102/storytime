⏰ Certified Late Comer

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

⏰ Chapter One – Madam Teresa Was Never Early

If there was an award for arriving late, Madam Teresa would not only win it, she would probably arrive late to collect the award.

That was how well everybody in the village knew Teresa.

If an event was announced for 12 noon, Teresa did not hear 12 noon. In her mind, 12 noon simply meant, “There is still plenty of time.” By the time other people were already sitting down, greeting one another, and wondering who else was coming, Teresa would probably still be at home deciding which dress to wear.

Teresa never believed that being late was a serious problem.

Sometimes, when people complained about waiting for her, she would simply laugh and say, “Am I the host of the event? So why should I be the first person to get there?”

That was Teresa for you.

She loved parties. She loved meetings. She loved church gatherings. She loved village celebrations. She loved anything where people would gather, eat, talk, laugh, and admire beautiful clothes.

But there was one thing she loved almost as much as the event itself.

Making a grand entrance.

Teresa enjoyed walking into a gathering when everybody had already arrived. She liked the moment when people turned their heads to look at her dress, her shoes, her handbag, or the gele she had spent almost an hour tying.

She took her sweet time bathing. She took her sweet time cooking. She took her sweet time choosing her clothes. And when it was time to tie her gele, nobody was allowed to disturb her.

“This gele must sit well,” she would say, looking at herself in the mirror.

Meanwhile, the people waiting for her had already started asking, “Where is Teresa?”

It was not only parties.

Teresa was late to church meetings. She was late to village women's meetings. She was even late to the market.

The women in her group had become so used to it that they eventually accepted it as part of Teresa's character. Whenever she arrived late for a meeting, she would simply pay the lateness fee like someone paying a monthly subscription.

“At least I have paid,” she would say proudly.

Her lateness was so famous that even her customers at the market knew about it.

Teresa sold vegetables at one of the busiest parts of the village market. Her vegetables were always fresh because they came straight from her husband Nnamdi's large farm.

Her tomatoes were fresh. Her peppers were fresh. Her ugu leaves were fresh. Even the women who bought from her knew that if they wanted to meet Teresa early in the morning, they had to be prepared to wait.

Sometimes customers would arrive at the market and ask, “Where is Teresa?”

Another woman would simply laugh and say, “Don't worry. She is probably still at home preparing to come.”

And she would be right.

Teresa herself saw nothing wrong with it.

“Even my vegetables are fresh,” she would say. “So why should I come to the market looking unfresh?”

But Teresa had not always been like this.

There was a time when she used to arrive early.

She would dress up carefully, leave home on time, and reach events before everybody else.

But nobody noticed her.

She would sit quietly and watch people arrive one after another. Nobody would say, “Teresa, you look beautiful today.” Nobody would admire her gele. Nobody would ask where she bought her dress.

Then one day, everything changed.

Teresa had another event to attend. She had prepared a beautiful outfit and a gele that looked perfect on her. But for some reason, she arrived late.

By the time she entered, almost everybody was already there.

Heads turned.

People looked at her.

Someone complimented her dress.

Another woman admired her gele.

Someone even said, “Teresa, you are looking very beautiful today!”

Teresa smiled.

She had finally discovered something.

When she arrived early, nobody noticed her.

But when she arrived late, everybody noticed her.

From that day, lateness slowly became her strategy.

She began to believe that arriving late made her entrance more important.

And before long, Teresa became known throughout the village as the woman who was never early.

She did not know that one particular naming ceremony would soon teach her a lesson she would never forget.

🌙 End of Chapter One

👗 Chapter Two – The Naming Ceremony

Seven days before the naming ceremony, Chifukoro stood before the villagers and made an important announcement.

His family had waited for years for the arrival of their newborn son, so this was not going to be an ordinary naming ceremony. It was going to be a big celebration.

There would be food, music, dancing, drinks, family members, friends, and plenty of people coming from different places.

As soon as Teresa heard the announcement, her eyes became bright.

“Ceremony?” she asked.

“New baby?”

“Party?”

Those were the only three things her ears seemed to remember.

She did not pay much attention to the other things Chifukoro was saying.

Instead, Teresa was already thinking about what she would wear.

The next morning, she went straight to her tailor.

“I want something beautiful,” she said.

She chose a fine fabric and began planning an outfit that would make people turn their heads when she arrived.

Of course, she had already decided that she would not arrive early.

What was the point of wearing such a beautiful outfit if nobody noticed her?

She wanted to make an entrance.

The day of the ceremony finally arrived.

The ceremony was scheduled for 10 in the morning.

But Teresa had one small problem.

Her husband, Nnamdi, was away on a business trip.

Normally, Nnamdi was her alarm clock.

When Teresa refused to wake up, he would call her name several times. If that did not work, he could even sprinkle a little water on her.

But that morning, there was nobody to disturb her.

Teresa slept peacefully.

She opened her eyes and looked around.

Then she looked at the time.

It was already noon.

“Ah!” she shouted as she sat up.

But even then, Teresa did not move as quickly as someone who had missed a 10 o'clock ceremony should move.

She took her bath slowly.

She dressed carefully.

She arranged her hair.

She brought out her beautiful outfit and admired herself in the mirror.

Then came the gele.

That one took even more time.

Teresa tied it, looked at herself, removed it, tied it again, and adjusted it until she was satisfied.

“Perfect,” she finally said.

She picked up her handbag and stepped outside.

But something felt strange.

The village was unusually quiet.

There were no children running around.

There were no women carrying plates.

There was no loud music coming from Chifukoro's compound.

There was no sound of people laughing or greeting one another.

Teresa looked around.

“Everybody must have gone already,” she said to herself.

She did not consider the possibility that something had changed.

After all, Teresa was already late. She simply assumed that everyone else had arrived before her.

She hurried toward Chifukoro's compound.

But when she got there, she stopped.

The place was almost empty.

There were still decorations hanging around.

Some bottles were lying around.

But there were no crowds.

No music.

No food being served.

No celebration.

Teresa stared at the compound in confusion.

“What happened here?” she asked.

She began looking around.

For a moment, she thought the ceremony had already ended.

“All this dressing for nothing?” she complained.

She looked at her beautiful outfit again.

There was no way she was going back home without being seen.

Then she remembered something.

“Maybe they have moved to the village square,” she said.

She remembered that the king sometimes attended important celebrations.

So Teresa quickly turned around.

She decided to head toward the village square and the palace.

She had no idea that the biggest surprise of the day was waiting for her on the road.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🎭 Chapter Three – The Masquerades

Teresa turned away from Chifukoro's empty compound and started walking toward the village square.

She was still confused, but she was not ready to accept that she had missed the ceremony.

“Maybe they have moved everybody to the palace,” she said to herself.

She held her handbag tightly and continued walking in her beautiful outfit.

Then, suddenly, she heard a strange sound.

It was coming from the road ahead.

Teresa stopped.

She looked carefully.

Then she saw them.

Two masquerades were coming down the road.

Her heart almost jumped out of her chest.

Teresa knew what that meant.

In the village, there was a tradition that when masquerades passed through the road, anyone who crossed their path had to appease them with the required cowries.

If you did not have what was required, you could be chased away.

Teresa quickly checked her handbag.

She searched inside.

She searched again.

Nothing useful.

“Oh my God,” she whispered.

She looked at the masquerades again.

They were getting closer.

“Where am I going to see cowries now?”

For the first time that day, Teresa regretted waking up late.

She regretted taking her time with her bath.

She regretted spending so much time choosing her outfit.

She even regretted the beautiful gele sitting proudly on her head.

But there was no time to think.

The masquerades were coming closer.

Teresa turned and started running.

“Somebody help me!” she shouted.

She gathered her tight dress with both hands and ran as fast as she could.

Her beautiful gele was no longer looking beautiful.

It was fighting for its own survival.

Her handbag was swinging from side to side.

Her shoes were making things even harder.

As she ran, Teresa kept looking back.

The masquerades were still following her.

“Why did I come to this place?” she cried.

She wished she had stayed at home.

She wished she had woken up earlier.

She wished she had listened properly when Chifukoro made the announcement.

But wishing could not help her now.

Just when Teresa thought she was in serious trouble, her phone rang.

She looked at the screen.

It was Nnamdi.

“Where are you?” he asked.

“Nnamdi! Please help me!” Teresa shouted.

She quickly explained what was happening.

Nnamdi listened and immediately understood the problem.

He was already returning from his business trip and had heard about the masquerades from a neighbouring village.

Thankfully, he had brought the required cowries with him.

Nnamdi hurried toward Teresa and appeased the masquerades.

At last, the chase stopped.

Teresa stood there breathing heavily.

Her dress was no longer as neat as when she left home.

Her gele was almost giving up.

And her pride had completely disappeared.

But there was still one question in her mind.

“Where is everybody?”

That was when Teresa finally discovered the truth.

The naming ceremony had not ended.

It had not even started in the place she went to.

Chifukoro had changed the venue.

Someone had warned the family that the masquerades from the neighbouring village would be passing through the area.

Because of the masquerade parade, it was not safe to hold the celebration in the open compound.

So the ceremony had been moved to Hall 6, a popular event hall in the area.

Hall 6 was known as a place people used when they needed protection from rain, strong wind, or unexpected situations.

And this was certainly an unexpected situation.

Chifukoro had actually warned everybody about the masquerades when he announced the ceremony.

He had advised everyone to come early and, if possible, carry the required cowries.

But Teresa had not stayed long enough to hear all of that.

She had heard only “new baby,” “ceremony,” and “party.”

Now she was standing on the road with a struggling gele, a tired body, and a lesson she was not ready to accept.

Meanwhile, the naming ceremony was going on peacefully at Hall 6.

People were eating.

People were dancing.

People were laughing.

And Teresa had missed all of it simply because she had decided that arriving late was not a problem.

🌙 End of Chapter Three
