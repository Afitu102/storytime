👩🏾 The Unwelcomed Daughter

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🏙️ Chapter One – The Daughter Who Came Home

The rain had just stopped in Abuja when a silver car slowly pulled into the compound of an old bungalow in Garki.

It was a quiet Saturday morning. The roads were still wet, the sky was grey, and the smell of rain filled the air.

Inside the house, a woman named Mama Adaeze was preparing breakfast.

She had lived in Abuja for more than twenty years. She was a respected businesswoman who had worked hard to raise her only daughter after her husband died.

Her daughter, Amara, had left Abuja three years earlier.

Since then, Mama Adaeze had heard very little from her.

They spoke on the phone sometimes, but the conversations were always short.

“I'm fine, Mama.”

“I have work to do.”

“I'll call you later.”

Those were the words Mama Adaeze had become used to hearing.

She had asked many times for Amara to come home, but her daughter always found an excuse.

So when the silver car stopped outside her gate that morning, Mama Adaeze assumed it was one of her customers.

She continued stirring the pot on the stove.

Then she heard a knock.

Three slow knocks.

She wiped her hands and walked toward the door.

“Who is there?”

There was no answer.

She opened the door slightly.

And froze.

A young woman stood outside.

She was dressed beautifully, wearing an expensive-looking outfit, dark glasses, and a handbag that looked like something Mama Adaeze had only seen in television advertisements.

The woman removed her glasses.

“Mama.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

For several seconds, she could not speak.

“Amara?”

The young woman smiled.

“Yes, Mama.”

Mama Adaeze looked at her from head to toe.

Her daughter had changed.

Not just her clothes.

Something about her was different.

Her voice sounded different. The way she stood was different. Even the way she looked at her mother was no longer the same.

Mama Adaeze stepped aside.

“Come inside.”

Amara entered the house and placed her handbag on the sofa.

“I missed home,” she said.

Mama Adaeze forced a smile.

“After three years?”

Amara laughed softly.

“Mama, please. I know I stayed away for too long.”

Her mother said nothing.

She returned to the kitchen and served breakfast.

As they ate, Mama Adaeze kept looking at her daughter.

There was something she could not explain.

Amara was sitting right in front of her, but somehow she felt as if she was looking at someone she had never met before.

“So,” Mama Adaeze finally asked, “what brought you back?”

Amara stopped eating.

“I just wanted to come home.”

“Just like that?”

“Yes.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

“You never come anywhere without a reason.”

Amara lowered her eyes.

“There is something I want to tell you.”

Mama Adaeze placed her spoon on the table.

“What is it?”

Amara looked toward the window.

“I didn't come back because I missed Abuja.”

Her mother waited.

“I came back because I have nowhere else to go.”

The words shocked Mama Adaeze.

“What happened?”

“Nothing happened.”

“Amara.”

“I said nothing happened.”

Her mother leaned closer.

“You disappeared for three years. You stopped answering my questions. You refused to tell me where you were living. Now you suddenly appear at my door looking like a completely different person and tell me you have nowhere to go.”

Amara's eyes became wet.

“I knew you would react like this.”

“Then why did you come?”

Amara looked directly into her mother's eyes.

“Because you're the only person I have left.”

Mama Adaeze's anger softened.

She reached across the table and held her daughter's hand.

“You are my child. Whatever happened, you can tell me.”

Amara squeezed her hand.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Then Amara suddenly pulled her hand away.

“Mama, there is one thing I need you to promise me.”

“What?”

“Whatever you hear about me, don't send me away.”

Mama Adaeze frowned.

“What are you talking about?”

Amara stood up.

“Just promise me.”

“I promise.”

Amara nodded.

But before she could say anything else, a loud knock came from the gate.

Mama Adaeze looked toward the compound entrance.

“Who could that be?”

Amara's face suddenly changed.

The confidence disappeared from her eyes.

She looked frightened.

“Mama, don't open the gate.”

“Why?”

“Please.”

Another knock came.

This time it was harder.

Mama Adaeze walked toward the window and looked outside.

A black SUV was parked near the gate.

Two men were standing beside it.

One of them was holding an envelope.

Mama Adaeze turned back toward her daughter.

“Amara, who are those men?”

Amara said nothing.

“Who are they?”

Her daughter slowly sat down.

Then she whispered something that made Mama Adaeze's heart begin to race.

“They are the reason I came back.”

🌙 End of Chapter One

😨 Chapter Two – The Men at the Gate

Mama Adaeze stared at her daughter as if she had just spoken a language she did not understand.

“The reason you came back?” she repeated.

Amara nodded slowly.

Outside, the two men were still standing beside the black SUV. One of them looked toward the house while the other kept his eyes on the gate.

Mama Adaeze moved closer to the window.

“Amara, what have you done?”

“Nothing, Mama.”

“Then why are people looking for you?”

Amara did not answer.

Her mother turned around angrily.

“You disappeared for three years. You barely called me. You refused to tell me where you were living. Now you come back without warning, and two strange men are waiting outside my house. You expect me to believe nothing happened?”

Amara's eyes filled with tears.

“I didn't know where else to go.”

Mama Adaeze's voice became softer.

“Come here.”

Amara walked toward her.

“Tell me everything.”

Before Amara could speak, someone knocked on the gate again.

This time, the sound was followed by a man's voice.

“Madam, please. We only want to talk to Amara.”

Amara immediately stepped backward.

“Mama, don't let them in.”

Mama Adaeze looked at her daughter.

“What exactly did you do?”

“I told you, I didn't do anything.”

“Then why are you afraid?”

Amara looked down.

“Because they won't believe me.”

The knocking stopped.

For a few seconds, the compound became completely quiet.

Then the men left.

Mama Adaeze watched the SUV disappear through the gate.

She turned back to her daughter.

“Now you will tell me the truth.”

Amara sat down.

“I met someone three years ago.”

“Who?”

“A man.”

Mama Adaeze sighed.

“So there is a man.”

“I never said there wasn't.”

“You said nothing happened.”

“I meant I didn't do anything wrong.”

Her mother waited.

Amara took a deep breath.

“He was wealthy. Very wealthy. I met him at a business event in Abuja. At first, he was just someone I spoke to. Then we became close.”

“Where is he now?”

Amara looked away.

“I don't know.”

“You don't know?”

“He disappeared.”

“When?”

“Almost two years ago.”

Mama Adaeze's face changed.

“And you have been living alone all this time?”

Amara nodded.

“I thought he would come back.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because you would have told me to leave him.”

“Maybe I would have.”

Amara smiled sadly.

“That is why I didn't tell you.”

Mama Adaeze wanted to be angry, but seeing the pain in her daughter's eyes made it difficult.

“What's his name?”

Amara hesitated.

“Daniel.”

“Daniel what?”

Amara remained silent.

“Amara.”

“I don't want to say his name.”

“Why?”

“Because the moment I say it, everything changes.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

“What are you hiding from me?”

Amara stood up and walked toward her handbag.

She opened it and brought out a small brown envelope.

“I brought this because I knew you would eventually ask questions.”

She placed the envelope on the table.

Mama Adaeze opened it.

Inside were several photographs.

The first showed Amara standing beside Daniel at what looked like a hotel.

The second showed them at a restaurant.

The third made Mama Adaeze stop breathing for a moment.

It showed Daniel standing beside an older woman.

The woman was wearing expensive clothes and jewelry.

Mama Adaeze looked closely at her face.

“I know this woman.”

Amara nodded.

“She is Daniel's mother.”

Mama Adaeze looked up.

“And what does she have to do with you?”

“Everything.”

Before Mama Adaeze could ask another question, her phone rang.

She looked at the screen.

It was an unknown number.

She answered.

“Hello?”

A man's voice spoke quietly.

“Madam, please listen carefully.”

Mama Adaeze stood still.

“Who is this?”

“Don't allow your daughter to leave the house today.”

Mama Adaeze's heart began to beat faster.

“Why?”

There was silence on the other end.

Then the man said,

“Because the woman looking for her is not Daniel's mother.”

The call ended.

Mama Adaeze slowly lowered the phone.

Amara was staring at her.

“What did they say?”

Her mother looked at the photograph again.

“Amara...”

“Yes?”

“Who is this woman?”

Amara's face suddenly went pale.

“I don't know.”

“But you just told me she was Daniel's mother.”

“I thought she was.”

Mama Adaeze stood up.

“Then what have you been involved in for the past three years?”

Amara opened her mouth to answer.

But before she could speak, someone pushed open the compound gate.

Mama Adaeze turned toward the entrance.

The same black SUV had returned.

But this time, the woman from the photograph was sitting in the back seat.

She stepped out slowly.

Then she looked directly at the house.

Amara grabbed her mother's arm.

“Mama... that's not Daniel's mother.”

Mama Adaeze stared at the woman.

“Then who is she?”

Amara whispered,

“She is the woman who made me leave Abuja three years ago.”
