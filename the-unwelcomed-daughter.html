👩🏾 The Unwelcomed Daughter

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🏙️ Chapter One – The Daughter Who Came Home

The rain had just stopped in Abuja when a silver car slowly pulled into the compound of an old bungalow in Garki.

It was a quiet Saturday morning. The roads were still wet, the sky was grey, and the smell of rain filled the air.

Inside the house, a woman named Mama Adaeze was preparing breakfast.

She had lived in Abuja for more than twenty years. She was a respected businesswoman who had worked hard to raise her only daughter after her husband died.

Her daughter, Amara, had left Abuja three years earlier.

Since then, Mama Adaeze had heard very little from her.

They spoke on the phone sometimes, but the conversations were always short.

“I'm fine, Mama.”

“I have work to do.”

“I'll call you later.”

Those were the words Mama Adaeze had become used to hearing.

She had asked many times for Amara to come home, but her daughter always found an excuse.

So when the silver car stopped outside her gate that morning, Mama Adaeze assumed it was one of her customers.

She continued stirring the pot on the stove.

Then she heard a knock.

Three slow knocks.

She wiped her hands and walked toward the door.

“Who is there?”

There was no answer.

She opened the door slightly.

And froze.

A young woman stood outside.

She was dressed beautifully, wearing an expensive-looking outfit, dark glasses, and a handbag that looked like something Mama Adaeze had only seen in television advertisements.

The woman removed her glasses.

“Mama.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

For several seconds, she could not speak.

“Amara?”

The young woman smiled.

“Yes, Mama.”

Mama Adaeze looked at her from head to toe.

Her daughter had changed.

Not just her clothes.

Something about her was different.

Her voice sounded different. The way she stood was different. Even the way she looked at her mother was no longer the same.

Mama Adaeze stepped aside.

“Come inside.”

Amara entered the house and placed her handbag on the sofa.

“I missed home,” she said.

Mama Adaeze forced a smile.

“After three years?”

Amara laughed softly.

“Mama, please. I know I stayed away for too long.”

Her mother said nothing.

She returned to the kitchen and served breakfast.

As they ate, Mama Adaeze kept looking at her daughter.

There was something she could not explain.

Amara was sitting right in front of her, but somehow she felt as if she was looking at someone she had never met before.

“So,” Mama Adaeze finally asked, “what brought you back?”

Amara stopped eating.

“I just wanted to come home.”

“Just like that?”

“Yes.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

“You never come anywhere without a reason.”

Amara lowered her eyes.

“There is something I want to tell you.”

Mama Adaeze placed her spoon on the table.

“What is it?”

Amara looked toward the window.

“I didn't come back because I missed Abuja.”

Her mother waited.

“I came back because I have nowhere else to go.”

The words shocked Mama Adaeze.

“What happened?”

“Nothing happened.”

“Amara.”

“I said nothing happened.”

Her mother leaned closer.

“You disappeared for three years. You stopped answering my questions. You refused to tell me where you were living. Now you suddenly appear at my door looking like a completely different person and tell me you have nowhere to go.”

Amara's eyes became wet.

“I knew you would react like this.”

“Then why did you come?”

Amara looked directly into her mother's eyes.

“Because you're the only person I have left.”

Mama Adaeze's anger softened.

She reached across the table and held her daughter's hand.

“You are my child. Whatever happened, you can tell me.”

Amara squeezed her hand.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Then Amara suddenly pulled her hand away.

“Mama, there is one thing I need you to promise me.”

“What?”

“Whatever you hear about me, don't send me away.”

Mama Adaeze frowned.

“What are you talking about?”

Amara stood up.

“Just promise me.”

“I promise.”

Amara nodded.

But before she could say anything else, a loud knock came from the gate.

Mama Adaeze looked toward the compound entrance.

“Who could that be?”

Amara's face suddenly changed.

The confidence disappeared from her eyes.

She looked frightened.

“Mama, don't open the gate.”

“Why?”

“Please.”

Another knock came.

This time it was harder.

Mama Adaeze walked toward the window and looked outside.

A black SUV was parked near the gate.

Two men were standing beside it.

One of them was holding an envelope.

Mama Adaeze turned back toward her daughter.

“Amara, who are those men?”

Amara said nothing.

“Who are they?”

Her daughter slowly sat down.

Then she whispered something that made Mama Adaeze's heart begin to race.

“They are the reason I came back.”

🌙 End of Chapter One

😨 Chapter Two – The Men at the Gate

Mama Adaeze stared at her daughter as if she had just spoken a language she did not understand.

“The reason you came back?” she repeated.

Amara nodded slowly.

Outside, the two men were still standing beside the black SUV. One of them looked toward the house while the other kept his eyes on the gate.

Mama Adaeze moved closer to the window.

“Amara, what have you done?”

“Nothing, Mama.”

“Then why are people looking for you?”

Amara did not answer.

Her mother turned around angrily.

“You disappeared for three years. You barely called me. You refused to tell me where you were living. Now you come back without warning, and two strange men are waiting outside my house. You expect me to believe nothing happened?”

Amara's eyes filled with tears.

“I didn't know where else to go.”

Mama Adaeze's voice became softer.

“Come here.”

Amara walked toward her.

“Tell me everything.”

Before Amara could speak, someone knocked on the gate again.

This time, the sound was followed by a man's voice.

“Madam, please. We only want to talk to Amara.”

Amara immediately stepped backward.

“Mama, don't let them in.”

Mama Adaeze looked at her daughter.

“What exactly did you do?”

“I told you, I didn't do anything.”

“Then why are you afraid?”

Amara looked down.

“Because they won't believe me.”

The knocking stopped.

For a few seconds, the compound became completely quiet.

Then the men left.

Mama Adaeze watched the SUV disappear through the gate.

She turned back to her daughter.

“Now you will tell me the truth.”

Amara sat down.

“I met someone three years ago.”

“Who?”

“A man.”

Mama Adaeze sighed.

“So there is a man.”

“I never said there wasn't.”

“You said nothing happened.”

“I meant I didn't do anything wrong.”

Her mother waited.

Amara took a deep breath.

“He was wealthy. Very wealthy. I met him at a business event in Abuja. At first, he was just someone I spoke to. Then we became close.”

“Where is he now?”

Amara looked away.

“I don't know.”

“You don't know?”

“He disappeared.”

“When?”

“Almost two years ago.”

Mama Adaeze's face changed.

“And you have been living alone all this time?”

Amara nodded.

“I thought he would come back.”

“Why didn't you tell me?”

“Because you would have told me to leave him.”

“Maybe I would have.”

Amara smiled sadly.

“That is why I didn't tell you.”

Mama Adaeze wanted to be angry, but seeing the pain in her daughter's eyes made it difficult.

“What's his name?”

Amara hesitated.

“Daniel.”

“Daniel what?”

Amara remained silent.

“Amara.”

“I don't want to say his name.”

“Why?”

“Because the moment I say it, everything changes.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

“What are you hiding from me?”

Amara stood up and walked toward her handbag.

She opened it and brought out a small brown envelope.

“I brought this because I knew you would eventually ask questions.”

She placed the envelope on the table.

Mama Adaeze opened it.

Inside were several photographs.

The first showed Amara standing beside Daniel at what looked like a hotel.

The second showed them at a restaurant.

The third made Mama Adaeze stop breathing for a moment.

It showed Daniel standing beside an older woman.

The woman was wearing expensive clothes and jewelry.

Mama Adaeze looked closely at her face.

“I know this woman.”

Amara nodded.

“She is Daniel's mother.”

Mama Adaeze looked up.

“And what does she have to do with you?”

“Everything.”

Before Mama Adaeze could ask another question, her phone rang.

She looked at the screen.

It was an unknown number.

She answered.

“Hello?”

A man's voice spoke quietly.

“Madam, please listen carefully.”

Mama Adaeze stood still.

“Who is this?”

“Don't allow your daughter to leave the house today.”

Mama Adaeze's heart began to beat faster.

“Why?”

There was silence on the other end.

Then the man said,

“Because the woman looking for her is not Daniel's mother.”

The call ended.

Mama Adaeze slowly lowered the phone.

Amara was staring at her.

“What did they say?”

Her mother looked at the photograph again.

“Amara...”

“Yes?”

“Who is this woman?”

Amara's face suddenly went pale.

“I don't know.”

“But you just told me she was Daniel's mother.”

“I thought she was.”

Mama Adaeze stood up.

“Then what have you been involved in for the past three years?”

Amara opened her mouth to answer.

But before she could speak, someone pushed open the compound gate.

Mama Adaeze turned toward the entrance.

The same black SUV had returned.

But this time, the woman from the photograph was sitting in the back seat.

She stepped out slowly.

Then she looked directly at the house.

Amara grabbed her mother's arm.

“Mama... that's not Daniel's mother.”

Mama Adaeze stared at the woman.

“Then who is she?”

Amara whispered,

“She is the woman who made me leave Abuja three years ago.”

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🚪 Chapter Three – The Woman at the Door

For a few seconds, nobody moved.

Mama Adaeze stood behind the door, staring at the woman outside. She looked calm, almost too calm for someone who had just arrived at a house after making Amara tremble with fear.

The woman was elegantly dressed in a dark blue suit. She carried a small handbag and wore sunglasses even though the sky was already dark with rain.

She slowly removed the sunglasses.

“Good evening,” she said.

Mama did not answer immediately.

Instead, she turned around and looked at Amara.

Her daughter had gone pale.

“Who is she?” Mama asked quietly.

Amara swallowed hard.

“Her name is Mrs. Kemi Durojaiye.”

The woman smiled when she heard her name.

“So you still remember me.”

Mama's eyes narrowed.

“Why are you here?”

Mrs. Durojaiye looked past her and stared directly at Amara.

“I came because your daughter has something that belongs to my family.”

Amara immediately shook her head.

“That is not true.”

Mrs. Durojaiye gave a small laugh.

“You haven't changed. You still deny everything.”

Mama stepped outside the doorway.

“Madam, this is my house. If you have a problem with my daughter, you will explain it here and now.”

The woman looked at Mama for a moment before speaking.

“Three years ago, Amara entered my family's life and everything changed. Daniel disappeared. His father suffered a stroke. His business almost collapsed, and Amara was the last person seen with him.”

Mama turned sharply toward her daughter.

“Amara?”

Amara's eyes filled with tears.

“I didn't hurt Daniel.”

Mrs. Durojaiye stepped closer.

“Then tell your mother where he is.”

Silence filled the compound.

Amara lowered her head.

“I don't know.”

“You expect me to believe that?”

“I have been trying to find him too!”

Mrs. Durojaiye's expression suddenly changed.

“Then why did you run away?”

Amara wiped her tears.

“Because you gave me no choice.”

Mama looked between them, completely confused.

“What happened three years ago?”

Amara took a deep breath.

“The night Daniel disappeared, I received a message telling me to meet him at a hotel in Abuja. I went there, but Daniel never came.”

She paused.

“When I came back outside, the police were waiting for me.”

Mama's face tightened.

“The police?”

Amara nodded.

“They said Daniel's family had reported me. They accused me of taking money from his account.”

Mrs. Durojaiye interrupted.

“Because money had been transferred to your account.”

“I never transferred it!” Amara shouted.

“Then who did?”

Amara opened her mouth but said nothing.

Mama stepped closer to her.

“Amara, what aren't you telling me?”

Her daughter began to cry.

“There was another person involved.”

“Who?”

Before Amara could answer, Mrs. Durojaiye's phone rang.

She looked at the screen.

For the first time, fear appeared on her face.

She quickly rejected the call.

Mama noticed.

“Who was that?”

“Nobody.”

The phone rang again.

Mrs. Durojaiye stared at it.

Then she answered.

“Hello?”

She listened silently for a few seconds.

Her face became completely white.

“What do you mean he has been found?”

Amara froze.

Mama stared at her.

Mrs. Durojaiye slowly lowered the phone.

Nobody spoke.

Then she looked directly at Amara.

“Daniel is alive.”

Amara's knees almost gave way.

“What?”

Mrs. Durojaiye's voice dropped to a whisper.

“And he has just asked for you.”

🌙 End of Chapter Three

😔 Chapter Four – The Daughter Nobody Wanted

Amara stood behind her mother, unable to take her eyes off the woman at the gate.

For three years, she had tried to forget that face. She had tried to forget the expensive house in Abuja, the harsh words, the accusations, and the night she had walked away with nothing but a small bag in her hand.

But now the same woman was standing in front of her mother's house.

Mama Adaeze opened the gate slightly.

“Who are you?” she asked.

The woman looked at her calmly.

“My name is Mrs. Durojaiye. I need to speak with Amara.”

“You can speak to me first.”

Mrs. Durojaiye looked past her.

“I don't think she has told you everything.”

Mama turned around.

“Amara?”

Amara stepped forward slowly.

“Let her come inside.”

Mama looked surprised, but she moved aside.

Mrs. Durojaiye entered the sitting room and sat down without saying a word.

For several seconds, there was complete silence.

Then she looked at Amara.

“You should never have come back.”

Mama immediately stood up.

“Enough! You came into my house, and the first thing you did was threaten my daughter?”

“I am not threatening her.”

“Then what do you call it?”

Mrs. Durojaiye lowered her voice.

“I am trying to protect her.”

Amara laughed bitterly.

“Protect me? You are the reason I had to leave Abuja.”

The woman's face changed.

“You don't understand what happened.”

“Then explain it.”

Mrs. Durojaiye looked at Mama.

“Three years ago, Daniel was planning to leave his family.”

Amara frowned.

“He wanted to marry me.”

“Yes.”

“Then why did you stop him?”

Mrs. Durojaiye remained silent.

Mama stared at her.

“Why?”

Finally, the woman spoke.

“Because Daniel's father had already arranged another marriage for him.”

Amara's eyes widened.

“What?”

“They wanted him to marry the daughter of a wealthy business partner. It was not just about marriage. It was about protecting the family business.”

Amara shook her head.

“So they destroyed my relationship with him because of money?”

“I tried to convince Daniel to accept his family's decision.”

“And when he refused?”

Mrs. Durojaiye looked down.

“I told him you were only using him.”

Mama's face hardened.

“And you believed that?”

“No.”

The answer surprised everyone.

“I knew Amara loved him.”

Amara stared at her.

“Then why did you lie?”

The woman took a deep breath.

“Because I was afraid of what would happen if Daniel went against his family.”

Amara's voice became softer.

“You made me feel like I wasn't good enough.”

Mrs. Durojaiye looked away.

“I know.”

“You called me a nobody.”

“I know.”

“You told Daniel that I would ruin his life.”

“I know.”

Tears rolled down Amara's cheeks.

“But you don't know what it did to me.”

The room became quiet.

Mama moved closer to her daughter and held her hand.

“You should have come home to me,” she whispered.

Amara looked at her mother.

“I thought you would also see me as a disappointment.”

Mama's eyes filled with tears.

“You are my daughter. How could you ever be a disappointment to me?”

Amara broke down and rested her head on her mother's shoulder.

For the first time in three years, she allowed herself to cry.

But the moment was interrupted by a sound from outside.

A car had stopped in front of the house.

Mrs. Durojaiye quickly stood up.

Her face suddenly became pale.

Mama noticed.

“What is it?”

The woman walked toward the window.

She looked outside and whispered,

“No...”

Amara stood up.

“Who is there?”

Mrs. Durojaiye did not answer.

A man stepped out of the car.

He stood at the gate and looked directly toward the house.

Amara's heart stopped.

She recognized him immediately.

It was Daniel's older brother.

But he was not alone.

Someone was sitting in the back seat of the car.

Someone Amara had spent three years believing she would never see again.

Then the back door slowly opened.

Amara took one step toward the window.

And when the man stepped out, she could barely breathe.

Daniel was standing outside.

Alive.

But he looked completely different from the man she remembered.

He looked at the house for a long moment.

Then his eyes met Amara's through the window.

Neither of them moved.

Finally, Daniel raised his hand and pointed toward the woman standing behind Amara.

His lips moved slowly.

“Don't trust her.”

🌙 End of Chapter Four
