👩🏾 The Unwelcomed Daughter

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🏙️ Chapter One – The Daughter Who Came Home

The rain had just stopped in Abuja when a silver car slowly pulled into the compound of an old bungalow in Garki.

It was a quiet Saturday morning. The roads were still wet, the sky was grey, and the smell of rain filled the air.

Inside the house, a woman named Mama Adaeze was preparing breakfast.

She had lived in Abuja for more than twenty years. She was a respected businesswoman who had worked hard to raise her only daughter after her husband died.

Her daughter, Amara, had left Abuja three years earlier.

Since then, Mama Adaeze had heard very little from her.

They spoke on the phone sometimes, but the conversations were always short.

“I'm fine, Mama.”

“I have work to do.”

“I'll call you later.”

Those were the words Mama Adaeze had become used to hearing.

She had asked many times for Amara to come home, but her daughter always found an excuse.

So when the silver car stopped outside her gate that morning, Mama Adaeze assumed it was one of her customers.

She continued stirring the pot on the stove.

Then she heard a knock.

Three slow knocks.

She wiped her hands and walked toward the door.

“Who is there?”

There was no answer.

She opened the door slightly.

And froze.

A young woman stood outside.

She was dressed beautifully, wearing an expensive-looking outfit, dark glasses, and a handbag that looked like something Mama Adaeze had only seen in television advertisements.

The woman removed her glasses.

“Mama.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

For several seconds, she could not speak.

“Amara?”

The young woman smiled.

“Yes, Mama.”

Mama Adaeze looked at her from head to toe.

Her daughter had changed.

Not just her clothes.

Something about her was different.

Her voice sounded different. The way she stood was different. Even the way she looked at her mother was no longer the same.

Mama Adaeze stepped aside.

“Come inside.”

Amara entered the house and placed her handbag on the sofa.

“I missed home,” she said.

Mama Adaeze forced a smile.

“After three years?”

Amara laughed softly.

“Mama, please. I know I stayed away for too long.”

Her mother said nothing.

She returned to the kitchen and served breakfast.

As they ate, Mama Adaeze kept looking at her daughter.

There was something she could not explain.

Amara was sitting right in front of her, but somehow she felt as if she was looking at someone she had never met before.

“So,” Mama Adaeze finally asked, “what brought you back?”

Amara stopped eating.

“I just wanted to come home.”

“Just like that?”

“Yes.”

Mama Adaeze stared at her.

“You never come anywhere without a reason.”

Amara lowered her eyes.

“There is something I want to tell you.”

Mama Adaeze placed her spoon on the table.

“What is it?”

Amara looked toward the window.

“I didn't come back because I missed Abuja.”

Her mother waited.

“I came back because I have nowhere else to go.”

The words shocked Mama Adaeze.

“What happened?”

“Nothing happened.”

“Amara.”

“I said nothing happened.”

Her mother leaned closer.

“You disappeared for three years. You stopped answering my questions. You refused to tell me where you were living. Now you suddenly appear at my door looking like a completely different person and tell me you have nowhere to go.”

Amara's eyes became wet.

“I knew you would react like this.”

“Then why did you come?”

Amara looked directly into her mother's eyes.

“Because you're the only person I have left.”

Mama Adaeze's anger softened.

She reached across the table and held her daughter's hand.

“You are my child. Whatever happened, you can tell me.”

Amara squeezed her hand.

For a moment, neither of them spoke.

Then Amara suddenly pulled her hand away.

“Mama, there is one thing I need you to promise me.”

“What?”

“Whatever you hear about me, don't send me away.”

Mama Adaeze frowned.

“What are you talking about?”

Amara stood up.

“Just promise me.”

“I promise.”

Amara nodded.

But before she could say anything else, a loud knock came from the gate.

Mama Adaeze looked toward the compound entrance.

“Who could that be?”

Amara's face suddenly changed.

The confidence disappeared from her eyes.

She looked frightened.

“Mama, don't open the gate.”

“Why?”

“Please.”

Another knock came.

This time it was harder.

Mama Adaeze walked toward the window and looked outside.

A black SUV was parked near the gate.

Two men were standing beside it.

One of them was holding an envelope.

Mama Adaeze turned back toward her daughter.

“Amara, who are those men?”

Amara said nothing.

“Who are they?”

Her daughter slowly sat down.

Then she whispered something that made Mama Adaeze's heart begin to race.

“They are the reason I came back.”
