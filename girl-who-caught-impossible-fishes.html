🐟 Girl Who Caught Impossible Fishes

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 25 min read

🐟 Chapter One – The Girl With the Lucky Net

The morning sun was just coming up over the village of Kitala.

The lake was calm.

Small waves touched the wooden canoes near the shore.

Birds flew low over the water.

And among the fishermen was a young girl named Amara.

Amara was known for one thing.

She could catch fish.

Not just one or two.

Sometimes, her small basket was fuller than those of grown fishermen.

The older fishermen often laughed about it.

"That girl must have a secret."

Amara would only smile.

"Maybe the lake likes me."

Her grandmother, Nkiru, always warned her not to become too proud.

"Luck is like a bird."

"It can sit on your shoulder today and fly away tomorrow."

Amara remembered those words every morning.

That day, she carried her small fishing net toward the shore.

Her younger cousin, Zuberi, followed behind her.

"Wait for me!"

Amara turned.

"You are too slow."

Zuberi laughed.

"Because you have long legs."

Amara laughed too.

They reached the lake.

Their mother had given them one basket for the morning catch.

If they caught enough fish, there would be food for the whole family that evening.

Amara stepped into the shallow water.

She spread her net.

She waited.

Nothing.

She moved farther.

Still nothing.

Zuberi looked worried.

"Maybe Grandma was right."

Amara smiled.

"About what?"

"Maybe your luck flew away."

Amara shook her head.

"Then we will call it back."

She lifted the net again.

She threw it toward a quiet part of the lake.

The net disappeared beneath the water.

Amara waited.

Then she felt something pull.

Her eyes widened.

"Zuberi!"

He grabbed the rope.

They pulled together.

The net came out of the water.

Fish jumped everywhere.

Zuberi screamed with excitement.

"We caught them!"

Amara laughed.

The basket quickly filled.

But then something strange happened.

Amara noticed one fish lying at the bottom of the net.

It was different from the others.

Its scales were bright gold.

It was still alive.

It looked directly at her.

Amara stopped smiling.

"Zuberi."

He came closer.

"What?"

Amara pointed at the fish.

Zuberi's mouth opened.

"I have never seen a fish like that."

The golden fish moved its tail.

Then it made a strange sound.

Not the sound of a fish.

It sounded almost like a whisper.

Amara leaned closer.

"What did you say?"

The fish moved again.

Amara heard the whisper clearly this time.

"Take me home."

Amara jumped backward.

Zuberi stared at her.

"What happened?"

Amara pointed at the fish.

"It spoke."

Zuberi laughed.

"Fish don't speak."

Amara looked at the golden fish again.

It was staring at her.

Then it whispered again.

"Take me home."

Zuberi stopped laughing.

They looked at each other.

Neither child touched the fish.

A moment later, Amara carefully placed it inside a smaller basket.

She covered the basket with a cloth.

Zuberi looked nervous.

"Shouldn't we throw it back?"

Amara shook her head.

"I don't know."

They started walking home.

People noticed the basket immediately.

The catch was much bigger than anything Amara had brought home before.

Her mother, Sade, came running out of the house.

"Amara!"

She looked inside the basket.

Her face filled with happiness.

"Look at all these fish!"

Neighbors gathered around.

Everyone celebrated.

Children danced.

The fishermen smiled.

For the first time in many weeks, Amara's family had enough food to share with others.

Her grandmother Nkiru came outside.

She looked at the fish.

Then she looked at Amara.

"Where did you catch these?"

Amara pointed toward the lake.

"Near the old fig tree."

Nkiru's smile disappeared.

"Did you catch anything else?"

Amara became quiet.

She looked at the covered basket.

Nkiru noticed.

"What's inside?"

Amara slowly removed the cloth.

The golden fish was still there.

Nkiru froze.

Nobody spoke.

Then the old woman whispered:

"Where did you find this?"

Amara answered:

"In my net."

Nkiru took one step backward.

"Put it back."

Amara looked confused.

"Grandma, why?"

Nkiru looked toward the lake.

"Because some gifts bring happiness."

"And some gifts ask for something in return."

Before Amara could answer, the golden fish moved.

Its mouth opened.

Everyone heard the whisper.

"Your family will never be hungry again."

The people around the house gasped.

Sade covered her mouth.

Zuberi stepped behind Amara.

But Nkiru did not look surprised.

She looked frightened.

"Amara."

Amara looked at her grandmother.

"Yes?"

Nkiru pointed toward the lake.

"Take that fish back before sunset."

Amara looked at the golden fish.

"But it gave us luck."

Nkiru shook her head.

"No."

She looked deeply into Amara's eyes.

"It gave you a promise."

Amara's smile disappeared.

Then the golden fish whispered one final thing.

"Tomorrow, bring your net alone."

Amara stared at it.

She suddenly realized something.

The fish had not asked her to return it.

It had asked her to come back.

Alone.
