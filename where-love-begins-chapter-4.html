Chapter 4 — The Man Behind the Door

Amara stood frozen outside the house.

Her eyes remained fixed on the slightly open door.

She could hear her own heartbeat.

Daniel stood beside her, his eyes scanning the compound.

“Stay behind me,” he whispered.

Amara swallowed hard.

“Who could be inside?”

Daniel did not answer.

Instead, he slowly moved toward the door.

He pushed it open with the back of his hand.

The door creaked.

Nothing happened.

There was no movement.

No voice.

No sound.

Daniel looked inside.

“Wait here.”

Amara grabbed his arm.

“No. I'm coming with you.”

Daniel looked at her.

“Amara, this is dangerous.”

“Everything about my life has suddenly become dangerous. I'm not staying outside.”

Daniel hesitated.

Then he nodded.

They entered the house together.

The room was dark.

The windows were closed, and the curtains had been pulled across them.

Amara immediately noticed something strange.

The small wooden chair where she usually sat every evening had been moved.

“Daniel...”

“I see it.”

There were footprints on the dusty floor.

Someone had walked through the room recently.

Daniel followed the footprints.

They stopped near the bedroom.

He placed his hand on the door.

Before he could open it, a man's voice came from inside.

“You took your time, Amara.”

Amara's entire body went cold.

She knew that voice.

It was the same deep voice she had heard outside the village earlier.

Daniel immediately stepped in front of her.

“Who are you?” he demanded.

The man laughed softly.

“Daniel, you still haven't learned when to stop protecting people who don't belong to you.”

Daniel's face changed.

“Come out.”

The bedroom door slowly opened.

The mysterious man stepped into the room.

Amara stared at him.

He looked older than Daniel, perhaps in his late forties. His face was serious, and a faint scar ran from the side of his forehead toward his cheek.

But there was something about his eyes that frightened Amara.

They looked as though he had known her for years.

“Who are you?” Amara asked.

The man looked at her for several seconds.

“My name is Emmanuel.”

Daniel's expression hardened.

“You shouldn't be here.”

Emmanuel smiled.

“Neither should you.”

Amara looked from one man to the other.

“You two know each other.”

Neither answered.

Amara stepped forward.

“I asked if you know each other.”

Daniel finally spoke.

“I have met him before.”

Emmanuel shook his head.

“That's not the truth.”

Daniel stared at him.

“Be careful.”

“You were there that night,” Emmanuel said.

Amara's heart skipped.

“What night?”

Emmanuel looked directly at her.

“The night your mother's house was burned.”

Amara's breath caught in her throat.

“You said you were there.”

“I was.”

“And Daniel was there too?”

Emmanuel remained silent.

Amara turned toward Daniel.

“Were you there?”

Daniel looked away.

That silence was enough.

“You were there,” Amara whispered.

“Yes.”

Amara took a step backward.

“Why didn't you tell me?”

Daniel opened his mouth, but no words came.

Emmanuel slowly reached inside his jacket.

Daniel immediately raised his hand.

“Don't.”

Emmanuel ignored him.

He brought out an old photograph.

He handed it to Amara.

Her hands trembled as she looked at it.

It showed her mother standing beside a young man.

Amara recognized the man immediately.

It was the same man Daniel had shown her in the photograph.

Her father.

But there was something else in the picture.

Standing behind her parents was another man.

Amara stared at him.

She knew that face.

She slowly looked at Emmanuel.

“That's you.”

Emmanuel nodded.

“I was your father's closest friend.”

Daniel interrupted.

“And you betrayed him.”

Emmanuel's expression suddenly became cold.

“You don't get to accuse me of betrayal, Daniel.”

“You disappeared when he needed you.”

“I disappeared because I was being hunted too.”

Amara looked between them.

“Enough!”

Both men became silent.

Amara held the photograph tightly.

“I have spent my whole life believing my father abandoned me. My mother died without telling me the truth. Now I discover that both of you knew things about my family.”

Her voice began to shake.

“I want the truth.”

Emmanuel looked at her.

“Then you must be ready for what comes with it.”

“I'm ready.”

Emmanuel looked toward Daniel.

“Tell her.”

Daniel shook his head.

“Not here.”

“Why?” Amara asked.

Daniel looked toward the windows.

“Because we're not alone.”

Suddenly, Emmanuel's face changed.

He walked quickly toward the window and moved the curtain slightly.

He looked outside.

“We're already too late.”

Amara's heart began pounding again.

“What is it?”

Emmanuel pointed toward the road.

A black vehicle was parked beneath a large tree.

Its engine was running.

Amara stared at it.

“Who are they?”

Daniel's voice became low.

“The people who have been looking for you.”

Amara's face went pale.

“What do they want from me?”

Emmanuel turned toward her.

“They don't want you.”

Amara frowned.

“Then what do they want?”

Emmanuel looked at the photograph in her hands.

“They want what your father left behind.”

“What did he leave behind?”

Before Emmanuel could answer, the sound of a vehicle door closing came from outside.

Daniel immediately moved toward the back door.

“We have to leave.”

Amara grabbed her mother's letters.

“I'm not leaving until someone tells me the truth.”

Emmanuel looked at her seriously.

“Your father left something for you, Amara.”

She froze.

“For me?”

“Yes.”

“What is it?”

Emmanuel took a small key from his pocket.

“This.”

Amara stared at the key.

“What does it open?”

Emmanuel looked at Daniel.

Daniel's face had gone completely pale.

“Daniel knows,” Emmanuel said.

Amara slowly turned toward Daniel.

“What does this key open?”

Daniel remained silent.

Then someone knocked on the front door.

Three slow knocks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Everyone in the room froze.

Emmanuel whispered, “They found us.”

Another knock came.

This time, a familiar voice spoke from the other side of the door.

“Amara.”

Her eyes widened.

She knew the voice.

It was Kelechi.

“Amara, please open the door.”

Amara rushed toward the door.

Daniel grabbed her before she could reach it.

“Don't!”

“That's Kelechi!”

“I know.”

“Then why won't you let me open the door?”

Daniel looked terrified.

“Because Kelechi is not supposed to be here.”

Amara stared at him.

Then the voice outside spoke again.

“Amara...”

But this time, the voice sounded different.

It was deeper.

Colder.

And suddenly, Amara realized something that made her blood run cold.

The person outside was pretending to be Kelechi.

Emmanuel quietly locked the door.

Daniel pulled Amara away from it.

Then the voice outside laughed.

“You should have listened to your mother.”

Amara looked at Daniel.

Her mother's warning echoed in her mind.

If Daniel tells you that your father is waiting for you, don't go to him.

Then Emmanuel whispered something that made Amara's heart stop.

“Your father isn't the one we should be afraid of.”

Amara stared at him.

“Then who are we running from?”

Emmanuel looked toward the locked door.

“The man who made your father disappear.”

Outside, footsteps slowly moved away from the house.

But before they disappeared completely, the stranger left one final message.

“Tell Amara her father is waiting for her.”

Daniel closed his eyes.

Amara looked at him.

For the first time, she wondered whether her mother's warning had been written because her mother knew Daniel would eventually find her.

And perhaps the most frightening question was no longer where her father was.

It was why Daniel had been searching for her in the first place.
