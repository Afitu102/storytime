🛍️ First Day in Village Market

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌅 Chapter One – Mercy Leaves Abuja

Mercy had lived in Abuja for many years.

She was the kind of woman people looked at and wondered how her life was so easy.

She had a good job.

She was a manager in a big oil company.

She lived in a beautiful house.

She had a car.

She dressed well.

And when Mercy woke up in the morning, breakfast was already waiting for her.

Lunch?

Already taken care of.

Dinner?

Her maid knew what to do.

Mercy would wake up, take her bath, dress for work, eat her food and leave.

That was her life.

Simple.

Comfortable.

But there was one small problem.

Mercy knew almost nothing about the things inside her own kitchen.

She knew how to cook.

Of course she did.

Her mother had taught her many years ago.

But cooking was not the same thing as knowing how to run a kitchen.

Mercy didn't know where her rice came from.

She didn't know how much a basket of tomatoes cost.

She didn't know the price of pepper.

She didn't know which market sold the best vegetables.

She didn't even know how to choose good tomatoes.

If the food finished, she simply told her maid,

"Please get more."

And that was it.

If there was no cooking oil, the maid bought it.

If there was no rice, the maid bought it.

If there was no meat, the maid bought it.

Mercy never bothered herself.

She was always busy.

Work had swallowed almost all her time.

And Mercy was single.

She had no husband.

No children.

Just her career, her house and her peaceful little routine.

Until one day, something happened that Mercy never expected.

She met a man.

His name was Daniel.

He was from Alaba village.

At first, Mercy did not think much about him.

She had met many men before.

But Daniel was different.

He did not rush her.

He did not care about her position at the oil company.

He did not spend every conversation talking about her money.

He simply wanted to know her.

And somehow, Mercy started looking forward to seeing him.

One conversation became another.

One meeting became many.

And before Mercy knew what was happening...

She had fallen in love.

Daniel eventually took her to Alaba village.

That was where Mercy began to understand that loving someone also meant learning about the person's home, family and culture.

Daniel's family welcomed her.

They laughed with her.

They asked her questions.

And after some time, the marriage proposal came.

Mercy's heart was full.

She had never imagined herself getting married like this.

Everything looked beautiful.

Until one evening, Daniel's mother said something that changed everything.

"Before the marriage, there is something Mercy must do."

Mercy smiled.

She thought it would be something simple.

Maybe a family meeting.

Maybe some traditional clothes.

Maybe some gifts.

Then Daniel's mother continued.

"She will go to the market and buy the food stuffs for the meal."

Mercy smiled.

"Okay."

She didn't see the problem.

Until they told her she had to go herself.

Not her maid.

Not a driver.

Not anybody else.

Mercy herself.

She would have to enter the market.

Choose the ingredients.

Ask for the prices.

Buy everything herself.

And prepare the meal.

Mercy nodded.

Inside her mind, she was already thinking,

"How hard can it be?"

She had money.

She had cooked before.

She could simply go there and buy what she needed.

But then Daniel's mother added one more thing.

"You must not use your own money."

Mercy looked at her.

"Not my money?"

"No."

"You must not send your maid either."

Mercy became quiet.

Then Daniel's mother brought out five thousand naira.

She placed the money in Mercy's hand.

"This is what you will use."

Mercy looked at the money.

Five thousand naira.

She looked at Daniel.

Daniel was trying hard not to laugh.

Mercy gave him a serious look.

"Why are you looking at me like that?"

Daniel quickly looked away.

"I'm not looking at you."

"You are laughing."

"No."

"You are."

Daniel finally laughed.

Mercy shook her head.

"Just wait. I will show all of you that I know how to buy food."

Everybody laughed.

But Mercy had no idea what was waiting for her.

That night, she tried to make a mental list of everything she needed.

Rice.

Tomatoes.

Pepper.

Onions.

Meat.

Oil.

And then she stopped.

What else?

She tried to remember.

"Ah..."

She couldn't remember the name of one of the ingredients.

She called her maid.

"What do we normally put in the stew?"

Her maid looked at her.

"Madam?"

"I mean... apart from tomato and pepper."

The maid started laughing.

Mercy frowned.

"Why are you laughing?"

"Nothing, madam."

"Tell me."

"Madam, you have been eating my food for years."

Mercy laughed too.

"Just tell me the name."

The maid told her.

Mercy quickly wrote it down.

The next morning, she dressed up.

She picked up her handbag.

She collected the five thousand naira.

And she stood at the door.

Her maid looked at her.

"Madam, should I come with you?"

Mercy shook her head.

"No."

"Are you sure?"

"Very sure."

Mercy took a deep breath.

"I can do this."

She entered her car.

And drove toward the market.

She had no idea that within the next few hours, almost everybody in that market would know that Mercy had never really gone shopping before.

And the funniest part?

Mercy herself was about to find that out.

When she finally stepped into the busy village market, she looked around.

Tomatoes everywhere.

Pepper everywhere.

Women shouting.

Men carrying baskets.

People bargaining.

Children running around.

And Mercy just stood there.

She held her five thousand naira tightly.

Then she whispered to herself,

"Where do I even start?"

She took a few steps forward.

A woman called out to her.

"Madam, come and buy tomato!"

Mercy turned.

Another woman shouted,

"Fresh pepper here!"

Another one called,

"Madam, come and see my onions!"

Mercy looked from one seller to another.

She didn't know who to answer.

She didn't even know how much anything cost.

Then she remembered something.

She had forgotten the name of another ingredient.

She opened her handbag.

Her list was not there.

Mercy searched again.

Nothing.

She checked her phone.

Nothing.

She stood there quietly.

Then a young boy passing beside her looked at her face.

He smiled.

"Madam..."

Mercy looked at him.

"Yes?"

He tilted his head.

"Is this your first time in a market?"

Mercy's mouth opened.

She looked around.

Then she looked at the boy again.

And that was when she realized...

She had just embarrassed herself in the middle of the market.

But that was only the beginning.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🛍️ Chapter Two – Mercy Meets the Market

"Is this your first time in a market?"

The young boy asked again.

Mercy looked at him.

She wanted to say no.

She wanted to tell him that she had been shopping since she was a little girl.

But one look around the market made her change her mind.

"Yes."

The boy smiled.

Mercy quickly added,

"Well... not exactly."

The boy laughed.

"Madam, you don't know where to start."

Mercy looked at him seriously.

"Are you a market inspector?"

The boy laughed even harder.

"No, madam."

"Then mind your business."

The boy nodded and walked away.

But he didn't go far.

He turned around and looked at Mercy again.

Mercy was still standing in the same place.

She looked at the tomatoes.

Then the onions.

Then the pepper.

Then the tomatoes again.

She whispered,

"Okay, Mercy. You are a manager. You manage people every day. You can manage a market."

She walked toward the first tomato seller.

"How much is this?"

The woman looked at her.

"Five hundred."

Mercy nodded.

"Okay."

She picked up the tomatoes.

Then she stopped.

"Five hundred for all?"

The woman laughed.

"No, my dear. Five hundred for this small basket."

Mercy looked at the basket.

It wasn't even full.

She slowly put it down.

"Ah."

The woman smiled.

"How much do you want?"

Mercy thought about it.

"Give me... one hundred naira."

The woman stared at her.

"One hundred?"

"Yes."

The woman started laughing.

"Madam, what will you cook with one hundred naira tomato?"

Mercy looked confused.

"I don't know."

The woman laughed again.

"Come. Let me help you."

She picked some tomatoes and put them inside a small nylon bag.

"Give me two hundred."

Mercy brought out the money.

She looked at the notes carefully.

Then she handed over two hundred naira.

"Good."

She put the tomatoes inside her bag.

One item down.

Mercy smiled.

"This isn't so hard."

She walked away.

Then she heard someone shout.

"Madam!"

Mercy turned.

"You forgot your change!"

She quickly returned.

The woman handed her the change.

Mercy laughed.

"I was testing you."

The woman laughed.

"Testing me?"

"Yes."

Mercy took the money and walked away before the woman could answer.

She reached the pepper seller.

"How much is pepper?"

"Two hundred."

Mercy nodded.

"Give me one."

The seller looked at her.

"One what?"

Mercy paused.

She suddenly realized she had no idea how pepper was sold.

"Just... give me the one that is enough."

The seller smiled.

"Enough for what?"

Mercy looked at her.

"Food."

The woman laughed.

"Madam, all of us are buying food."

Mercy laughed too.

"Please help me."

The woman picked some pepper.

Mercy paid her.

She moved to onions.

Then garlic.

Then ginger.

Then she stopped.

"Ginger..."

She stared at the ginger.

Something about it looked familiar.

But she couldn't remember if she needed it.

She checked her phone again.

Nothing.

She called her maid.

The phone rang.

"Hello, madam."

"Please, what else do we normally put in the food?"

There was silence.

"Madam?"

"Yes."

"Are you still in the market?"

Mercy looked around.

"Yes."

Her maid started laughing.

"Madam, please don't tell anybody I am your maid."

"Why?"

"Because they will laugh at me."

Mercy laughed.

"Just tell me what I need."

Her maid began mentioning the ingredients one after another.

Mercy quickly tried to remember them.

"Wait, wait!"

She brought out a pen.

"Say it again."

The people around her began looking at her.

Mercy didn't care anymore.

She was determined to finish the shopping.

After some time, she ended the call.

She looked inside her bag.

Tomatoes.

Pepper.

Onions.

Ginger.

Garlic.

A little oil.

And a few other ingredients.

She smiled.

"I am doing well."

Then she remembered the meat.

She walked toward the meat sellers.

The smell hit her immediately.

Mercy stopped.

There were different types of meat everywhere.

She looked at one seller.

"How much is meat?"

The woman answered.

"Three thousand."

Mercy's eyes widened.

"Three thousand?"

"Yes."

Mercy looked inside her purse.

She had already spent part of the five thousand.

She quickly calculated what she had left.

"Madam..."

The meat seller looked at her.

"I have only..."

Mercy counted the money again.

She looked at the meat.

Then at the money.

Then back at the meat.

She whispered,

"How am I supposed to buy everything with this money?"

The meat seller smiled.

"Who told you to buy everything here?"

Mercy looked at her.

"What do you mean?"

The woman pointed toward another side of the market.

"Go there. You will get what you need for less."

Mercy thanked her.

She picked up her bags and walked away.

But she had barely taken a few steps when she heard a familiar voice.

"Madam!"

Mercy turned.

It was the same young boy from earlier.

He was standing beside an older woman.

The woman looked at Mercy.

Then she looked at the bags in Mercy's hands.

She smiled.

"Come."

Mercy walked toward them.

The woman looked at her list.

Then she looked at the money Mercy had left.

She shook her head.

"My daughter..."

Mercy became worried.

"Yes?"

"Who sent you to this market?"

Mercy smiled.

"My future husband's family."

The woman's face suddenly changed.

She looked at Mercy carefully.

"Your future husband is from Alaba village?"

Mercy nodded.

"Yes."

The woman smiled.

"Ah..."

She looked at the boy beside her.

Then back at Mercy.

"Now I understand."

Mercy became curious.

"Understand what?"

The woman smiled again.

"Come with me. If you go back home with what you have bought now, your future mother-in-law will ask you a question you may not be ready for."

Mercy's smile disappeared.

"What question?"

The woman picked up Mercy's bag.

"First, let us finish your shopping."

Mercy followed her.

But she kept thinking about the woman's words.

What exactly did she mean?

And what was Mercy about to discover about the tradition she thought was just a simple market trip?
