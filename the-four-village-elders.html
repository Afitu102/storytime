🔥 The Four Village Elders

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌙 Chapter One – The Four Elders by the Fire

Every village has a secret.

Some secrets are small.

Some secrets can make people laugh.

But the secret kept by the people of Umu-Obi was different.

It was a secret that had been sitting quietly in the village for many years.

Nobody knew it.

Not the young men.

Not the women.

Not even the children who were always watching everything that happened around them.

The secret belonged to four old men.

They were known as the four elders of the village.

Their names were Elder Nwafor, Elder Chukwuma, Elder Eze, and Elder Maduka.

They were not brothers by blood.

They did not come from the same family.

But the people of the village respected them because of their age, wisdom, and the many years they had spent serving their people.

During the day, the four elders lived like ordinary men.

Elder Nwafor was a farmer.

Elder Chukwuma was known for settling quarrels.

Elder Eze was a keeper of old village stories.

Elder Maduka was a man who understood the land, the rivers, and the seasons better than almost anyone else.

When people saw them during the day, they saw four old men going about their normal lives.

But when night came, something strange happened.

The four men would quietly leave their homes.

They would walk away from the village.

They would pass the last houses.

They would pass the farms.

They would continue until they reached an open place near the edge of the forest.

There was an old tree there.

The tree was large.

Its branches spread across the night sky.

The elders had been meeting there for many years.

Nobody knew why.

Every night they went there, they carried dry wood.

They would arrange the wood in the middle of the open ground.

Then they would light a fire.

The fire would burn brightly against the darkness.

The sound of the fire could be heard from a distance.

Sometimes the flames would rise high.

Sometimes they would become small.

But the four elders were always there.

They would sit around the fire.

They would look at one another.

Then they would begin to talk.

The villagers thought they were simply old men who had chosen a quiet place to talk.

Some believed they went there to remember the past.

Others believed they were sharing old stories.

Some people even laughed and said the elders were afraid of their wives and needed a place where nobody could hear them.

The elders never explained themselves.

Whenever somebody asked why they always disappeared at night, they only smiled.

"Old men need fresh air," Elder Maduka would say.

That answer was enough to make people laugh.

Nobody asked again.

But there was something the village did not know.

The four elders were not going to the forest simply to talk.

They were not going there to gossip.

They were not going there to complain about their children.

They were not going there to remember the days of their youth.

They were going there to speak with powers that no ordinary person could see.

The first time it happened, the four elders were still young men.

It was many years before the story became known among them.

There had been a terrible season in the village.

The rain had stopped.

The farms were becoming dry.

The river was getting smaller.

People were worried.

Food was becoming difficult to find.

Children were going to bed hungry.

The elders of that time had gathered to find a solution.

One night, they went to the forest to pray.

They sat under the same old tree.

They lit a fire.

They prayed for the village.

Then something happened.

The wind stopped.

The fire became strangely quiet.

The sounds of the forest disappeared.

Even the insects became silent.

The four men looked at one another.

Then they heard a voice.

It did not come from the tree.

It did not come from the forest.

It seemed to come from everywhere around them.

"Why do you ask only for yourselves?"

The men were afraid.

Nobody moved.

The voice spoke again.

"Ask for the people."

The four men bowed their heads.

They asked for rain.

They asked for food.

They asked for wisdom.

They asked for peace.

They asked that the village should not suffer.

Before the night ended, the voice told them something they never forgot.

"A village grows when its people think beyond themselves."

The next morning, clouds covered the sky.

Rain fell.

It rained for days.

The farms came alive again.

The river became full.

The people were happy.

The four men knew what had happened.

From that night, they continued meeting.

Years passed.

The four men became old.

The village changed.

Children grew into adults.

New houses were built.

New farms appeared.

But the meetings never stopped.

The four elders had become the keepers of the secret.

And on this particular night, they once again walked into the darkness.

The moon was hidden behind thick clouds.

Only a few stars could be seen.

The forest was quiet.

Elder Nwafor arrived first.

He carried a bundle of dry wood on his shoulder.

He dropped it beside the old tree.

A few minutes later, Elder Chukwuma arrived.

Then Elder Eze.

Finally, Elder Maduka came.

Nobody spoke for a while.

They prepared the fire.

Soon the flames rose.

Orange light touched their old faces.

Elder Eze looked into the fire.

"The village is changing," he said.

Elder Nwafor nodded.

"It is."

"We have more children now," Elder Chukwuma said.

"And more problems," Elder Maduka added.

They became quiet again.

This was why they had come.

Every meeting had a reason.

Sometimes it was about the farms.

Sometimes it was about the river.

Sometimes it was about sickness.

Sometimes it was about disagreements between families.

Sometimes it was about the future of the children.

They believed the village had been given a special responsibility.

They did not want to become rich while their people suffered.

They did not want power for themselves.

They wanted the village to live in peace.

They wanted the children to have food.

They wanted the farms to produce.

They wanted the hunters to return safely.

They wanted the women to trade without fear.

They wanted the young men to find honest work.

And most of all, they wanted the village to remain united.

Elder Maduka lifted his eyes toward the dark sky.

"It is time."

The other three elders nodded.

They joined hands.

The fire suddenly became brighter.

The wind began to move around them.

The leaves above them started shaking.

But the fire itself did not move.

It remained straight.

Then a deep voice came from the darkness.

"You have come again."

The four elders bowed their heads.

"We have come," Elder Nwafor answered.

"What troubles your people?"

The elders began to speak.

They talked about the farms.

They talked about the river.

They talked about the young people leaving the village.

They talked about families fighting over land.

They talked about the fear of wild animals near the farms.

They talked about the future.

The voices listened.

Sometimes the powers answered.

Sometimes they remained silent.

The elders had learned that silence could also be an answer.

That night, they were given instructions.

They were told where to build a new water path.

They were told when to plant.

They were warned about a coming storm.

They were told to settle a disagreement before it became a serious fight.

The four elders listened carefully.

No one else could hear the voices.

No one else could see the strange presence around the fire.

To the village, it was simply four old men sitting outside at night.

But to the four elders, it was much more.

They were speaking with powers they believed watched over their people.

And every answer they received had to be used wisely.

They could never tell the village everything.

They had made a promise.

The secret would remain with them.

When the meeting ended, the fire became small.

The wind stopped.

The forest became quiet again.

The four elders looked at one another.

Elder Chukwuma smiled.

"We have work to do tomorrow."

Elder Eze laughed softly.

"We always do."

They covered the fire.

They picked up their walking sticks.

Then they walked back toward the sleeping village.

Behind them, the old tree stood silently under the stars.

Nobody in the village knew what had happened that night.

Nobody knew that four elders had just spoken about the future.

Nobody knew that the answers they received would soon change the village.

And nobody knew that the secret under that old tree was about to become more important than ever.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🔥 Chapter Two – The Fire That Knew Their Names

The next morning, the village woke up as usual.

The women swept their compounds.

The farmers picked up their hoes.

The young men went to the farms.

Children ran through the narrow paths between the houses.

The sound of people talking filled the village.

Nobody knew what had happened under the old tree the night before.

Nobody knew that the four elders had spoken with the mysterious powers.

To everyone else, it was just another night.

But the four elders knew that something had changed.

They had received a warning.

A strong wind was coming.

The river would rise.

Some farms near the low ground would be destroyed if nothing was done.

The elders could not sit and wait.

They had to act.

That morning, Elder Nwafor went straight to the village square.

He found some young men sitting under a tree.

"My sons," he called.

They stood up.

"Good morning, Elder."

"Go to the farms near the river."

"Why?"

"Move the people away from the low ground."

One of the young men looked confused.

"There is no rain."

Elder Nwafor looked at him.

"Do what I said."

The young man nodded.

Soon, the message spread.

Families living near the river began moving their baskets, tools, animals, and food to higher ground.

Some people complained.

Some laughed.

Others said the elders were becoming too old and too afraid.

But the four elders did not argue with anyone.

They simply continued with their work.

By evening, the sky had changed.

Dark clouds appeared from the west.

The wind became strong.

Then the rain came.

It fell hard.

It fell through the night.

The river rose quickly.

Water covered the low ground.

Some farms were destroyed.

But the families had already moved.

No child was lost.

No family member was trapped.

The village survived the storm.

The next morning, people gathered around the elders.

"How did you know?"

Elder Nwafor only smiled.

"We watched the sky."

That answer satisfied nobody.

The villagers knew the elders were old.

They knew the elders understood the land.

But this was different.

They had warned everyone before the storm.

Still, nobody knew their real secret.

That night, the four elders returned to the old tree.

The ground was still wet.

Small streams of water ran between the grass.

Elder Maduka touched the soil with his walking stick.

"Our people are safe."

Elder Eze nodded.

"Because we listened."

They built the fire again.

The flames rose slowly.

For a while, nobody spoke.

Then Elder Chukwuma looked at the fire.

"Will they ever know?"

Elder Nwafor shook his head.

"They do not need to know everything."

"But they may think we are hiding something."

"We are."

The four elders laughed.

Then the laughter stopped.

The fire suddenly changed.

The flames became tall.

A blue light appeared in the middle of the fire.

The four elders became silent.

They knew what it meant.

The visitors had arrived.

A soft voice came from the darkness.

"You protected the people."

Elder Nwafor bowed.

"They are our people."

"You listened."

"We must listen."

The voice became quiet.

Then another voice spoke.

"Your village will grow."

The elders looked at one another.

"How?"

"Teach the young."

The four elders listened carefully.

"Knowledge that stays with one man dies with him."

"Knowledge that is shared becomes a village."

Those words stayed in their minds.

The elders understood.

Their secret meetings were not only about asking for help.

They also had to prepare the next generation.

They could not remain young forever.

One day, their walking sticks would be left behind.

One day, their voices would become silent.

One day, another group of elders would have to sit around that same fire.

Elder Eze looked into the flames.

"Then we must begin."

The voice answered.

"Begin with the children."

The fire became quiet again.

The strange presence disappeared.

The elders remained seated.

They did not speak for a long time.

Finally, Elder Maduka smiled.

"I know what I will do."

"What?"

"I will teach the young farmers."

Elder Chukwuma smiled.

"I will teach the young men how to settle quarrels."

Elder Eze raised his hand.

"I will teach the children our stories."

Elder Nwafor laughed.

"And I will teach them how to listen."

The four elders laughed around the fire.

They knew that the work ahead would not be easy.

But they also knew they were not alone.

The next few weeks brought changes to the village.

The young farmers began working together.

They repaired the paths around the farms.

They cleared the blocked water channels.

They helped older people with their farms.

The women also began meeting more often.

They shared ideas about trading.

They helped one another during difficult times.

The children started gathering around Elder Eze.

Every evening, he told them stories.

Some were funny.

Some were frightening.

Some taught them about honesty.

Some taught them about patience.

Some taught them why a person should never forget where he came from.

Slowly, the village began to change.

People started working together.

The farms became better.

The market became busier.

The young people began taking their work seriously.

Visitors started coming from nearby villages.

They wondered why Umu-Obi was becoming more peaceful and successful.

The villagers simply said,

"We have good elders."

That was all they knew.

But every night, under the old tree, the four elders continued their secret meetings.

They spoke about what had happened during the day.

They spoke about the people.

They spoke about problems that had not yet reached the village.

And sometimes, when the fire burned strangely bright, they knew the unseen visitors had returned.

One night, however, the voice that came from the darkness sounded different.

It was deeper.

It carried a warning.

"Something is coming."

The elders became still.

Elder Nwafor leaned forward.

"What is coming?"

The fire became taller.

"A stranger."

The elders looked at one another.

"Who is he?"

The voice did not answer.

Instead, it said,

"His arrival will test the wisdom of your village."

The flames suddenly went down.

The forest became silent.

The four elders remained around the fire.

None of them spoke.

They knew the village was entering another chapter.

And this time, wisdom alone might not be enough.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🪶 Chapter Three – The Stranger at the Village Gate

Three days passed after the four elders received the warning.

Life in Umu-Obi continued as usual.

The farmers went to their farms.

The women went to the market.

The children went to learn and play.

The young men worked around the village.

Nobody knew that the four elders were waiting for someone.

Every morning, Elder Nwafor would look toward the village gate.

Elder Chukwuma noticed it.

"You are looking for someone."

Elder Nwafor smiled.

"Maybe."

"Is it the stranger?"

"Maybe."

The two old men laughed.

But deep inside, they were not laughing.

They remembered the warning.

A stranger would come.

His arrival would test the wisdom of the village.

They did not know his name.

They did not know where he was coming from.

They did not know what he wanted.

They only knew that he was coming.

On the fourth morning, a young boy came running into the village square.

"Elders!"

Everyone turned to him.

The boy was breathing heavily.

"What happened?" Elder Eze asked.

"There is a man at the village gate."

The four elders looked at one another.

"Where is he from?"

"I don't know."

"What does he want?"

"I don't know."

"Go and tell him to wait."

The boy ran back.

Soon, people began gathering near the gate.

Nobody wanted to miss the arrival of a stranger.

Some women stood behind their doors.

Young men gathered near the road.

Children tried to get closer.

Then the stranger appeared.

He was a tall man.

He carried a brown bag over his shoulder.

His clothes were old.

Dust covered his feet.

He looked like a man who had travelled for many days.

He stopped at the gate.

Elder Nwafor walked toward him.

"Welcome."

The stranger bowed his head.

"Thank you."

"What is your name?"

"My name is Okechukwu."

"Where are you coming from?"

The stranger looked toward the road behind him.

"Far away."

Some villagers laughed.

Elder Chukwuma frowned.

"Far away is not a village."

Okechukwu smiled.

"I know."

"Then tell us where you came from."

The stranger looked at the four elders.

"I came from a village that no longer exists."

The laughter stopped.

Everyone became quiet.

Elder Eze stepped forward.

"What happened to the village?"

Okechukwu looked at the ground.

"People stopped listening to one another."

Nobody spoke.

"The elders fought."

"The young people stopped respecting them."

"The families stopped helping one another."

"Then one problem came after another."

Okechukwu looked up.

"Now there is almost nothing left."

Elder Maduka looked at the other elders.

They understood the warning.

The stranger was not an ordinary visitor.

Something about his story was connected to the message they had received.

Elder Nwafor invited him inside.

"You may stay in our village for some days."

Okechukwu bowed.

"Thank you."

The villagers returned to their work.

But many of them continued talking about the stranger.

Some said he looked dangerous.

Some said he looked poor.

Others said he might be hiding something.

One young man even said,

"Why should we allow a stranger to stay here?"

Elder Chukwuma heard him.

"Because tomorrow, you may also become a stranger somewhere."

The young man became quiet.

That evening, Okechukwu was given food.

He sat with some villagers.

He ate quietly.

He did not ask for more.

He thanked everyone.

A young woman watched him.

She noticed something strange.

The stranger never looked directly into the fire.

Whenever the flames rose, he moved his eyes away.

She wondered why.

Nobody knew.

That night, the four elders met again.

They walked to the old tree.

The fire was lit.

They sat around it.

Elder Maduka spoke first.

"The stranger has arrived."

Elder Eze nodded.

"And he carries pain with him."

Elder Chukwuma looked into the flames.

"But is he the danger?"

Nobody answered.

The fire began to crackle.

A cold wind moved through the trees.

Then the flames became blue.

The four elders lowered their heads.

The unseen visitors had come.

Elder Nwafor spoke.

"We have received the stranger."

A voice answered.

"Good."

"Is he the one we were warned about?"

Silence.

Elder Nwafor waited.

Then the voice said,

"Every stranger carries a story."

The elders listened.

"Some strangers bring trouble."

"Some bring wisdom."

"Some bring both."

Elder Eze looked at the fire.

"What should we do?"

The voice answered.

"Watch his actions."

"Do not judge his face."

"Do not judge his clothes."

"Do not judge his past."

"Watch what he does when he is given power."

The elders became still.

"Power?"

The voice became quiet.

Then it said,

"Give him a chance to help."

The blue light disappeared.

The fire returned to normal.

Elder Chukwuma looked at his friends.

"So that is the test."

Elder Maduka nodded.

"We must give him a chance."

The next morning, Elder Nwafor took Okechukwu to the farms.

"I want you to help us here."

Okechukwu looked at the fields.

"What should I do?"

"Help the farmers clear the water path."

Okechukwu dropped his bag.

He picked up a tool.

He worked.

He did not complain.

He did not act like a visitor.

He worked beside the young men.

By afternoon, the water path was clear.

The young men were surprised.

One of them said,

"This man can work."

Another laughed.

"He works more than some of us."

Okechukwu smiled.

"I am only doing what I know."

The elders watched from a distance.

Elder Nwafor smiled.

"His hands are honest."

But that was not the end of the test.

A few days later, something happened at the market.

Two traders began fighting over a piece of land.

Their voices became loud.

People gathered around them.

One man pushed the other.

The situation was becoming dangerous.

Okechukwu walked toward them.

"Stop."

Nobody listened.

He raised his voice.

"Stop!"

Everyone became quiet.

Okechukwu looked at the two men.

"Tell me what happened."

They both began talking at the same time.

He raised his hand.

"One at a time."

They listened.

He heard both sides.

Then he asked the elders to come.

The four elders arrived.

Okechukwu explained the problem.

He did not choose a side.

He only helped them understand one another.

The elders checked the old records.

They found the truth.

The land belonged to both families.

The two men had misunderstood an old agreement.

They apologized to each other.

The market returned to peace.

That evening, Elder Chukwuma said something to the other elders.

"The stranger may not be the danger."

Elder Eze nodded.

"Maybe he is part of the answer."

But before they could say more, a loud sound came from the forest.

The villagers stopped what they were doing.

It sounded like a large animal.

Then they heard another sound.

And another.

The animals were moving closer.

The four elders looked toward the forest.

Okechukwu also looked.

His face suddenly changed.

He knew that sound.

He had heard it before.

But he did not tell anyone what it meant.

He only turned toward the elders.

"We need to move the children away from the forest."

Elder Nwafor looked at him carefully.

"Why?"

Okechukwu looked toward the dark trees.

"Because something is coming."

And once again, the four elders remembered the warning they had received under the old tree.

The stranger had arrived.

And now the real test was beginning.
