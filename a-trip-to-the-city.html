🚌 A Trip to the City

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌅 Chapter One – The Man Who Wanted More

In a quiet village called Akwete, there lived a man named Daniel.

Daniel was a farmer.

Every morning before the sun came out fully, he was already on his farm.

He planted cassava.

He planted yam.

He carried baskets.

He worked until his hands became rough.

But no matter how hard he worked, life always seemed to remain the same.

His wife, Grace, noticed that Daniel had become quiet.

One evening, she found him sitting outside their small house, looking toward the road that led out of the village.

"Daniel, what are you looking at?"

Daniel did not answer immediately.

He looked at his wife and sighed.

"Grace, I am tired."

She sat beside him.

"Tired of what?"

Daniel looked at the farm in the distance.

"Tired of working so hard and having nothing to show for it."

Grace became quiet.

She knew what he meant.

The harvest had been poor that year.

The children needed school things.

There was hardly enough money for food, clothes, and other needs.

Daniel looked toward the road again.

"I want to go to the city."

Grace looked at him.

"The city?"

Daniel nodded.

"Yes."

"I hear there is work there."

"People make money there."

"Maybe I can find something that will change our lives."

Grace held his hand.

"But Daniel, what if the city is not what you think?"

Daniel smiled.

"Then I will learn."

His oldest son, Samuel, had been listening from the doorway.

"Papa, are you leaving us?"

Daniel turned around.

He called his son closer.

Samuel walked over and sat beside him.

"I am not leaving you forever."

"I am going to look for a better life for all of us."

His younger son, David, came running from inside.

"Will you bring me a big toy from the city?"

Everyone laughed.

Daniel pulled him close.

"If I find one, I will bring it."

Grace smiled, but her heart was not completely happy.

She knew Daniel was determined.

The next morning, Daniel packed a few clothes into a small bag.

He carried some food for the road.

His wife helped him tie the bag properly.

The children stood nearby watching him.

Before he left, Daniel looked at his family for a long moment.

"Take care of yourselves."

Grace nodded.

"And you too."

Daniel hugged his wife.

He hugged Samuel.

Then he lifted little David into his arms.

"I will come back."

David held his father's neck tightly.

"Don't stay too long."

Daniel smiled.

"I won't."

He finally put David down.

Then he started walking.

Grace stood at the entrance of the house.

The children stood beside her.

They watched Daniel walk down the dusty road with his small bag on his shoulder.

He turned around one last time.

He raised his hand.

Grace raised hers too.

Then Daniel continued toward the city.

He did not know how long the journey would take.

He did not know what the city had waiting for him.

But inside his heart was one strong hope.

He wanted to return home one day with something better for his family.

And that morning, Daniel began the journey that would change his life forever.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🚌 Chapter Two – The Road to the City

Daniel walked for a long time before he reached the main road.

The morning sun was already getting hot.

His small bag was on his shoulder, and the little food his wife packed for him was tucked safely inside.

He looked back at the village one more time.

Everything looked small from where he stood.

The farms.

The houses.

The narrow roads.

Even the sound of the village seemed far away.

"Daniel!"

He turned.

It was his old friend, Michael, running toward him.

"Are you really going to the city?"

Daniel smiled.

"Yes."

"I have made up my mind."

Michael looked at the small bag on his shoulder.

"Do you know anybody there?"

Daniel shook his head.

"Not really."

Michael became quiet.

"Then be careful."

Daniel nodded.

"I will."

A bus soon arrived.

Daniel climbed inside.

He found a seat near the window.

As the bus moved, he watched the village disappear behind him.

He thought about Grace.

He thought about Samuel.

He thought about little David asking for a toy.

Daniel smiled.

"I must make this work."

The journey became longer than he expected.

The road became busy.

There were cars everywhere.

There were buses.

There were traders carrying baskets.

There were people rushing in every direction.

Daniel looked through the window in amazement.

"So this is the city."

When he finally arrived, the noise surprised him.

Cars were moving everywhere.

People were shouting.

Drivers were calling passengers.

Traders were selling things along the road.

Daniel stood with his bag in his hand.

For a moment, he did not know where to go.

A young man named Peter noticed him.

"Brother, you look like you are new here."

Daniel smiled.

"I am."

Peter laughed.

"I knew it."

"Where are you going?"

Daniel mentioned the name of the area where he hoped to find work.

Peter pointed toward a nearby bus stop.

"Take that bus."

"It will take you closer."

Daniel thanked him.

The city was very different from home.

Everything seemed to move quickly.

Nobody seemed to have time to stand and talk.

Daniel finally reached the place he had been told about.

He found a small room he could afford.

It was not much.

The room was hot.

The window was small.

The bed was old.

But Daniel looked around and smiled.

"This is where I will start."

That evening, he called Grace.

"Hello, my wife."

Grace's voice became happy immediately.

"Daniel! You have arrived?"

"Yes."

"How is the city?"

Daniel looked through the window.

"It is very big."

Grace laughed.

"Are you scared?"

Daniel smiled.

"A little."

Grace became quiet for a moment.

"Please take care of yourself."

"I will."

Before they ended the call, David shouted from the background.

"Papa! Don't forget my toy!"

Daniel laughed.

"I remember!"

The call ended.

Daniel placed the phone beside his bed.

The room became quiet.

He looked at the ceiling.

Tomorrow, he would start looking for work.

He did not know that the city would test his patience.

He did not know that some days would be difficult.

He did not know that there would be moments when he would miss home more than anything.

But he had made his decision.

He had come to the city looking for a better life.

And he was ready to begin.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🏙️ Chapter Three – The City Was Not Easy

The first few weeks in the city were not easy for Daniel.

Every morning, he woke up early and went out looking for work.

He asked at building sites.

He asked at shops.

He asked at restaurants.

He asked people on the streets.

Sometimes they told him to come back later.

Sometimes they simply shook their heads.

One afternoon, Daniel sat under a tree beside a busy road.

His feet were hurting.

He had walked almost the whole day.

He looked at the few coins in his pocket.

"How will I survive like this?"

He thought about going back home.

Then he remembered Grace.

He remembered his children.

He remembered the reason he came.

Daniel stood up again.

"I cannot give up yet."

The next morning, he went to a small warehouse.

A man named Mr. Anthony Cole was looking for workers.

Daniel walked up to him.

"Sir, I am looking for work."

Anthony looked at him.

"What can you do?"

Daniel thought for a moment.

"I can farm."

Anthony smiled.

"This is a warehouse, not a farm."

Daniel smiled too.

"I know, sir."

"But I can work hard."

Anthony looked at him again.

Something about Daniel's answer made him interested.

"Come tomorrow morning."

Daniel could hardly believe what he heard.

"Thank you, sir."

He hurried back to his room.

That evening, he called Grace.

"Grace, I found work."

Her voice became excited.

"Really?"

"Yes."

The children shouted in the background.

"Papa has work!"

Daniel laughed.

For the first time since arriving in the city, he felt hopeful.

The work was difficult.

He carried heavy boxes.

He cleaned the warehouse.

He worked long hours.

But Daniel did not complain.

He knew where he came from.

He knew what his family needed.

Every month, he sent part of his salary home.

Grace used it carefully.

She bought food.

She paid the children's school needs.

She kept some money aside.

Daniel kept very little for himself.

Sometimes he ate simple food for days.

Sometimes he could not afford the things he wanted.

But whenever he felt discouraged, he remembered his family.

One evening, Peter visited him.

"Daniel, you have been working too hard."

Daniel laughed.

"I came here to work."

Peter shook his head.

"Yes, but you also need to think bigger."

Daniel looked at him.

"What do you mean?"

Peter told him about a small transport business he was starting with another friend.

"You know farming."

"You know how to work with your hands."

"You are good with people."

"Maybe one day you can start something of your own."

Daniel thought about it.

He had never imagined himself as a businessman.

But the idea stayed in his mind.

Months passed.

Daniel continued working.

He saved little by little.

He learned how the city worked.

He met different people.

He learned from his mistakes.

He became more confident.

Back home, Grace and the children missed him terribly.

David still asked about his father every evening.

"When is Papa coming home?"

Grace would smile.

"Papa is working."

But as the months passed, the phone calls became less frequent.

Daniel's work became more demanding.

Sometimes he had no money for airtime.

Sometimes his phone stopped working.

Sometimes he was simply too tired.

Grace kept waiting.

She believed he would call.

She believed he would return.

But she did not know that the next call would take much longer than she expected.

Daniel was about to enter the hardest part of his journey.
