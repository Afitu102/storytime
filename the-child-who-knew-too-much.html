🕯️ The Child Who Knew Too Much

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🕯️ Chapter One – The Child Who Knew Too Much

Every evening, the women of Mambala village gathered around the cooking fire.

They talked while they peeled yam, roasted corn, and stirred big pots of soup.

Children played nearby.

Some chased one another.

Others sat quietly, hoping someone would give them a piece of roasted plantain.

But one child never played with the others.

His name was Kweku.

He was ten years old.

And Kweku had a strange habit.

He listened to grown-ups.

Not because he wanted to be rude.

He simply remembered everything they said.

One evening, his aunt, Maame Esi, was talking with two women beside the fire.

Kweku was sitting a little distance away.

He was peeling an orange.

The women thought he was not listening.

"Don't tell anyone about the room."

One woman said this very softly.

Maame Esi looked around.

"Not even the children."

Kweku stopped peeling his orange.

He looked at them.

The women continued talking.

"The key is still with the chief."

"And if he opens it?"

Maame Esi shook her head.

"He won't."

Kweku quietly stood up.

He walked toward them.

"What room?"

The three women became silent.

Maame Esi stared at him.

"What did you hear?"

Kweku shrugged.

"Everything."

One of the women quickly stood.

"Children should not listen to adult talk."

Kweku looked at her.

"Then why were you talking where I could hear you?"

The woman had no answer.

Maame Esi took Kweku by the arm.

"Go home."

"But I want to know about the room."

"There is nothing to know."

Kweku looked at her carefully.

"Then why are you afraid?"

Maame Esi's hand stopped.

She looked at the boy.

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then she let go of his arm.

"Go."

Kweku went home.

But he did not forget the conversation.

The next morning, he asked his grandmother about it.

Her name was Adwoa.

She was sitting in the courtyard, sorting beans.

"Grandma, what is behind the chief's house?"

Adwoa did not look up.

"Why?"

"Is there a locked room?"

Her fingers stopped moving.

Kweku noticed.

"There is, isn't there?"

Grandma Adwoa slowly looked at him.

"Who told you?"

"I heard Aunt Maame Esi."

Grandma sighed.

"You hear too much."

"Is that bad?"

She gave him a small smile.

"Sometimes."

Kweku sat beside her.

"What is inside the room?"

Grandma Adwoa returned to sorting the beans.

"Nothing."

Kweku smiled.

"That is what Aunt Maame Esi said."

Grandma looked at him again.

"And you don't believe her?"

"No."

Grandma laughed softly.

"You are becoming troublesome."

"I only ask questions."

"That is how trouble begins."

Kweku thought about that.

"Did trouble begin when you were young?"

Grandma's smile disappeared.

She put the beans down.

"Who told you that?"

Kweku frowned.

"Told me what?"

Grandma looked toward the gate.

A man was standing outside.

He wore a clean white shirt.

He carried a small leather bag.

Kweku had never seen him before.

The man smiled when their eyes met.

Then he walked away.

Kweku watched him.

"Who is that?"

Grandma Adwoa picked up the beans again.

"I don't know."

Kweku looked at her.

"You're lying."

Grandma dropped a bean.

She looked at Kweku for a long moment.

"Never say that to an elder."

"But you are lying."

Grandma stood up.

"Go inside."

Kweku obeyed.

But he looked through the window.

The strange man was now standing beside the chief's house.

He looked around.

Then he took something from his leather bag.

A key.

Kweku's eyes widened.

The man walked toward the locked room.

Kweku ran outside.

"Grandma!"

Adwoa rushed after him.

"What happened?"

Kweku pointed toward the chief's house.

"That man has the key."

Grandma Adwoa froze.

"What key?"

"The key to the room."

She grabbed his hand.

"Come with me."

They hurried through the narrow path between the houses.

But before they reached the chief's compound, they heard a loud sound.

CLANG!

The locked door had opened.

People began coming out of their houses.

The chief rushed toward the room.

Kweku and his grandmother stood behind the crowd.

The strange man was gone.

The door stood wide open.

The chief entered.

A moment later, he came back out.

His face looked frightened.

"Who has been talking about this room?"

Nobody answered.

The chief looked at the crowd.

"Who told the boy?"

Kweku looked at his grandmother.

Grandma Adwoa looked away.

Kweku understood something.

She knew more than she had said.

The chief suddenly pointed toward Kweku.

"Bring that boy here."

Kweku walked forward.

"Yes, Chief?"

The chief bent down.

"Kweku, did you see the man who opened this door?"

Kweku nodded.

"Yes."

"Do you know him?"

Kweku shook his head.

"No."

The chief looked relieved.

Then Kweku added:

"But my grandmother does."

Everyone turned toward Adwoa.

She stood very still.

The chief looked at her.

"Adwoa."

She said nothing.

The chief stepped closer.

"Is that true?"

Grandma Adwoa finally raised her head.

"Yes."

The crowd began to whisper.

Kweku looked at the open room.

Something inside had been removed.

A large wooden box sat in the corner.

Its lid was open.

Kweku pointed at it.

"What was inside that box?"

Nobody answered.

Then Grandma Adwoa spoke.

"A child's name."

Kweku frowned.

"How can a name be inside a box?"

Grandma looked directly at him.

"Because that name was never supposed to be spoken again."

Kweku's heart began to beat faster.

"What was the name?"

Grandma opened her mouth.

But before she could speak, the chief shouted.

"Nobody say it!"

The whole compound went silent.

Then someone behind Kweku whispered the name.

Kweku heard it clearly.

It was his own name.

He turned around.

Nobody was standing there.

But on the ground was the strange man's leather bag.

It was open.

Inside was a small piece of paper.

Kweku picked it up.

Only one sentence was written on it.

"Ask your grandmother why she gave you that name."

Kweku slowly looked at Grandma Adwoa.

For the first time in his life, she looked afraid of him.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌙 Chapter Two – The Name Nobody Wanted

Kweku could not stop thinking about the paper.

He had read the words many times.

"Ask your grandmother why she gave you that name."

That night, he waited until everyone had gone to sleep.

Then he quietly walked to Grandma Adwoa's room.

She was still awake.

She sat on her bed with a small lamp beside her.

Kweku stood at the doorway.

"Grandma?"

She looked up.

"Why are you awake?"

"I want to ask you something."

Grandma Adwoa became quiet.

"What is it?"

Kweku walked inside.

"Why did you give me my name?"

Grandma looked at him for a long time.

Then she turned away.

"Go back to bed."

Kweku did not move.

"The man who opened the room told me to ask you."

Grandma Adwoa stood up quickly.

"What man?"

"The man with the leather bag."

Her face changed.

"You must stay away from him."

"Why?"

"Because he is looking for something."

"What?"

Grandma looked at him.

"You."

Kweku felt a chill run through him.

"Why would he be looking for me?"

Grandma sat down again.

Her voice became soft.

"Because he knows what happened before you were born."

Kweku moved closer.

"What happened?"

Grandma shook her head.

"Not tonight."

"Then when?"

She looked toward the window.

"When you are old enough."

Kweku frowned.

"But I am old enough to know why people are afraid of my name."

Grandma's eyes filled with tears.

"Who told you people are afraid of your name?"

Kweku held up the paper.

"This."

Grandma took it from him.

She looked at the writing.

Her hands began to shake.

"Where did you get this?"

"Inside the man's bag."

Grandma folded the paper.

Then she placed it inside her wrapper.

"You never saw this."

"But—"

"Promise me."

Kweku hesitated.

"Why?"

Grandma leaned closer.

"Because some people can hurt you with a name."

Kweku did not understand.

Before he could ask another question, they heard footsteps outside.

Someone was walking slowly across the courtyard.

Grandma blew out the lamp.

"Don't make a sound."

Kweku stood still.

The footsteps came closer.

Then stopped outside the door.

Someone knocked.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Grandma did not answer.

Kweku held his breath.

A man's voice came through the door.

"Adwoa."

Grandma closed her eyes.

Kweku knew the voice.

It was the stranger.

"I know the boy is inside."

Kweku looked at his grandmother.

She placed one finger over her lips.

The man knocked again.

"Give me what belongs to me."

Grandma whispered:

"You have nothing here."

The man laughed softly.

"You still lie the same way."

Kweku looked at Grandma.

Her face was pale.

The man continued.

"You cannot hide him forever."

Then the footsteps moved away.

Kweku waited.

Grandma waited too.

Neither of them spoke.

After a while, Kweku whispered:

"Grandma, who was that man?"

She did not answer.

"Grandma?"

She finally spoke.

"His name is Badu."

Kweku's eyes widened.

"Is he from this village?"

Grandma nodded.

"He was."

"What happened to him?"

Grandma looked down.

"He left many years ago."

"Why?"

Grandma took a deep breath.

"Because of your mother."

Kweku froze.

"My mother?"

Grandma nodded.

"Your mother knew the truth."

Kweku's voice became weak.

"What truth?"

Grandma looked toward the door.

"The truth about the night you were born."

Kweku stared at her.

He had always believed he was born in the village.

His mother had told him so many times.

But now Grandma was saying something else.

"Where was I born?"

Grandma remained silent.

"Grandma, where was I born?"

She looked at him.

"Not here."

Kweku stepped back.

"Then where?"

Grandma reached beneath her bed.

She pulled out a small wooden box.

She placed it in front of Kweku.

"Your mother left this for you."

Kweku stared at the box.

There was no lock.

He slowly opened it.

Inside was a small piece of cloth.

A faded bracelet.

And a tiny wooden tag.

Kweku picked up the tag.

His name was written on it.

But underneath his name was another word.

"SECOND."

Kweku looked at his grandmother.

"Second what?"

Grandma began to cry.

"Second child."

Kweku stared at her.

"But I am an only child."

Grandma wiped her tears.

"That is what everyone was told."

Kweku's heart began to pound.

"Then where is the first child?"

Grandma looked at the door.

"That is what Badu came back to find."

Kweku slowly closed the box.

Then someone knocked on the window.

Both of them turned.

A small handprint appeared on the glass.

Kweku stepped backward.

Another handprint appeared beside it.

Then a child's voice whispered from outside.

"Kweku..."

Kweku looked at Grandma.

The voice came again.

"Kweku, I am your brother."

Grandma covered her mouth.

Kweku stared at the window.

He had never had a brother.

Or so he had always believed.
