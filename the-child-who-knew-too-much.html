🕯️ The Child Who Knew Too Much

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🕯️ Chapter One – The Child Who Knew Too Much

Every evening, the women of Mambala village gathered around the cooking fire.

They talked while they peeled yam, roasted corn, and stirred big pots of soup.

Children played nearby.

Some chased one another.

Others sat quietly, hoping someone would give them a piece of roasted plantain.

But one child never played with the others.

His name was Kweku.

He was ten years old.

And Kweku had a strange habit.

He listened to grown-ups.

Not because he wanted to be rude.

He simply remembered everything they said.

One evening, his aunt, Maame Esi, was talking with two women beside the fire.

Kweku was sitting a little distance away.

He was peeling an orange.

The women thought he was not listening.

"Don't tell anyone about the room."

One woman said this very softly.

Maame Esi looked around.

"Not even the children."

Kweku stopped peeling his orange.

He looked at them.

The women continued talking.

"The key is still with the chief."

"And if he opens it?"

Maame Esi shook her head.

"He won't."

Kweku quietly stood up.

He walked toward them.

"What room?"

The three women became silent.

Maame Esi stared at him.

"What did you hear?"

Kweku shrugged.

"Everything."

One of the women quickly stood.

"Children should not listen to adult talk."

Kweku looked at her.

"Then why were you talking where I could hear you?"

The woman had no answer.

Maame Esi took Kweku by the arm.

"Go home."

"But I want to know about the room."

"There is nothing to know."

Kweku looked at her carefully.

"Then why are you afraid?"

Maame Esi's hand stopped.

She looked at the boy.

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then she let go of his arm.

"Go."

Kweku went home.

But he did not forget the conversation.

The next morning, he asked his grandmother about it.

Her name was Adwoa.

She was sitting in the courtyard, sorting beans.

"Grandma, what is behind the chief's house?"

Adwoa did not look up.

"Why?"

"Is there a locked room?"

Her fingers stopped moving.

Kweku noticed.

"There is, isn't there?"

Grandma Adwoa slowly looked at him.

"Who told you?"

"I heard Aunt Maame Esi."

Grandma sighed.

"You hear too much."

"Is that bad?"

She gave him a small smile.

"Sometimes."

Kweku sat beside her.

"What is inside the room?"

Grandma Adwoa returned to sorting the beans.

"Nothing."

Kweku smiled.

"That is what Aunt Maame Esi said."

Grandma looked at him again.

"And you don't believe her?"

"No."

Grandma laughed softly.

"You are becoming troublesome."

"I only ask questions."

"That is how trouble begins."

Kweku thought about that.

"Did trouble begin when you were young?"

Grandma's smile disappeared.

She put the beans down.

"Who told you that?"

Kweku frowned.

"Told me what?"

Grandma looked toward the gate.

A man was standing outside.

He wore a clean white shirt.

He carried a small leather bag.

Kweku had never seen him before.

The man smiled when their eyes met.

Then he walked away.

Kweku watched him.

"Who is that?"

Grandma Adwoa picked up the beans again.

"I don't know."

Kweku looked at her.

"You're lying."

Grandma dropped a bean.

She looked at Kweku for a long moment.

"Never say that to an elder."

"But you are lying."

Grandma stood up.

"Go inside."

Kweku obeyed.

But he looked through the window.

The strange man was now standing beside the chief's house.

He looked around.

Then he took something from his leather bag.

A key.

Kweku's eyes widened.

The man walked toward the locked room.

Kweku ran outside.

"Grandma!"

Adwoa rushed after him.

"What happened?"

Kweku pointed toward the chief's house.

"That man has the key."

Grandma Adwoa froze.

"What key?"

"The key to the room."

She grabbed his hand.

"Come with me."

They hurried through the narrow path between the houses.

But before they reached the chief's compound, they heard a loud sound.

CLANG!

The locked door had opened.

People began coming out of their houses.

The chief rushed toward the room.

Kweku and his grandmother stood behind the crowd.

The strange man was gone.

The door stood wide open.

The chief entered.

A moment later, he came back out.

His face looked frightened.

"Who has been talking about this room?"

Nobody answered.

The chief looked at the crowd.

"Who told the boy?"

Kweku looked at his grandmother.

Grandma Adwoa looked away.

Kweku understood something.

She knew more than she had said.

The chief suddenly pointed toward Kweku.

"Bring that boy here."

Kweku walked forward.

"Yes, Chief?"

The chief bent down.

"Kweku, did you see the man who opened this door?"

Kweku nodded.

"Yes."

"Do you know him?"

Kweku shook his head.

"No."

The chief looked relieved.

Then Kweku added:

"But my grandmother does."

Everyone turned toward Adwoa.

She stood very still.

The chief looked at her.

"Adwoa."

She said nothing.

The chief stepped closer.

"Is that true?"

Grandma Adwoa finally raised her head.

"Yes."

The crowd began to whisper.

Kweku looked at the open room.

Something inside had been removed.

A large wooden box sat in the corner.

Its lid was open.

Kweku pointed at it.

"What was inside that box?"

Nobody answered.

Then Grandma Adwoa spoke.

"A child's name."

Kweku frowned.

"How can a name be inside a box?"

Grandma looked directly at him.

"Because that name was never supposed to be spoken again."

Kweku's heart began to beat faster.

"What was the name?"

Grandma opened her mouth.

But before she could speak, the chief shouted.

"Nobody say it!"

The whole compound went silent.

Then someone behind Kweku whispered the name.

Kweku heard it clearly.

It was his own name.

He turned around.

Nobody was standing there.

But on the ground was the strange man's leather bag.

It was open.

Inside was a small piece of paper.

Kweku picked it up.

Only one sentence was written on it.

"Ask your grandmother why she gave you that name."

Kweku slowly looked at Grandma Adwoa.

For the first time in his life, she looked afraid of him.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌙 Chapter Two – The Name Nobody Wanted

Kweku could not stop thinking about the paper.

He had read the words many times.

"Ask your grandmother why she gave you that name."

That night, he waited until everyone had gone to sleep.

Then he quietly walked to Grandma Adwoa's room.

She was still awake.

She sat on her bed with a small lamp beside her.

Kweku stood at the doorway.

"Grandma?"

She looked up.

"Why are you awake?"

"I want to ask you something."

Grandma Adwoa became quiet.

"What is it?"

Kweku walked inside.

"Why did you give me my name?"

Grandma looked at him for a long time.

Then she turned away.

"Go back to bed."

Kweku did not move.

"The man who opened the room told me to ask you."

Grandma Adwoa stood up quickly.

"What man?"

"The man with the leather bag."

Her face changed.

"You must stay away from him."

"Why?"

"Because he is looking for something."

"What?"

Grandma looked at him.

"You."

Kweku felt a chill run through him.

"Why would he be looking for me?"

Grandma sat down again.

Her voice became soft.

"Because he knows what happened before you were born."

Kweku moved closer.

"What happened?"

Grandma shook her head.

"Not tonight."

"Then when?"

She looked toward the window.

"When you are old enough."

Kweku frowned.

"But I am old enough to know why people are afraid of my name."

Grandma's eyes filled with tears.

"Who told you people are afraid of your name?"

Kweku held up the paper.

"This."

Grandma took it from him.

She looked at the writing.

Her hands began to shake.

"Where did you get this?"

"Inside the man's bag."

Grandma folded the paper.

Then she placed it inside her wrapper.

"You never saw this."

"But—"

"Promise me."

Kweku hesitated.

"Why?"

Grandma leaned closer.

"Because some people can hurt you with a name."

Kweku did not understand.

Before he could ask another question, they heard footsteps outside.

Someone was walking slowly across the courtyard.

Grandma blew out the lamp.

"Don't make a sound."

Kweku stood still.

The footsteps came closer.

Then stopped outside the door.

Someone knocked.

Three times.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Grandma did not answer.

Kweku held his breath.

A man's voice came through the door.

"Adwoa."

Grandma closed her eyes.

Kweku knew the voice.

It was the stranger.

"I know the boy is inside."

Kweku looked at his grandmother.

She placed one finger over her lips.

The man knocked again.

"Give me what belongs to me."

Grandma whispered:

"You have nothing here."

The man laughed softly.

"You still lie the same way."

Kweku looked at Grandma.

Her face was pale.

The man continued.

"You cannot hide him forever."

Then the footsteps moved away.

Kweku waited.

Grandma waited too.

Neither of them spoke.

After a while, Kweku whispered:

"Grandma, who was that man?"

She did not answer.

"Grandma?"

She finally spoke.

"His name is Badu."

Kweku's eyes widened.

"Is he from this village?"

Grandma nodded.

"He was."

"What happened to him?"

Grandma looked down.

"He left many years ago."

"Why?"

Grandma took a deep breath.

"Because of your mother."

Kweku froze.

"My mother?"

Grandma nodded.

"Your mother knew the truth."

Kweku's voice became weak.

"What truth?"

Grandma looked toward the door.

"The truth about the night you were born."

Kweku stared at her.

He had always believed he was born in the village.

His mother had told him so many times.

But now Grandma was saying something else.

"Where was I born?"

Grandma remained silent.

"Grandma, where was I born?"

She looked at him.

"Not here."

Kweku stepped back.

"Then where?"

Grandma reached beneath her bed.

She pulled out a small wooden box.

She placed it in front of Kweku.

"Your mother left this for you."

Kweku stared at the box.

There was no lock.

He slowly opened it.

Inside was a small piece of cloth.

A faded bracelet.

And a tiny wooden tag.

Kweku picked up the tag.

His name was written on it.

But underneath his name was another word.

"SECOND."

Kweku looked at his grandmother.

"Second what?"

Grandma began to cry.

"Second child."

Kweku stared at her.

"But I am an only child."

Grandma wiped her tears.

"That is what everyone was told."

Kweku's heart began to pound.

"Then where is the first child?"

Grandma looked at the door.

"That is what Badu came back to find."

Kweku slowly closed the box.

Then someone knocked on the window.

Both of them turned.

A small handprint appeared on the glass.

Kweku stepped backward.

Another handprint appeared beside it.

Then a child's voice whispered from outside.

"Kweku..."

Kweku looked at Grandma.

The voice came again.

"Kweku, I am your brother."

Grandma covered her mouth.

Kweku stared at the window.

He had never had a brother.

Or so he had always believed.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌿 Chapter Three – The Boy Behind the Wall

Kweku could not move.

He stared at the window.

The small handprint was still there.

Grandma Adwoa held his shoulder.

"Don't open that window."

Kweku swallowed.

"But he said he is my brother."

"I heard him."

"Then let me see him."

Grandma shook her head.

"You don't know what you are asking."

The voice came again.

"Kweku."

This time it sounded closer.

"Please."

Kweku moved toward the window.

Grandma grabbed his hand.

"No."

"Grandma, if he is really my brother, why are you afraid?"

She looked down.

"Because I failed him."

Kweku became quiet.

"What did you do?"

Grandma did not answer.

Instead, she walked to the back door.

"Come."

Kweku followed her.

They stepped into the courtyard.

The night was still.

Grandma walked toward an old mud wall behind the kitchen.

She stopped there.

"Your mother stood here the night you were born."

Kweku looked at the wall.

"Why?"

Grandma touched one of the bricks.

"Because she was trying to protect two babies."

Kweku's heart jumped.

"Me and my brother?"

Grandma nodded.

"Yes."

Kweku stared at her.

"Where was he taken?"

Grandma pointed at the wall.

"He was hidden here."

Kweku touched the wall.

One brick felt loose.

He looked at Grandma.

"Can I move it?"

Grandma hesitated.

"Your mother told me never to."

"Why?"

"She said the truth would come out when you were ready."

Kweku slowly pulled the brick.

It came out easily.

Behind it was a small hollow space.

Inside was a bundle wrapped in blue cloth.

Kweku reached for it.

Grandma whispered:

"That belonged to your brother."

Kweku opened the cloth.

Inside was a little wooden whistle.

There was also a faded picture.

Two babies lay beside each other.

Kweku stared at the picture.

They looked exactly alike.

He turned it over.

There was a date written behind it.

And underneath the date were four words.

"One must leave before dawn."

Kweku looked at Grandma.

"What does this mean?"

Grandma's eyes filled with tears.

"Your mother had to choose."

"Choose what?"

Grandma took a slow breath.

"Which child would stay."

Kweku's face became pale.

"She chose me?"

Grandma nodded.

"She thought she was saving you."

"And my brother?"

Grandma looked toward the window.

"He was taken away before the sun came up."

Kweku held the picture tightly.

"By who?"

Grandma did not answer.

A voice came from behind the wall.

"By Badu."

Kweku dropped the picture.

Grandma stepped backward.

The voice spoke again.

"Grandma knows."

Kweku looked around.

Nobody was there.

"Where are you?"

A soft laugh came from the other side of the wall.

"Close."

Kweku walked around the wall.

There was a narrow space between the kitchen and the next house.

A boy stood there.

He looked almost exactly like Kweku.

But he was a little taller.

He wore a faded blue shirt.

Kweku stared at him.

"Are you really my brother?"

The boy smiled.

"At last."

Grandma hurried toward them.

"Don't go near him!"

The boy looked at her.

"You told him I was gone."

Grandma lowered her head.

"I had no choice."

"You always had a choice."

Kweku looked between them.

"What is your name?"

The boy smiled sadly.

"They gave me many names."

"But my mother gave me one."

Kweku waited.

"Kojo."

Kweku repeated it softly.

"Kojo."

The boy nodded.

"Yes."

Kweku stepped closer.

"Why did you come back?"

Kojo's smile disappeared.

"I didn't come back."

Kweku frowned.

"What do you mean?"

Kojo looked at Grandma.

"She knows where I have been."

Grandma began to cry.

"Kojo, please."

Kojo shook his head.

"You have kept him away from the truth for ten years."

Kweku looked at Grandma.

"What truth?"

Kojo answered before she could.

"Your mother did not give you my life."

"She gave me yours."

Kweku stared at him.

"What does that mean?"

Kojo took the wooden whistle from Kweku's hand.

"This was mine."

He blew it once.

A sharp sound crossed the compound.

Grandma suddenly grabbed Kweku.

"Run!"

Kweku looked confused.

"Why?"

Grandma pointed toward the main gate.

Badu was standing there.

He was holding the same leather bag.

But this time, he was not alone.

Two men stood behind him.

Kojo stepped back.

"They found us."

Kweku looked at Grandma.

"Who are those men?"

Grandma held his hand tightly.

"The people your mother was running from."

Badu slowly entered the compound.

He looked at Kweku.

Then he looked at Kojo.

A strange smile appeared on his face.

"Ten years."

He took another step.

"Ten years, and the two of you are finally together."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

Kojo whispered:

"Don't believe anything he says."

Badu heard him.

"Your brother has already lied to you."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"What lie?"

Kojo's face changed.

Badu smiled.

"Ask him what happened to your mother."

Kweku's heart stopped.

"What happened to my mother?"

Kojo looked down.

He did not answer.

That silence told Kweku more than any words could have.

Something terrible had happened.

And Kojo had been there.

"Kojo..."

The boy finally looked at him.

"Your mother is not dead."

Kweku stared at him.

Badu's smile disappeared.

Kojo pointed toward the road.

"She is waiting for us."

Kweku's heart began to race.

Then Badu spoke quietly.

"No."

"She is waiting for you."

Kweku looked at him.

"Why only me?"

Badu opened his leather bag.

Inside was a second wooden tag.

He held it up.

Kweku saw his name.

But this time, another word was written underneath it.

"RETURN."

Kweku slowly stepped back.

He finally understood why Badu had come.

He was not looking for Kojo.

He had come for him.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🌖 Chapter Four – The Woman at the Old Market

Kweku could not take his eyes off the wooden tag.

RETURN.

The word was still in his mind.

He looked at Badu.

"Where is my mother?"

Badu closed the leather bag.

"If you want to see her, come with me."

Grandma Adwoa stepped in front of Kweku.

"He is not going anywhere with you."

Badu smiled.

"You said the same thing ten years ago."

Grandma's face became hard.

"And I would say it again."

One of the men behind Badu moved forward.

Kojo quickly stood beside Kweku.

"Stay close to me."

Kweku looked at him.

"Do you know where our mother is?"

Kojo nodded.

"I have seen her."

Kweku's eyes widened.

"When?"

"Yesterday."

Kweku felt angry.

"You knew she was alive and never told me?"

Kojo looked down.

"I was afraid."

"Afraid of what?"

Kojo looked at Badu.

"Of bringing you to her."

Badu turned away.

"Enough talking."

Grandma grabbed Kweku's hand.

"Take the back path."

Kweku looked at her.

"What about you?"

"I will slow them down."

Kweku shook his head.

"No."

Grandma smiled sadly.

"You have been asking questions all your life."

"Tonight, go and find your own answers."

Kojo pulled Kweku away.

They ran behind the kitchen.

They passed the old grinding stone.

They crossed a small garden.

Then they slipped through a narrow opening between two houses.

Kweku looked back.

Grandma was still standing in the courtyard.

Badu was walking toward her.

Kweku wanted to turn back.

Kojo held him.

"She wants you to go."

Kweku swallowed his tears.

They kept running.

After some time, they reached the old market.

The place was empty at night.

The wooden stalls stood in rows.

A few old baskets lay on the ground.

Kojo stopped near a closed food stall.

"Wait here."

Kweku grabbed his arm.

"Where are you going?"

"To find someone."

"Who?"

Kojo pointed toward the far end of the market.

"Her name is Yaa."

"Who is Yaa?"

Kojo looked at him.

"Someone our mother trusted."

Kweku waited.

A few minutes later, an old woman came from behind one of the stalls.

She carried a small lantern.

She looked at Kojo.

"You brought him."

Kojo nodded.

The woman looked at Kweku.

"So this is the boy."

Kweku frowned.

"Who are you?"

"I am Yaa."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"Can she take us to our mother?"

Yaa lowered her lantern.

"Yes."

Kweku felt hope rise in his chest.

"Then let's go."

Yaa did not move.

"First, you must know why she left you."

Kweku became still.

"She didn't leave me."

Yaa looked at Kojo.

"Did you tell him?"

Kojo shook his head.

Yaa sighed.

"Then I will."

She sat on a wooden bench.

Kweku sat opposite her.

Kojo remained standing.

"Your mother had two sons."

Kweku nodded.

"I know."

"She loved both of you."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"Then why did she hide him?"

Yaa was quiet for a moment.

"Because someone told her one of her children would bring great trouble to the village."

Kweku frowned.

"Who said that?"

"A man who read signs for the elders."

Kweku shook his head.

"That makes no sense."

Yaa smiled.

"Many things make no sense until you learn the whole story."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"Was it him?"

Kojo shook his head.

"No."

Yaa continued.

"The man said the first son would bring trouble."

"So your mother hid Kojo."

Kweku looked at his brother.

"But I am the second son."

Yaa nodded.

"That is what you were told."

Kweku's face tightened.

"What are you saying?"

Yaa looked directly at him.

"You are not the second son."

Kweku stared at her.

Kojo looked away.

"Then who is older?"

Yaa pointed at Kojo.

"He is."

Kweku shook his head.

"But the tag said second."

Yaa nodded.

"That tag was made to fool the people who came looking for you."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"So I am the first?"

Kojo finally spoke.

"Yes."

Kweku's heart sank.

"Then why did you let me believe you were the first?"

Kojo stepped closer.

"Because if they believed you were the first, they would come for you."

Kweku looked at him.

"And they did."

Kojo nodded.

"Yes."

A sound came from the entrance of the market.

Yaa quickly stood.

"They are here."

Kweku looked behind him.

Badu appeared between the wooden stalls.

Grandma Adwoa was not with him.

Kweku's heart dropped.

"Where is my grandmother?"

Badu smiled.

"She told me where to find you."

Kweku shook his head.

"She would never do that."

Badu reached into his bag.

He brought out a small necklace.

Kweku recognized it immediately.

It belonged to Grandma Adwoa.

"What did you do to her?"

Badu did not answer.

Instead, he looked at Kojo.

"Tell him."

Kojo became pale.

"Tell me what?"

Badu smiled.

"Tell your brother who his real father is."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

Kojo said nothing.

Yaa slowly stepped backward.

Kweku noticed.

"Yaa, you know too?"

She looked at him.

"Yes."

Kweku's voice shook.

"Who is my father?"

Nobody answered.

Then a familiar voice came from behind the market stalls.

"I am."

Kweku turned.

His mother was standing there.

She looked older than he remembered.

She was crying.

Kweku stared at her.

"Mother?"

She took one step toward him.

"My son."

Kweku wanted to run to her.

But Kojo grabbed his arm.

"Don't."

Kweku looked at him.

"Why?"

Kojo pointed at their mother.

"Because that is not the woman who gave birth to us."

Kweku's body went cold.

The woman smiled through her tears.

Then she said something only their real mother could have known.

"Kweku, you still sleep with your left hand under your pillow."

Kweku stopped breathing.

Nobody else knew that.

Not even Kojo.

He looked at the woman again.

For the first time, he wondered if Kojo had been wrong.

Then the woman whispered:

"Come home with me."

Kweku took one step forward.

And behind him, Yaa whispered:

"Don't let her touch you."

🌙 End of Chapter Four

✨ Chapter Five – The Truth Behind the Name

Kweku stood still.

He looked at the woman who claimed to be his mother.

Then he looked at Kojo.

Nobody spoke.

The woman slowly stretched out her hand.

"Come to me, Kweku."

Kweku wanted to move.

He wanted to run into her arms.

But Yaa's warning stayed in his mind.

"Don't let her touch you."

Kweku stopped.

"If you are really my mother, tell me something only she would know."

The woman smiled.

"I already did."

Kweku shook his head.

"That is not enough."

The woman's face became serious.

"What else do you want to know?"

Kweku thought for a moment.

"What did you call me when I was a baby?"

The woman opened her mouth.

Nothing came out.

Kweku looked at Kojo.

Kojo's eyes were fixed on the woman.

"You don't know."

The woman became angry.

"Enough!"

She stepped toward Kweku.

Kojo pulled him backward.

"Stay away from her."

Badu moved forward.

"Kojo, you have caused enough trouble."

Kojo stood his ground.

"You won't take him."

Badu looked at Kweku.

"You still don't understand."

Kweku stared at him.

"Understand what?"

Badu opened his leather bag.

He brought out an old piece of cloth.

It was the same faded blue cloth Kweku had found behind the wall.

He placed it on the ground.

There were two names stitched into it.

KOJO.

KWEKU.

Kweku stared at the names.

"Who stitched this?"

Badu looked at the woman.

"Your mother."

Kweku looked at the woman again.

She began to cry.

"I was trying to protect you."

Kweku shook his head.

"Then why did you leave us?"

The woman wiped her face.

"Because they gave me a choice."

"One child could stay."

"The other had to disappear."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"And you chose me?"

Kojo nodded.

"She did."

Kweku's eyes filled with tears.

"Why?"

The woman answered.

"Because you were sick."

Kweku frowned.

"What?"

"You were born very weak."

"The elders said you would not live."

She looked at Kojo.

"Your brother was strong."

"So I begged them to take him instead."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"You knew?"

Kojo nodded.

"I knew everything."

Kweku lowered his head.

For years, he had thought his brother had been taken because someone wanted him.

Now he knew the truth was different.

Kojo had been taken because their mother had been desperate to save him.

But there was still one question.

"Then why did Badu come back?"

Everyone became quiet.

Badu slowly closed his bag.

"Because the people who took Kojo made a mistake."

Kweku looked at him.

"What mistake?"

Badu pointed at Kweku.

"They thought Kojo was the first child."

"But he was not."

Kweku felt his stomach tighten.

"So I am the child they wanted?"

Badu nodded.

"Yes."

The woman stepped forward.

"But you are not going with them."

Badu looked at her.

"You know what happens if he stays."

"I know."

Kweku looked between them.

"What happens?"

The woman took a deep breath.

"They will come for every person who helped hide you."

Kweku's face changed.

"Grandma."

The woman nodded.

"Your grandmother knew."

Kweku turned to Badu.

"Where is she?"

Badu looked away.

"She is safe."

"Tell me where she is!"

Badu pointed toward the road.

"At home."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"We have to go back."

Kojo nodded.

They began walking.

The woman followed.

Yaa remained behind.

Kweku turned.

"Yaa, aren't you coming?"

She smiled.

"My part is finished."

Kweku frowned.

"What part?"

Yaa pointed at the blue cloth.

"Making sure the right child found the right truth."

Kweku did not understand.

But he kept walking.

When they reached home, Grandma Adwoa was sitting outside.

She looked tired.

But she was safe.

Kweku ran to her.

"Grandma!"

She hugged him tightly.

"You came back."

"Yes."

Kweku pulled away.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

Grandma looked at Kojo.

Then she looked at Kweku.

"I wanted you to have a normal childhood."

"But I knew something was wrong."

Kweku nodded.

"I know."

Grandma smiled.

"Now you know everything."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

"Not everything."

Grandma's smile disappeared.

"What do you mean?"

Kweku held up the wooden tag.

"Why does it say RETURN?"

Grandma became silent.

The woman who claimed to be their mother looked at the ground.

Kojo looked frightened.

Kweku slowly understood.

"There is something else."

Nobody answered.

Then Grandma Adwoa stood.

"Yes."

Kweku waited.

"What?"

She looked toward the old house.

"The name Kweku was never meant for you."

Kweku stared at her.

"Then whose name is it?"

Grandma pointed at Kojo.

"His."

Kweku looked at his brother.

Kojo's eyes filled with tears.

Grandma continued.

"And the name Kojo was meant for you."

Kweku stood speechless.

The two brothers had lived with each other's names for ten years.

But that was not the worst part.

Grandma reached into her pocket.

She brought out one final piece of paper.

She handed it to Kweku.

He opened it.

Only one sentence was written there.

"The child who carries the wrong name will one day remember who he really is."

Kweku looked up.

"Grandma, what does this mean?"

She looked at him with tears in her eyes.

"It means the story was never about your brother."

Kweku's heart began to race.

"Then who was it about?"

Grandma looked at him.

"You."

Kweku looked at Kojo.

Then at his mother.

For the first time, he understood why everyone had been afraid of his name.

He was not the child who knew too much.

He was the child who had forgotten too much.

🌿 Moral of the Story

Sometimes the truth may be hidden to protect us, but a lie can also make us forget who we are. We should value the truth, listen to those who care for us, and never judge someone before knowing their full story.
