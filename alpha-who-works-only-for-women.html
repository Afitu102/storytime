🐺 Alpha Who Works Only for Women

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌅 Chapter One – The Man Who Chose Women

In a small village called Oduama, there lived a man named Alpha.

Alpha was not a chief.

He was not the richest man in the village.

He did not own many farms.

He did not have a big house.

But almost everybody in Oduama knew his name.

Women knew him.

Men knew him.

Hunters knew him.

Traders knew him.

Even people from far villages had heard about him.

People called him Alpha because of the way he carried himself.

He was quiet.

He watched people carefully.

He did not talk too much.

And when he decided to help somebody, he gave the person all his attention.

Alpha was known for spiritual work.

He had learned the old ways from his grandfather.

His grandfather was an old man who lived at the edge of the village.

People went to him when life became hard.

Some came because their businesses were failing.

Some came because their farms were not doing well.

Some came because they wanted peace in their homes.

Some came because they were afraid.

The old man never promised anybody that life would become easy.

He only taught them what he knew.

He taught Alpha about herbs.

He taught him about old prayers.

He taught him about respect.

He taught him how to listen before speaking.

Most of all, he taught him that spiritual work was not something to play with.

"Never use what you know to destroy another person," his grandfather always told him.

Alpha remembered those words.

For many years, he worked for both men and women.

He did not care whether a person was rich or poor.

If a man came to him with a problem, Alpha listened.

If a woman came to him, Alpha listened too.

He helped farmers who wanted better luck in their work.

He helped traders who wanted to grow their businesses.

He helped people who were afraid to take a new step in life.

He also worked with people who wanted to become known for their talents.

Alpha became very good at what he did.

People started travelling from other villages to meet him.

Sometimes, a man would arrive before sunrise.

He would sit outside Alpha's small house and wait.

Sometimes, three or four people would wait in a line.

Alpha never rushed them.

He would bring out a wooden stool.

He would sit down.

Then he would ask questions.

"What is your problem?"

"What have you tried?"

"What do you really want?"

Many people found his questions strange.

They expected him to start his work immediately.

But Alpha believed that he needed to understand the person first.

One day, a wealthy man named Chief Okoro came to him.

Chief Okoro had a large farm.

He had many workers.

He had plenty of money.

But his business had started going down.

People stopped buying from him.

His workers were leaving.

His farm was also doing badly.

Chief Okoro believed somebody was working against him.

He went to Alpha.

"I need your help," he said.

Alpha looked at him.

"What kind of help?"

"I want my business back."

Alpha asked him questions.

He listened to the whole story.

Then he told the chief something he did not want to hear.

"Your problem is not another man."

Chief Okoro became angry.

"What do you mean?"

"You are losing your workers because you do not treat them well."

The chief was silent.

Alpha continued.

"You pay them late."

"You shout at them."

"You take all the praise when the work goes well."

"And when something goes wrong, you blame them."

The chief did not like what he heard.

But he still listened.

Alpha did his work with him.

He also told him to change the way he treated his workers.

The chief slowly changed.

His workers started staying.

His farm became better.

His business started growing again.

Soon, people started saying that Alpha was powerful.

They said he could see things other people could not see.

They said he knew what was hidden.

They said he could turn a person's life around.

Alpha never allowed the praise to enter his head.

But then something happened.

A man named Bako came to him.

Bako was a businessman from another village.

He wanted Alpha to help him become more successful.

Alpha agreed.

They worked together for some time.

Bako's business improved.

More people started buying from him.

His name became known in nearby villages.

But Bako wanted more.

He wanted to become the richest man in the whole area.

Alpha told him to be patient.

Bako refused.

He wanted Alpha to do something that Alpha did not agree with.

Alpha refused.

Bako became angry.

"You work for other men," he said.

"Why can you not do this for me?"

"Because it is wrong," Alpha answered.

Bako left.

Alpha thought that was the end of the matter.

It was not.

A few weeks later, people started talking.

They said Alpha had taken money from Bako and refused to help him.

Some said Alpha had cheated him.

Others said Alpha had done spiritual work against him.

The story spread very fast.

Alpha was shocked.

He had done nothing wrong.

He went to Bako.

"Why are you telling people these things?"

Bako looked at him.

"I did not say anything."

"People are saying your name."

Bako smiled.

"Then let them talk."

Alpha knew something was wrong.

The matter became worse.

Some men who had once trusted Alpha stopped coming to him.

Some even insulted him.

One man came to his house and shouted at him.

"You are a fake man!"

Another said,

"You think you are better than everybody!"

Alpha said nothing.

He remembered his grandfather.

"Never answer anger with anger."

He stayed quiet.

But inside him, something had changed.

He began to ask himself a question.

"Why do I keep helping people who turn around and hurt me?"

He did not have an answer.

One evening, Alpha sat inside his room.

His grandfather's old things were still there.

An old wooden bowl.

A small leather bag.

A walking stick.

And an old piece of cloth his grandfather used during his work.

Alpha looked at them for a long time.

He remembered all the men he had helped.

Some had thanked him.

Some had forgotten him.

And some had betrayed him.

Then he remembered the women.

The women who came quietly.

The mothers.

The traders.

The widows.

The young women trying to build something for themselves.

They usually came with problems.

But they also came with respect.

They listened.

They followed his advice.

And when things became better, many of them came back to thank him.

Alpha thought about it all night.

By morning, he had made a decision.

He would no longer work for men.

From that day, Alpha would work only for women.

People laughed when they heard.

"Alpha has lost his mind."

"How can a man work only for women?"

"He will soon come back."

Alpha did not care.

He placed a small wooden sign outside his house.

The sign had only one sentence on it.

"Women Only."

The first morning after he put up the sign, nobody came.

Alpha sat outside his house.

He watched the road.

Nothing happened.

By afternoon, he almost laughed at himself.

Then an old woman appeared.

Her name was Mama Nneka.

She carried a small basket.

She stopped in front of the sign.

She read it.

Then she looked at Alpha.

"Is this sign real?"

Alpha nodded.

"Yes."

"You no longer work for men?"

"No."

"Why?"

Alpha smiled.

"That is a long story."

Mama Nneka sat down.

"Then tell me."

Alpha looked at her.

For the first time in many days, he felt peace.

He knew his life had entered a new road.

He did not know that this decision would soon make his name famous far beyond Oduama.

He did not know that one woman from Lagos would soon walk into his life.

Her name would be Miss Clara.

She would come with one big dream.

She wanted the whole country to know her name.

And Alpha would become the man who helped her believe that the dream was possible.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌿 Chapter Two – The Women Who Came to Alpha

After Alpha put the small wooden sign outside his room, many people in Oduama noticed it.

The sign was simple.

It said:

“SPIRITUAL WORK FOR WOMEN ONLY.”

Some people laughed when they saw it.

Some people shook their heads.

Some people stood in front of Alpha’s compound and talked about him.

“Look at Alpha again,” one man said.

“He has changed.”

“After everything he did for men, he now says he will not work for men again.”

Another man laughed.

“Let us see how long this one will last.”

Alpha heard them.

But he did not answer.

He had already made up his mind.

He remembered Bako.

He remembered how he had helped him.

He remembered how Bako later turned around and accused him of things he never did.

Alpha had spent many nights thinking about it.

He knew that not every man was like Bako.

He knew there were good men in the world.

But he also knew what his heart wanted now.

He wanted peace.

He wanted to work without people fighting him.

He wanted to help people who came to him with respect.

And for some reason, women were the people who kept coming to him with the kind of problems he understood.

The first woman to come after Alpha made his decision was Mama Nneka.

She was an older woman who sold dry fish near the village market.

She had known Alpha for many years.

One early morning, she came to his compound carrying a small basket.

She knocked on his wooden door.

“Alpha.”

Alpha opened the door.

“Mama Nneka.”

The old woman smiled.

“I have come to talk to you.”

Alpha moved aside and allowed her to enter.

She sat down on the small wooden chair beside the window.

For a while, she said nothing.

Alpha waited.

Finally, she looked at him.

“I heard what happened with Bako.”

Alpha looked away.

“Mama, that matter is finished.”

“I know,” she said.

“But sometimes a matter can be finished outside and still be living inside a person's heart.”

Alpha looked at her.

Mama Nneka continued.

“You have helped many people in this village.”

“I know.”

“You helped my daughter when she was having problems with her business.”

Alpha nodded.

“You helped my sister too.”

He nodded again.

Mama Nneka held the edge of her wrapper.

“But you cannot allow one person to make you stop doing what you know how to do.”

Alpha smiled a little.

“I am not stopping.”

Mama Nneka raised her eyebrows.

“You are only working for women now?”

“Yes.”

She laughed.

“Then I have come to test this new Alpha.”

Alpha laughed too.

“What do you want me to do?”

Mama Nneka told him about her fish business.

For weeks, she had been losing money.

She would buy fish in the morning and by evening, something would happen.

Sometimes the fish would spoil faster than usual.

Sometimes customers would promise to pay later and never return.

Sometimes she would return home with almost nothing.

She had become tired.

Alpha listened carefully.

He did not rush her.

He asked questions.

He listened to every detail.

Then he told her what she needed to do.

He also reminded her that she needed to change some things in the way she handled her business.

Mama Nneka followed his advice.

She became more careful with her money.

She stopped giving fish to people who always promised to pay later.

She started keeping a proper record of her sales.

Within a few weeks, things began to change.

Her business became better.

People noticed.

Soon, Mama Nneka started telling other women about Alpha.

That was how the visitors began to come.

One woman came.

Then another.

Then another.

Some came from nearby villages.

Some came from places Alpha had never heard of.

They came with different problems.

One wanted help with her business.

Another wanted peace in her home.

Another wanted to understand why her life had suddenly become difficult.

Another woman wanted to become successful in her work.

Alpha listened to all of them.

He never shouted at them.

He never made them feel small.

He would sit quietly and listen before saying anything.

This was one of the things people liked about him.

He did not behave like a man who knew everything.

He would tell a woman when she needed to be patient.

He would tell her when she needed to stop trusting the wrong people.

He would tell her when she needed to work harder.

And when he believed spiritual work could help, he would do what he knew.

The women started calling him different names.

Some called him Alpha.

Some called him the Women's Helper.

Some called him the Man Who Understands Women.

But one name slowly became popular.

“Alpha of the Women.”

Alpha did not ask anyone to call him that.

The name simply spread.

It moved from one woman to another.

From the market to the farms.

From one village to another.

Soon, women would arrive at his compound before sunrise.

Some would sit under the mango tree and wait for their turn.

Others would come in the afternoon.

Some came quietly because they did not want anyone to know they had visited him.

Alpha treated every woman with the same respect.

He did not care whether she was rich or poor.

He did not care whether she came from a powerful family or from a small hut.

To him, a person was a person.

But the more women came, the more the men of Oduama became curious.

Some men started saying Alpha was making a mistake.

“He will soon lose everything,” one man said.

“Women do not have money like men,” another said.

But they were wrong.

The women kept coming.

And many of them told others about Alpha.

His small room became a place many women knew about.

His name began to travel farther than Oduama.

One evening, while Alpha was sitting outside his room, Mama Nneka came to see him again.

She sat beside him.

“Alpha.”

“Yes, Mama?”

“You know your name is no longer only in Oduama.”

Alpha laughed.

“Mama, I am still sitting here.”

“You may be sitting here, but your name is travelling.”

Alpha looked at her.

She smiled.

“There is a woman coming.”

“From where?”

“Lagos.”

Alpha became quiet.

“Lagos?”

“Yes.”

“What does she want?”

Mama Nneka looked at him.

“She wants to become famous.”

Alpha laughed.

“Famous?”

“Yes.”

“What is her name?”

Mama Nneka leaned closer.

“Her name is Clara.”

Alpha repeated the name.

“Clara.”

“Yes. Miss Clara from Lagos.”

That night, Alpha thought about the woman.

He had never met her.

He did not know what she looked like.

He did not know what kind of life she lived.

He did not know why she wanted fame so badly.

But there was something about the way Mama Nneka spoke about her that made Alpha curious.

Three days later, a strange vehicle entered Oduama.

People stopped what they were doing and looked.

The vehicle moved slowly through the village road.

It stopped in front of Alpha's compound.

The door opened.

A young woman stepped out.

She was dressed differently from the women of Oduama.

She looked around the village.

Then she looked at Alpha's small wooden house.

A woman who came with her pointed at the house.

“That is where Alpha stays.”

Miss Clara looked at the house for a long moment.

Then she walked toward the door.

Alpha was inside.

He heard the knock.

He opened the door.

For a few seconds, neither of them spoke.

Clara looked at him.

Alpha looked at Clara.

Then she smiled.

“Are you Alpha?”

“Yes.”

“My name is Clara.”

Alpha nodded.

“I know.”

Clara smiled again.

“Then you already know why I came.”

Alpha stepped aside.

“Come inside.”

Clara entered.

She did not know it yet, but her visit would change Alpha's life.

And it would also change her own life in a way she had never imagined.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌺 Chapter Three – Miss Clara from Lagos

Clara sat quietly inside Alpha's small room.

She looked around the room before looking back at him.

There was nothing fancy inside.

There was a wooden table, two wooden chairs and a small shelf near the wall.

A few bottles stood on the shelf beside some old books.

Through the small window, Clara could see people moving along the village road.

She had travelled all the way from Lagos to meet this man.

She had heard many stories about Alpha before coming.

Some people said he was very good at spiritual work.

Some said women came from far places to see him.

Others said he had helped women who had already lost hope.

Clara did not know what to believe.

She only knew that she wanted her life to change.

Alpha sat opposite her.

He looked at her quietly.

“What brought you here?” he asked.

Clara took a deep breath.

“I want to become famous.”

Alpha looked at her for a moment.

“Famous for what?”

Clara smiled.

“I want people to know my name.”

Alpha nodded slowly.

“Tell me more.”

Clara began telling him about her life in Lagos.

She had always wanted to become somebody important.

She had tried different businesses.

She had sold clothes.

She had sold beauty products.

She had tried making videos.

She had posted pictures online.

Sometimes people noticed her.

But the attention never lasted.

One person would praise her today and forget her tomorrow.

Another person would promise to help her and later stop answering her calls.

Clara became tired of starting again and again.

She wanted something that would last.

She wanted people to see her and remember her.

She wanted brands to look for her.

She wanted invitations to big events.

She wanted people to talk about her work.

Most of all, she wanted to prove to the people who once laughed at her that she could become somebody.

Alpha listened without interrupting her.

When she finished, he asked another question.

“Why do you want fame so much?”

Clara became quiet.

She looked at the floor.

“Because I am tired of being nobody.”

Alpha shook his head.

“You are not nobody.”

Clara looked at him.

“You don't know me.”

“I may not know everything about you,” Alpha said, “but that does not mean you are nobody.”

Clara smiled a little.

For the first time since she entered the room, she felt relaxed.

Alpha asked about her work.

He asked what she had already tried.

He asked about the people she worked with.

He asked what she wanted people to remember about her.

Clara answered everything.

Alpha soon noticed something about her.

She was hardworking.

She was also very impatient.

She wanted everything to happen quickly.

She wanted success immediately.

She wanted people to notice her without waiting.

Alpha told her something she did not expect.

“Fame is not something you can hold in your hand.”

Clara looked at him.

“What do you mean?”

“You can become famous today and people can forget you tomorrow.”

Clara nodded slowly.

“But I still want it.”

“I know.”

She opened the small bag she brought with her.

Inside was money.

She placed it on the table.

Alpha looked at the money but did not touch it.

“What is this?”

“My payment.”

“For what?”

“For the work you are going to do for me.”

Alpha pushed the money back toward her.

“I have not agreed to anything yet.”

Clara looked surprised.

“But people told me you help women.”

“I do.”

“Then help me.”

Alpha looked at her carefully.

“I can help you, but I cannot promise that everybody in Lagos will suddenly know your name.”

Clara frowned.

“Then what can you do?”

“I can do my part.”

“And what is my part?”

“Your work.”

Clara was silent.

Alpha continued.

“If you want people to know you, you must give them something worth knowing you for.”

Those words stayed in Clara's mind.

She had expected Alpha to tell her about a quick secret.

She had expected something that would make everything happen overnight.

Instead, Alpha was telling her that she still had to work.

She almost became angry.

But she remembered why she came.

She wanted her life to change.

So she nodded.

“I am ready.”

Alpha agreed to work with her.

He gave her some instructions.

He told her to take her work seriously.

He told her to stop copying other people.

He told her to be careful about the people she trusted.

He also told her that attention could be dangerous when a person did not know how to handle it.

Clara promised to listen.

The next morning, she returned to Lagos.

Before entering her vehicle, she turned to Alpha.

“Alpha.”

“Yes?”

“I believe you.”

Alpha smiled.

“Then believe in yourself too.”

Clara entered the vehicle.

The vehicle slowly left Oduama.

Alpha stood outside his compound and watched until it disappeared from sight.

He did not know what would happen next.

But Clara followed his advice.

She returned to Lagos and became more serious about her work.

She stopped trying to copy every popular person she saw online.

She started showing people what made her different.

She worked harder.

She became more careful.

She stopped telling everybody about her plans.

Then one evening, something happened.

Clara posted a video.

At first, she did not think much about it.

She put her phone down and continued with her work.

A few hours later, she checked her phone.

She was surprised.

The video had received many views.

She refreshed the page.

The number kept rising.

People were sharing it.

People were talking about her.

Some people asked who she was.

Others asked where they could find her.

Clara could not believe what she was seeing.

She called her friend immediately.

“Look at this!”

Her friend screamed when she saw the numbers.

“Clara! What did you do?”

“I don't know!”

They both laughed.

The next morning, the attention was even bigger.

Companies began sending messages.

People wanted her to promote their products.

Event organizers started asking her to attend their events.

People who had ignored her before suddenly wanted to speak with her.

Clara remembered Alpha.

She called him.

“Alpha!”

He answered calmly.

“What happened?”

“It is working!”

“What is working?”

“People are noticing me!”

Alpha smiled.

“That is good.”

“Good? Alpha, it is more than good!”

Clara laughed.

“My phone has not stopped ringing.”

Alpha told her to remain calm.

“Do not let the attention change you.”

“I won't.”

“And do not trust everybody who comes to you.”

“I understand.”

Clara kept working.

Her name became more popular.

People began recognizing her.

Her pictures were shared.

Her videos reached more people.

She started receiving invitations.

Soon, people began calling her a rising star from Lagos.

Clara was happy.

She had spent years trying to get this kind of attention.

Now it was happening.

She never forgot Alpha.

Whenever someone asked how she started getting serious about her life, she told them about the man in Oduama.

Soon, more women heard about Alpha.

Some wanted to meet him.

Some wanted the same kind of help Clara received.

The women coming to Alpha's compound increased.

They came from different places.

Some came from nearby towns.

Some came from far away.

Some came from Lagos.

Alpha's small compound was becoming known outside Oduama.

But with fame came something else.

New people began entering Clara's life.

Some were honest.

Some only wanted to benefit from her new popularity.

Clara did not notice it at first.

She was too happy about the new life she was enjoying.

One afternoon, a man approached her after an event.

He introduced himself as Victor.

He told Clara that he knew many important people.

He promised to take her career to another level.

Clara listened.

She did not know that Victor had his own plan.

And before long, his plan would bring trouble into Clara's life.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

⚠️ Chapter Four – The Man Behind the Smile

For some time, everything looked good for Clara.

Her name was growing.

Her phone was always busy.

People wanted to meet her.

Companies wanted her to promote their products.

Event organizers wanted her at their events.

People who once ignored her were now calling her.

Clara was enjoying the new life.

But Alpha had warned her many times.

“Not everybody who smiles at you is your friend.”

Clara remembered those words, but she did not think much about them.

She believed she could handle herself.

Then Victor came into her life.

Victor always dressed well.

He knew how to speak to important people.

He knew how to make a person feel important.

Whenever he spoke to Clara, he called her “Madam Star.”

Clara liked the name.

“You are going somewhere big,” Victor would tell her.

“You have no idea how far you can go.”

Clara would smile.

“I hope so.”

Victor always had another person to introduce to her.

One day, it was a businessman.

Another day, it was an event organizer.

Another day, it was someone who claimed to know people in the entertainment world.

Victor slowly became one of the people Clara trusted.

He started helping her with some of her arrangements.

He spoke to people on her behalf.

He helped her find events.

He even told her that he could help her become one of the biggest names in Lagos.

Clara believed him.

Then one evening, Victor brought a new offer.

“There is a big event coming,” he told her.

Clara looked excited.

“What kind of event?”

“A very big one.”

“Where?”

“Lagos.”

“Who will be there?”

“Important people.”

Clara wanted to know more.

Victor told her that many business owners and public figures would attend.

He told her that if she performed well, many doors would open for her.

Clara agreed immediately.

But there was one problem.

Victor said she needed to pay money before everything could be arranged.

“How much?” Clara asked.

Victor mentioned a large amount.

Clara became quiet.

“That is a lot.”

“It is an investment,” Victor said.

“You are not paying for nothing.”

Clara thought about it.

She wanted the opportunity.

She wanted to become bigger.

So she agreed.

She sent the money.

Victor thanked her.

“Trust me,” he said.

“You will thank me later.”

The days passed.

Then the event date came closer.

Clara started preparing.

She bought a new dress.

She prepared her speech.

She planned what she would say.

She was excited.

Then she called Victor.

“Are we still ready?”

“Of course,” he said.

“Everything is under control.”

Clara smiled.

But on the day of the event, something went wrong.

When Clara arrived at the place, nobody knew about her.

The people at the entrance looked at her name.

They checked their list.

Her name was not there.

Clara became confused.

“I am supposed to be here.”

The security man checked again.

“I am sorry, Madam. Your name is not on the list.”

Clara called Victor.

He did not answer.

She called again.

Still nothing.

She sent messages.

No reply.

Clara stood outside the event for almost an hour.

People walked past her.

Some recognized her.

Some took pictures.

But she could not understand what was happening.

Finally, she went home.

She was angry.

She was hurt.

She felt embarrassed.

That night, Victor finally called.

“Clara, I am sorry.”

“What happened?”

“There was a problem.”

“What problem?”

“I will explain tomorrow.”

Clara became angry.

“You took my money.”

“I know.”

“You told me everything was ready.”

“I know.”

“So what happened?”

Victor did not answer.

Then he ended the call.

Clara stared at her phone.

Something did not feel right.

The next morning, she tried calling him again.

His phone was switched off.

She became worried.

She asked around.

Nobody knew where Victor was.

Days passed.

Victor disappeared.

Clara began hearing stories about him.

Other people said he had done the same thing to them.

Some said he had collected money from them and disappeared.

Clara felt foolish.

She remembered Alpha's warning.

“Not everybody who smiles at you is your friend.”

She picked up her phone.

She called Alpha.

He answered.

“Clara.”

“Alpha.”

Her voice was weak.

“What happened?”

She told him everything.

Alpha listened until she finished.

He did not laugh at her.

He did not say, “I told you so.”

He simply said:

“Come.”

Clara travelled back to Oduama.

When she arrived, Alpha was sitting under the mango tree.

She sat beside him.

“I made a mistake.”

Alpha nodded.

“Yes.”

“I trusted the wrong person.”

“Yes.”

“I feel stupid.”

“You are not stupid.”

Clara looked at him.

“But I should have listened to you.”

“You should learn from what happened.”

Clara became quiet.

Alpha told her something important.

“Your fame is growing, Clara. That means more people will come to you. Some will come because they love your work. Some will come because they want something from you.”

Clara listened.

“You must learn how to know the difference.”

Clara nodded.

“I understand.”

Alpha then asked her another question.

“Do you still want to be famous?”

Clara looked toward the village road.

For a moment, she said nothing.

Then she smiled.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because I now know that fame is not the most important thing.”

Alpha smiled.

“Good.”

Clara continued.

“I still want to succeed. But I want to succeed without losing myself.”

Alpha nodded.

“That is better.”

Clara stayed in Oduama for several days.

During that time, she thought deeply about her life.

She realized that her biggest mistake was not wanting success.

Her mistake was believing that every person who promised success was good.

When she returned to Lagos, she changed.

She became more careful.

She started checking every business offer before accepting it.

She stopped giving people control over her money.

She also stopped allowing people to make decisions for her.

Her fans noticed the change.

Clara became more confident.

She became more focused.

Her popularity continued to grow.

But this time, she was not chasing fame.

She was building something real.

She started helping young women who were also trying to build their own businesses.

She shared what she had learned.

She told them about hard work.

She told them about patience.

She warned them about fake friends.

People began respecting her even more.

Her story was no longer only about becoming famous.

It was now about becoming wiser.

And whenever people asked her who helped her change her life, she always mentioned Alpha.

Back in Oduama, the number of women visiting Alpha continued to grow.

His name was becoming bigger.

But Alpha remained the same quiet man.

He still woke early.

He still worked in his small room.

He still listened to the women who came to him.

He still refused to work for men.

And he still remembered the betrayal that made him choose that path.

But something was about to happen that would make Alpha's name travel even farther.

Clara was planning something.

Something much bigger than anything she had done before.

And this time, she wanted Alpha to be part of it.

🌙 End of Chapter Four

🌟 Chapter Five – The Woman Who Never Forgot

Clara returned to Lagos with a different heart.

She was still famous.

People still knew her name.

Her phone still rang every day.

Companies still wanted to work with her.

People still stopped her on the street to take pictures.

But Clara was no longer the same woman who first went to Oduama.

She had learned something important.

Success was not only about being known.

It was also about knowing who you were.

It was about knowing the people around you.

It was about knowing when to trust and when to be careful.

Most of all, Clara had learned that one bad experience should not destroy a good dream.

She decided to build her career properly.

She started her own small team.

She hired people she trusted.

She checked every agreement before signing it.

She stopped allowing people to rush her.

She also started helping young women who wanted to start their own businesses.

Some wanted to become fashion designers.

Some wanted to sell beauty products.

Some wanted to become content creators.

Some simply wanted to become independent.

Clara remembered how she once felt.

She remembered sitting in Alpha's room and saying that she was tired of being nobody.

Now she wanted other women to understand that they did not need fame before they could become valuable.

She began speaking to women wherever she went.

She told them her story.

She told them about the good days.

She told them about the bad days.

She told them about Victor.

She told them about the mistake she made.

And she told them about Alpha.

One afternoon, Clara sat in her office looking through some old pictures.

She saw the first picture she took before travelling to Oduama.

She smiled.

She remembered how nervous she was that day.

She remembered the small room.

She remembered Alpha's words.

“Believe in yourself too.”

Those words never left her.

Clara picked up her phone.

She called Alpha.

“Alpha.”

“Yes, Clara.”

“I want to come back.”

“You are always welcome.”

“I want to do something for the women.”

Alpha became quiet.

“What do you want to do?”

“I want to build a place where women can come and learn.”

Alpha smiled.

“That is a good idea.”

“I want you to be there.”

“I will be there.”

A few weeks later, Clara returned to Oduama.

But this time she did not come alone.

She came with some women from Lagos.

She also came with clothes, books, business materials and money to support the women in the village.

The people of Oduama were surprised.

The young woman they had heard about was now standing in front of them.

She was the famous Clara from Lagos.

But she did not behave like somebody who was better than them.

She greeted the elders.

She greeted the women.

She greeted the children.

She laughed with the people.

Then she walked straight to Alpha's compound.

Alpha was sitting under the mango tree.

When he saw Clara, he smiled.

“You came back.”

“I told you I would.”

Clara sat beside him.

For a few moments, neither of them spoke.

Then Clara looked around.

“Your place has not changed.”

Alpha laughed.

“Why should it?”

Clara laughed too.

“I expected you to build a big house by now.”

“I don't need one.”

Clara looked at him.

“That is why people respect you.”

Alpha shook his head.

“People should respect themselves first.”

That afternoon, Clara told Alpha about her plan.

She wanted to create a small center for women.

A place where women could learn skills.

A place where young women could talk about their dreams.

A place where women who had failed could start again.

Alpha listened.

He liked the idea.

The following morning, the women gathered.

Clara stood before them.

She looked at the women and smiled.

“I came here years ago because I wanted to become famous.”

The women listened.

“I thought fame would solve everything.”

She shook her head.

“But I learned that fame is only one part of life.”

She told them about Victor.

She told them about the money she lost.

She told them about the shame she felt.

Then she told them what Alpha said to her.

“He did not tell me that I would become famous.”

“He told me to work.”

The women smiled.

“He told me to believe in myself.”

“And that was the beginning of my real journey.”

The women clapped.

Alpha watched from a distance.

He did not want praise.

He did not want people to put him on a high seat.

He only wanted the women to learn.

That day became one of the biggest days Oduama had seen in a long time.

Clara gave business support to several women.

Some received money to start small businesses.

Some received materials.

Some received training.

Others simply received encouragement.

Clara did not forget the women who had nothing.

She wanted them to have a chance.

After the event, one young woman walked up to Alpha.

She looked nervous.

“Sir.”

Alpha looked at her.

“Yes?”

“I want to become something.”

Alpha smiled.

“You already are something.”

The young woman smiled.

Then she walked away with confidence.

Clara saw what happened.

She smiled.

She finally understood why so many women respected Alpha.

It was not only because of the work he did.

It was because of the way he treated them.

He made them feel that they still had a future.

He made them believe that their mistakes did not have to become the end of their lives.

Years passed.

Clara became even more successful.

Her name became known in many places.

She built businesses.

She helped women.

She became a role model to many young people.

But whenever people asked her where everything started, she always remembered Oduama.

She remembered the small room.

She remembered the wooden chairs.

She remembered the man who listened to her.

And she remembered the day she first met Alpha.

Alpha also continued his work.

He remained committed to helping women.

He never returned to the way he worked before.

The betrayal he suffered from men had changed his path forever.

But he no longer allowed the pain to control his heart.

He simply chose the work he believed he could do best.

Women continued coming to him.

Some came with problems.

Some came with dreams.

Some came with fear.

Some came with nothing except hope.

And Alpha continued listening.

The story of Alpha became bigger than the story of one man.

It became a story about trust.

It became a story about betrayal.

It became a story about starting again.

And it became a story about a woman from Lagos who wanted fame but found something much more important.

She found purpose.

Clara never forgot the lesson.

And she made sure other women did not forget it either.

One evening, Clara stood outside her office in Lagos.

She looked at the busy road.

Cars moved from one side to another.

People hurried home.

The city lights were shining.

Her phone rang.

It was Alpha.

She smiled before answering.

“Alpha.”

“How are you?”

“I am fine.”

“Are you still helping the women?”

Clara smiled.

“Every day.”

Alpha laughed.

“Then keep going.”

“I will.”

They ended the call.

Clara looked at the city again.

She remembered the woman she used to be.

She remembered the woman she had become.

Then she smiled.

She had wanted the whole world to know her name.

But in the end, she learned that the greatest success was not having people know your name.

The greatest success was using your name to help somebody else rise.

And that was exactly what Clara chose to do.

She never forgot Alpha.

She never forgot Oduama.

And she never forgot the lesson that changed her life.

📖 The End
