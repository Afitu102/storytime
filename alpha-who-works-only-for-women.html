🐺 Alpha Who Works Only for Women

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌅 Chapter One – The Man Who Chose Women

In a small village called Oduama, there lived a man named Alpha.

Alpha was not a chief.

He was not the richest man in the village.

He did not own many farms.

He did not have a big house.

But almost everybody in Oduama knew his name.

Women knew him.

Men knew him.

Hunters knew him.

Traders knew him.

Even people from far villages had heard about him.

People called him Alpha because of the way he carried himself.

He was quiet.

He watched people carefully.

He did not talk too much.

And when he decided to help somebody, he gave the person all his attention.

Alpha was known for spiritual work.

He had learned the old ways from his grandfather.

His grandfather was an old man who lived at the edge of the village.

People went to him when life became hard.

Some came because their businesses were failing.

Some came because their farms were not doing well.

Some came because they wanted peace in their homes.

Some came because they were afraid.

The old man never promised anybody that life would become easy.

He only taught them what he knew.

He taught Alpha about herbs.

He taught him about old prayers.

He taught him about respect.

He taught him how to listen before speaking.

Most of all, he taught him that spiritual work was not something to play with.

"Never use what you know to destroy another person," his grandfather always told him.

Alpha remembered those words.

For many years, he worked for both men and women.

He did not care whether a person was rich or poor.

If a man came to him with a problem, Alpha listened.

If a woman came to him, Alpha listened too.

He helped farmers who wanted better luck in their work.

He helped traders who wanted to grow their businesses.

He helped people who were afraid to take a new step in life.

He also worked with people who wanted to become known for their talents.

Alpha became very good at what he did.

People started travelling from other villages to meet him.

Sometimes, a man would arrive before sunrise.

He would sit outside Alpha's small house and wait.

Sometimes, three or four people would wait in a line.

Alpha never rushed them.

He would bring out a wooden stool.

He would sit down.

Then he would ask questions.

"What is your problem?"

"What have you tried?"

"What do you really want?"

Many people found his questions strange.

They expected him to start his work immediately.

But Alpha believed that he needed to understand the person first.

One day, a wealthy man named Chief Okoro came to him.

Chief Okoro had a large farm.

He had many workers.

He had plenty of money.

But his business had started going down.

People stopped buying from him.

His workers were leaving.

His farm was also doing badly.

Chief Okoro believed somebody was working against him.

He went to Alpha.

"I need your help," he said.

Alpha looked at him.

"What kind of help?"

"I want my business back."

Alpha asked him questions.

He listened to the whole story.

Then he told the chief something he did not want to hear.

"Your problem is not another man."

Chief Okoro became angry.

"What do you mean?"

"You are losing your workers because you do not treat them well."

The chief was silent.

Alpha continued.

"You pay them late."

"You shout at them."

"You take all the praise when the work goes well."

"And when something goes wrong, you blame them."

The chief did not like what he heard.

But he still listened.

Alpha did his work with him.

He also told him to change the way he treated his workers.

The chief slowly changed.

His workers started staying.

His farm became better.

His business started growing again.

Soon, people started saying that Alpha was powerful.

They said he could see things other people could not see.

They said he knew what was hidden.

They said he could turn a person's life around.

Alpha never allowed the praise to enter his head.

But then something happened.

A man named Bako came to him.

Bako was a businessman from another village.

He wanted Alpha to help him become more successful.

Alpha agreed.

They worked together for some time.

Bako's business improved.

More people started buying from him.

His name became known in nearby villages.

But Bako wanted more.

He wanted to become the richest man in the whole area.

Alpha told him to be patient.

Bako refused.

He wanted Alpha to do something that Alpha did not agree with.

Alpha refused.

Bako became angry.

"You work for other men," he said.

"Why can you not do this for me?"

"Because it is wrong," Alpha answered.

Bako left.

Alpha thought that was the end of the matter.

It was not.

A few weeks later, people started talking.

They said Alpha had taken money from Bako and refused to help him.

Some said Alpha had cheated him.

Others said Alpha had done spiritual work against him.

The story spread very fast.

Alpha was shocked.

He had done nothing wrong.

He went to Bako.

"Why are you telling people these things?"

Bako looked at him.

"I did not say anything."

"People are saying your name."

Bako smiled.

"Then let them talk."

Alpha knew something was wrong.

The matter became worse.

Some men who had once trusted Alpha stopped coming to him.

Some even insulted him.

One man came to his house and shouted at him.

"You are a fake man!"

Another said,

"You think you are better than everybody!"

Alpha said nothing.

He remembered his grandfather.

"Never answer anger with anger."

He stayed quiet.

But inside him, something had changed.

He began to ask himself a question.

"Why do I keep helping people who turn around and hurt me?"

He did not have an answer.

One evening, Alpha sat inside his room.

His grandfather's old things were still there.

An old wooden bowl.

A small leather bag.

A walking stick.

And an old piece of cloth his grandfather used during his work.

Alpha looked at them for a long time.

He remembered all the men he had helped.

Some had thanked him.

Some had forgotten him.

And some had betrayed him.

Then he remembered the women.

The women who came quietly.

The mothers.

The traders.

The widows.

The young women trying to build something for themselves.

They usually came with problems.

But they also came with respect.

They listened.

They followed his advice.

And when things became better, many of them came back to thank him.

Alpha thought about it all night.

By morning, he had made a decision.

He would no longer work for men.

From that day, Alpha would work only for women.

People laughed when they heard.

"Alpha has lost his mind."

"How can a man work only for women?"

"He will soon come back."

Alpha did not care.

He placed a small wooden sign outside his house.

The sign had only one sentence on it.

"Women Only."

The first morning after he put up the sign, nobody came.

Alpha sat outside his house.

He watched the road.

Nothing happened.

By afternoon, he almost laughed at himself.

Then an old woman appeared.

Her name was Mama Nneka.

She carried a small basket.

She stopped in front of the sign.

She read it.

Then she looked at Alpha.

"Is this sign real?"

Alpha nodded.

"Yes."

"You no longer work for men?"

"No."

"Why?"

Alpha smiled.

"That is a long story."

Mama Nneka sat down.

"Then tell me."

Alpha looked at her.

For the first time in many days, he felt peace.

He knew his life had entered a new road.

He did not know that this decision would soon make his name famous far beyond Oduama.

He did not know that one woman from Lagos would soon walk into his life.

Her name would be Miss Clara.

She would come with one big dream.

She wanted the whole country to know her name.

And Alpha would become the man who helped her believe that the dream was possible.
