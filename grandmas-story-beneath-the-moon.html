🌙 Grandma's Story Beneath the Moon

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🌙 Chapter One – Come Closer, My Children

"Come closer."

That was how Grandma Sewa always began her stories.

Not with a book.

Not with a drum.

Just three words.

"Come closer, my children."

So the children moved closer.

The young men moved closer too.

Even those who pretended not to care about old stories came to sit near her.

It was a cool night in the village.

The moon was bright.

It looked so close that little Timi stretched his hand toward it.

"Grandma, can someone touch the moon?"

Grandma Sewa laughed.

"If your hand is long enough."

Everyone laughed.

She shook her head and pulled her blue wrapper tighter around her shoulders.

Then she looked at the moon.

Her smile slowly left her face.

"But there was once a girl who came very close."

The laughter stopped.

Young Kelechi leaned forward.

"How close?"

Grandma Sewa looked at him.

"Close enough for the moon to know her name."

Nobody spoke.

Even the fire seemed to burn more quietly.

Grandma Sewa picked up a small stick.

She drew a circle in the soft earth.

"Her name was Amaka."

Amaka lived in a small village near a wide river.

She was not the richest girl in the village.

She did not wear fine beads.

She did not live in a big house.

But she had one thing that made people remember her.

She could hear things other people could not hear.

She could hear fish moving under the river.

She could hear birds sleeping inside trees.

She could hear the sound of rain long before clouds came.

But there was one sound she could never explain.

A soft voice that called her name whenever the moon was full.

"Amaka..."

The voice came from the forest.

Every full moon.

Always at midnight.

Amaka never told anyone.

Not even her mother.

She was afraid they would laugh.

One night, she decided to follow it.

She waited until everyone in the house was asleep.

She quietly opened the door.

The night air touched her face.

She looked back at the sleeping house.

Then she stepped outside.

The village was silent.

No dog barked.

No child cried.

Even the old palm trees were still.

Amaka walked toward the forest.

She carried a small lantern.

Its flame shook as she walked.

Then she heard it.

"Amaka..."

She stopped.

The voice was closer.

"Amaka..."

She raised the lantern.

Nothing.

Only trees.

Only leaves.

Only the bright moon above her.

She took another step.

A dry branch broke behind her.

Crack.

Amaka turned quickly.

Her heart jumped.

A small white goat stood on the path.

Amaka breathed out.

"You scared me."

The goat looked at her.

Then it walked away.

Amaka followed.

The goat stopped beside a large tree.

Amaka raised her lantern.

There was something carved into the tree.

A moon.

And beneath the moon was a small hand.

Amaka touched it.

The tree became warm.

She pulled her hand away.

The goat ran into the bushes.

Amaka followed it with her eyes.

Then she heard a sound.

Not from the forest.

From above.

She looked up.

The moon was moving.

Slowly.

Very slowly.

It was coming down.

Amaka stepped backward.

The light grew brighter.

The trees around her began to glow.

The river in the distance started shining like silver.

Then a woman appeared beneath the moon.

She wore a long white dress.

Her hair was silver.

Her feet did not touch the ground.

Amaka wanted to run.

But her legs would not move.

The woman looked at her.

"You came."

Amaka swallowed.

"Who are you?"

The woman smiled.

"Someone who has been waiting."

Amaka looked around.

"Waiting for me?"

The woman nodded.

"Yes."

Amaka wanted to ask why.

But before she could speak, the woman pointed toward the river.

"Look."

Amaka turned.

The river was moving backward.

Water was leaving the village.

The riverbed was slowly becoming dry.

Amaka's eyes widened.

"What is happening?"

The woman looked at her.

"The moon is tired."

Amaka shook her head.

"I don't understand."

The woman came closer.

Her voice became soft.

"Your village has forgotten something."

Amaka looked toward the village.

The lights of the houses were going out one by one.

A strange wind moved through the trees.

Then every bird in the forest flew into the sky at once.

Amaka looked up.

The moon was no longer round.

A dark mark had appeared across it.

The woman reached into her dress.

She brought out a small wooden key.

She placed it in Amaka's hand.

"Take this home."

Amaka looked at the key.

"What does it open?"

The woman smiled.

"You will know when you see the door."

Then the woman began to disappear.

Amaka reached for her.

"Wait!"

The woman looked back.

"And Amaka..."

Amaka froze.

"Do not let your grandmother hear the moon calling."

Then she vanished.

The forest became dark.

The river became silent.

Amaka stood alone.

She looked at the wooden key.

A tiny word was carved into it.

HOME.

Amaka ran.

She ran through the trees.

She ran past the old palm tree.

She ran across the narrow path.

She ran until she reached the first house in the village.

Then she stopped.

Her mother was standing outside.

Her father was beside her.

And behind them stood Grandma Sewa.

Grandma Sewa was not smiling.

She was staring at the key in Amaka's hand.

Amaka slowly walked toward them.

"Grandma..."

The old woman raised one hand.

"Where did you get that?"

Amaka looked at the key.

"The moon gave it to me."

Grandma Sewa's face changed.

She looked toward the sky.

The moon was still there.

But now it was almost completely dark.

Grandma Sewa whispered something.

Amaka could not hear it.

Then the old woman turned to everyone.

"Nobody leaves this house tonight."

The wind suddenly stopped.

A knock came from the forest.

Three slow knocks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Grandma Sewa looked at Amaka.

For the first time, the old woman looked afraid.

"She has found us."

Amaka held the wooden key tighter.

Then something knocked again.

But this time...

the sound came from inside the house.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌿 Chapter Three – Promise Beneath the Moon

Grandma Sewa did not sleep that night.

She sat beside the dead fire.

Her hands rested on her walking stick.

Amaka sat close to her mother.

Her father stood near the window.

Nobody wanted to speak first.

Outside, the village was quiet.

Too quiet.

Then Grandma Sewa finally spoke.

"Some stories are better told before the sun comes up."

Amaka looked at her.

"Then tell us."

Grandma Sewa looked toward the window.

The moon was still hiding behind dark clouds.

"Many years ago, this village was almost destroyed by a long dry season."

The river became small.

The farms became dry.

The goats had little grass to eat.

People walked far just to find water.

Children woke up thirsty.

Even the old trees began to lose their leaves.

One night, the elders gathered beside the river.

They asked for rain.

They prayed.

They sang.

They waited.

But nothing happened.

Then a young woman came to the river.

Her name was Nneka.

She carried a small wooden bowl.

She stood at the riverbank and looked at the moon.

The moon was bright that night.

Nneka raised the bowl.

"If you can hear us, please help my people."

The river moved.

Just once.

A small wave touched her feet.

Nneka stepped backward.

Then a voice came from the water.

"What will you give?"

Nneka looked around.

Nobody was there.

"I will give anything."

The voice answered.

"Anything?"

Nneka nodded.

"Anything."

The moon became brighter.

Rain began to fall.

At first, it was only a few drops.

Then it became heavy.

The dry river filled.

The people shouted with joy.

Children danced in the rain.

Farmers ran into their fields.

Nobody asked what Nneka had promised.

But Nneka knew.

She had promised that whenever the moon called, someone from her family would answer.

Grandma Sewa stopped speaking.

Amaka stared at her.

"That was the promise?"

Grandma Sewa nodded.

"Yes."

Amaka looked at the moon.

"But why is it calling me?"

Grandma Sewa lowered her eyes.

"Because the promise was never finished."

Amaka's father stepped forward.

"Mother, enough."

Grandma Sewa looked at him.

"You know I am right."

He looked away.

Amaka stood.

"What are you hiding from me?"

Her father said nothing.

Amaka looked at her mother.

Her mother was crying quietly.

"Mum?"

Her mother wiped her face.

"I remember the river now."

Amaka moved closer.

"What happened there?"

Her mother took a slow breath.

"I was there when your grandmother tried to end the promise."

Grandma Sewa tightened her grip on the walking stick.

"We made a mistake."

Amaka waited.

Her mother continued.

"We thought we could hide the promise."

The wind moved through the room.

"So we hid the key."

Amaka looked down.

The wooden key was still in her hand.

Her face became pale.

"You hid it?"

Grandma Sewa nodded.

"We hid it inside this house."

Amaka looked around.

The old walls.

The wooden cupboard.

The small windows.

The fire.

Everything suddenly felt different.

Then three knocks came from beneath the floor.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Everyone froze.

Amaka looked down.

"Something is under us."

Grandma Sewa stood slowly.

She walked to the middle of the room.

She tapped the floor with her stick.

One part sounded empty.

She tapped again.

The same sound.

Amaka's father moved closer.

Together, they pushed aside an old mat.

There was a small wooden door beneath it.

Amaka stared at it.

The key in her hand became warm again.

"That is the door."

Grandma Sewa nodded.

"Yes."

Amaka placed the key into the lock.

It turned by itself.

Click.

The door opened.

A cold wind came from below.

There was a narrow staircase.

It went deep under the house.

Amaka lifted the lantern.

The light showed old drawings on the walls.

A moon.

A river.

A woman.

And a small child.

Amaka moved closer.

The child in the drawing looked like her.

She touched the wall.

The drawing changed.

The child became older.

Then older again.

Then it became Amaka.

She pulled her hand away.

"Grandma..."

The old woman stepped beside her.

"Don't be afraid."

Amaka looked at the dark stairs.

"Where does this lead?"

Grandma Sewa looked down.

"To the place where Nneka made her promise."

A soft sound came from below.

Someone was humming.

The same song from the forest.

Amaka held the lantern tighter.

Then a child's voice came from the darkness.

"Amaka..."

She stopped.

"Who is there?"

The voice laughed softly.

"You came back."

Amaka looked at Grandma Sewa.

The old woman had gone completely still.

"Grandma?"

Grandma Sewa whispered one name.

"Nneka."

The lantern flame suddenly went out.

Darkness swallowed the staircase.

Then two small feet appeared on the step below Amaka.

Bare feet.

Wet with river water.

Amaka raised the lantern.

The flame came back.

A little girl stood in the darkness.

She looked about eight years old.

She wore a blue cloth.

The same blue cloth Grandma Sewa had found.

The girl looked at Amaka.

Then she smiled.

"Grandma told you the wrong story."

Amaka felt the cold run through her body.

"What do you mean?"

The girl pointed deeper into the darkness.

"Ask her who really made the promise."

Amaka turned toward Grandma Sewa.

The old woman was crying.

And for the first time that night, she had no answer.
