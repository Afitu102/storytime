🍲 Mama Bisi Food

🌍 African Folktales • 🕒 30 min read

🍲 Chapter One – Mama Bisi's Free Food

Every morning, before the village had fully awakened, the smell of delicious food already filled the air.

It was the kind of smell that could make a hungry man forget where he was going.

The aroma travelled through the narrow village streets, passed the farms, entered people's homes, and seemed to whisper one name into everyone's ears.

“Mama Bisi.”

Mama Bisi was the most popular food seller in the village.

Her small food stall stood beside the busy village road, decorated with bright fabrics and surrounded by large steaming pots. From early morning until sunset, she cooked rice, stew, yam, beans, soup, and other delicious meals that made the villagers lick their fingers and ask for more.

But there was something strange about Mama Bisi's food.

She never asked anyone for money.

Not even a coin.

Anyone who came to her stall could eat.

Farmers came after working in their fields. Children came after school. Traders came during the afternoon. Even elderly villagers who could barely afford a meal were welcomed with a warm smile and a full plate.

“Eat, my child,” Mama Bisi would say with a cheerful smile.

“Take another plate if you are still hungry.”

The villagers loved her for it.

They praised her generosity everywhere they went.

“God will bless Mama Bisi,” they often said.

“She has the kindest heart in this village.”

But nobody knew what was hiding behind that beautiful smile.

Mama Bisi was not giving the food away because she was simply kind.

There was something else she wanted.

Something far more valuable than money.

Something the villagers could not see.

Every time someone ate her food, Mama Bisi quietly watched them.

She watched their eyes.

She watched their smiles.

She watched the way they returned their empty plates and asked for another serving.

And every time someone said,

“Mama Bisi, this food is too delicious!”

A strange satisfaction appeared in her eyes.

The villagers did not notice it.

They were too busy enjoying themselves.

Soon, something unusual began happening in the village.

People stopped cooking in their own homes.

Why would they spend hours preparing food when Mama Bisi was giving away better food for free?

Mothers stopped preparing breakfast. Farmers stopped carrying food from home. Young men began visiting Mama Bisi's stall every morning. Children started asking their parents when they would go and eat Mama Bisi's food again.

Even families who had once cooked together every evening slowly abandoned their kitchens.

The entire village was becoming dependent on one woman.

And Mama Bisi loved every moment of it.

She would stand behind her cooking pots with a smile on her face while watching the villagers line up for her food.

But whenever she turned away from them, that smile disappeared.

Her eyes became cold.

Because Mama Bisi was not merely feeding the villagers.

She was taking something from them.

Something they could not see.

Something they did not know they were giving away.

There was, however, one person in the village who had begun to notice.

Her name was Mrs. Adaeze, the teacher at the village primary school.

Unlike everyone else, the teacher had never eaten Mama Bisi's food.

She watched from a distance.

She noticed how people who ate the food always came back.

Again.

And again.

And again.

She noticed that some villagers would become restless whenever they went a day without eating Mama Bisi's food.

Most disturbing of all, she noticed that Mama Bisi seemed to know exactly who would return the next day.

One afternoon, the teacher finally walked to Mama Bisi's stall.

Mama Bisi welcomed her with the same bright smile she gave everyone else.

“Teacher, come and eat. I have prepared something special today.”

The teacher looked at the steaming plate in front of her.

Then she looked directly into Mama Bisi's eyes.

“No, thank you.”

Mama Bisi laughed softly.

“Why are you refusing my food? Everyone in this village eats here.”

The teacher remained silent for a moment.

Then she asked the question that made Mama Bisi's smile suddenly freeze.

“How do you manage to cook this much food every single day without asking anyone for money?”

For a few seconds, there was complete silence.

Mama Bisi stared at her.

Then she burst into laughter.

“Ah, Teacher! You think too much. Eat first. We can talk later.”

But the teacher still refused.

She walked away from the stall without tasting a single spoonful.

That evening, while the rest of the village enjoyed Mama Bisi's food, the teacher sat alone in her room thinking about what she had seen.

Something was wrong.

She could feel it.

And that night, she made a decision.

She would find out where Mama Bisi got the power to feed an entire village every single day.

So, when darkness covered the village and everyone went to sleep, the teacher quietly left her house.

She followed Mama Bisi from a distance.

Past the last houses.

Past the farms.

Deeper into the darkness.

Until Mama Bisi finally stopped beside the village river.

The teacher hid behind a large tree and watched.

Mama Bisi looked around to make sure nobody was following her.

Then she opened a small bag.

She brought out several strange objects.

The teacher held her breath.

Then Mama Bisi began to speak names into the darkness.

One name.

Then another.

Then another.

The teacher's heart began beating faster.

Because the names Mama Bisi was calling were not strangers.

They were the names of the villagers.

The teacher covered her mouth to stop herself from screaming.

What was Mama Bisi doing at the river in the middle of the night?

And why did she have the names of the villagers with her?

The teacher stayed hidden.

She watched.

And what she saw next would change everything she believed about Mama Bisi.

🌙 End of Chapter One

🌙 Chapter Two – What Happened at the River

The teacher remained behind the large tree, afraid to make even the smallest sound.

The village was completely silent.

There was no sound of children playing, no sound of people talking, and no sound of cooking coming from the nearby houses.

Everyone was asleep.

Everyone except Mama Bisi.

She stood alone beside the river with a small wooden bowl in her hands. Inside it were strange objects the teacher could not clearly see from where she was hiding.

Mama Bisi placed the bowl on the ground.

Then she began whispering.

At first, the teacher could not understand what she was saying.

But as Mama Bisi raised her voice, the words became clearer.

She was calling out the names of the villagers.

One after another.

“Chinedu...”

“Adaku...”

“Emeka...”

“Nneka...”

The teacher's heart began pounding.

These were the names of people she taught, people she greeted every morning, people who were happily eating Mama Bisi's food without knowing what was happening at the river.

Mama Bisi continued with the ritual.

She moved closer to the water and whispered words the teacher could not understand.

Then she dipped her fingers into the river.

She lifted a little water and allowed it to fall back into the river.

The teacher watched in disbelief.

“What kind of woman is this?”

she whispered to herself.

Suddenly, Mama Bisi stopped.

She slowly turned her head toward the trees.

The teacher froze.

For a terrifying moment, she thought Mama Bisi had seen her.

But Mama Bisi turned back toward the river and continued what she was doing.

The teacher waited until Mama Bisi finally picked up her bowl and began walking home.

She waited a few more minutes before quietly following her.

When Mama Bisi disappeared into her compound, the teacher returned home.

She could not sleep.

She kept thinking about the villagers' names.

She thought about the people who had become strangely attached to Mama Bisi's food.

She thought about the way people behaved whenever they had not eaten her food for a day.

Everything suddenly began to make sense.

The next morning, the teacher went to school as usual.

But she could not concentrate.

During break time, she watched the children carefully.

Several of them were talking about Mama Bisi's food.

One little girl told her friend,

“I cannot wait for afternoon. Mama Bisi will give us food again.”

Another child laughed.

“My mother does not cook anymore. She says there is no need because Mama Bisi always gives us food.”

The teacher's heart sank.

This was becoming more serious than she had imagined.

That afternoon, she went back to Mama Bisi's stall.

The usual crowd was already there.

People were laughing, eating, and praising the food.

Mama Bisi saw the teacher approaching and smiled.

“Teacher! Come and eat today. You have been watching me for too long.”

The teacher stopped.

She looked at Mama Bisi carefully.

“You know I have been watching you?”

Mama Bisi laughed.

“Of course I know. You are a teacher. Teachers notice everything.”

The teacher looked at the steaming food.

She could smell the delicious aroma, but she refused to move closer.

“I have a question for you.”

Mama Bisi smiled.

“Ask.”

“How can you afford to cook so much food every day when you don't collect money from anyone?”

For the first time, Mama Bisi's smile became faint.

But only for a moment.

Then she laughed again.

“Maybe I simply love feeding people.”

The teacher did not smile.

“And the things you do at the river every night? Do you also do that because you love feeding people?”

The atmosphere suddenly changed.

Mama Bisi's face became completely still.

The people around them continued eating, unaware of what was happening.

Mama Bisi slowly stepped closer to the teacher.

Her voice became quiet.

“Teacher... some things are better left unseen.”

The teacher looked directly into her eyes.

“And some things are too dangerous to ignore.”

Mama Bisi stared at her for several seconds.

Then she smiled again as though nothing had happened.

“Be careful, Teacher.”

The teacher walked away.

But as she left, she noticed something that made the hairs on her arms stand.

On Mama Bisi's table was the same small wooden bowl she had seen beside the river.

And inside it was a folded piece of paper.

The teacher stopped walking.

She looked back.

Mama Bisi was watching her.

Their eyes met.

Mama Bisi slowly placed her hand over the bowl.

The teacher continued walking.

That night, she made up her mind.

She would follow Mama Bisi again.

But this time, she would not return home until she discovered exactly what Mama Bisi was doing at the river.

And what the teacher saw on the following night would make her realize that the villagers were in far greater danger than she had ever imagined.

🌙 End of Chapter Two

🌑 Chapter Three – The Secret at the River

The following night, the teacher waited until the whole village had gone quiet.

She sat beside her window, watching the moon slowly rise above the palm trees.

She had tried to convince herself that what she saw the night before was nothing.

Perhaps Mama Bisi was simply performing some strange traditional ritual. Perhaps there was another explanation.

But deep inside, the teacher knew better.

There was something terribly wrong.

So, just before midnight, she left her house again.

She wrapped a dark cloth around herself and quietly followed the narrow path leading away from the village.

She kept a safe distance behind Mama Bisi, who was walking slowly toward the river with the same small wooden bowl in her hands.

The night was unusually quiet.

Even the crickets seemed to have stopped making noise.

When Mama Bisi reached the river bank, she looked around.

The teacher quickly hid behind a large tree.

Mama Bisi knelt beside the water and placed her bowl on the ground.

Then she brought out several small pieces of paper.

Each piece had a name written on it.

The teacher leaned forward carefully.

She could barely see them from where she was standing, but then Mama Bisi began reading the names aloud.

“Chinedu...”

“Nneka...”

“Emeka...”

“Obinna...”

“Adaku...”

The teacher's heart began racing.

These were the names of people she knew.

People who had eaten Mama Bisi's food.

Mama Bisi placed each paper beside the bowl.

Then she closed her eyes and began chanting words the teacher could not understand.

Her voice was low at first.

Then it grew louder.

The teacher watched as Mama Bisi moved her hands over the bowl and whispered the names again.

Suddenly, the river water began moving strangely.

There was no wind.

Yet small waves appeared on the surface.

The teacher held her breath.

Mama Bisi smiled.

It was not the warm smile she showed the villagers during the day.

This smile was cold.

Almost frightening.

She picked up one of the papers and held it close to the flame of a small lamp.

The teacher watched the paper burn.

As the flame consumed it, Mama Bisi whispered something into the darkness.

“Stay with me.”

The teacher covered her mouth.

She wanted to run.

But her feet refused to move.

Mama Bisi continued with the ritual.

One name after another.

One paper after another.

And with every name she called, she seemed more satisfied.

Then she opened a small container and poured a dark liquid into the river.

The water became completely still.

Mama Bisi bowed her head and whispered,

“They will keep coming back.”

The teacher's eyes widened.

Suddenly, she understood why the villagers could not stay away from the food.

It was not simply the taste.

Something was drawing them back.

Something they could not resist.

Mama Bisi slowly stood up.

Then she turned toward the village.

“Tomorrow, I will feed them again.”

The teacher felt a cold shiver run through her body.

She finally understood that Mama Bisi was not feeding the villagers out of love.

There was a purpose behind every plate of food.

Every smile.

Every free meal.

Every invitation to eat again.

The teacher quietly stepped backward.

But her foot landed on a dry branch.

CRACK!

Mama Bisi immediately turned around.

The teacher froze.

For several seconds, neither woman moved.

Mama Bisi stared into the darkness.

The teacher pressed herself against the tree, barely breathing.

Then Mama Bisi picked up her bowl and slowly walked toward the sound.

One step.

Then another.

The teacher closed her eyes.

She could hear Mama Bisi getting closer.

But just before Mama Bisi reached the tree, a loud owl cried from the opposite side of the river.

Mama Bisi stopped.

She looked toward the sound.

Then she turned around and walked away.

The teacher waited until Mama Bisi disappeared into the darkness.

Only then did she run.

She ran all the way back to the village without looking behind her.

When she reached her house, she locked the door and sat on the floor, breathing heavily.

She knew what she had to do.

She had to warn the villagers.

The next morning, she went from house to house telling people what she had seen.

But nobody believed her.

Instead, they laughed.

One man told her,

“Teacher, you are thinking too much. Mama Bisi feeds us for free. What evil can be inside free food?”

Another villager shook his head.

“You have refused to eat her food, so you don't know how delicious it is.”

Even some of the people she trusted refused to listen.

They had become too attached to Mama Bisi's cooking.

The teacher stood among them helplessly.

She had seen the river.

She had seen the names.

She had seen the ritual.

But how could she convince people who no longer wanted to hear the truth?

That evening, as the villagers gathered around Mama Bisi's food stall once again, the teacher noticed something that frightened her more than anything she had seen at the river.

Mama Bisi was standing behind her cooking pots.

Smiling.

Watching the villagers eat.

And when her eyes met the teacher's from across the crowd, that smile slowly disappeared.

Mama Bisi knew.

She knew the teacher had seen her.

And now the teacher realized something even more frightening.

She was no longer simply investigating Mama Bisi.

Mama Bisi was watching her too.

🌙 End of Chapter Three

🙏 Chapter Four – The Teacher's Warning

The teacher had never felt so helpless in her life.

She had seen Mama Bisi at the river. She had seen the strange objects, the names of the villagers, and the mysterious ritual she performed every night.

She knew something evil was happening.

But the villagers refused to listen.

Every morning, they still gathered around Mama Bisi's food stall.

The same people who had once cooked their own meals now waited eagerly for her pots to open.

And the more they ate, the more they wanted.

The teacher tried again.

She went from house to house, warning families to stop eating Mama Bisi's food.

“Please, listen to me. There is something wrong with that food.”

But the villagers laughed.

One woman shook her head.

“Teacher, you are simply jealous because you don't want to eat with us.”

Another man laughed and said,

“If Mama Bisi is evil, why has nothing happened to me?”

The teacher had no answer that could make them believe her.

She could not tell them everything she had witnessed at the river without putting herself in danger.

So she decided to keep watching.

Every night, she followed Mama Bisi from a distance.

And every night, Mama Bisi returned to the river.

The ritual continued.

Names were whispered.

Objects were placed beside the water.

And strange words disappeared into the darkness.

The teacher began writing down everything she observed.

She recorded the names she heard. She recorded the nights Mama Bisi visited the river. And she carefully watched the villagers who seemed most attached to the food.

Then something frightening happened.

One of the villagers who had been eating Mama Bisi's food every day suddenly became extremely weak.

His family took him home, but he kept asking for Mama Bisi's food.

“I need her food,” he kept saying.

The teacher's fear grew stronger.

This was no longer something she could ignore.

The next morning, she went directly to the village church.

There, she met the Reverend Father.

He was an elderly man who had lived in the village for many years and was deeply respected by the people.

The teacher told him everything.

She told him about Mama Bisi's free food. She told him how the villagers had become dependent on it. She told him about the river. She told him about the names. She told him about the strange rituals she had witnessed in the middle of the night.

The Reverend Father listened without interrupting her.

When she finished, he remained silent for a long moment.

Then he asked,

“Are you certain of what you saw?”

The teacher nodded.

“I have followed her several times. I am certain.”

The Reverend Father looked deeply concerned.

“Then we must be very careful.”

The teacher lowered her voice.

“Father, I have tried warning everyone. Nobody believes me.”

He looked toward the village through the church window.

“People will not abandon something they enjoy simply because someone tells them it is dangerous. Sometimes they must see the truth for themselves.”

The teacher asked,

“So what do we do?”

The Reverend Father stood up.

“We pray first. Then we confront what is happening.”

That evening, something unexpected happened at Mama Bisi's food stall.

The villagers gathered as usual.

Mama Bisi served plate after plate, smiling as people praised her cooking.

But this time, the Reverend Father was standing at a distance, watching her.

Mama Bisi noticed him.

Their eyes met.

Her smile disappeared.

For several seconds, neither of them looked away.

Then Mama Bisi slowly turned back to her cooking.

But the teacher noticed something strange.

Mama Bisi's hands had begun to tremble.

For the first time since the teacher had started watching her, Mama Bisi looked afraid.

The Reverend Father quietly walked away.

And that night, he began preparing for something Mama Bisi could never have expected.

Before sunrise, he gathered a few trusted elders and the teacher.

There was only one question left.

Could they expose Mama Bisi before she took control of even more villagers?

Nobody knew that the answer would come sooner than they expected.

🌙 End of Chapter Four
